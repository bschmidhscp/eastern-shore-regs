BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS FOR SOMERSET COUNTY MAY 19, 2026 PUBLIC HEARING Proposed FY 2027 Budget 

‘At 7:00 p.m., in the Meeting Room of the County Commissioners Office, Commissioner President Charles Laird, declared a Public Hearing open regarding Somerset County's Budget for Fiscal Year 2027. Also, in attendance were Commissioner Vice-President Randy Laird, Commissioner Eldon Willing, Commissioner Darryl Webster. Sr., County Administrator Emest J. Leatherbury Jr., Internal Auditor, Sharon Muir, Grant and Budget Administrator Cynthia Sapp, Finance Director Phil Thompson, Executive Aide, Lory E. Ebron, and Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez. Commissioner Craig N. Mathies, Sr., was absent from tonight’s meeting. 

President Laird asked Ms. Sapp, Ms. Muir, and Mr. Thompson to come forward. 

Mr. Leatherbury then read the Notice of Public Hearing into record as follows: 

### PUBLIC HEARING 

PROPOSED Fiscal Year 2027 Budget Notice is hereby given that the Board of County Commissioners will hold a Public 

Hearing on: 

Tuesday, May 19, 2026 at 7:00 P.M. In the County Commissioners Meeting Room Somerset County Office Complex, Room 11 1 11916 Somerset Avenue Princess Anne, Maryland 21853 

Fair Summary 

The purpose of the public hearing is to consider the Fiscal Year 2027 Operating Budget, proposed fees, rates, and charges. The Board of County Commissioners is not considering a property tax rate increase. 

‘The meeting will be held in-person to receive public comment. Individuals wishing to make oral or written comments are encouraged to attend the hearing. Anyone wishing to testify will be allowed to speak in person, The proposed FY27 budget will be made available at the public hearing on May 19, 2026 at 7 pm and will continue to be posted for public viewing through June 1, 2026, during regular office hours, weekdays 8:30 a.m. — 4:30 p.m., at the Office of the Somerset County Commissioners, Room 111, Somerset County Office Complex, 11916 Somerset Avenue, Princess ‘Anne, Maryland; and online at on the county’s website: www.somersetmd.us 

BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS FOR SOMERSET COUNTY 

Emest J. Leatherbury Jr. County Administrator NOTICE advertised on May 6 & 13, 2026

‘The proposed FY27 budget was made available to those in attendance, as well as to the 

County Website. Ms. Sapp proceeded to present the following FY 2027 Budget Highlights in her 

PowerPoint presentation (also available on the county website), summarized as follows: 

- The Commissioners have proposed to retain the current real property tax rate for FY27 at $1.00. 

- - The County will expect an increase in FY27 real property tax revenue due to increase in assessments from the State Department of Assessments and Taxation, estimated to be 8.5% or approximately $1.6 Million, and Personal Property tax increasing 2.7% or $122,900. 

- - There is $4.5 Million in total capital projects proposed, and a summary of the major projects was provided. The largest projects totaling $500,000 or more included the EMS Building for $1 Million, the new Landfill Mitigation Site for $750,000, the Airport Hangar Project for $674,000, the Detention Center Ventilation System for $500,000. 

- - There are no capital projects budgeted for the Board of Education in FY27, for the 6" year in a row. Operational funding is included within the Maintenance of Effort (MOE). 

- - Total appropriation approved for County Roads is $2.5 Million, a minimal decrease from the prior year. 

- _ The County has proposed a significant increase in Sheriff salaries offering competitive pay to attract and keep highly trained deputies ensuring public safety is a high priority in protecting our community. 

- - $5.8 Million of General Fund is being used to balance the budget. - The current County tax rate by fiscal year was presented, shown to be at $1.00 since FY16. 

- - County revenues was presented for Real Property and Railroad for FY27 totaling $2,075,573,000. A breakdown of<sup>revenuefromProperty Taxes (FY27$24,901,445)</sup> was also provided in comparison to FY26 ($23,203,555). 

- - Other significant revenue breakdown for FY27 ($63,111,411) versus FY26 ($65,766,421) was also reviewed. A pie chart presented the percentage breakdown of revenue, with it noted that the most significant categories included Property Taxes at 39% being the highest, followed by Local Income Tax at 21%, State Disparity Grant at 13% and Fund Balance at 9%. 

- - FY27 ($63,111,411) expenditures by category was also provided, and compared to FY26 ($65,766,421). A pie chart was also provided to show the percentage of expenditures, with the most si ignificant categories being Public Safety at 32%, Education at 21%, General Government at 19%, and Public Works at 12%. 

- ~ A breakdown of Education expenditures for FY27 ($13,406,071) as compared to FY26 ($13,434,354) was also provided. 

Ms. Sapp concluded her presentation at this time. 

President Charles Laird asked if there was anyone present wishing to comment in favor of 

the FY27 Budget being presented. There were none. 

President Charles Laird asked if there any opponents present wishing to speak. 

Mr. Mike McCready, 11341 Hayman Drive, Princess Anne, MD came forward. Mr. McCready questioned if the County and the City of Crisfield still share airport upgrades (50%/50%). President Laird explained that the City is a partner but does not have the financial input ability to contribute as expected. Regarding the hangar project, it was noted that the County will be receiving any of the proceeds from the project, being the City was unable to contribute funding. Mr. McCready noted previously being a commissioner for 8 years, and questioned why

the budget has increased 50% in four years, going from $43 Million to $67 Million. He noted income in our county is not going up versus the increase in taxes, also questioning the Roads appropriation, noting he assumes we are keeping the salaries going. He stated citizens are worried about taxes and assessments going up, questioning why the FY27 tax rate is remaining at $1.00 versus the constant yield of $.92.6, creating a tax income increase of 7.5%. If the tax rate would be set at $92.6, it would help the citizens with their tax payments. He stated it keeps just going up, questioning affordability for people to live here. He stated he is seriously worried, acknowledging how long the commissioners and their families have lived here and opposes a 50% increase as previously stated. 

Mr, William Michel, 11338 Hayman Drive, Princess Anne MD came forward. Mr. Michel stated he looked at what was paid in taxes, noting it has gone up 40% in two years, noting in the budget it was $56 Million versus $65 Million. He questioned if taxes are going up 20% or more, stating it concerns him, looking back 20 years ago when the amount of taxes would go up and down, based on the cost of inflation, which was noted as being 3% last year. He feels the budget is getting out of hand and something is not being done right. He feels that the County should be run as a business, and should be more stringent adding that if you can’t afford the steak tonight, eat chicken. 

Mr. Robert J. Mcintyre, 18101 Mt. Vernon Road, Princess Anne MD came forward. He first advised that no one can go thru the budget packet 10 minutes before the meeting and be prepared to talk about anything. He noted spending money for the Golf Course ($200,000). He also discussed complaining about the 1.7 miles of Polks Road, which continues to be a danger for the community. He advised he was told by Mr. Barnes that it would cost $700,000 to fix 1.6 miles of road, noting other spending, he feels he would rather have the road fixed. He also discussed people out of state using the road, to access the ferry. He noted the presentation provided at the two-o’clock meeting, commenting on Vice President Randy Lairds comment on the topic of residents who do not have water that was presented during Board of Health meeting, stating he does not have an understanding about that and did not realize it is happening in our county and in other counties. He advised that we have to start taking care of our local people, mentioning Allen and Polks road both being horrible, the tide coming in, and questioning what's going to happen when Smith Island goes under water in the next 20 years; where will those people go.
