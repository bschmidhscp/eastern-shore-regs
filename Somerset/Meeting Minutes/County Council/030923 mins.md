Somerset County Sanitary Commission Minutes of March 9 2023 

A regular session of the Somerset County Sanitary Commission was held on Thursday, March 9, 2023 at 1:00 p.m. in Room 111 of the Somerset County Office Complex. Members present were Adam Riggin, Chairman, Charles Bagley, Vice-Chairman, Dennis Williams, Secretary-Treasurer, Maxine Landon, Member (exited meeting at 2:00 p.m.), and Barbara Somers, Member. Also in attendance were Anthony Stockus, General Manager, Earl Ludy, Superintendent, Starr Mister, Office Manager, Jennie Marshall, Senior Administrative Aide, Trish Raab, Administrative Assistant, Heather Konyar, Attorney, Cockey, Brennan & Maloney, Josh Taylor, P.E., Davis Bowen & Friedel, and Richard Crumbacker, Somerset County Times. There were no Public Attendees. 

Mr. Riggin called the meeting to order at 1:00 p.m. 

### General Business — Information 

Mr. Stockus was happy to announce, Adam Riggin, Commissioner of the Crisfield Peripheral Area, has been reappointed by the Somerset County Commissioners to serve another sixyear term with the Sanitary District Commission Board. 

The Commission presented Earl Ludy with a recognition plaque displaying Mr. Ludy’s feature in Maryland Rural Water Association’s, The Maryland Current, Summer Edition. Mr. Stockus highlighted Mr. Ludy’s knowledge and his commitment to the water and wastewater industry. 

### Princess Anne - Information 

Mr. Stockus informed the Commission, there has been no movement since the last session regarding the Princess Anne Wastewater Treatment Plant ENR Upgrade Project. 

Mr. Stockus informed the Board, there has been no movement regarding the Princess Anne Color Removal Project. SCSD is awaiting determination of MDE State Revolving Fund Drinking Water Application for funding. 

Mr. Stockus updated the Commission regarding the Princess Anne Wastewater Treatment Plant Lab Project stating the Lab Construction Bid Opening was held on March 7, 2023; Mr. Ludy has prepared a recommendation to award bid during the action portion of the Commission meeting. 

Mr. Stockus informed the Board of a potential customer joining the Industrial Park, a company specializing in bio-digesting chicken litter into methane gas. Mr. Ludy has concerns with capacity, as they would require pretreatment for their processing, using around 700,000 gallons of water a day. Mr. Stockus stated at this point it is preliminary information.

## Authorization of Legal Counsel Change 

During the Regular Session held on March 12" 2020 the Commission voted to hire Webb, Cornbrooks, Wilber, Vorhis, Douse, Leslie & Mathers, LLP for all legal matters containing the City of Crisfield and the Crisfield Peripheral area. At the time, Cockey, Brennan & Maloney P.C. law firm were representing both the Sanitary District and the City of Crisfield, creating a potential conflict of interest. The City of Crisfield has recently discontinued legal services with CBM and has contracted Webb, Cornbrooks, Wilber, Vorhis, Douse, Leslie & Mathers for their

legal matters. With these recent changes, Management is recommending the Sanitary District return all legal matters regarding Crisfield back to CBM. Ms. Somers seconded the motion; the motion carried unanimously. 

Princess Anne — Action 

#### Award of Bid 

The Construction Bids for the Princess Anne Wastewater Treatment Plant Lab Building were opened and read aloud on March 8" 2023. There were 2 bids for the project, Delmarva Buildings with a bid of $374,010 and Pole Buildings Unlimited with a bid of $183,761. Mr. Ludy is recommending Pole Buildings Unlimited (low Bidder) as their bid falls in line with the Engineering estimate of $180,000. Mr. Bagley made a motion to award the construction bid for the Princess Anne Wastewater Treatment Plant Lab to Pole Buildings Unlimited. Ms. Somers seconded the motion; the motion carried to approval unanimously. 

### Repair estimate 

Mr. Ludy presented an estimate to repair one of the three BNR pumps at the Princess Anne Wastewater Treatment Plant. These pumps are over twenty years old and are necessary for the proper operation of the WWTP. The cost for the repair is $16,959. A new replacement pump would be $25,255 and take 12 weeks to arrive. Mr. Bagley made a motion to approve the repair estimate provide by Hill’s Industrial in the amount of $16,956.50. Ms. Somers seconded the motion; the motion carried unanimously to approval. 

### Request for additional credit 

During the Regular Session held on February 9" 2023 Nicole Potosky, owner of 11825 Beechwood Street, appealed her leak credit she received for 2 leaks occurring during the September and December 2022 quarters. Ms. Potosky was unhappy with the credit and requested to be present on the phone during the Commission decision. Ms. Potosky spoke during the meeting and expressed her many concerns. Ms. Potosky hada lot of questions for the Board, too many to address during the meeting. The Commission requested Ms. Potosky provide the District with her concerns and questions in writing so the Board/Staff can be better prepared to address her concerns at the next meeting; Ms. Potosky agreed. The Commission tabled the additional credit request. 

Ms. Potosky has since sent Management her questions and concerns. Ms. Mister addressed a few items Ms. Potosky had, specifically monthly billing, stating the District has explored this option in the past but found it to be unfeasible as cost of postage and number of bills would increase dramatically. Ms. Potosky also questioned the accuracy of the water meter. Management recommended to take no action on her request at this time in order to send the

Smith Island — Action 

## Change Order #2 

The Commission were presented with a non-compensitory change order for the (SICWP) Pump Stations project. The purpose of this change order is for a time extension of 83 days due to abnormal weather delays (tornado) and lack of access to the Ewell Dock. Ms. Landon made a motion to approve Change Order #2 for the SICWP Pump Stations project. Mr. Bagley seconded the motion; the motion carried unanimously. 

### Amendment #5 

George, Miles & Buhr, LLC has presented the District with an amendment to the SICWP Owner-Engineer Agreement for additional engineering services to include work related to the rebid of the Subaqueous Force Main construction contract. The first bid expired without award and was rejected due to lack of funding. This amendment will increase the engineering costs for the project by $23,000 but is necessary inorder to move forward with the project. Ms. Landon made a motion to approve Amendment #5 to the SICWP Budget. Mr. Bagley seconded the motion; the motion unanimously carried to approval. 

### Transportation purchase 

During the regular session held on March 9" 2023, the Commission tabled the purchase of a golfcart for Tylerton because Ms. Landon expressed her concerns with the purchase price and suggested a small car would be more valuble than a golfcart. Ms. Landon offered to shop around for a small car in the meantime. 

Ms. Landon has since found a small sedan (2001 Saturn) with a purchase price of $2,000 plus $150 for cost of barge. After weighing cost and savings for both the car and the golfcart, the Commission believed the car would be more beneficial to the Island. Ms. Landon made a motion to purchase the 2001 Saturn contingent upon passing inspection at Seller's Cost (John’s Auto). Mr. Williams seconded the motion; the motion carried unanimously to approval.
