Somerset County Sanitary Commission Minutes of September 11, 2025 

A regular session of the Somerset County Sanitary Commission was held on Thursday, September 11, 2025 at 1:00 p.m. in Room 111 of the Somerset County Office Complex. Members present were Rev. Charles Bagley, Interim Chairman, Dennis Williams, Secretary-Treasurer, Maxine Landon, Member, Barbara Somers, Member, and Faith Elliott, Member. Also in attendance were Josh Taylor, General Manager, Richard Padgette, Superintendent, Cody Bradshaw, Assistant Superintendent, Starr Mister, Office Manager, Jennie Marshall, Senior Administrative Aide, Trish Raab, Administrative Assistant, Heather Konyar, SCSD Legal Counsel, Cockey, Brennan, & Maloney, Chris Derbyshire, P.E., George, Miles, & Buhr, Jesse Bedsworth, Chief Water Operator, and Richard Crumbacker, Somerset County Times. #### General Business — Information 

Jesse Bedsworth, Chief Water Operator, and Dillon West, Water Operator, (not present) were recognized for 10 years of service with the Sanitary District. Mr. Taylor and the Commission presented Mr. Bedsworth with a plaque thanking him for his hard work and dedication over the last decade. 

The Financial Quarterly Analysis report was previously distributed to the Board for review. The fourth quarter (FY25) revenues exceeded expenses by $234,839.79. The Commission had no questions or comments regarding the quarterly report. Mr. Taylor informed the Board the District has drafted a Water Quality Complaint response form for our customers to complete if they feel there is a problem with their water quality. Although the District doesn’t receive many complaints, this procedure will allow the customer to be heard and will give our Field Technicians a clear perspective of their issue with the water. This form will also serve as documentation that could help the District receive funding in the future for improvements by MDE (Maryland Department of Environment) and USDA (United States Department of Agriculture). It will have a field for description of the complaint as well as the actions taken by SCSD to help resolve the matter. The Commission were in favor of this form as a good solution to help with resolving customer water quality complaints.

##### Smith Island - Information 

Mr. Taylor updated the Commission regarding the Smith Island Clean Water Project (SICWP) stating the WWTP Upgrade project is 94% complete, a punch list inspection will be addressed during final walk through prior to project completion. ### Fairmount/Rumbley - Information 

As discussed during the August 14" 2025 Commission meeting, on June 24" 2025, the Sanitary District received a petition requesting extension of sewer service to Frenchtown/Rumbley. The petition was signed by approximately 26 property owners, which is a quarter of the 104 properties in the Frenchtown/Rumbley water service area. Prior to the August meeting, the District sent a letter to each of the petition signers letting them know that the petition would be placed on the agenda for introductory discussion during August's Sanitary Commission meeting. During the meeting the Board received comments from residents both for and opposed to the extension.

<!-- page 4 -->
3 

On August 20" 2025 the District sent a letter to all residents who would be affected by the extension (of whom are currently served water service by the District) informing them of the petition and potential sewer line extension to Rumbley/Frenchtown and encouraging input from the residents. 

Mr. Taylor stated this evaluation is only entering the preliminary planning phase. Next, the Sanitary District will be collaborating with the Somerset County Commissioners to start the process of evaluating options to serve the area with sewer service. The District will engage a professional engineering firm to prepare a Preliminary Engineering Report (PER) and an Environmental Report (ER), if the County Commissioners are willing to fund these reports. If the project moves forward thereafter, the reports will likely be reimbursed by either MDE or USDA. The PER and ER will evaluate the following: 

1. Different methods and options for serving the area with sewer. 

2. Health and Environmental impacts of the proposed project and different service options. 

3. Capital Costs to serve the area and financial impacts of the project. 

4. O&M (Operation and Maintenance) Costs to serve the area and long-term budget impacts of the project. 

The PER and ER will be used to determine if service to this area is physically and financially feasible, and plan/budget for any impacts. If the project moves forward, the PER and ER would be utilized to submit for grant and loan funding from various federal and state agencies. We will keep Somerset County Commissioner, Eldon Willing, representative for District 3, and the County updated if the planning phase of the project moves forward, and provide project updates at future Sanitary Commission meetings.

#### Request for credit 

Helen Ogden, property owner of 8196 Upper Hill Road, had a leak repaired by her plumber on August 4'" 2025. After the repair Ms. Ogden called the District to have her water turn back on. Upon arrival our Field Technicians observed the meter spinning and documented on the work order that they believed a leak was still present. Based on this assumption, the water was cut back off. Our Field Technician called Ms. Ogden and informed her she still had a leak as the meter was spinning. Ms. Ogden then called her plumber back to her residence to check for leaks, he informed her the leak was indeed repaired there were no additional leaks. It now appears that the meter was spinning because of something else and not an active leak. Although we did not advise Ms. Ogden to hire a plumber, once we communicated our Field Technician’s observation, she sought the services of her plumber. Ms. Ogden is requesting reimbursement for her plumber’s fee of $149.00 to return and inspect for additional leaks, as well as the four miscellaneous fees of $35.00 each to turn the meter off and on as she feels this is not her responsibility and she was misinformed by the District. After investigating the matter, Management recognizes that although our staff did not directly advise her to hire a plumber, the work order comment went beyond factual observation, and the assumption that may have influenced her decision to hire a plumber. In the future, Field Technicians will be instructed to document only what is observed, for example, noting that the meter is spinning without drawing conclusions about the cause. Determining whether a plumber is

##### Request for Credit 

Brandy Teagle, was not present for her requested appointment with the Board to discuss, and possibly request a credit, her parent's property located at 30432 Oak Street. 

Ms. Mister gave the Board a background of what Ms. Teagle was addressing in the event she reschedules her appointment or requests action be taken in the future. 

First, it’s important to clarify that Brandy Teagle is not the owner of the property in question, she is representing her parents, who are the actual owners. Until now, all the communications have been directly with her parents. Follow-up communications with Brandy Teagle were on the phone. The District acknowledges that on November 12! 2024, the property owners contacted the District requesting that water service be cut off at the property. At that time, the District office staff explained the District's policy that a cutoff form must be completed (to ensure the property is not occupied and to verified the property owner is making the request). A copy of the cutoff form was mailed to the address provided by the customer; however, the completed form was never returned to the District. As a result, no cutoff was performed.

<!-- page 10 -->
9 

Because the bill was unusually high and the owners disputed it, we took the additional step of sending the water meter out for independent, third-party testing, at the District’s cost. The testing confirmed the meter met AWWA (American Water Works Association) standards and that the readings were accurate and verified. 

In response to the unusually high bill, the District provided the owners with a leak credit form. If they can identify and document a leak, they may qualify for a leak credit under the District's policy. This credit can range from two hundred dollars to thousands of dollars depending on the type of leak and whether the water entered and was treated by the sewer system. To ensure an independent review, we are also recommending the owners engagea local licensed plumber to inspect the property. Having a third-party professional, with no prior involvement, will help provide clarity and fairness to the situation. 

If the owners have reason to believe this issue stems from criminal conduct, such as water theft, we encourage them to file a police report with the appropriate authorities. In addition to help ease the financial burden on the customer, Management is also willing to recommend that the Commission waive the accrued interest on this account. Furthermore, we are prepared to recommend offering a two-year, Zero percent interest payment plan. However, we do want to be transparent, if the property is sold before the balance is paid, the full amount would be due upon settlement. 

Ms. Mister also stated, just to note, in her time at the District, the Commission has never written off verified consumption. Any adjustments to an account have always been as a result of the leak credit policy. Management understands this is a stressful and difficult situation for this family, and we are committed to working with them in a fair and compassionate way while still following our established policies. 

The commission tabled this matter due to the fact that Ms. Teagle was not present to discuss and request a credit on her parent's behalf.
