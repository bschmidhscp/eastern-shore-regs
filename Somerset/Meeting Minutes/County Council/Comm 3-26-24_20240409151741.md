Mr. Gary Powell, Emergency Services, came forward at this time to discuss the bid received from Chesapeake Mission Critical LLC regarding Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS) Replacements for the Princess Anne Tower Site and the Marion Tower Site totaling $102,138.00. Mr. Powell explained that the cost individually breaks down to Princess Anne in the amount of $49,664.00 and Marion for $52,474.00, which are very important pieces of equipment at our tower sites. The proposal came in $42,138 over budget; however, he explained the importance of these tower site units that are 20 years old and have been having problems recently, explaining that when the backup generator kicks on the Princess Anne tower unit goes down, it affects the 911 call center. 

Commissioner Mathies made a motion to approve the bid submitted by Chesapeake Mission Critical LLC in the amount of$102,138.00. Commissioner Willing seconded the motion. By show of hands, the motion carried 5-0. 

Mr. Jesse Drewer, Director ofTechnical and Community Services came forward. First, Mr. Drewer presented the final draft of the Nuisance Code Amendment for final adoption. This amendment is designed to address Non-Migratory Birds (such as chickens, roosters, fowl) not currently regulated by our Nuisance Code. Mr. Drewer advised that Attorney Kirk Simpkins has reviewed the language proposed, and recommends that the wording used, “non-migratory birds” be revised to be specific to the types of birds intended, which are chickens and roosters. One written comment was received from Mr. Somers which explained “histoplasmosis”, an infectious disease of the lungs caused by a fungus. The Board discussed their issues of concern, which included residents who have larger lots, questioning if allowances need to be included in the language regarding the acreage of the property. It was also noted that there has only been one instance occurring between neighbors resulting in the complaint which initiated the request to review the matter within the ordinance. Concern was expressed as well how this wording amendment may unintentionally impact a commercial chicken farmer as well. President Laird

Next, Mr. Drewer presented the proposed updated Chesapeake Bay Critical Area Maps for adoption. He explained the State ofMaryland Critical Area Program, established in direct response to growing concems over the decline of the quality and productivity of the waters of the Chesapeake Bay and its tributaries. It is a requirement of the State of Maryland Critical Area Commission to review the maps and ordinances periodically. He reminded the Board that a public information session was held in May 2023, with modifications made based on public comment. The Board held their public hearing on February 27, 2024, with maps being made available for viewing. Mr. Drewer advised that overall, the county gained 1,846 acres added as critical area, with 2,071 being removed; netting a total critical area loss of-225 acres, It was noted that the maps included in the agenda today did not include street names, making it hard to identify the areas marked. Mr. Drewer stated that larger maps are available in his department to view. It was questioned if the county can appeal the map designations as presented by the State, Mr, Drewer did not know, and stated he will inquire. He did note that based on the revised maps presented, his department currently has a case submitted for a project on a property that will be allowed due to the revised map; so the revision is benefiting some, but not others. 

Therefore, Vice President Randy Laird made a motion to table approval of the amended Critical Area Map adoption and requested to see a clearer/better map. Commissioner Mathies seconded the motion. By show of hands, the motion carried 5-0.

Last, Mr. Drewer presented his request for a FY24 budget reallocation. He explained that the departments 36” Cannon Plotter/Scanner that is used to print and scan site and projects plans is 14 years old and has suffered two breakdowns this fiscal year. Parts are no longer manufactured for the unit, and the maintenance lease vendor has notified that the contract will not be renewed when it comes due in July 2024. With this said, Mr. Drewer advised that replacement of the unit was planned as a fiscal year 2025 expense; however, through departmental savings and unexpended budgeted funds, there is sufficient funds in his FY24 budget to cover the cost of anew machine, totaling $14,554.75, which includes a 2-year extended warranty and a $500 trade in discount for the existing machine if the quote is accepted before March 31, 2024. 

Vice President Randy Laird made a motion to approve the FY24 budget reallocation request to purchase a new Plotter/Scanner for the department at a cost of $14,554.75. 

Commissioner Mathies questioned if this equipment is used often. Mr, Drewer said yes, noting that other departments, such as the County Engineer and Maintenance Department also use this unit in addition to his department for both printing and scanning regarding site plans and other documents that are presented during board and commission meetings his department holds. 

With it noted there was a motion made on the floor, Commissioner Webster seconded the motion. Commissioner Willing voted in favor, with Commissioner Mathies and President Charles Laird opposed; therefore, the motion carried 3-2-0.

Next, the Board discussed the Smith Island Tipping Fee. Noting there was confusion during the last meeting, it was clarified that in accordance with Ordinance No. 1140, effective June 30, 2019, the current fee set for Smith Island is $60.00 per developed land parcel, matching what was the current fee was for other county residents. 

Commissioner Willing asked why is Smith Island residents billed per developed land parcel, and it was explained by Mr. Cavanaugh that the fee for Smith Island resident’s is applied to the county tax bill versus the requirement to have a sticker being they’re on the island. Commissioner Willing questioned why it can’t be done county wide this same way, as previously discussed. Mr. Taylor noted that based on current code, it is only allowed for Smith Island. The county would have to propose legislation to have the process changed and could be submitted as proposed legislation. Mr. Cavanaugh also noted that the City and Town residents are billed to their municipality tax bill; however, City residents can opt out ofthe billing should they want to. Town residents do not have the option to opt out. 

The discussion then moved to a water issue on Riley Roberts Road, with it noted by Commissioner Willing that there are four roads at Danes Quarter that are flooded repeatedly. Commissioner Willing also stated that the tide gate was missing, which Mr. Cavanaugh confirmed. Mr. Cavanaugh explained that they are using different gates now, that deteriorate less, and tidal surge doesn’t cause the gate doors to float out. It was also noted that the tide levels have water coming up higher that the gates as well. Long Point Road flooding was also noted, with it stated that it was raised in a section that experienced frequent flooding. President Charles Laird also discussed flooding near the canal, affecting Jacksonville Road, where there is a missing tide gate, and he stated only one side is holding. Mr. Cavanaugh said he has spoken to the Park Manager, noting the problem is going to be expensive and is on State property. Tide gate repairs and ditching had also been performed within the past 6-7 years in the park. 

Mr. Taylor redirected the discussion to return to the matter of the Smith Island Transfer Station fee, and if the Board wished to raise the fee per developed land. The discussion continued, with it questioned if ALL county residents should pay the same. It was noted that it’s not really comparing apples to apples to consider the Smith Island Fee (charged to tax bill per developed lot) to the rest of the county which allows the purchase of the sticker to be optional.
