Ms. Yvette Cross, Emergency Services Director came forward to accept sealed bids submitted regarding the Uninterrupted Power Supply Replacement Project. President Charles Laird proceeded to open the single sealed bid received from Carber Power Technologies in the amount of $70,708.00. Ms. Cross advised she would review the bid received with the experienced professionals and return with a recommendation at the November 18, 2025 meeting. 

Mr. Jesse Drewer, Department of Technical and Community Services (DTCS) Director came forward to present the SFY26 Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) application and resolution for approval. He explained that applications are due by October 24, 2025. Since the FY22 Housing Program ended on September 30, 2025, all $325,000 in funds were spent by the deadline. The proposed FY26 application will be a two-year program and be submitted for $350,000 ($215,000 for rehab grants, $100,000 for emergency grants and $35,000 administration costs). A public hearing was held on October 7, 2025 with no public comments received. 

Commissioner Craig N. Mathies, Sr., made a motion to approve the FY26 CDBG Rehabilitation Grant Application and Resolution No. 1215 as presented. Vice President Randy Laird seconded the motion. By show of hands, the motion carried 4-0. 

Next, Mr. Drewer presented a rezoning request submitted by Chesapeake Renewable Energy LLC who own approximately 28.52 acres of land on Route 13 and Costen Road. The owner is requesting a portion of the property (8 acres) to be rezoned from Agricultural Residential (AR) to General Industrial (I-2). Per county ordinance, this project is required to be referred to the Planning Commission for a public hearing and review by the County Commissioners. Following the decision from the Planning Commission, the County Commissioners will then hold a second public hearing. 

Commissioner Craig Mathies made a motion to forward the rezoning request from. Chesapeake Renewable Energy LLC to the Planning Commission. Commissioner Eldon Willing seconded the motion. By show of hands, the motion carried 4-0. 

Mr. Drewer presented a request for Release of Lien regarding 157 S Fourth Street, Crisfield Maryland in the amount of $17,646. The grant funds from the housing rehabilitation grant program were used to fund repairs at the property, and had a fifteen (15) year lien, which expired on April 28, 2018. In order to formally close out and release the grant, the Commissioners must approve the lien release.

Mr. Taylor next discussed the Sewer Service Extension Request received for the Rumbley- 

Frenchtown area as he learns how the process works regarding new and/or extension sewer services. A petition form has been received from residents, which met the requirement of at least 25 signatures, who are currently served with drinking water and fire protection to have the sewer line extended to serve their community. Historically it was noted that the Sanitary District evaluates the petition, and brings it to the county commissioners for consideration. The next step in the process to proceed will be to have an engineering evaluation and environmental impact analysis, completed by two ‘on board’ engineers, to determine the most cost-effective option for sewer service, completed at a cost of $25,000 - $30,000 paid for by the county. Mr. Taylor discussed various funding avenues, advising that should the project move forward, grant funding would be obtained to reimburse the county. Should the county choose not to fund the project, the Sanitary District would go to the petitioners to see if they want to fund the project, but it was noted that obtaining funding would be the first step. Mr. Taylor advised that this discussion for the project is strictly informational today. 

President Charles Laird noted other area’s in his district whereby water and sewer availability are also needed. Although costly, he feels providing these services, would be an asset and tool in keeping residents in the county. 

Commissioner Willing questioned how the sewer line would be installed for the RumbleyFrenchtown project. Mr. Taylor advised probably directional boring along public roadways to minimize disturbance. Mr. Taylor left the meeting at this time. 

Next, Public Works Director Woody Barnes came forward to provide an update regarding the Detention Center Ventilation Project. He first reviewed the first phase, which addressed known ventilation concerns such as duct work, air handlers, dampeners and louvers, fan motors and belts as well as the cleaning out of duct work and vents. The initial engineers estimate to perform the work came in at $515,000 however, the work was completed for $75,000. He discussed that even though the system is old, they are still able to get parts. Mr. Barnes explained that the next phase for the project was to address broilers and plumbing which will include hot water heaters and replacement of the air handlers and ducts and also include new controls as well as upgrades to mechanical devices in Phase 1. He advised that the Detention Center is a windowless facility, which must now meet today’s regulatory standards, therefore Gipe Associates is recommending acquiring consulting assistance from Incendia, a third-party vendor

Ms. Brittany Bozman, Child Support Division came forward to present the CSEA/CRA FY 2026-023 Contract. The total cost of the program will be $386,621.00, with the county share being $146,057. Ms. Bozman explained there is a total decrease to the FY26 program cost of $62,532 due to the elimination of the part time position proposed last year, which was found to not be necessary, equipment that was already purchased and salary reduction with the retirement of Ms. Alice Bromley. This is a decrease to the county share of $23,623 for FY26. The county will be reimbursed 66% of the cost of the program under this contract. 

Commissioner Mathies made a motion to approve the CSEA/CRA FY 2026-023 Contract as presented. Commissioner Willing seconded the motion. By show of hands, the motion carried 4-0. Ms. Bozman was thanked for presenting the contract, and left the meeting at this time.

Next, Mr. Frank Parks, Franks Island requested to address the Board. Mr. Parks stated he had questions for more information. He asked where the county was in the process, explaining the history of the rebuilding following the fire of the structure in January. He stated that he has been working on the rebuilding process since March, explaining the denial by the Critical Area Commission, followed by his appeal, which he won because they agreed to do more planting. The latest hurdle has now been a zoning problem, as he has been told they are not properly zoned. He advised that he questioned zoning earlier this year, and was told by Mr. Comish that a zoning change was not needed in an email dated June 2025 in that the property was considered nonconforming use if rebuilding occurs within 12 months, so he was surprised to hear that this is now an issue as he thought it was addressed last June. He also advised that the parking issue has also been resolved. He is concerned that if the property is rezoned, other restrictions may be added on. He stated he wanted to express his concerns as he was told the Board would be discussing the matter in closed session and he wanted to share a background for what was being proposed.
