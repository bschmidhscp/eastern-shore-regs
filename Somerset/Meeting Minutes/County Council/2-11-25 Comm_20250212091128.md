### BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS FOR SOMERSET COUNTY 

January 28, 2025 

At 2:00 p.m., President Charles Laird called the Board of County Commissioners for Somerset County to open in Regular Session. With Mr. Drewer remaining present, Mr. Leatherbury advised that during the December 3, 2024 meeting, Mr. Cornish presented a request from Bohler Engineering, on behalf of Nexamp Solar to proceed with the project, located on Kemp’s Nursery Road. No action was taken during that meeting. For Nexamp to proceed, the Board of Commissioners are required to recommend the project to the Planning Commission for their review and to hold a Public Hearing. Mr. Drewer explained the process to proceed in compliance with the Somerset County Zoning Code requires approval of the Utility Scale Solar Energy Facility Floating Zone (SEF). Following the Planning Commission Public Hearing the project will then come back to the County Commissioners for amendments to the zoning map, and approval of the floating zone as the final deciding authority. A discussion was held, and it was noted that this is a step that the county is required to do, according to our ordinance. Commissioner Webster expressed his disapproval of being forced to vote favorably for something he adamantly disagrees with. Frustration was also expressed regarding lack of authority Somerset County has regarding these solar projects being mandated by the state. 

Vice President Randy Laird asked if there is anything the county can do at our local level to avoid this. Mr. Drewer stated, unfortunately no, and advised that a new legislative bill is expected to be dropped this year which is even more aggressive with encouraging solar energy and removing even more local authority and will most likely require a total re-write of our solar code to preempt state standards. 

Reluctantly, Vice President Randy Laird made a motion to proceed, recommending referral of the Nexamp project to the Planning Commission for a Public Hearing. Commissioner Willing seconded the motion. Commissioner Webster voted in favor and President Charles Laird opposed the motion. Therefore, the motion carried 3-1-0.
