BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS FOR SOMERSET COUNTY **WORK SESSION TUESDAY FEBRUARY 3, 2026** 

At 2:00 pm, the Somerset County Commissioners met in a work session. Present for today’s meeting were President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, and Commissioner Craig Mathies, Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Darryl Webster. 

Also in attendance were County Administrator Ernest Leatherbury and Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez. 

President Charles Laird called the meeting to order and asked Commissioner Craig Mathies to lead those present in Devotion and the Pledge of Allegiance. 

Mr. Marty Koerner, Chairman of the Police Accountability Board, was invited to come forward to present the Somerset County Police Accountability Board (SCPAB) End of Year Report (dated December 17, 2025). He advised that the board met quarterly during 2025 in accordance with the Maryland Police Accountability Act of 2021 and Somerset County Resolution No. 1,177 to review citizen complaints and discuss policing matters in Somerset County. He then provided a brief reminder of board members for each board: the SCPAB members appointed by the Somerset County Commissioners are Dr. Kirkland Hall Sr., Bruce Parkinson, Julie Thomas, Ivan Lankford, and Chairman Martin Koerner; and the Somerset County Administrative Charging Committee (SCACC) members are Reverend Charles Bagley, Pastor James Riley, Pastor Stephen Willing, Reverend Doctor Doretha Whittington, and Chairman Martin Koerner. Cathy Landon serves as the Civilian Trial Board member. 

Moving into the report, Mr. Koerner advised that, during 2025, the SCPAB met with local law enforcement leadership to review case outcomes and discuss issues related to policing in Somerset County. The Board also responded to media inquiries. The SCACC reviewed all cases presented during the year and issued decisions in a timely manner. From January 1, 2025 through the present reporting period, ten citizen complaints were reported to the SCPAB. Two complaints were filed against the Somerset County Sheriff’s Office alleging unprofessional conduct, with one case under investigation. Eight complaints were filed against the Princess Anne Police Department involving allegations of inappropriate conduct or a departmental collision, with two cases under investigation. No complaints were reported against the Crisfield Police Department. Citizen complaints decreased from sixteen in 2024 to ten in 2025, a 37.5 percent reduction. In 2024, three complaints were substantiated and resulted in minor administrative corrective action. In 2025, no complaints have been substantiated to date and several were determined to be unfounded. Based on the Board’s review, no police accountability trends were identified. The Maryland State Police and the University of Maryland Eastern Shore Police Department report complaint investigations directly to the State of Maryland Police Accountability Board rather than the SCPAB. During each quarterly meeting, Mr. Koerner advised that the Board asked law enforcement leadership whether any police accountability trends existed, whether the SCPAB could assist in improving policing practices, and whether any matters should be brought to the attention of the Somerset County Commissioners. Mr. Koerner advised that any questions (which do not happen often) are discussed amongst the board and forward to Mr. Leatherbury for further discussion. Another item of discussion was the need for time limits for filing citizen complaints and recommended that each law enforcement agency establish a reasonable policy addressing this issue. Mr. Koerner then stated that the SCPAB also selected the first Law Enforcement Officer of the Year for 2025. The award will be presented during the March 2026 SCPAB meeting. In conclusion, Mr. Koerner advised that the SCPAB will continue working with law enforcement agencies and county officials to review complaints and improve policing practices in Somerset County. 

The Board thanked Mr. Koerner for his report and the work of the SCPAB and SCACC. 

With no further business to discuss in work session, a motion was made by Commissioner Darryl Webster and Commissioner Craig Mathies to adjourn work session and enter into special session. By a show of hands in favor by all present members of the board, the motion carried and the board adjourned work session and entered into special session at 2:10 pm.
