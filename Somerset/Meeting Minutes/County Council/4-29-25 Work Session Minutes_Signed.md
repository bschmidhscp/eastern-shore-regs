# BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS FOR SOMERSET COUNTY **WORK SESSION TUESDAY APRIL 29, 2025** 

At 1:00 p.m., President Charles Laird called the Board of Commissioners for Somerset County to open in a Work Session. Present for today’s meeting were President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, and Commissioner Craig Mathies. Commissioner Eldon Willing and Commissioner Darryl Webster were not present for today’s meeting. 

Also in attendance were Deputy County Administrator Ernie Leatherbury and Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez. 

President Charles Laird asked Commissioner Craig Mathies to lead those present in devotion and the Pledge of Allegiance. 

Following this, the Board invited Dr. Ava Tasker-Mitchell, Ms. Linda Johnson, Ms. Tracey Bartemy, and Ms. Christina South to come forward to provide an overview of the Somerset County Public School budget process. 

The meeting began with Ms. Linda Johnson referring the Board to the distributed packets, which included the proposed FY26 budget, a line-item breakdown, and a list of budget cuts. Ms. Johnson advised that the process began with input from principals and supervisors, which was consolidated into a comprehensive request. The initial budget version showed a $11.5 million overage of the projected revenues for FY26. Since then, multiple rounds of cuts have brought the budget into balance, aided by a $1 million funding request to the County submitted the prior week. The cuts were cross-referenced with the full budget document for clarity. 

Ms. Johnson outlined the budget’s organization according to state-mandated categories; including administration, instruction, transportation, and operations. Ms. Johnson then directed attention to revenue estimates, noting the county’s required contribution had risen to $12,100,404.00 (an increase of approximately $1.6 million over the prior year) while state aid had decreased by $537,000. This shortfall was partially offset by $300,000 in carryover funds. The overall budget reflected a $2.3 million increase from the previous year (or $1.3 million excluding the new funding request). 

Ms. Johnson then provided a line-by-line review of expenditures, noting that the Board of Education budget remained unchanged. Executive Administration increased with the reinstatement of the Deputy Superintendent, while Finance & Business saw minor adjustments due to rising bank

fees. Human Resources cost expanded with the addition of a director and a half position for benefits. Mid-Level Administration decreased slightly, largely due to reduced technology spending. The Office of the Principal saw modest increases tied to staffing changes at Washington High. Ms. Johnson then advised of the instructional program and development budget, noting the $127,000 increase is temporary and reflects estimated step and COLA costs for Unit 2 administrative staff. These costs will be properly allocated after negotiations conclude. The CTE Principal’s office budget covers the principal, an administrative assistant, and supplies. Instructional salaries for non-special education staff (including teachers, paraprofessionals, and counselors) show temporary increases for estimated raises in Units 1 and 3, pending final negotiations. Textbook and supply costs increased modestly by $10,000. Other instructional costs rose mainly due to software licensing, including a $55,000 MAP assessment program now funded through the general budget rather than grants. Special education costs increased in consultant fees due to difficulties hiring in-house therapists, requiring agency contracts. Administrative staffing in special education remains stable. Student personnel and health services budgets decreased due to reallocating a supervisor to a math position. The mental health coordinator was promoted, and a new hire will lead to salary adjustments. Transportation costs dropped in general school bus insurance but remained steady for special education routes. Additional transportation for athletics and “families in transition” continues to rise, with coordination between counties helping control costs. Utility expenses are expected to increase by approximately $100,000. Maintenance costs grew due to athletic field upkeep, building repairs, and reallocating staff to assist with growing regulatory compliance in operations, transportation, and maintenance. 

At the conclusion of the budget discussion, Commissioner Craig Mathies commended the presentation, calling it the most transparent in his 14 years of service. 

Commissioner Randy Laird expressed concern about recurring mid-year reallocations, particularly from Special Education. 

Ms. Johnson confirmed that such transfers may occur again, particularly to cover instructional salaries, as budgets must be adjusted to prevent any category from ending in deficit. Ms. Johnson then clarified that funds previously moved from Special Education remained within the program, specifically redirected for transportation needs. 

Commissioner Charles Laird echoed the earlier praise, affirming this as the clearest and most detailed budget presentation the board has received.

For clarification on the budget presentation, Dr. Tasker-Mitchell advised that any boxed numbers in the budget indicate a corresponding detail page. The first column reflects initial requests, while the second column shows what remains in the current budget. Many items show zeros, indicating cuts. New staff position requests were also reviewed, with notes on which were included or removed. Clarification was also provided regarding the staffing at Washington High School, specifically the presence of three assistant principals. This was explained as a temporary reassignment to support administrative leadership. Board-requested cuts were also noted in the budget. 

A discussion followed concerning community perceptions and misinformation, particularly around staffing and school leadership decisions. The Board emphasized the importance of understanding and clearly communicating these decisions to the public to reduce confusion. Retention and recruitment were discussed as growing challenges. Recent resignations were attributed to more competitive pay in neighboring counties, despite many employees expressing a love for Somerset. The need to retain high-quality, local teachers was also stressed, and plans for exit surveys were shared to better understand staff departures. Dr. Tasker-Mitchell advised of Superintendent initiatives, including launching a Superintendent's Attendance Advisory group and a Student Advisory Committee next year with the goal to increase parent involvement and strengthen school-community relationships. 

At the conclusion of the meeting, the Board advised, meaningfully, that division between the school board and staff harms public perception and the county's overall progress. A unified, respectful approach (especially in public forums) was encouraged and that the focus must remain on the students' success above politics. 

The Board was advised for the need of a Special Session. 

Therefore, at 1:35pm, on a motion made by Commissioner Craig Mathies, seconded by Vice President Randy Laird, and carried on a vote in favor by all present members of the Board, the Board adjourned Work Session and entered into Special Session.
