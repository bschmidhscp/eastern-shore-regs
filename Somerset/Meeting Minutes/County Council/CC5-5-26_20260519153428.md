Commissioner Mathies made a motion to approve the minutes as presented. Commissioner Willing seconded the motion. All those present voted aye, therefore the motion carried 3-0. 

Mr. Woody Bares, Public Works Director, came forward at this time to present the bid recommendation regarding proposals received for the Somerset County EMS Station #10 project. He explained that the five qualified bids received were reviewed with George, Miles and Buhr, LLC (GMB), ranging from a low bid of $1,285,000.00 to a high bid of $1,822,000.00. A copy of the letter detailing the results of the review were provided in the agenda package. Mr. Barnes explained that bidders were requested to offer a total of five deductive cost alternates as itemized. Based on the base bid, the County’s selected alternates and allowances, and evaluation, GMB made the recommendation to award the bid to Whayland Company, in the amount of $1,274,775.00, plus a 10% contingency of $128,000 and allowances of $50,000.00 for the APS alert system and exhaust system upgrades for a total contract value of $1,503,275.00. 

Commissioner Eldon Willing made a motion to approve the bid contract from Whayland Company for a total contract value of $1,503,275.00. Commissioner Craig N. Mathies Sr., seconded the motion. All those present voted aye, therefore the motion carried 3-0. 

Mr. Barnes advised that he will have the County Attorney draft the contract agreement, and a pre-construction meeting will be held the last week of May. 

Next, Mr. Barnes presented the bids received regarding the Fairmount Landfill Leachate Tank proposal for engineering, permitting and consulting proposals. He discussed the problems continuing with the current tank, advising that the end result is that the tank needs to be replaced. Two bids were received as follows: 

- George, Miles and Buhr, LLC (GMB) in the amount of $44,950.00 - Pennoni in the amount of $94,600.00 

Mr. Barnes made the recommendation to award the bid to GMB for $44,950.00. He also 

noted that $125,000 had been budgeted in the FY26 budget. 

Commissioner Craig N. Mathies made a motion to approve the bid received from GMB in the amount of $44,950.00. Commissioner Eldon Willing seconded the motion. All those present voted aye, therefore the motion carried 3-0.

Ms. Manaia explained that after negotiating with CareFirst, they were able to have the cost of employee insurance premiums increase 6%, which is down from the original proposal of a 14% increase. Additionally, it was noted that the retiree plan is increasing slightly at 1.9%, as well as a slight increase to the dental and vision plans, due to increases in dental claims. Ms. Chris Widdowson, 33220 West Post Office Road requested to address the Board. She explained the Rosehip Solar project, initiated in 2019, which the community has been fighting both the concept and location of the project with the county office. Ultimately, the project was approved through the Public Service Commission (PSC) in 2023. She expressed her appreciation of the county’s attempts to halt the project, noting the community recognizes the time and expense exerted. They continue to push for the company to be in compliance. It was noted that the bond required was only $29,642 for this project, exceedingly far less than is required by other more recent projects ($500,000+) due to changes in county code. She advised that the project is on the agenda Item # 22 for the May 13, 2026 PSC meeting, noting that she has not seen a position taken by the County to date. Also, the company has submitted 6 site plans, and are still not in compliance. She discussed the county required 15-foot wide maintenance area setback, as noted in all of their plans, however the final order states a 10-foot vegetative setback from all ditches and drainage swales advising that trees are being planted in what is designated as the maintenance area. Ms. Widdowson stated that once the final order came out, all of the setbacks were gone, stating the developer is stating that no solar panels are in the setbacks, only trees. She expressed concem that signing off on this project may be setting a precedent, noting there are about 10 other projects out there right now. She advised they will continue to push the PSC, asking them to follow procedures, although they are accepting the fact that they can’t stop the project. She expressed her gratitude to Commissioner Darryl Webster, for his active response to her text, calls, emails, and his support
