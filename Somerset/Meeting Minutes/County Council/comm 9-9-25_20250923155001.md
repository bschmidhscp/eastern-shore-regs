Mr. Drewer first explained that explained that in June 2024, Somerset County entered into a contract with Smith Planning and Design to update the adopted 1996 Comprehensive Plan, a Code of Maryland requirement. Ideally, the plan should be reviewed and updated every 10 years (no more than 20 years); with Mr. Drewer advising that the last amendment to our plan was in 2010 (adding a Water Resource Element). Each chapter of the plan has now been reviewed over the past two years regarding long-range planning and is being presented today in this draft form. He also noted that on August 7, 2025 the Somerset County Planning Commission held a public hearing for this draft 2025 Comprehensive Plan, advising that no public comments were received. A favorable recommendation was made to forward the plan to the County C‘ommissioners to hold their public hearing and consider the plan for adoption.

Ms. Smith explained how they have been working with the DTCS Department, the Planning Commission, as well as Local, State and Community partners on each chapter. Comments (over 300) received during many various meetings were addressed, with drafts available online throughout the process, and concluded in this final full draft of plan being presented today that had been made available for public viewing throughout the county.

President Charles Laird asked if there were any opponents present wishing to speak. There were none. 

Mr. Leatherbury advised that written public comment would be taken for 10 days, until September 19, 2025 at 4:30 p.m. The public hearing was declared closed at 2:07 p.m. 

Mr. Drewer next presented a request to reappoint three members currently serving on the Housing Board of Review; Ms. Rosalie White, Ms. Jackie Wheatley-DeMoss and Mr. Eugene Borden whose terms expire in October 2025. All three have expressed their willingness to continue serving. 

Commissioner Darryl Webster made a motion to reappoint Ms. White, Ms. DeMoss and Mr. Borden to serve a three-year term on the Housing Board of Review. Commissioner Craig N. Mathies Sr., seconded the motion. By show of hands, the motion carried 5-0. 

Last, Mr. Drewer presented a request received from Elder George Todd/Christ Fellowship Bible Church, to approve their fee waiver request in the amount of $109.56 regarding a pre-built building they will be using for storage on church property. 

Vice President Randy Laird made motion to approve the fee waiver request in the amount of $109.56, Commissioner Darryl Webster seconded the motion. Commissioner Eldon Willing abstained; therefore, the motion carried 4-0-1. Mr. Drewer left the meeting at this time. 

Mr. John Redden, County Engineer joined the meeting by phone at this time. However, additional funding has been added to the Homestretch Grant, and therefore, amendments are needed to be approved for the agreements in place with our three service providers; Charter, Choptank and Comcast. Mr. Redden advised he is presenting the amendment for service provider Charter who will be adding 42 additional properties to serve under the agreement. He explained

President Charles Laird asked Ms. Tammy Ryman, 23244 Paul Benton Road, Wenona to come forward to address the Board. Ms. Ryman explained that she requested to speak to the commissioners today regarding non-migratory bird/backyard bird issues she is experiencing in her neighborhood. She explained that her neighbor/friend initially had flocks of chickens, guinea hens, growing to geese and ducks, and more recently goats, who have been wandering off his property and onto neighboring properties. She advised that the issue was brought up last year during meetings held on February 13 and 27, 2024, noting that even news media (WBOC and the County Times) became involved. Upon reviewing meeting minutes, she researched online, she noted that during the meeting held on March 26, 2024, Mr. Drewer presented a final draft for a resolution addressing the issue, which was reviewed by the county attorney, and included a change in language as requested by the board. It was said at that time that one instance initiated the request, but Ms. Ryman assured the board that it has been more than just one instance, and the issue is a problem through out the county. She discussed how Mr. Drewer was last asked to look into what other counties are doing, noting there has been no follow up since then. She spoke on how nasty goose feces is in that she can’t even safely use her own yard, or even let her own dog go out in the yard, She has spoken to her neighbor who will not correct the problem. She has called the Health Department and the Sherriff’s office who say they can’t address the problem with having an ordinance giving them the authority. She is repeatedly told to complain to the county commissioners who must provide an ordinance to legally address the problem. Planning and Zoning staff are advising that Somerset County is an agricultural county so don’t expect anything
