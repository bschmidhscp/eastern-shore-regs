# BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS FOR SOMERSET COUNTY **WORK SESSION TUESDAY DECEMBER 12, 2023** 

At 2:00 p.m., President Charles Laird call the Board of Commissioners for Somerset County to open in Special Session. Present for today’s meeting were President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig Mathies, Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Darryl Webster. 

Also in attendance were County Administrator Ralph D. Taylor, Deputy County Administrator Ernie Leatherbury, and Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez. 

President Charles Laird called the meeting to order and asked Commissioner Eldon Willing to lead those present in Devotion and the Pledge of Allegiance. 

Mr. Taylor welcomed Senator MaryBeth Carozza and Delegate Charles Otto to discuss Proposed Local Legislation for 2024. Senator Carozza highlighted Somerset County’s efforts to foster partnerships with neighboring counties, focusing on Crisfield tourism, UMES, and local schools. She emphasized the importance of advocating for rural counties like Somerset in state decisions and discussed public safety bills addressing violent crime and juvenile justice, with strong bipartisan support. Senator Carozza also mentioned her new role on the Apprenticeship 2030 Commission, aiming to expand apprenticeship opportunities for youth. She stressed the need for equitable transportation funding for the Eastern Shore following cuts to highway user funds and announced upcoming meetings to assess their impact. Senator Carozza then encouraged local leaders to propose flexible revisions to the Kerwin Blueprint to better suit regional needs. The discussion then shifted to juvenile crime in schools. Commissioner Craig Mathies called for more parental accountability, as current disciplinary policies contribute to teacher burnout. Senator Carozza proposed exploring behavioral learning centers or external facilities for students with severe behavioral issues. Commissioner Darryl Webster raised concerns about underreporting incidents to protect funding, emphasizing the need for transparency. 

The Board then presented several important legislative proposals, including: 

> 1. **Fire Company Appropriations** : A proposal for a 2% raise for fire companies from 2025 to 2030 was discussed (HB425 effective 7-1-24). The raise, which is implemented every five to six years, requires new legislation to continue as the current authority expires at the end of the year. 

> 2. **Volunteer Fire Rescue and EMS Administration** : Commissioners discussed the need for Somerset County to create an entity or body to administer volunteer fire rescue and EMS affairs, with potential for creating an Emergency Services Advisory Council. Additionally, the possibility of introducing a fire tax to ensure financial sustainability was suggested.

3. **County Surplus Property Sales** : A proposal to allow the County Commissioners to sell property within the Somerset County Industrial Park without requiring General Assembly approval for each transaction was raised. This would provide greater flexibility for economic development. 

4. **Liquor Bill for Service Clubs** : A request was made to enable service clubs to purchase alcohol from retail outlets instead of being restricted to specific distributors. This proposal aims to address inefficiencies and potential pricing issues. 

5. **Bond Requests and Funding Gaps** : Bond requests were discussed, including the need for additional funds for airport hangar construction, Board of Elections office renovations, and Courthouse renovations. 

6. **Board Education Projects:** Support for Greenwood Elementary’s school occupied rehab and addition project; and strong support for Princess Anne Elementary’s social-emotional program, offering a facility for children to work through issues instead of being sent home. 

The Board then addressed barriers to Career and Technology Education (CTE) access for homeschooled students, particularly regarding taxpayer-funded technical schools like Somerset County Technical High School. There was a push to allow more local students, including those from non-public schools, access to these facilities, with a proposal for county control over their use to bypass state restrictions. The Board of Education opposed including non-public school students, citing funding and state regulations, prompting a suggestion for a legislative approach, possibly in collaboration with MACo, to push for more flexibility. The proposal of staggering commissioner elections to avoid full turnover was briefly discussed, with a potential local bill. Additionally, an invitation was extended by Senator Carozza to testify at key hearings, and efforts to leverage the Tri-County Council for Somerset County’s economic development were ongoing. Lastly, requests were made for more details on the Senate Bill related to violent crime and juvenile crime legislation. 

With no further business to discuss, the Board thanked Senator Carozza and Delegate Otto for attending today’s meeting. A motion was then made by Commissioner Darryl Webster and seconded by Vice President Randy Laird to adjourn the Work Session. By a show of hands in favor by President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig Mathies, Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Darryl Webster, the motion carried and the meeting was adjourned at 3:09 p.m.
