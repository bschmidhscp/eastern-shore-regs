BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS FOR SOMERSET COUNTY January 30, 2024 

At 2:00 p.m., Vice President Randy Laird called the Board of County Commissioners for Somerset County to open in Regular Session. Also present for today’s meeting were Commissioner Darryl Webster, Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Craig N. Mathies, Sr. President Charles Laird was absent from today’s meeting. 

County Administrator Ralph D. Taylor, Deputy Administrator Emest J. Leatherbury Jr., and Executive Aide Lory E. Ebron were also present. 

Closed Session Summary Report 

On Tuesday, January 30, 2024 from 2:33 p.m. until 3:18 p.m., Vice President Randy Laird, upon a motion made by Commissioner Webster and seconded by Commissioner Mathies opened in Closed Session by Authority of the General Provisions Article of the Annotated Code of Maryland §3-305 (b)(1) the appointment, employment, assignment, promotion, discipline, demotion, compensation, removal, resignation, or performance evaluation of an appointee, employee, or official over whom it has jurisdiction. Commissioner Craig N. Mathies, Sr., Commissioner Eldon Willing voted in favor; therefore, by show of hands, the motion carried 4-0. President Charles Laird was absent from today’s meeting. 

County Administrator Ralph D. Taylor, Deputy County Administrator Ernest J. Leatherbury Jr., Ms. Lory Ebron, Executive Aide, Ms. Kim Widdowson, HR Supervisor, Acting Warden Jamie Balderson, and Charles Cavanaugh, Director of Solid Waste and Maintenance were also present. Topics discussed: (1) Hiring of Finance Director (2) Hiring of HR Director (3) Promotion to Sergeant (4) Emergency Appointment. Actions taken during closed session: (1) Approval to hire Finance Director (2) Approval to hire HR Director (3) Approval for promotion to Sergeant (4) Approval to hire Emergency Appointment. 

—_—_—_—— 

Vice President Randy Laird asked Commissioner Willing to lead those present in Devotion and the Pledge of Allegiance. 

Meeting minutes were presented for approval from the January 16, 2024 meetings. Commissioner Mathies made a motion to approve the Commissioner and Closed Session meeting minutes as presented from the January 16, 2024 meeting. Commissioner Webster seconded the motion. All those present voted in favor; therefore, the motion carried 4-0.

Next, Mr. Taylor asked Mr. Elwood “Andy” Evans and Jonathan Todd recognizing their many years of service to the County. Mr. Evans was thanked for his 30 years of service and Mr. Todd for 25 years. 

Next, Ms. Sherree Marshall, Commissioner on Aging, came forward to present her application for the Statewide Special Transportation Assistance Program (SSTAP) grant in the amount of $117,447. Notice of Opportunity for a Public Hearing was properly advertised, with there being no requests to hold a public hearing. Funds will be used for the elderly and handicapped to provide transit services. Ms. Marshall requested Board approval to submit her grant application as presented. 

Commissioner Mathies made a motion to approve the FY24 SSTAP grant in the amount of $117,447 as presented. Commissioner Webster seconded the motion. All those present voted in favor; therefore, the motion carried 4-0.

Mr. Drewer explained that in October 2023 the Community Development Block Grant Application (CDBG) for the Ewell Recreation Center was presented to the Board for consideration. Mr. Drewer explained at that time, that due to CDBG requirements, the County must be the applicant and oversee management of the grant, with the funds subrecipient to Smith Island United and the Ewell Volunteer Fire Company. The Board was in approval to move forward at that time. The application is being submitted for the maximum amount ($120,000) and will be used to replace the roof, portions of the siding on the building and portions of the interior ceiling that has been damaged

It was stated that public comment will be taken for ten (10) days, or until February 9, 2024 at 4:30 p.m. The Public Hearing was declared closed at 2:11 p.m. 

Mr. Drewer next presented the Maryland Agricultural Land Preservation Foundation (MALPF) local matching program. He explained the program is funded through dedicated revenues from the State’s Agricultural Transfer Tax. The County has $2,752.85 of funds that are within the six-year funding cycle. If approved to be submitted, these funds will be committed to the Matching Program for the upcoming 2025 easement offer cycle. A letter of intent is required to be submitted by Thursday. 

Commissioner Webster made a motion to approve the submission of $2,752.85 as presented to the MALPF Matching program. Commissioner Willing seconded the motion. All those present voted in favor; therefore, the motion carried 4-0. 

Next, Zoning Text Amendments, presented during the public hearing held on January 16, 2024 were discussed. Mr. Drewer advised that there were no public comments received during the

Mr. Drewer then explained that legislation has been introduced (SB537) that would change how the county restricts the businesses to be in an industrial zone, to there being no restriction, and the allowance to treat cannabis regulations with the same enforcements used for alcohol laws. Upon consultation with legal counsel, the tecommendation was made that the county move forward with the cannabis zoning amendments as presented; and should SB537 pass legislation, amend the Zoning Code amendments to follow law guidelines as established. Commissioner Mathies confirmed that he is hearing the same discussions during the Rural County Meeting he attended. It was noted that the moratorium will expire on April 10, 2024. 

Commissioner Mathies made a motion to proceed with the Cannabis Zoning Code Amendments as presented and address any needed amendments should legislation pass. Commissioner Willing seconded the motion. Those present voted in favor; therefore, the motion carried 4-0. 

Mr. Jared Parks, Lower Shore Land Trust came forward to present the FY25 Grant Application for the Dividing Creek Rural Legacy Area (RLA). The application must be submitted by the second Tuesday in February. He noted he works with Somerset and Worcester Counties, and generally Somerset has more easement at a lower dollar purchase than Worchester (6 in Somerset and 4 in Worcester proposed acquisitions for FY25). Commissioner Webster asked about the projects, and Mr. Parks provided descriptions of some of the projects submitted. If approved, the grant agreement will be prepared for county signature and presented at a futuremeeting. 

Commissioner Willing made a motion to approve the Dividing Creek Rural Legacy Area FY25 Grant Application. Commissioner Webster seconded the motion. All those present voted in favor; therefore, the motion carried 4-0. Mr. Parks was thanked for coming to the meeting today. 

Last, Mr. Drewer next presented a building permit fee waiver request in the amount of $755.04 was received from St. Paul’s Episcopal Church for the demolition and replacement of the existing chapel. 

Commissioner Mathies made a motion to approve the fee waiver request in the amount of $755.04. Commissioner Webster seconded the motion. All those present voted in favor; therefore, the motion carried 4-0. 

Ms. Alice Bromley, Child Support Division, came forward at this time to present the CSEA/CRA/24-023 Contract for 2023-2024. The total cost for the program was noted as $395,928, with the local county cost share being $149,573. It was noted that the program cost increased 4

Mr. Taylor continued with correspondence and discussion items. 

A letter was received from Andy Fish, Senior Director, Office of Finance Programs/MD Department of Commerce regarding the 2024 Private Activity Bond Initial Allocation received annually. Historically, Somerset County transfers the allocation to the state because we don’t have the capacity to administer a program. Mr. Taylor noted that 21 out of the 23 counties in Maryland transfer their authority to DHCD. 

Commissioner Webster made a motion to authorize the transfer Somerset County’s 2024 Private Activity Bond Initial Allocation to the state. Commissioner Mathies seconded the motion. All those present voted in favor; therefore, the motion carried 4-0. 

A letter was sent advising of boat slip subleases approved during the last meeting. 

Next, Mr. Taylor presented proposed legislative bill letters for the Boards consideration. First, a letter of opposition was drafted for HB443, which would consolidate local assessment offices and property tax appeals boards. The county feels these crucial decisions should be made by those that live within our county. 

Commissioner Mathies made a motion to approve the letter of opposition regarding HB443. Commissioner Webster seconded the motion. Those present voted in favor; therefore, the motion carried 4-0. 

The letter of support drafted regarding HB443 as amended was presented. The passage of this bill would allow county Class C Fraternal or Sororal Organizations to purchase alcohol from local retailers rather than three specific distributors; thereby lessoning the financial hardship being placed on these organizations. 

Commissioner Webster made a motion to approve the letter of support regarding HB443. Commissioner Willing seconded the motion. All those present voted in favor; therefore, the motion carried 4-0. 

Next, Mr. Taylor presented the proclamation for formal approval, drafted for the Crisfield Volunteer Fire Company’s 150" Celebration of Volunteer Service, and approved by phone poll.

A letter of support request was received from Verizon for their FY23 Connect Maryland Network Infrastructure grant application that will provide broadband service to Smith Island. Commissioner Webster made a motion to approve the letter of support as presented. Commissioner Willing seconded the motion. All those present voted in favor; therefore, the motion carried 4-0, 

At this time, Mr. George Fridley, Chesapeake Avenue, Crisfield Maryland came forward. He wished to express his concern of the increase in taxes, noting that living in City limits he is hit double. Being retired, his income is limited. With assessments being performed every three years, he’s concerned that the increase can be at 200% for those living in the City in future years. Vice President Randy Laird explained that Somerset County can only control the local tax rate; which has not been raised in 7-8 years, Assessments are performed by the State. Mr. Fridley stated that maybe the impact ofthe issue could be addressed by the County. He understands there is no easy answer, Mr. Fridley was thanked for his comments. 

At 2:33 p.m., with no further business, Vice President Randy Laird, upon a motion made by Commissioner Webster and seconded by Commissioner Mathies, advised of the need of a Closed Session by Authority of the General Provisions Article of the Annotated Code of Maryland §3-305 (b)(1) the appointment, employment, assignment, promotion, discipline, demotion, compensation, removal, resignation, or performance evaluation of an appointee, employee, or official over whom it has jurisdiction. All those present voted in favor; therefore, the motion carried 4-0, The Commissioner and Closed meetings adjourned at 3:18 p.m.
