Somerset County Sanitary Commission Minutes of February 13, 2025 

A regular session of the Somerset County Sanitary Commission was held on Thursday, February 13, 2025 at 1:00 p.m. in Room 111 of the Somerset County Office Complex. Members present were Charles Bagley, Interim Chairman, Dennis Williams, SecretaryTreasurer, and Barbara Somers, Member. Also in attendance were Anthony Stockus, General Manager, Richard Padgette, Superintendent, Cody Bradshaw, Assistant Superintendent, Starr Mister, Office Manager, Jennie Marshall, Senior Administrative Aide, Josh Taylor, P.E, Davis, Bowen & Frediel, Heather Konyar and Kyle Gankiewicz, SCSD Legal Counsel, Cockey, Brennan, & Maloney, and Richard Crumbacker, Somerset County Times. Maxine Landon, Member, was absent. The Crisfield (Peripheral Area) Board position is currently vacant. Robin Street was the only member of the Public present. 

Mr. Bagley called the meeting to order at 1:00 p.m. 

#### General Business — Information 

Mr. Stockus was pleased to announce, Rev. Charles Bagley, Commissioner for the Westover service area, has been reappointed by the Somerset County Commissioners to serve another six-year term with the Sanitary District Commission Board. 

The District's Financial Quarterly Analysis report was previously distributed to the Board for their review. Expenses exceeded revenues by $748,914.93, this shortfall is due to outstanding project reimbursements. 

### Princess Anne - Information 

Mr. Stockus updated the Board regarding the Princess Anne Wastewater Treatment Plant (WWTP) ENR Upgrade Project stating the District received a Preliminary Eligibility letter from Matthew Marshall, Maryland Department of Environment (MDE). The eligible funds are based on the Preliminary Engineering Report (PER) sent to MDE by Davis, Bowen & Friedel (DBF). MDE has determined they will cover 60% of construction costs through Biological Nutrient Removal (BNR) grant and the remaining 40% will be paid by local share (SCSD). This preliminary eligibility determination is subject to change based on design review and will be adjusted based on actual bid prices. 

Mr. Stockus stated there has been no movement regarding the Princess Anne Color Removal Project since November’s commission meeting where he announced SCSD received official notice of funding through MDE’s FFY 2024 Intended Use Plan for the Drinking Water State Revolving Fund (DWSRF). 

Mr. Padgette updated the Commission regarding the Princess Anne Wastewater Treatment Plant Lab Project by stating the existing Lab is shutdown now and they are

Mr. Stockus informed the Board, Princess Anne Redevelopment Company LLC (Bret Davis and Kurt Davis) is suggesting the Sanitary District bring the pump station and infrastructure at Manokin Village (Clipper Circle, previously Hawk Plaza) up to engineering standards and take over maintaining the infrastructure and pump station. Mr. Stockus reminded the Commission, a meeting was held prior to the sale of this property on November 9, 2022, at Davis, Bowen, & Friedel’s Office, members present included SCSD’s Engineers (DBF), Legal Counsel (CBM), and Management as well as Bret Davis and Kurt Davis. At that meeting, Mr. Davis was presented with DBF’s Hawk Plaza Site Review which detailed all requirements needed to the pump station, both pumps, and the water and sewer infrastructure before the District would consider taking over maintaining the pump station. 

Ms. Konyar, attorney for both the Sanitary District and Princess Anne Redevelopment Company, was present to discuss Manokin Village infrastructure and pump station. Ms. Konyar stated Bret Davis was under the impression they were also purchasing the common areas of Manokin Village, making them responsible for bringing the pump station and infrastructure up to engineering standards. He has since learned they have no title to the common areas, as there were errors made from the beginning of development with titling and land records. Mr. Davis has since met with the District to discuss this matter and explain that he feels it’s no longer their responsibility to pay to bring the pump station and infrastructure up to standards after learning they have no ownership of the common area of Manokin Village. Ms. Konyar stated Mr. Davis is still willing to partially help with costs of upgrades/improvements to the pipes but does not feel solely responsible for the expense to bring the infrastructure and pump station up to standards. This is contrary to Sanitary District policy, which is to not accept infrastructure into its inventory unless it already meets standards, so partnering with Mr. Davis would require the Commissioners to set the matter in as an action item on a future agenda. Ms. Konyar stated this is being brought to the Board as an information item and no action would be taken at this time. The Commissioners did not express interest in the proposal and did not desire to set this in as a future action item.

### PKS Contract Extension 

SCSD's contract with PKS & Company for external auditing ended with the completion of FY24 Audit. ### Princess Anne — Action 

### Authorization to payoff loan 

Ms. Mister informed the Commission, the District has roughly $16,000 left over money in PNC Bank from the Princess Anne Subdistrict Expansion (PASE) project. Ms. Mister consulted with Ashley Stern, PKS, to inquire if these funds can be utilized to pay off the PASE loan (est. $5,000) and the remaining balance go into the Debt Service savings account to help pay existing debt. Ms. Stern determined this was permittable. Ms. Somers made a motion to authorize the payoff of the Hebron Savings Bank PASE loan with the PASE project funds and put the remaining balance into the Debt Service account. Mr. Williams seconded the motion; the motion unanimously carried to approval.

Bowman Consulting Group, on behalf of Horace & Elwath LLC, are requesting an allocation for 80 EDUs of water and sewer for their proposed Somerset Gardens subdivision on Old Princess Anne Road. ## Request for sewer credit 

The Commission tabled the sewer credit request for 11341 Stewart Neck Road during the December 2024 Commission meeting due to not receiving the application in time for the meeting. The tenant, Maceo Agurcia, has since submitted the application with proof of purchase for parts. The Account Technician was able to issue a credit to the sewer portion of their 9/24 and 12/24 quarter water and sewer bills, since the water did not enter the sewer system for treatment. The sewer credit totaling $8,065.09 leaves the account with a remaining balance of $4,815.06. Mr. Agurcia is requesting a courtesy credit to his water portion based on his average consumption, as the remainder due has caused a financial burden. Ms. Mister stated although she sympathizes with Mr. Agurcia, the water was treated by the District, unlike the sewer which leaked out on the ground and did not enter the sewer system for treatment. Management is recommending taking no action on Mr. Agurcia’ s request for a courtesy credit, but instead offer a 2-year payment plan with no interest. Ms. Somers made a motion to offer a 2-year interest free payment plan for 11341 Stewart Neck Road. Mr. Williams seconded the motion, the motion carried unanimously to approval.

#### Fairmount — Action 

### Request for sewer credit 

The District received a leak credit application for 27620 Fairmount Road for a toilet leak during the 9/24 quarter. The property owners, Dana and Daniel Pointer, explained in their application they repaired the leak themselves with equipment they had on hand, therefore, they are unable to provide the District with a receipt for parts or Plumber's statement. Ms. Mister stated the Account Technician verified the leak was repaired by performing an audit on the meter which determined the leak has subsided. It’s Management's recommendation to issue a sewer leak credit based on policy since the leak has subsided. Ms. Somers made a motion to approve a sewer leak credit for 27620 Fairmount Road based on the sewer leak policy. Mr. Williams seconded the motion, the motion unanimously carried to approval.
