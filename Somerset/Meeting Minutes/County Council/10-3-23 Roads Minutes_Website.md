# **SOMERSET COUNTY ROADS BOARD TUESDAY OCTOBER 3, 2023** 

At 2:00 p.m., the Somerset County Roads Board met in regular session with President Charles Laird. Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig N. Mathies, Sr., Commissioner Eldon Willing and Commissioner Darryl Webster. President Charles Laird and Commissioner Eldon Willing were not in attendance for today’s meeting. 

Also present for today’s meeting were County Administrator Ralph D. Taylor, County Roads Director Woody Barnes, County Roads Superintendent Gary Beauchamp, and Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez. 

President Charles Laird called the meeting to order and asked Commissioner Eldon Willing to lead those present in Devotion and the Pledge of Allegiance. 

Minutes from the August 8, 2023 and September 5, 2023 meeting were presented for approval and approved on a motion made by Vice President Randy Laird, seconded by Commissioner Darryl Webster, and voted in favor by a show of hands by President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig Mathies, Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Darryl Webster. 

# **CTP TOUR** 

Secretary of Transportation Paul Wiedefield was invited to come forward to present the 2024 Consolidated Transportation Program (CTP) for FY2024-FY2029. Secretary Wiedefield began by thanking the Board for meeting with them during the MACo summer conference and introducing the staff accompanying him to the meeting today, including Matt Baker, Deputy Administrator for Project Development; Mark Crampton, District 1 Engineer; Melissa Williams, Director of Planning and Program Development; Travis Johnston, Director, Office of Local Transit Support;  Leslie Dews, Deputy Administrator; and Ashish J. Solanki, Director, Office of Regional Aviation Assistance. 

Next, Secretary Wiefefield began his presentation advising that, following their meeting at MACo, State Highway has supplied the County Administrator with additional State and Federal sign regulations and how these apply to roads. He also advised of a study of MD 675 that will allow State Highway to investigate various improvements to that intersection in order to accommodate turning for trucks. The Board was informed of the focus of the department, which is on the Governor’s priorities (social equity, environmental protection, and sustainable

Secretary Wiedefield then advised that, as head of his department, his focus is on: safety (road fatalities), “Complete Streets Program” (focusing on pedestrians), preserving the system in place, and climate change. Mr. Baker also expressed their focus on safety (roadway fatalities), pedestrian and bicyclist safety (PSAP), and organizational efficiency (project delivery and faster permit approvals, and procurement timelines). Mr. Baker concluded with addressing the projects (completed and in process) that SHA is currently working with the County on; such as: Bike Trail (UMES Circulation Trail), US 13 and MD 822 Signal Light, and the MD13 Rail Trail. 

Mr. Matt Baker and Mr. Mark Crampton returned to the audience. 

Next, Ms. Melissa Williams came forward to provide an update on the Bay Bridge Study. She advised that the study is analyzing alternatives for providing safety and capacity improvements while also looking at road reliability. The corridor they are studying extends along 5301 from east of the Severn River Bridge to the 5301 split. To address concerns about the congestion at the Bay Bridge, they plan to continue with this project, share alternatives with the public in 2024 (with MDTA’s recommended alternative being shared in 2025), and selecting one in 2026. 

Ms. Williams returned to the audience. 

Mr. Travis Johnston reported on what MTA is doing to support frequent, reliable, and easy to use transit throughout Maryland.  He advised that they are continuing to support locally operated public transportation services on the Eastern Shore. He further advised of the funding provided to these transportation services for maintenance and buses. Mr. Johnston then advised that, beginning next fiscal year, funding will be adjusted so that the allocation distributions are more equitable and responsive to changes in local jurisdictions. 

Mr. Johnston returned to the audience.

# **ROADS DIVISION** 

Mr. Barnes provided an update on the NOAA Grant Application, advising that (due to the grant application process) they have to drop the bridges and rural roads as the grant opportunity does not sponsor these construction activities. He further advised that the grant application is still in progress and focuses on the Ewell Basin and the Crisfield Berm (shoreline resiliency). 

Mr. Barnes then addressed a second NOAA Grant Application sponsored by Ducks Unlimited to reestablish wildlife on the Martin Wildlife Refuge with an additional focus on shoreline resiliency; specifically, the 3,000 linear feet on Marsh Road and the Easter Point dredge spoil site. 

Next, Mr. Barnes briefed the Board on a FEMA grant opportunity (FEMA BRIC Program) that will bring the outer boundaries of Crisfield on the left side by Somers Cove around to Hall Highway up to an elevation up to 5 feet. On the east side it would be Somerset Ave, Gandy Lane, Lindon Lane, Hinman Lane, some portions of Bird Road and Jacksonville Rd. This will be a $60

# **WATERWAYS** 

Mr. Barnes provided an update on the Webster’s Cove Improvement Project, advising the top decking on the loading dock has been replaced and the crew is starting work on the east side replacing the decking. The electric has been addressed as well. 

An update was provided on the Lower Wicomico Dredge project. Mr. Barnes advised the Webster’s Cove will not be included in this dredge cycle due to the latest survey that shows the area designated on Deal Island at full capacity. Mr. Barnes advised that the Army Corp of Engineers has promised Webster’s Cove will be on the next dredge cycle; which occurs roughly every other year. 

Mr. Barnes presented a request from Contrell Contracting to utilize the field behind the Somerset County Office Complex to construct the last 4,000 feet of pipe. In return, Contrell will assist with the one area in the harbor that needs to be dredged. 

A motion was made by Commissioner Darryl Webster and seconded by Commissioner Eldon Willing to approve the request from Contrell Contracting to utilize the field behind the Somerset County Office Complex to construct the last 4,000 feet of pipe. By a show of hands in

Vice President Randy Laird inquired about the grass growing up in the cracks on Boone Road and expressed his concerns. Mr. Barnes advised they will have to monitor and make patches as needed. 

Commissioner Darryl Webster advised of a drainage issue at 13761 Bobtown Road. 

Commissioner Eldon Willing inquired when dredging would begin on the lower Wicomico, to which Mr. Barnes advised October 15<sup>th</sup> . A discussion was held in regards to the Army Corp wanting to shut down Riley Roberts Road during the dredging; however, Mr. Barnes advised that DNR is not in favor of this. 

Commissioner Eldon Willing inquired when the Dames Quarter Boat Ramp construction will begin, and Mr. Barnes advised around the first of December. Commissioner Eldon Willing further inquired about lighting, and Mr. Barnes advised that it’s in the contract.
