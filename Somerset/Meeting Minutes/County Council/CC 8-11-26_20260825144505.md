Next, Mr. Woody Barnes, Public Works Director and Mr. Gary Kitching, Landfill Superintendent came forward. 

First, Mr. Barnes distributed a letter received from the Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) which advised that they have completed their review of Somerset County’s 2026 progress report regarding the 2021-2030 Solid Waste Management Plan. It was noted that the county has improved recycling rates from 6.08% in 2022 to 15.5% in 2024, and was commended for the improvement. However, the county is still falling short of the projections outlined in the Plan, set at 20% by the State. Mr. Barnes reviewed two options available to address this issue. The first is to request a reduction of the State-Mandated Rate of 20%, lowering the rate to 12%, which Mr. Barnes feels we can achieve, as recently we have been in the 14%-15% range. The second option is a revision of the current plan, which would require incorporating different strategies to reach the 20% milestone, which Mr. Bames feels will not occur, uncertain as to what can be changed to reach that milestone, as well as our population size affecting the percentage. ‘Additionally, the county would have to stop working on the current plan, and be required to hold a public hearing in order to revise the plan, and would also need approval from MDE. Mr. Barnes recommended the first option, and requested the Boards approval to submit a request to MDE to lower the Somerset County recycling rate to 12%. A discussion was held regarding how the information is tracked and calculated which includes tires, cardboard, metal, oil and antifreeze. If approved, Mr. Bares advised that the next step in the process will be to send a letter to MDE by August 14, 2026. 

Noting tires are a part of the calculation, Vice President Randy Laird questioned if a Tire Day can be offered, as it has not been done in quite some time. Mr. Kitching advised he will look into the request and report back (Tire Day is a State funded event).
