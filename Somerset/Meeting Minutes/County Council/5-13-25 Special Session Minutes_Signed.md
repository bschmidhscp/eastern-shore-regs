BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS FOR SOMERSET COUNTY **SPECIAL SESSION TUESDAY MAY 13, 2025** 

At 1:00 pm, President Charles Laird called the Board of Commissioners to open in Special Session. Present for today’s meeting were President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, and Commissioner Craig Mathies, Commissioner Eldon Willing and Commissioner Darryl Webster. 

Also in attendance were Interim County Administrator Ernie Leatherbury and Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez. 

President Charles Laird asked for Commissioner Eldon Willing to lead those present in devotion and the Pledge of Allegiance. 

The Board then invited Dr. Ava Tasker-Mitchell, Ms. Tracey Bartemy, Ms. Christina South, and Mr. Matt Lankford to come forward to discuss revisions to the Somerset County School Budget for FY26. 

Mr. Lankford began by highlighting the need for strategic financial planning to elevate Somerset County schools to be among the best in the state of Maryland. He continued by emphasizing that the objective of the budget is to eliminate wasteful spending (before seeking additional funding assistance from the County Commissioners), and to realign existing funds to address areas of weakness (primarily student performance and staff compensation) as Somerset County Schools can no longer rely on COVID Relief Funding to balance the budget. With that said, Mr. Lankford advised that the total amount budgeted must remain below the maintenance of effort amount of $51,563,953. It was noted that this amount does not include federal funding; however, by law, federal funding must be included in the budget. Mr. Lankford advised that since the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, the district’s budget has grown substantially. A number of new positions were introduced during this period, yet student proficiency scores have continued to decline. Prior to the pandemic, the per-student expenditure was approximately $18,000. In the proposed FY26 budget, that figure has risen to nearly $25,000 per student. Despite this significant increase in spending, essential staff within Somerset County Public Schools remain among the lowest paid in the state, creating challenges in both staff retention and recruitment. Mr. Lankford stressed the importance of redirecting attention and resources to eliminate classroom disruptions, strengthen school safety for both students and staff, and reestablish respect within the school

It was advised that this proposed financial plan is intended not only to maintain core operations but also to strengthen student performance and workforce stability without placing undue burden on local taxpayers. 

Following this brief overview, an updated budget was presented by Mr. Lankford that reflected revisions made since Superintendent Dr. Tasker-Mitchell last addressed the Commissioners. Among the most significant adjustments was a proposed $1 million increase above the state-mandated maintenance of effort; however, Mr. Lankford noted the absence of federal funding figures (which had not yet been finalized at the time of the initial draft). He advised that on May 9, 2025, the district received its updated federal grant information (a comprehensive 783 page report). Due to the size and complexity of this documentation, it required several days for thorough review. From this new information, it was determined that the school district expects to receive approximately $14,658,209 in federal restricted grants. When combined with the previously presented local and state funds totaling $51,563,953, the district's full proposed budget now stands at approximately $66,222,162. 

Commissioner Darryl Webster inquired, of the proposed budget presented, how much of that funding was directed to salaries (teachers, sanitation, health, dietary, etc.). Mr. Lankford was unable to provide a total number of funds as requested, but advised that all of the documentation is outlined in the proposed budget handout provided to the Board members and a total could be calculated from that. 

Commissioner Craig Mathies inquired if any members of the Somerset County Board of Education held any background(s) in education (in any capacity). Mr. Lankford advised that he did not and was not aware or able to speak on behalf of his fellow board members. A discussion was then held in regards to the importance of retaining experienced, certified teachers (many of whom are considering leaving due to significantly higher salaries offered in neighboring counties). It was questioned whether the district had explored ways to match these offers to prevent the loss of valuable staff. Mr. Lankford acknowledged that administrative staffing, particularly the number of vice principals, may be excessive and suggested reallocating those salary funds toward hiring school security and increasing teacher salaries. He further emphasized that addressing discipline and safety in schools is essential for learning, and that competitive pay could not only help retain teachers but also attract high-quality educators from neighboring counties. Dr. Tasker-Mitchell then addressed the Board to provide context and clarification on the proposed budget, emphasizing her role in guiding the Board of Education with accurate and informed recommendations. She highlighted the district’s goal of building a student-focused, staffsupportive budget while managing financial constraints linked to declining enrollment and limited funding. Somerset County’s student population has dropped from 3,000 to 2,600, impacting state and federal allocations and requiring a higher local share this year. She then outlined efforts to reduce central office spending by eliminating or repurposing several administrative roles (including positions in HR, instructional technology, purchasing, and special education). Many of these roles were moved back into schools to directly support instruction (particularly in math). She clarified that many positions appearing as new in the budget were actually existing roles being transitioned from expiring grant funding into the local budget. She also defended the necessity of key positions such as vice principals, media specialists, and paraprofessionals, noting their importance in supporting school safety, literacy, and students with special needs. She advised that reducing these roles would compromise services and possibly violate federal requirements. To provide compensation increases for staff, she presented three budget options: 1. Full $1 million in county support would allow raises across all staff categories; 2. Partial support could be supplemented using the Concentration of Poverty (COP) grant; 3. No additional county funding would force reliance on COP funds, resulting in smaller raises. Discussion continued with the Board being advised that the district has already received 11 teacher resignations since March, with more expected by July (most cited salary as the reason for leaving). Despite this, the district has already secured four new teachers and continues active recruitment. Dr. Tasker-Mitchell closed by reinforcing that the proposed budget balances stability, staff retention, and student needs, and asked for the Commissioners’ support to move the district forward without job losses or program cuts. 

Discussion then moved to the importance of protecting special education paraprofessional positions, particularly those required to fulfill Individualized Education Programs (IEPs). There are currently 42 special education paraprofessionals, increasing to 48 in the proposed budget due to a rise in students requiring these services. Some students need one-on-one support, while others

Commissioner Craig Mathies raised concerns about the legal implications of breaking staff contracts if positions are cut, asking whether the Board of Education or the County Commissioners would bear the financial responsibility in such cases. While no lawsuits have occurred, it was acknowledged that breaching contracts could lead to potential legal action. 

The discussion then moved to the cost-effectiveness and role of media specialists within Somerset County Public Schools. Mr. Lankford raised concerns about the high salaries and benefits of media specialists given the low number of books checked out from school libraries. He questioned whether, amid a teacher shortage, if these certified educators could be reassigned to classroom teaching where they are most needed. In response, Ms. Bartmey clarified that most media specialists hold specialized certifications that are not interchangeable with classroom teaching credentials. Transitioning them to core subject teaching roles, such as English Language Arts (ELA), would require entirely new certification pathways under state regulations (COMAR). It was emphasized that the district remains compliant with COMAR requirements for media services and plans to retain two media specialists to fulfill those legal obligations. Moving back to special education paraprofessionals, Mr. Lankford reiterated that the number of such positions remains unchanged in the new budget with six new roles added due to student needs. The loss of a sign language interpreter was noted, and overall staffing was reported to have increased slightly. Mr. Lankford emphasized the BOE’s intent to maintain current staffing while repositioning some individuals to areas of greater instructional need; not to eliminate jobs, but to strengthen the system and use resources more effectively. 

Commissioner Darryl Webster expressed concern over a widespread public misconception that the County Commissioners are making decisions about internal school operations, such as staffing positions. He clarified that the Commissioners’ role is strictly limited to funding, not determining specific personnel decisions like media specialists or paraprofessionals. He emphasized the importance of clear communication and unity within the Board of Education and school leadership. Before the Commissioners can approve funding (including any amount above the maintenance of effort) they need to be confident that the school board and administration are aligned and working collaboratively toward a shared plan. Without that unity, it becomes difficult for the Commissioners to justify additional financial support. He urged all parties to commit to

Vice President Randy Laird inquired if the Board of Education concurred with Dr. TaskerMitchell’s request for an additional one million dollars in funding and (if it’s approved) is the BOE unified with the district on how it will be used. Mr. Lankford responded passionately, noting that he serves voluntarily without pay and ran for the position solely to advocate for the children of Somerset County. He stated that his recommendations reflect the views of his constituents and that his efforts stem from genuine concern, not personal gain. He expressed frustration that the board’s early budget recommendations were repeatedly dismissed by staff and claimed that, despite efforts to collaborate, some individuals were reluctant or afraid to engage with him, hindering progress. He also raised concerns about school safety, sharing examples of teachers resigning after experiencing threats and violence. He stressed that community members are tired of the lack of discipline and safety in schools and feel that certain positions in central office are filled based on favoritism rather than merit; particularly when underperforming administrators are reassigned instead of held accountable. He concluded by reaffirming his commitment to improving the school system and the safety of students and staff, urging for more transparency, accountability, and collaboration moving forward. Commissioner Darryl Webster clarified that that his call for unity was misunderstood. He emphasized that he was not suggesting Mr. Lankford should step aside, nor did he question his leadership or commitment. On the contrary, he views Mr. Lankford as a strong leader and valuable asset to the community, the Board of Education, and the children of Somerset County. He reiterated that his only request was for all parties to work together collaboratively. He stressed that continued division and conflict will prevent the school system from reaching its goals, and unity is essential for meaningful progress. 

Dr. Tasker-Mitchell addressed the Board once more, expressing a strong commitment to the students of Somerset County and voiced frustration over the county’s reputation for low academic rankings. She emphasized that Somerset County students deserve the same academic opportunities as their peers in neighboring counties like Worcester and Wicomico. Her goal is to advance student achievement through collaborative efforts involving the board, families, and community support. She also stressed the importance of following proper guidance and processes

Vice President Randy Laird once more inquired of Mr. Lankford if the BOE felt the additional one million dollars was needed. Mr. Lankford advised no. 

President Charles Laird then thanked everyone involved for their dedication and hard work. He acknowledged the long-standing challenges and tensions surrounding school system issues in Somerset County, emphasizing that real change also depends on stronger parental involvement, which is beyond the reach of school officials and commissioners. He addressed taxpayer concerns about how funds are used, firmly stating his belief that decisions being made are thoughtful, responsible, and in the best interest of the students and the county. He praised the BOE for its unprecedented effort to truly engage and problem-solve, encouraging collaboration and compromise among all parties to ensure the success of the educational system. He continued by emphasizing that while the County Commissioners manage the funding, they trust the school leadership to make staffing and operational decisions. He stressed that all parties are united by a shared goal: supporting the children of Somerset County. He urged everyone to remain positive, continue working together, and make thoughtful, collective decisions for the benefit of the students, closing by expressing optimism and appreciation for everyone’s contributions. 

Dr. Ava Tasker-Mitchell, Ms. Tracey Bartemy, Ms. Christina South, and Mr. Matt Lankford left the meeting. 

Moving on to public comments, Ms. Lynelle Fletcher-Pugh addressed the Board. As President of the Princess Anne Chamber of Commerce, she provided an update on efforts to revive the Chamber following inactivity during COVID-19. Since assuming leadership, she secured office space and established a website and phone line, but the Chamber remains financially strained, with expenses personally covered by her and her husband. She is requesting $7,500 to assist with covering rent, operational costs, and to fund community events and scholarships. The Chamber currently relies on donations and membership fees and cannot apply for grants. Previous town funding was $1,500, which she stated was insufficient. The Board advised that her request will be considered, and appreciation was expressed for her efforts to rebuild the Chamber.

She highlighted her responsibilities, which include teaching art, college and career readiness, journalism, and running the yearbook. She emphasized that removing media specialists from the budget would still require hiring others to teach these classes, making the cost-cutting measure ineffective. She stressed the importance of investing in students’ education and voiced strong support for maintaining media specialist positions, stating that students need this support to succeed. 

With no further business to discuss open session, a motion was made by Commissioner Darryl Webster and seconded by Vice President Randy Laird to adjourn Special Session at 2:28 p.m. and enter into Closed Session by authority of the General Provisions Article of the Annotated Code of Maryland § 3-305 (b)(1) the appointment, employment, assignment, promotion, discipline, demotion, compensation, removal, resignation, or performance evaluation of an appointee, employee, or official over whom it has jurisdiction. By a show of hands in favor of all present Board members, the motion carried.
