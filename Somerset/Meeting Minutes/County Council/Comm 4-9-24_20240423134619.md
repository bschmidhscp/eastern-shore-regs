Board of Commissioners Meeting 4-9-24 

Mr. Herb Geary and Ms. Audrey McKenrick, UHY came forward to present the Somerset County Fiscal Year 2024 Audit Report. Mr. Taylor thanked Ms. Faith Redmond, Finance Director and Mr. Willis Dryden, Interim Finance Director who assisted with the audit and who were present today at the meeting, as well as Ms. Sharon Muir, Internal Auditor who was not in attendance today. 

Mr. Geary thanked the Board for having him attend the meeting today, apologizing for not being available earlier to attend an earlier meeting due his schedule. He noted some of the changes that have occurred with new personnel on board, advising that that with the support of current staff they worked through the process. He then gave the floor to Ms. McKenrick who provided the report. 

Ms. McKenrick referred the Board to the audit report provided and outlined the following 

highlights of the report: 

- An Unmodified Opinion (the highest level of assurance) was given on Governmental Activities, business-type activities, major and aggregate remaining fund information. 

- - The Independent Auditors’ Report opinion gave a “Qualified” opinion regarding Component Units, specifically resulting from the lack of a year end audit report ofthe Liquor Control Board. This will not be an issue going forward with the abolishment of the Liquor Control Board. 

- The County adopted new accounting guidance (GASB Statement No. 96) as required. No impact to the report as the County did not have anything that qualified in that category. 

- - Management's Discussion and Analysis was prepared by Mr. Taylor and Ms. Muir which explains the County’s financial overview in understandable language. 

- - The Statement of Net Position was reviewed as well as the Balance Sheet with it noted that the General Fund, at $35.5 Million increased $6.2 Million (increase in cash) from the prior year, with total liability was at $10 Million, increasing by $4 Million over last year, noting Deferred Revenue was at about $5 Million. 

- - Total Fund Balance for the General Fund increased $1.4 Million, with the Total Fund Balance noted as $24.7 Million. Total revenue increased $2.1 Million over last year, mainly due to local property income tax revenue, and interest increasing. 

- Salary and personnel cost resulted in a net change of $1.4 Million 

- - Roads Department assets were up about $125,000 with no change in liability. Revenue increased $200,000 (State Aid funding received). 

- Golf Course revenue and expenses were reviewed, resulting in total operating transfers totaling $441,000 versus $325,000 for last year. 

- = General Fund budgeted and actual spending was noted at $28.8 Million budgeted versus $31 Million actual. 

- - Internal Control procedures tested two programs (Coronavirus funding and Economic Adjustments Assistance and were given a clean opinion for compliance and internal control. 

- = Recommendation was made regarding the Audit Communication Report and it was noted that the County has multiple internal control structures that may create higher overall risk. Streamlining and stricter internal controls were recommended and discussed with Finance Staff. Economic Development Commission oversight and Planning and Zoning grant management/disbursement tracking was also recommended. There were no findings to report.

Board of Commissioners Meeting 4-9-24 

Mr. Geary commended the county on their clean audit report. Mr. Taylor asked for audit opinion regarding fund balance to overall operation expenses. Mr. Geary stated that the County is well positioned with its debt ratio, following within the 1/3 (or over 30%) recommendation of fund balance to overall expense. Mr. Geary and Ms. McKenrick left the meeting at this time. 

Mr. Jesse Drewer, Planning and Zoning Director came forward. First, Mr. Taylor advised that although the Critical Area Map Ordinance was placed on the agenda, it would thought to be best to table the ordinance until a full board is present; therefore, the Board took no action at this time. 

Next, Mr. Drewer presented a request to amend the Electrical Inspection Resolution to add United Inspections Agency as an approved Electrical Inspection Agency for Somerset County. Mr. Drewer explained that proper documents have been submitted, and the County is currently only using one agency, First State Inspection Agency to perform inspections. Only having one agency can cause delays, and it would be helpful to have more than just one choice. 

Commissioner Webster made a motion to approve Resolution No. 1199 that will add United Inspection Agency as an approved Inspection Agency for Somerset County. Commissioner Willing seconded the motion. Those present voted aye; therefore, the motion carried 3-0. Last, Mr. Drewer asked Mr. Casey Goldsborough to come forward. A request for approval to ask the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD) to increase the maximum project amount from $30,000 to $40,000 was presented and it was explained that recently, the department is experiencing increased costs of<sup>building materials,withbidscoming</sup> in that exceed the $30,000 limit. As example, Mr. Goldsborough explained that the main projects are septic systems and wells, and the current amount will only cover the tanks and not the drain fields for those types of projects. Mr. Drewer explained that his department has spoken with Ms. Cindy Stone, DHCD, who stated that a formal letter must be submitted from the President requesting the increase to $40,000 to her office in order to proceed with the request which will be handled as a grant amendment. 

Commissioner Willing made a motion approving signature on the letter as requested. Commissioner Willing seconded the motion. Those present voted aye; therefore, the motion 

carried 3-0.

Board of Commissioners Meeting 4-9-24 

Next, Mr. John Redden and Mr. Woody Barnes joined Mr. Drewer to present the 2024 Somerset County Priority Letter for Recommended Transportation Improvements that has been drafted for the Boards consideration. Mr. Redden explained that many of the requested projects are the same as has been requested in previous years; with two notable changes. The first was the addition of improved vehicular access at US 13 North and Stewart Neck Road. The second was the Chesapeake Bay Passenger Ferry. 

Mr. Barnes explained the Stewart Neck Road proposal, explaining safety concerns with large trucks with trucks coming off of Stewart Neck Road onto Route 13. Discussions have been held with State representatives, who have evaluated the situation. The recommendation was made to not make any changes because of issues that were caused. The project would involve U-Tum access that would provide a safer alternative a little further down the road. Mr. Redden modified the language for this project. 

Regarding the ferry, it was noted that a feasibility study is currently on going, and Mr. Thompson, Mr. Sterling and Mr. Redden have attended meetings with the consultant, meeting almost every week. The revised language is what the other counties are using to obtain funding, so that all counties are on a consistent front. 

Mr. Bares discussed two projects that the County considers a priority (Deceleration Lane Route 413 at MD 361 and Signalization of Railroad Crossings) which have remained on the request, but not prioritized as high by the State. 

Mr. Barnes also noted that not included in the letter was the matter of drainage issues in the County. The process is lengthy when SHA is involved. A discussion was held regarding current problem areas, The Board was in agreement to add drainage projects to the letter as discussed to include, MD 361 (Fairmount Road), MD 362 (Mt. Vernon Road), MD 380 (Main Street), MD 363 (Deal Island Road) and MD 667 (Crisfield Marion Road) and will be addressed directly with the Secretary. 

Commissioner Willing made a motion to approve the projects as presented, adding drainage projects as a priority as well. Commissioner Webster seconded the motion. Those present voted aye; therefore the motion carried 3-0. An updated letter will be provided when completed. 

Mr. Taylor continued with correspondence and discussion items at this time.
