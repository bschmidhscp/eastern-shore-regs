Somerset County Sanitary Commission Minutes of February 9" 2023 

A regular session of the Somerset County Sanitary Commission was held on Thursday February 9, 2023 at 1:00 p.m. in Room 111 of the Somerset County Office Complex. Members present were Adam Riggin, Chairman, Charles Bagley, Vice-Chairman, Dennis Williams, Secretary-Treasurer, Maxine Landon, Member, and Barbara Somers, Member. Also in attendance were Anthony Stockus, General Manager, Earl Ludy, Superintendent, Jennie Marshall, Senior Administrative Aide, Trish Raab, Administrative Assistant, Josh Taylor, P.E., Davis Bowen & Friedel, and Richard Crumbacker, Somerset County Times. Public Attendees included Anthony Reid, Joe Williams, Paul Bidez, John Lewis Jr., Shannon Evans, Andrew Karolick, Rick Kellogg, David Dixon, and Jeremy Cox. Nicole Potoski joined remotely during her requested action item. 

Mr. Riggin called the meeting to order at 1:00 p.m. and announced the cancellation of Executive Session. 

### General Business — Information 

The Financial Quarterly Analysis report was previously distributed for review. The second quarter expenses exceeded revenues by $390,197.89; however, the District is awaiting project reimbursement from the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) in the amount of $467,192.00. The Commission had no questions or comments regarding the quarterly report. 

Mr. Taylor, DBF, informed the Commission he has submitted MDE State Revolving Fund Drinking Water Applications for the ‘Somerset County Sanitary District Lead Service Line Inventory’, ‘Jones Creek Watermain Crossing’, and ‘Princess Anne Water Supply and Treatment Improvements’ projects. The Commission will be informed if funding is approved. 

Mr. Stockus informed the Commission, due to the slow turn-around for project reimbursements from the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) and the Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE), Management has decided to increase the existing Line of Credit of $750,000 to $2,000,000. The Line of Credit serves as a funding backup. The Commission had no concerns with the District increasing the Line of Credit amount. Princess Anne - Information Mr. Stockus updated the Commission about the Princess Anne Wastewater Treatment Plant ENR Upgrade Project stating there has been no updates following MDE’s response to the submission of the Preliminary Engineering Report (PER). 

Mr. Stockus updated the Board regarding the Princess Anne Color Removal Project stating we have an Acceptance of Petition during the action portion of the meeting. 

Mr. Ludy updated the Commission about the Princess Anne Wastewater Treatment Plant Lab Project stating the Lab Construction Pre-Bid is scheduled for February 24" 2023. The Bid

# Amendment to Policy 

The Commission were previously distributed a draft of the Amended Credit Card Use Policy for their review. Management has revised the policy to include, coverage of fees be paid by customer when fraudulence is reported to their credit card company. Ms. Marshall explained, when these instances occur, the Sanitary District incurs administrative costs, similar to returned checks. Mr. Bagley made a motion to approve the amendment to the Credit Card Policy; Ms. Somers seconded the motion which carried unanimously to approval.

### Princess Anne — Action 

### Acceptance of Petition 

The residents of Somerset Landing have signed a Petition in support of the Sanitary District and Town of Princess Anne in their efforts to secure funding and resources to improve the water quality for the residents of Princess Anne. The petition states that while the residents understand that by EPA (U.S. Environmental Protection Agency) standards their water is drinkable, it is not ascetically pleasing and is having adverse effects resulting in residents’ having to install water treatment systems to improve the color and taste of the water. Mr. Bagley made a motion to accept the petition for the residents of Somerset Landing, Ms. Somers seconded the motion; the motion carried to approval unanimously. Mr. Stockus cautioned, funding may be unobtainable as this is not a drinkability issue. 

### Front Footage Write-off 

Ms. Marshall explained to the Commission, 11472 Peters Avenue, a property the Sanitary District serves water and sewer to, was recently sold. When the new deed and transfer charges were received by the District, our staff mistakenly missed a second account associated with the property and did not collect the front footage amounts due. Since the deed has been stamped clear of all charges, the District needs to write-off the remaining balance of $148.00. Ms. Somers made a motion to approve the write-off of front footage balance in the amount of $148.00 for 11472 Peters Avenue; Mr. Bagley seconded the motion; the motion was unanimously approved. 

### Request for additional credit 

Nicole Potosky, owner of 11825 Beechwood Street, is appealing her leak credit she received for 2 leaks occurring during the September and December 2022 quarters. The September quarter bill, read on September 13" 2022, total usage of 835,748 gallons, was billed $11,523.54; there was also a requested transfer reading performed on November 15 2022 total usage of 272,444 gallons, was billed $3,612.08. According to Ms. Potosky there were two leaks at the property, toilet and hot water heater. Documentation of these leaks were received by the Sanitary District and a total credit of $10,091.54 was applied to the sewer account (according to leak policy). Ms. Potosky was unhappy with the credit and requested to be present on the phone during the Commission decision. Ms. Potosky spoke during the meeting and expressed her many concerns. Ms. Potosky had a lot of questions for the Board, too many to address during the meeting. The Commission requested Ms. Potosky provide the District with her concerns and questions in writing so the Board/Staff can be better prepared to address her concerns at the next meeting; Ms. Potosky agreed. The Commission tabled the additional credit request.

### Request for additional credit 

Hattie Fields, owner of 30533 Dr. William Hytche Boulevard, is requesting additional credit for her toilet leak that occurred during the September 2022 quarter. Ms. Fields already received the $200 maximum credit for her toilet leak but is requesting additional relief for the remaining balance of $959.97. It is management's recommendation to abide the toilet leak policy and take no action on additional credit, but offer Ms. Fields a 2-year payment plan to help her pay down the balance affordability. The Commission took no action on this request, but did authorized the Office Manager to make a 2-year agreement at Ms. Fields’ request for the balance currently due.

Fairmount — Action 

## Approval of Fire Hydrant request 

The Fairmount Volunteer Fire Company is seeking the Sanitary District's help to supply a fire hydrant to their property on Fairmount Road to fast fill its engines and tankers. Mr. Stockus stated Fairmount VFC are the only station on the Sanitary District's system that doesn’t have a fast-fill hydrant. Mr. Stockus and Commission agreed this is a public safety issue and want to help. Mr. Stockus proposed the District pay the estimated cost of $16,000.00 out of construction funds, as this constitutes an extraordinary expense that benefits the health, safety, and welfare of the public. Mr. Bagley made a motion to approve the cost and installation of a fire hydrant to Fairmount VFC as an extraordinary expense (to be paid through construction); Ms. Somers seconded the motion; the motion carried to approval unanimously. Fire company members present were gracious for the Commission’s decision. 

Crisfield - Action 

### Approval of Minor Subdivision 

This request was tabled pending approval by the City of Crisfield of 1 EDU for additional lot. 

## 2:00 p.m. Appointment 

Andrew Karolick, property owner on Annemessex Road in Crisfield, was present to express his concerns and frustrations with his failed septic system. Mr. Karolick, whose home is currently connected to a holding tank, stated there is no viable solution as the Health Department will not approve a new septic system. Mr. Karolick requested public sewer be extended to the Annemessex Ridge subdivision.

<!-- page 9 -->
8 

Mr. Stockus explained Annemessex Ridge is not in a Priority Funding Area (PFA) meaning they are not eligible for grant funding from governmental agencies such as the USDA and MDE. Mr. Stockus also stated, the City of Crisfield owns the facility to which the sewer would be maintained, therefore, their approval would need to be acquired before the Sanitary District could consider. 

#### Public Input 

Joe Williams, Annemessex Ridge Resident and Homeowner’s Association President, expressed his opposition to Mr. Karolick’s request for the County to extend the sewer lines to Annemessex Ridge. Mr. Williams stated most of the residents he spoke with expressed concerns about cost (installation and quarterly bills); others have septic’s that are working fine, while some are content with using the holding tank method. 

Mr. Stockus reiterated that the Sanitary District will not consider pursuing sewer (at this time) to Annemessex Ridge for reasons stated to Mr. Karolick.
