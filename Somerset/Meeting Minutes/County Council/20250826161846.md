Mr. Drewer explained that in March 2022, Somerset County was awarded a $335,000 Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) for the Somerset County Housing Rehabilitation Program. Funding received was as follows:

- $75,000 for Emergency Housing Repairs 

- $35,000 for Project Administration 

Mr. Drewer advised that throughout the course of the grant, 13 projects have been completed totaling $276,035.60 leaving a remaining balance of $58,964.40. Those projects included 4 septic replacements, 4 roofs and several emergency repairs. It is expected that the remaining grant funds will be spent before the amended deadline of September 30, 2025. Mr. Jeremy Michalski, Apprenticeship Maryland Facilitator, Somerset County Technical High School came forward to discuss the proposal of a Student Apprenticeship Program for Somerset County. Mr, Michalski provided a PowerPoint presentation as follows: 

- Maryland’s population is aging slightly faster than the national average, noting that 50+ individuals are making up 37% of the state’s population. The 85 and older demographic is the fastest-growing segment within the group, projected to increase by 158% between 2020 and 2045. 

- This larger aging population means fewer people in the traditional workforce. Overall labor force participation rate in Maryland was 64.7% in June 2025, down from 69.3% in December 2019. 

- The estimated cost per apprentice is $7,543.00 which includes Social Security and Workman’s Comp Insurance. The request today is asking for the Board of County Commissioners to agree to fund one or more apprenticeships for our youth in any of the County departments. Some areas of learning which the county may be able to accommodate are carpentry, landscape, automotive and business. Ten positions would be ideal. 

- Success stories were shared which showed the benefits that are obtained with the apprenticeship program. 

Commissioner Mathies clarified that the investment being asked for today is $75,000 for 

10 apprenticeship positions. He questioned if the request was presented to the Board of Education. Mr. Michalski advised that the request was denied as it could not be budgeted at this time. Communication was also made with Tri-County Council as well. Mr. Michalski was thanked for attending the meeting today.

Crisfield. She explained that this non-profit corporation was started by herself, Mr. Steve Raab and Mr. John Phoebus to hold a music festival in Somerset County and to bring Tourism into the county. 

Ms, Raab explained they are a not-for-profit, with all proceeds going back into the local community. On October 11, 2025 they are having a Wine Festival at Somers Cove Marina. They will also have local vendors attending (produce and craft) as well as local wineries. She explained that part of the permit process allows vendors to sell locally made wines using their State-issued off-site permits if the event is designated as a “county agricultural fair”. Although Rising Tide Crisfield Inc could sell local wines under a one-day special event alcoholic beverages license, they would prefer to give these venders the ability to sell their wines directly to the attendees using their off-site permits. Authority to do this is defined in state law under the Alcoholic Beverages article, Annotated Code of Maryland. In order to allow this to occur, Ms. Raab advised that they are asking the Board of County Commissioners to designate the October 11, 2025 Crisfield Uncorked Wine Festival event as a Somerset County Agricultural Fair by proclamation to enable the holders of Class 3 Winery and Class 4 Limited Winery off-site permits to sell wines at the event directly to attendees. 

Mr. Steve Raab joined Ms. Raab at this time.
