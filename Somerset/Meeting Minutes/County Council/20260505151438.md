Mr, Woody Barnes, Public Works Director, came forward at this time to discuss the Boards thoughts regarding Landfill Tipping Fees and Transfer Station Permit Fees. He reviewed the current fees in place (Tipping Fee $75 and Transfer Station Permit Fee $64) the revenue generated at the current cost and comparisons to what our local adjoining counties are charging. 

Commissioner Mathies asked Mr. Barnes what his recommendation would be, noting increase costs for fuel and other items. Mr. Bares advised that he has the ability to segregate the Commercial Tipping Fee from the Residential Fee, and that the fee does not need to be the same, as has been done historically. A discussion was held, with it noted that many commercial vendors are utilizing our Landfill due to the cost savings of being lower than other counties are charging. He recommended keeping the Residential Tipping Fee at $75.00, and increasing the Commercial Tipping fee to $90.00 per ton, It was noted however, that any Somerset County Commercial vendors would be billed at the Residential Fee ($75). It was the overall opinion to keep the costs for the Transfer Station Permits the same. 

Commissioner Craig N. Mathies Sr., made a motion to approve an increase to the Commercial Tipping Fee for Non-Somerset County vendors to $90.00 per ton. The Tipping Fee for Residential vendors will remain the same at $75.00 per ton. Commissioner Willing seconded the motion. By show of<sup>hands,themotioncarried5-0.</sup> 

Vice President Randy Laird made a motion to have the Transfer Station Permit Fee remain the same at $64.00. Commissioner Mathies seconded the motion. By show of hands, the motion 

carried 5-0.

Regarding discussion items, Ms. Sharon Muir, Internal Auditor explained that the County has budgeted $522,488.00 for FY26 Ambulance Grants to Municipalities. The City of Crisfield pays for the EMS personnel that operate the Lower Somerset Ambulance and Rescue Squad, with the County reimbursing on a quarterly basis. The third quarter invoice was received totaling $195,451.37 which is $82,432.47 over the FY26 budget, with Ms. Muir noting that fourth quarter billing will be received in July. Ms. Muir requested a motion to approve an additional budget appropriation in the amount of $82,432.47 to cover this shortfall for the third quarter billing. It was also noted that an additional budget appropriation request will need to be presented for fourth quarter billing which should probably happen in early July. 

Vice President Randy Laird made a motion to approve the additional budget appropriation in the amount of $82,432.47 as presented. 

‘A discussion was held, with President Laird expressing his frustration, and questioning what the City is doing to pay their bilis/shortfalls, and not ask for handouts, stating he has a problem with the shortage, feeling that the City of Crisfield needs to re-work what they are doing. 

Vice President Randy Laird noted what the County has done for the EMS ambulance services the county has taken over, noting that what Lower Somerset Ambulance and Rescue is asking for is much less that what is being paid out for EMS Ambulance service. He also questioned what the Town of Princess Anne is providing towards the expense for ambulance services. It was noted only fuel, which Crisfield also pays. 

President Laird acknowledged the importance of Lower Somerset Ambulance, but stated that our ambulance services needs to work as one, feeling that if we work together we canfunction better. 

Commissioner Webster agreed, noting the Presidents original question, asking what the City is doing, stating he is hearing that the City is broke, asking what the Mayor is doing to correct their fiscal situation, with President Charles Laird questioning if the City has a plan, other than relying on grants and the county for funding. It was also noted that the funding is billed to the City, and the City asks for reimbursement from the County.

Mr. Erik Emely, Briarwood Circle, Crisfield requested to address the Board. First, Mr. Emely recalled events during Hurricane Sandy, when the Crisfield VFD was out all night long helping residents during the storm. During that event, he advised he never saw one shed floating in the flooding, questioning why the Planning and Zoning office is requiring that his shed have FEMA approved flood vents installed. He advised that just himself and another resident received the letters, noting that there are thousands of sheds in the City, who have not received a letter. He feels their time can be better used other than addressing sheds, He also commented on the discussion held regarding the Lower Somerset Ambulance and Rescue Squad, saying that we need to make sure about what is going on. He felt a comparison needs to be done to compare to see why the budget is not equal to activity, and who is watching to see they don’t go over budget, and how the City is distributing the funds. He did note that improvements that have been made with Crisfield EMS, and how much improved it is compared to a year ago. Also, regarding the approval to increase the tipping fee for out of county vendors using the Somerset County Landfill, he noted that Seagull collects trash for Crisfield, and although the vendors are being charged more, he feels the additional cost will be passed onto to City residents. Mr. Emely added that he has spoken to Mr. Barnes and Mr. Kitching, and are being told that will not be the case for Crisfield residents. 

Additionally, regarding Lower Somerset Ambulance and Rescue, Mr. Emely noted that there is the volunteer side, and the paid side, advising that he thought the Chief of Police in Crisfield is charge of monitoring the paid side of ambulance and rescue service.
