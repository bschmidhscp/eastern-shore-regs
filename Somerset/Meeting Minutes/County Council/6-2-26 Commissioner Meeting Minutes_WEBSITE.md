# Tuesday, June 2<sup>nd</sup> , 2026 at 2PM Somerset County Office Complex County Commissioners Meeting Room (Room 111) 11916 Somerset Avenue Princess Anne, Maryland 21853 

In accordance with the Critical Area Ordinance (#1071) of Somerset County Maryland, Chapter 1-117 (a,d,e,f), Somerset County is considering an amendment of Chapter 1-108 Resource Conservation Area Restaurants and Retreats to concur with the Critical Area Commission approval of these uses in the Resource Conservation Area.

<!-- page 3 -->
Members of the public are invited to attend the Public Hearing and comment on the draft amendments to the ordinance.  The County Commissioners will consider all public comment prior to making a decision on the Ordinance amendments. 

# **<u>Fair Summary</u>** 

Somerset County is required by State Law to regulate land use activities within the Chesapeake Bay Critical Area, defined as those lands within 1,000 feet of the tidal waters of the Chesapeake Bay and its tributaries.  The County has regulated these lands since September 10, 1988, through its Zoning Ordinance, and since 2014 through Ordinance #1071 the Critical Area Ordinance. Somerset County understands the desire to allow Restaurant and Retreats on properties located in the Resource Conservation Area (RCA) and through this amendment will establish standards for such events in concurrence with Critical Area Commission Approval. 

For further information please contact the Somerset County Department of Technical and Community Services at 410-651-1424 or by email at cskeeter@somersetmd.us

Mr. Woody Barnes and Mr. Gary Kitching joined the meeting to discuss Smith Island Waste Handling Operations. Mr. Barnes explained that the current operation is costly and inefficient, with FY2026 expenses projected at approximately $375,000. After meeting with the Maryland Environmental Service (MES), it was recommended ending the MES contract and transitioning operations to the county. MES agreed to continue providing ash testing, technical assistance, training, and operational support as needed. Mr. Barnes advised that the proposed plan includes hiring the island's licensed incinerator operator and an additional part-time County employee, allowing the facility to operate six days per week instead of three. The county would also continue providing dumpsters, transporting waste and debris to the mainland, expanding trash collection in Tylerton, and maintaining its agreement with Otis Tyler for transporting white goods and incinerator ash. The proposed FY2027 budget is approximately $360,000. While the cost savings are modest, the plan would improve service, provide greater operational control, and expand facility hours. Mr. Barnes noted that the Maryland Department of the Environment supports the proposal. 

A motion was made by Commissioner Darryl Webster and seconded by Vice President Randy Laird to approve the plan as presented. By a show of hands in favor by all present members of the Board, the motion carried. 

Mr. Barnes also advised of plans to relocate the waste transfer containers from the school property to A&N Electric property (with MDE’s support). Before concluding, the Board recognized Mr. Kitching and the Public Works crew for their work on floodgates, berms, and drainage improvements. Mr. Kitching reported that all permits have been received for the Long Point and Hudson White projects, with construction expected to begin soon.

Next, EMS Deputy Chief Cory Polidore came before the board to request approval to accept a $75,950 Public Safety Program Apprenticeship Grant from the Maryland Department of Labor. Deputy Chief Polidore advised the grant will fund the first year of an apprenticeship program designed to support Emergency Medical Technicians (EMTs) in obtaining their paramedic certification through Wor-Wic Community College. The funding will cover tuition costs for three employees during the first year, with plans to enroll two additional employees the following year. Deputy Chief Polidore noted that the county may apply annually for additional funding of $5,000 per apprentice participating in the program. He also reported that all three apprenticeship positions for the first year have already been filled, pending approval of the grant. A question was presented regarding obtaining a commitment from the participants, and Deputy Chief Polidore explained that the apprenticeship grant requires participants to sign a retention agreement committing to remain employed with the County for a specified period. He further advised that the retention agreements will be presented to the board for approval once the grant has been finalized. 

A motion was made by Commissioner Craig Mathies and seconded by Commissioner Darryl Webster to accept a $75,950 Public Safety Program Apprenticeship Grant from the Maryland Department of Labor as presented. By a show of hands in favor by all present members of the board, the motion carried. 

Deputy Chief Polidore left the meeting.
