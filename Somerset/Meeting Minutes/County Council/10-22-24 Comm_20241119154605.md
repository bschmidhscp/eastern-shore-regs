BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS FOR SOMERSET COUNTY October 22, 2024 

At 2:00 p.m., President Charles Laird called the Board of County Commissioners for Somerset County to open in Regular Session. Also present for today’s meeting were Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Eldon Willing, Commissioner Craig N. Mathies Sr., and Commissioner Darryl Webster. 

County Administrator Ralph D. Taylor, Deputy Administrator Emest J. Leatherbury Jr., and Executive Aide Lory E. Ebron were also present. 

Closed Session Summary Report 

On Tuesday, October 22, 2024 from 3:05 p.m., until 3:17 p.m., President Charles Laird, upon a motion made by Commissioner Eldon Willing, seconded by Commissioner Darryl Webster opened in Closed Session by Authority of the General Provisions Article of the Annotated Code of Maryland §3-305 (b)(1) the appointment, employment, assignment, promotion, discipline, demotion, compensation, removal, resignation, or performance evaluation of an appointee, employee, or official over whom it has jurisdiction. Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig Mathies Sr., and President Charles Laird voted in favor, therefore the motion carried 5-0. 

County Administrator Ralph D. Taylor, Ms. Lory Ebron, Executive Aide, Mr. Clint Sterling, Recreation and Parks/Tourism Director and Ms. Jamie Manaia, HR Director, were also present. Mr. Emest Leatherbury Jr., was not present in today’s meeting. 

Topics discussed: (1) Hiring for part time Golf Course Groundskeeper, fulltime Grass Cutter/Maintenance Worker (2) Probationary to permanent employee status. Topics Approved: (1) Approval to hire part time Golf Course Groundskeeper, fulltime Grass Cutter/Maintenance Worker (2) Approval to move probationary employee to permanent employee status.

Ms. Tracey Taylor, Lower Eastern Shore Regional Office, Mr. John Slaughter, Director UAS Research and Operation Center University of Maryland and Mr. Chris Hartman, University of Maryland Eastern Shore came forward to present an overview of the drone pilot program for medical delivery on the Eastern Shore. Ms. Taylor advised that the $1.7 Million project was announced by the Governor’s Office on October 8, 2024, and she has spoken to Mr. Eddie Somers, Smith Island United who is supportive of this project. They presented a PowerPoint presentation 

that outlined the following: 

- Funding by the Strengthening Mobility and Revolutionizing Transportation (SMART) grant by the US Department of Transportation 

- Team consists of Maryland Department of Planning, University of Maryland (UAS Research & Operations Center), University of Maryland Eastern Shore and Tidal Health 

- Drone Deliveries using Uncrewed Aircraft Systems (UAS) delivering packages (Trial markets at Walmart in Dallas and Ft. Worth Texas and Amazon at College Station Texas. Also, convenience items and prescriptions under ten pounds for deliveries that are faster and cost competitive 

- It was noted that the year 2026 is being titled the Year of the Drone as possibilities using drones in many different areas will be increasing 

- Benefits include convenience, use by fire, police and rescue, improved health outcomes, efficiency and economic development 

- The $1.7 Million project includes planning and prototyping the use of innovative aviation technology to improve transportation efficiency and safety. The Maryland Eastern Shore Drone Medical Delivery Project will involve transport of medical packages from Crisfield to Smith Island 

- Virginia is wrapping up a similar project linking Onancoke and Tangier Island. They are using information obtained and working closely with those working on that project to use as an example 

- = Possible concerns noted were privacy, noise and safety. 

- The schedule plan is to get a Drone Service Provider on contract, obtain public outreach, get site surveys completed this winter with flying starting in the spring of 2025, continuing through the summer 

The noise level was discussed which was noted as being minimal. Additionally, it was felt 

that local pharmacies can participate in the program should they want to. It was noted that this will be a trial program after which details for long term use will be determined. Mr. Slaughter noted that they will be looking at all types of creative and innovative uses for this program. 

Ms, Tracey Taylor next advised that she is retiring from her position as Director, MDP Lower Easter Shore Regional Office as she has accepted a position as Planning Director at Wicomico County. She expressed her thanks to all present, noting she has been in her current position for 27 years. The Board of Commissioners wished Ms. Taylor the best. 

Next, Ms. Danielle Weber, Health Officer, joined by Ms. Diane Waller and Ms. Christie Taylor came forward present a proposal ofchanges regarding the Somerset County Environmental Health Application Fees.

Board of Commissioners Meeting 10-22-24 

Correspondence was received from the Maryland Department of Commerce advising of the Private Activity Bond Allocation of Maryland State Ceiling. Historically, Somerset County transfers its allocation to the State because we don’t have the capacity of administer a County-run housing program. County residents can apply for the program through the State. No action is needed. 

Mr. Taylor next advised that Mr. Matthew Peter, Local Government Insurance Trust advised the office that upon conducting an audit of premium billed since 2015, it was determined that Somerset County was overbilled by $65,404. A check for that amount was provided and it was noted that corrective measures have been taken to correct the error and additional reviews will be conducted going forward. 

A letter was received from the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) releasing any final claim to the it has on any equipment inside the former Smith Island Crab Cooperative Building and agrees with the County’s request to use the building to serve the purpose of fire rescue on the island. As the building is owned by the county, it would fall under the rules for surplus property. 

The options for surplus property was discussed and the consensus was to lease the building to the Tylerton Volunteer Fire Company for $1.00. However, should the fire company dissolve, the lease agreement would end. Mr. Taylor was asked to have Attorney Simpkins draft a lease agreement with the stated terms. 

Vice President Randy Laird made a motion to have Attorney Simpkins draft a lease agreement between Somerset County and Tylerton Volunteer Fire Company to lease the former Smith Island Crab Cooperative Building for $1.00. Should the Fire Company dissolve, the lease will be terminated. Commissioner Webster seconded the motion. By show of hands, the motion carried 5-0. 

A pauper burial request was received from the family of 10-month old Wizdom L. Sturgis. Her mother is a resident in Princess Anne and she is looking for assistance to give her daughter a basic funeral. 

Commissioner Mathies made a motion to approve pauper burial assistance in the amount of $250.00 to Anthony Ward Funeral Home as requested. Commissioner Webster seconded the motion. By show of hands the motion carried 5-0.
