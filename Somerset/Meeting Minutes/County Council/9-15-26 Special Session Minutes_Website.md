## BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS FOR SOMERSET COUNTY **SPECIAL SESSION TUESDAY SEPTEMBER 15, 2026** 

At 2:00 pm, President Charles Laird called the Board of Commissioners to open in Special Session. Present for today’s meeting were President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig Mathies, Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Darryl Webster. 

Also in attendance were County Administrator Ernest Leatherbury and Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez. 

# **<u>Closed Session Summary Report for September 15, 2026</u>** 

On September 15, 2026, from 2:43 p.m. until 3:18 p.m., at 11916 Somerset Avenue, Room 111, upon a motion made by Commissioner Craig Mathies, seconded by Commissioner Darryl Webster, and carried on a vote in favor by all present members of the board to enter into Closed Session by Authority of the General Provisions Article of the Annotated Code of Maryland § 3-305 (b)(1) the appointment, employment, assignment, promotion, discipline, demotion, compensation, removal, resignation, or performance evaluation of an appointee, employee, or official over whom it has jurisdiction. 

Present for the meeting were President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig Mathies, Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Darryl Webster. County Administrator Ernest Leatherbury, Senator Mary Beth Carozza, EMS Deputy Chief Cory Polidore, Director Jamie Manaia, and Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez were also present. Topics discussed were: (1) Topic: Personnel; Action Taken: Probationary to Permanent Employee Status. 

President Charles Laird called the meeting to order and asked Commissioner Eldon Willing to lead those present in devotion and the pledge of allegiance. 

Mr. Leatherbury began by advising that the purpose of today’s meeting was for the annual the Somerset County Consolidated Transportation Program (CTP) Tour. He also acknowledged and welcomed Senator Mary Beth Carozza and Princess Anne Town Manager Dr. Drummond. 

At this time, Secretary of Maryland’s Department of Transportation Katie Thompson came forward to provide an overview of the drafted six-year Consolidated Transportation Program (CTP) and discuss the department’s transportation priorities. Secretary Thompson noted MDOT’s continued partnership with Somerset County and referenced transportation concerns previously raised at MACo, including safety at MD413 and Fairmount Road, and advised how they are considering additional signage to remind motorists of the stop sign at that location. Secretary Thompson then continued by identifying safety, maintaining existing infrastructure, and making the most of available funding as the three main priorities of the six-year capital plan. The drafted CTP includes $21.9 billion in capital funding and, due

<!-- page 3 -->
to rising construction, operating costs, and limited revenues, she noted that the plan focuses on the most critical needs while continuing efforts to improve how transportation projects are prioritized and funded. Secretary Thompson also reported that statewide roadway fatalities have declined over the past two years but emphasized that additional safety work is needed. She highlighted ongoing partnerships with local governments and the “Slow the Fast Down” campaign, which focuses on reducing excessive speeding. 

Next, the presentation was then turned over to State Highway Administrator Will Pines. Mr. Pines provided updates on several transportation projects in Somerset County while reiterating MDOT’s focus on safety, maintaining existing infrastructure, and supporting economic development while working within current budget constraints. Mr. Pines reported that work is continuing on the County’s top transportation priority, the 12-mile shared-use trail along MD413 between Westover and Crisfield with construction of Phase 2 underway. He further advised that property coordination has affected the schedule for the Phase 2A and 2B segments, and SHA has stated that right-of-way issues have been addressed and that it is coordinating with the Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) to move the required permit forward. Once the permit is cleared, SHA will provide the County with an updated schedule and coordinate next steps. Mr. Pines also advised that SHA is also advancing the design of an acceleration lane on US13 at MD667, with the design expected to be completed by the end of the year. He also noted several pavement projects completed throughout the County over the past year before congratulating the County on the upcoming groundbreaking for renovations at Somers Cove Marina in Crisfield and noted MDOT and SHA’s role in helping facilitate the $5.95 million grant for the project in coordination with the Department of Natural Resources and the Department of General Services. District Engineer Mark Crampton thanked the County for its letter of support regarding a road dedication and noted that the support will help move the process forward. He also emphasized the ongoing communication between SHA, County officials, and local representatives regarding transportation and development issues. 

The presentation was then handed over to Melissa Williams of the Maryland Transportation Authority who provided an update on Bay Bridge safety and planned improvements. Ms. Williams advised that MDTA recently updated www.baybridge.com with an interactive map and real-time camera views to help motorists monitor conditions and plan travel along the Bay Bridge and Route 50 corridor. She also reported that the preferred alternative for the Bay Bridge crossing study includes two new four-lane bridge spans with shoulders and the removal of the two existing spans with more than 1,000 public comments that have been received since January during the draft environmental document review. Pending funding and completion of the final environmental review and Federal Highway Administration approval, preliminary design procurement is expected to begin next, with design anticipated to start in 2028 and construction potentially beginning as early as 2032. Phase 1 of the eastbound Bay Bridge deck replacement remains on schedule for completion in spring 2027. Ms. Williams also noted that MDTA’s facilities are primarily funded through toll revenues rather than the MDOT Transportation Trust Fund. Due to inflation

<!-- page 4 -->
and increased costs, MDTA is evaluating its long-term financial needs. Any future toll changes would be subject to a required public process and approval by the MDTA Board, potentially in FY 2028. 

Following this, the presentation was then handed over to Travis Johnston, the Director of the Office of Local Transit Support for Maryland Transit Administration. Mr. Johnston provided an update on transit funding included in the drafted CTP, noting that the plan prioritizes transit system maintenance and improvements to strengthen service statewide. He reported that Somerset County will receive nearly $5.2 million in grants to support local transit operations through Shore Transit, in partnership with Worcester and Wicomico Counties. The funding will support preventative maintenance, mobility management, facility improvements, and the purchase of two new medium-duty buses. Mr. Johnston thanked the County for its partnership and continued support of local transit services before turning the presentation over to Ms. Leslie Dews, Deputy Administrator for Field Operations at the MVA. 

Ms. Dews reported that the MVA’s average customer wait time at branch offices has remained at approximately five minutes for the third consecutive year and that nearly 3 million Marylanders are enrolled in the “My MVA” account, which now allows customers to complete approximately 60 transactions online, including electronic titling services. She also highlighted outreach efforts in Somerset County, including events at the Somerset Senior Center to help older Marylanders improve the fit and comfort of their vehicles for increased safety. Ms. Dews then reported that three people were killed in roadway crashes in Somerset County last year, with one fatality reported so far this year. She noted that while the decrease represents progress, continued efforts are needed to reduce roadway fatalities and announced $2,000 in highway safety grants for organizations and law enforcement in Somerset County to address distracted, impaired, and aggressive driving, as well as pedestrian and bicyclist safety. 

Next, the presentation was then turned over to Chelsea Dorman, Aviation Services Manager with Maryland Aviation Administration in the Office of Aviation Assistance. Ms. Dorman provided an update on the state’s aviation system and its role in supporting travel, tourism, and economic development. She also reported that the FY2027 statewide aviation grants program will provide $3.5 million in funding for regional airports, including $21,000 for design work related to runway reconstruction at CrisfieldSomerset County Airport. Ms. Dorman highlighted the $841 million capital program, which focuses heavily on maintaining and improving existing airport infrastructure. Planned work includes runway and apron rehabilitation and terminal improvements involving baggage systems, security, roofing, flooring, passenger boarding bridges, elevators, and escalators. It was noted that these investments are intended to support the safe and reliable operation of Maryland’s airports and continued service to passengers, airlines, and local communities. 

Secretary Thompson then advised that this concluded the CTP Tour and that staff would be happy to answer any questions the board may have.

Vice President Randy Laird expressed major safety concerns regarding the turn into the transfer station off of MD413, advising that this was initially brought up during the MACo conference and would like to inquire if any solutions have been discussed. Mr. Pines advised that no final solution has been identified but they are evaluating a potential restriping of the shoulder to create a legal bypass around vehicles waiting to make a left turn into the transfer station. He further noted that the shoulder would need to be able to support traffic, including heavy trucks and equipment, and additional review is needed to determine whether the improvement can be completed through restriping alone. The proposed approach would not widen MD413 but would use the existing pavement more effectively to provide a short bypass in the area. It was noted that similar designs are used at other locations in the state, including Holland Crossing. Vice President Randy Laird expressed concern that signs and pavement markings alone may not be sufficient to prevent a serious incident, and Mr. Pines explained that the proposed design would provide a clearly marked bypass for motorists rather than relying solely on signage. He advised that staff will continue to evaluate the location and determine whether the existing shoulder can accommodate the proposed improvement. 

Commissioner Craig Mathies raised concerns about vehicles failing to slow down when approaching and entering the roundabout on UMES Boulevard, noting several instances of vehicles traveling through the roundabout without yielding and creating a potential safety hazard. Mr. Pines agreed to review the location and consider possible improvements, including additional pavement markings, flexible posts or other physical measures to encourage drivers to slow down. Other design options, such as lane shifts and rumble strips, may also be considered, although it was noted that rumble strips could create noise concerns for nearby residents. It was confirmed that yield signs are currently posted at the roundabout, and the discussion also referenced MDOT’s “Slow the Fast Down” campaign, which addresses aggressive and excessive speeding. 

Commissioner Darryl Webster commended the MVA for the significant improvements in customer service and reduced wait times, and noted that the increased use of technology, online services, and customer kiosks has made MVA visits much quicker and more convenient compared with previous years. 

President Charles Laird inquired about planned access to the proposed Wawa development from US13. Mr. Pines reported that the access permit process is ongoing and that the department is working with the Town of Princess Anne on the dedication of the road behind Hardee’s as a public road. Once the road is dedicated, MDOT will be able to coordinate the necessary connections and provide additional access to the development. He further explained that the proposed access will include a slip ramp into the Wawa facility with the roadway connecting US13 and MD675 serving as the local access road. It was confirmed that there will be northbound access from US13 into the Wawa, but the development will not have direct highway access in the traditional sense.

At this time, Senator Mary Beth Carozza was invited to come forward to speak. Senator Carozza expressed her appreciation for MDOT’s accessibility and its continued partnership with Somerset County, stating the importance of working collaboratively to address transportation issues, including the ongoing efforts to resolve issues related to the Crisfield Highway Trail. The involvement of former Delegate Carl Anderton, District Engineer Mark Crampton, and Mr. Clint Sterling was recognized for helping address concerns and avoid the need for legal action. Senator Carozza also recognized MVA Administrator Christine Nizer for her longstanding commitment to safety, customer service, and improving the experience for Maryland residents using MVA services. The discussion also highlighted the importance of Shore Transit to rural communities, particularly in addressing workforce transportation needs and providing access to employment and medical services. 

Next, Mayor Darlene Taylor of Crisfield was invited to come forward and began by thanking SHA for its continued partnership with the City of Crisfield. Mayor Taylor highlighted SHA’s assistance with the water pump located in the median along the main highway, which has helped address flooding concerns, as well as recent efforts by SHA staff to assess issues in the city’s medians. Mayor Taylor also thanked MDOT staff for assistance with a planning grant and other efforts to identify funding and solutions for local road improvements. Repairing and repaving Crisfield’s roads was identified as a top priority, as many roads have not been paved in 20 years or more. Mayor Taylor concluded by emphasizing that improving the condition of Crisfield’s roads is both a safety and infrastructure priority and expressed her desire to continue working closely with MDOT and SHA on funding and resilience-related solutions. 

Dr. Drummond, Town Manager for Princess Anne, was next invited to come forward to speak. Dr. Drummond expressed her appreciation for MDOT’s responsiveness and collaboration regarding the Wawa project and noted that questions and requests related to easements, roads, and the dedication of the roadway have been addressed promptly and thanked MDOT for its continued support of the Town. 

With no public comments or further business needed in open session, the board was advised for the need of a closed session. 

Therefore, at 2:43 p.m., a motion was made by Commissioner Craig Mathies and seconded by Commissioner Darryl Webster to enter into Closed Session by authority of the General Provisions Article of the Annotated Code of Maryland § 3-305 (b) (1) the appointment, employment, assignment, promotion, discipline, demotion, compensation, removal, resignation, or performance evaluation of an appointee, employee, or official over whom it has jurisdiction. By a show of hands in favor by all present members of the board, the motion carried.
