# **SOMERSET COUNTY ROADS BOARD TUESDAY FEBRUARY 18, 2025** 

At 2:00 p.m., the Somerset County Roads Board met in regular session with President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, and Commissioner Craig N. Mathies, Sr. Commissioner Eldon Willing and Commissioner Darryl Webster were not in attendance. 

Also present for today’s meeting were Deputy County Administrator Ernie Leatherbury, Public Works Director Woody Barnes, County Roads Assistant Superintendent Gary Beauchamp, and Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez. 

President Charles Laird called the meeting to order and asked Commissioner Craig Mathies to lead those present in Devotion and the Pledge of Allegiance. 

Minutes from the January 21, 2025 meeting were presented for approval. A motion was made by Commissioner Craig Mathies, seconded by Vice President Randy Laird and carried by a show of hands in favor by President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, and Commissioner Craig Mathies. 

# **ROADS DIVISION** 

Mr. Barnes provided an update on the BRIC DTA project, advising that the kickoff meeting took place on January 29th-30th with FEMA and various partners in attendance. Discussions covered community needs, action steps, and BRIC DTA support. A site visit included the Crisfield tidal dike where the team observed existing maintenance efforts, with additional stops in Fairmount, Dames Quarter, and Oriole to address nuisance flooding and road elevation issues. Mr. Barnes advised that while the meeting was productive, an executive order on February 14<sup>th</sup> has paused the project for 30 days. 

Next, Mr. Barnes advised of ongoing concerns regarding countywide railroad crossings, referencing a January 14, 2021 meeting with State Highway (SHA) that focused on prioritizing safety concerns, particularly near schools. Mr. Barnes advised that, at the time, Delmarva Central had recently completed major repairs, and most concerns were tabled due to a lack of crash data to justify funding. He then advised that new funding opportunities have recently emerged, prompting a diagnostics meeting with SHA and Delmarva Central. He continued by stating that safety remains a key issue, especially following two recent railway accidents at Loretto Road and Wallace Taylor Road. Additionally, crossings continue to deteriorate, despite minor repairs, such as at Perry Road, which is why improving crossing conditions is now a top priority.

A discussion was held regarding a 925-foot section of College Backbone Road, along the Manokin Branch, that is experiencing shoulder erosion due to water flow. Mr. Barnes advised that a citizen has raised concerns about traffic volume, speed, and safety **,** and has requested a guardrail installation. He continued by advising that a traffic study conducted from January 27<sup>th</sup> to February 2<sup>nd</sup> found that motorists are generally adhering to the speed limit. Mr. Barnes advised that the estimated cost to install a guardrail is $43,000, which is an unbudgeted expense requiring board approval. In the meantime, Mr. Barnes advised that safety improvements have been made, including larger signage, shoulder repairs, and delineators to mark the drop-off. Further vegetation work is planned for the spring. Mr. Barnes advised that, at this time, a guardrail is not recommended due to the lack of crash data. 

Next, Mr. Barnes reported damage to several county culverts discovered during a spot inspection by Mr. Gary Kitching following the installation of fiber utilities. The installation company failed to adhere to county standards, causing damage by not boring underneath the culverts as required but instead cutting directly through them. Mr. Barnes advised that the installation company is currently in negotiations with the county for reimbursement. Repairs are underway, though pipe installation costs are significant; but the company’s insurance is expected to cover the expenses, and the reimbursement process is in progress. 

A discussion was held regarding drainage issues on MD413. Mr. Barnes explained that the county had rerouted a ditch along the bike trail to address water flow, but water remained trapped between Route 413 and the bike trail. He then advised that, after meeting with SHA, they claimed no responsibility for the issue as the transfer station exit to Route 413 was not authorized. A solution to trench across the bike trail was proposed, but SHA later insisted they owned the trail and would only allow work if the county restored the pavement and obtained permits. Mr. Barnes expressed frustration with SHA’s lack of cooperation, especially considering potential issues like mosquito breeding and advised that the county is still working to resolve the situation. 

# **WATERWAYS** 

Mr. Barnes provided an update on the St. Peters Creek Dock project, noting that all grant funding has been secured through DNR. George Young’s office is finalizing the construction drawings, and the county will not need permits from the Maryland Department of Environment as the project involves replacing the dock in kind without exceeding its original footprint. Construction is planned to begin in late October or early November, with the goal of completing

An update was provided on the Ewell County Dock with Mr. Barnes advising that the county has received design and engineering funds from DNR for the project, and the contract was awarded to George Young. Mr. Young has conducted an inspection of the facility and is currently assessing the best course of action. Next, Mr. Barnes advised that the design phase for the Tylerton Breakwater project is complete. However, the county has been informed that the permit application process has been redirected to a new division of the Maryland Department of Environment for a permit, rather than the waterways division. While waiting for the permit, the county has been in discussions about utilizing concrete from parts of the Key Bridge for the project, which could provide cost savings compared to the usual materials.

# **DISCUSSION** 

Vice President Randy Laird advised of a dead deer on the road before Gandy Lane. Mr. Beauchamp advised that it has been removed. 

Vice President Randy Laird then advised that Mr. Duke Marshall has proposed steps be installed at County boat ramps to assist with people getting on and off their boats. 

The meeting then ended with a brief discussion regarding the severe winter storm forecasted for this week. Mr. Barnes expressed concerns for the high winds and snow drifts, and advised that trucks (minus one that is down) will be on the roads starting tomorrow and carry on through Friday. 

With no further business to discuss, a motion was made by Vice President Randy Laird, seconded by Commissioner Craig Mathies, and carried on a vote in favor by a show of hands by President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, and Commissioner Craig Mathies, to adjourn the Somerset County Roads Board meeting at 2:24 p.m.
