# BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS FOR SOMERSET COUNTY **WORK SESSION TUESDAY OCTOBER 14, 2025** 

At 2:24 pm, President Charles Laird called the Board of Commissioners to open in Work Session. Present for today’s meeting were President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig Mathies, and Commissioner Eldon Willing. Commissioner Darryl Webster. Was absent for today’s meeting. 

Also in attendance were Interim County Administrator Ernest Leatherbury and Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez. 

Director of Recreation, Parks & Tourism Clint Sterling was now present before the board provide a comprehensive update on the Somerset County Bike Trail project. County Engineer John Redden participated by phone. 

Mr. Sterling began by advising that the objective of his presentation today was to maintain transparency with the board and the public, while offering a detailed report on progress, responsibilities, and concerns associated with the trail. Mr. Sterling then offered a brief history of the bike trail project, starting off by stating that the first phase was constructed entirely with federal and state funding (with no local tax dollars utilized). This segment was developed under the direction of Somerset County in partnership with the State Highway Administration (SHA) and as part of that agreement the County committed to a maintenance contract that included keeping the trail itself clear of debris and vegetation, as well as maintaining a three-foot buffer on either side of the trail. Beyond this, mowing and other landscaping responsibilities remain under SHA. However, Mr. Sterling advised that the county has taken on the (unrequested) initiative to maintain adjacent areas, including parking lots and access points, to improve the overall appearance and usability of the trail. Mr. Sterling also advised that in efforts to enhance maintenance that they have recently rented a mini-excavator to reach hard-to-access areas. These actions, Mr. Sterling stated, are a continued commitment to keeping the trail both functional and attractive, despite resource limitations. 

Discussion then turned to the second phase of the trail, which Mr. Sterling advised is currently under construction and being managed entirely by the state. This segment, which extends south from the recreation facility in Westover, has seen limited local input in terms of design or execution. While Mr. Sterling and Mr. Redden have attended planning meetings and provided

feedback, the state has led the process. He then acknowledged the importance of state funding, stating that without such support, this phase would not have been possible. Mr. Sterling further advised that, although the county has not directed construction, it will be responsible for maintaining the trail segment once it is complete (under terms identical to those of the first phase). The board was informed that landscaping and stormwater features are actively being installed along the new stretch, and that construction is currently ahead of schedule, within budget, and  no major issues reported to date. 

Continuing, Mr. Sterling advised that the third phase of the project (intended to connect the existing portions of the trail and include a bridge) has reached the final design review stage. However, the permitting process has been delayed due to objections raised by an adjacent property owner. The property owner, who is also a biologist, expressed concerns about specific engineering practices, tree removal, and the scale of certain proposed structures. He was particularly troubled by the removal of mature cedar trees and the planting of new trees that, according to previous communication, would supposedly not have overhanging limbs. The property owner also questioned the necessity and cost of the proposed bridge, which appears to be engineered to accommodate emergency vehicles, rather than solely pedestrian traffic; raising questions about whether the scale and associated cost of the structure were appropriate. Mr. Sterling reiterated that the county has no role in the design or funding decisions for this phase. However, it has been conveyed to SHA that the county does not wish to assume maintenance responsibility for major structures such as the bridge or the boardwalk planned near the fire department in Marion. 

It was also noted during the meeting that the property owner had been promised an opportunity to meet with state engineers on-site, which did not occur, contributing to his dissatisfaction. Mr. Sterling advised that he has made every effort to maintain open communication with the landowner and to advocate for a balanced resolution. 

Mr. Sterling advised that concerns were also raised by other landowners along the trail corridor regarding continued access across the path with farm or logging equipment. He confirmed that such access will not be denied and is working to ensure these rights are formalized in writing to prevent future disputes. It was also acknowledged that any delay in phase three caused by a public hearing or other procedural challenges could risk the loss of federal funding, and any significant delays could put the project’s future in jeopardy.

The discussion concluded with Mr. Sterling reiterating that the county remains committed to maintaining the sections of trail under its responsibility but does not intend to assume maintenance of large, costly infrastructure such as bridges or boardwalks. The county will continue to maintain the trail path and its immediate surroundings, but ultimate responsibility for design, construction, and funding remains with the state. Mr. Sterling emphasized that there is no intention of raising local taxes for this project, and no local funding has been used in any phase. 

With no further business to discuss, a motion was made by Vice President Randy Laird and seconded by Commissioner Eldon Willing to adjourn the Work Session. By a vote in favor by all present members of the Board, the motion carried and the meeting adjourned at 2:42 p.m.
