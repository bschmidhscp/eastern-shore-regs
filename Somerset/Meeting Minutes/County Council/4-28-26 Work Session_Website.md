# BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS FOR SOMERSET COUNTY **WORK SESSION TUESDAY APRIL 28, 2026** 

At 6:00 pm, President Charles Laird called the Board of Commissioners to open in a Work Session. Present for today’s meeting were President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Darryl Webster. Commissioner Craig Mathies, was absent today. 

Also in attendance were County Administrator Ernest Leatherbury and Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez. 

President Charles Laird called the meeting to order and asked for Commissioner Eldon Willing to lead those present in devotion and the Pledge of Allegiance. 

President Charles Laird called the meeting to order and asked for Commissioner Eldon Willing to lead those present in devotion and the Pledge of Allegiance. 

Members of the Somerset County Volunteer Fireman’s Association (SCVFA) came forward for discussion. 

Mr. John Barnette began by thanking the commissioners for supporting the recently implemented burn ban, stating that dry conditions to expected continue, making this ban necessary for public safety. It was then advised that EMS Advisory Council meetings are now open to the public (except during executive sessions). Mr. Barnette then inquired if they county had succeeded in hiring a permanent EMS Chief, and Mr. Leatherbury stated that applications are still under review and interviews have not yet begun, but the process is moving forward. Next, Mr. Barnette addressed the recent fatal grain engulfment incident that happened in Delaware. He advised that the county currently possesses two engulfment tubes used for victim extrication during such incidents, but acknowledged that additional technical training is needed to effectively perform rescues. The planned training will be coordinated through the UMES and will involve technical rope rescue systems and mechanical lifting operations necessary to safely remove trapped victims from grain bins. Discussion then shifted to the county’s Insurance Services Office (ISO) ratings and mitigation assessments, which affect insurance premiums and can support requests for additional staffing and funding. Mr. Barnette noted that ISO evaluations now consider

<!-- page 3 -->
multiple areas, including communications systems and building code compliance, rather than just fire department capabilities. 

SCVFA representatives also cited frustrations between volunteer fire departments and Acting EMS Chief Polidore regarding emergency alerting systems. They explained that all nine county departments currently use an alerting application called “Chief Backstage” through the 911 system and that departments are highly satisfied with its performance. However, Acting EMS Chief Polidore has reportedly advocated replacing it with another platform known as “Active 911” in an effort to save approximately $5,000 to $6,000 annually. It was also stated that the fire departments strongly oppose the change because they believe the proposed program does not perform as effectively and because 911 personnel who have tested the system were reportedly unimpressed with its capabilities. The chiefs emphasized that their frustration stems not only from the proposed savings measure itself, but also from broader concerns about what they perceive as excessive spending elsewhere within EMS operations while attempts are simultaneously made to reduce costs in areas they consider critical and already successful. The board reassured the association members that they maintain authority over budget approvals and emphasized that no changes would be made without careful review. The board reiterated that ongoing communication meetings were essential because they rely on feedback from frontline emergency personnel when evaluating operational and budgetary decisions. Questions were raised regarding the status of workman’s compensation coverage improvements, retirement benefits, and proposed tax incentives for volunteer firefighters. Mr. Leatherbury stated that a public hearing concerning workman’s compensation is tentatively planned for the 26th of the month. Representatives were also advised that the tax incentive proposal remains under review and may require support from local delegates and state legislation. The fire department representatives then asked to help draft and review any proposed language to ensure it reflects the needs of emergency service personnel. 

Next, Mr. Eric Emely addressed the board next with concerns about declining volunteer firefighter recruitment and retention across Somerset County. Preliminary survey findings were shared, suggesting there are only about 30 volunteer firefighters under the age of 30 countywide, raising concerns about the aging workforce and long-term

<!-- page 4 -->
sustainability of emergency services. Mr. Emely suggested a need to better introduce young residents to emergency services careers and volunteer opportunities, recalling successful school-based outreach programs from the late 1980s and 1990s, when EMS, fire, and police personnel regularly visited middle schools to engage students and promote public safety careers. The board acknowledge this and proposed meeting with the school superintendent (with fire department representation) to explore educational and recruitment pathways for students interested in emergency services careers. 

SCVFA representatives then addressed the board regarding EMS spending and future programs, raising concerns that Somerset County may be considering costly EMS programs, such as the blood grant, because larger counties have adopted them. Mr. Emely added to this with an inquiry of the EMS Advisory Board reviewing EMS programs and proposals, and whether the County Commissioners actively approves initiatives or simply receive updates. Deputy Chief Polidore provided an answer to both of these concerns, explaining that the proposed blood program would be funded through a federal rural health transportation grant for about five years, and that several nearby counties have applied for similar programs (the board and fire department representatives still questioned how the program would be funded long-term once grant money ends and noted that Maryland State Police helicopters can already deliver blood products quickly during emergencies if needed). He then addressed the question regarding EMS Advisory Board meetings, stating that proposals are discussed and voted on during advisory board meetings. Fire department representative requested access to meeting minutes so non-board members can stay informed, and it was agreed to provide them moving forward. 

Lastly, an inquiry was made regarding EMS billing, and Deputy Chief Polidore advised that EMS billing officially began on April 17th and that the first billing export had already been processed. 

This concluded the end of discussion with the Somerset County Volunteer Fireman Association. All members in attendance were thanked for attending the meeting and the discussions held. 

Next, Director of Public Works Woody Barnes addressed the board to present the FY27 Consolidated Transportation Priority Letter. New to the priority this year is the widening of the intersection of U.S. Route 13 and Maryland Route 413 due to expected

<!-- page 5 -->
traffic increases from the planned recreational complex. Mr. Barnes then addressed projects being removed from the transportation priority list, including a proposed pedestrian crossing at Route 363 and Route 13 and several Safe Routes to School projects as the state declined funding for those projects and was too costly and extensive for the County to pursue alone. Additional discussion focused on railroad crossing signal upgrades, drainage improvements in tidal communities, acceleration lane requests, and roadway widening efforts. Some railroad crossing upgrades are expected later this year, while other projects remain under state review. The board also raised ongoing traffic safety concerns near the county transfer station on Route 413 and said they plan to continue pressing the state to revisit possible improvements in the area. 

Following this, the board was advised for the need of a special session to enter into closed session. A motion was made by Commissioner Darryl Webster and seconded by Commissioner Eldon Willing to adjourn work session and enter into special session. By a vote in favor by all present members of the board, the motion carried and the work session was adjourned at 6:31 p.m.
