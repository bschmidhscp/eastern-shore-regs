#### BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS 

FOR SOMERSET COUNTY WORK SESSION 

July 21, 2026 

At 2:00 p.m., Vice President Randy Laird called the Board of County Commissioners for Somerset County to open in Work Session. Also present for today’s meeting were Commissioner Darryl Webster, Commissioner Craig N. Mathies Sr., and Commissioner Eldon Willing. President Charles Laird was absent from today’s meeting. 

County Administrator Ernest J. Leatherbury Jr., and Executive Aide Lory E. Ebron were 

also present. 

Vice President Randy Laird asked Commissioner Eldon Willing to lead those present in Devotion and the Pledge of Allegiance. 

Mr, Jesse Drewer, Director of the Technical and Community Services Department came forward at this time. 

First, Mr. Drewer advised that during the 2026 Maryland Legislative Session, SB624 ~ the Jillian and Lindsay Wiener Short-Term Rental Safety Act was passed and signed into law. The law mandates certain requirements for short term rentals including evacuation diagrams, emergency telephone numbers, fire extinguishers, and smoke and carbon monoxide alarms. In addition, each rental unit is required to be inspected annually, and reported to the State Fire Marshall. He explained the impact on the county in that his department will be required to start a rental licensing and inspection program, which is not in place at this time. The first report is due July 2028; however, he explained the need to start working on the process now, as time will be needed to coordinate efforts regarding the program such as creating the job description and hiring a person for the position, researching rental units in the county via various communication efforts such as mailings, social media, etc. His target date to have the program in place is July 2027, with inspections being performed and documented in the fall/winter of 2027. He noted this is an unfunded mandate. Some costs can be recouped by charging fees for the inspections; however, Vice President Randy Laird noted that unfortunately this fee will be likely to be passed down to renters in that case. On a positive note this mandate will assist the county with rentals needing improvements, including water, which has been discussed during the Board of Health meeting previously held, Commissioner Webster asked Mr. Drewer when he will need this additional person. Mr. Drewer advised by year end, so that out-reach can be started which will take some

Next, a discussion was held regarding the pressures being applied across the state regarding Data Centers. Mr. Drewer advised it is a hot topic being discussed lately, distributing an article recently written by the Washington Post. He advised that there are two counties (Worcester and Caroline) who have already passed one-year moratoriums to allow more time to research and review potential impacts these Data Centers will have on their counties. The purpose of<sup>initiating</sup> a moratorium was questioned, Mr. Drewer explained that currently by not having the language incorporated within Zoning Code, an entity can go to the Zoning Appeals Board, and be approved because there are no guidelines in place. Commissioner Webster questioned if there is any power in having a moratorium. Mr. Drewer explained that any interested parties would have to wait should a moratorium be put in place. He explained how the process would work, similar to what was done for Cannabis, advising a moratorium would “tap the brakes” on the projects and provide some protection to the county while the what, where's and how’s are figured out. It is felt that these projects will most likely not be employee generating businesses for our residents. 

Commissioner Mathies also added that during the Choptank Board of Directors meeting yesterday, it was noted that Federalsburg is looking into getting one, asking the CEO of Choptank to give some insight on the issue of Data Centers with the strain that will be placed on electric companies, as well as rate increases that may fall on citizens doing to increases in usage, and will companies be willing to offset some of the increased costs. Mr. Drewer referenced an article provided in the packet addressing the Federalsburg matter, advising that Hartford County has chosen to ban Data Centers entirely. Commissioner Willing questioned what State legislature stand on this issue is going to be, noting again the previous comment made by Commissioner Webster in that state mandates will trump local zoning codes such as with solar. Mr. Drewer advised that legislature has not yet presented their stance, but he does foresee seeing something coming out on the matter this year; he is just not sure what. Commissioner Willing questioned that if legislature comes out in favor of Data Centers, where will that leave local county jurisdictions. Mr. Drewer noted that if that happens, the county will have to taylor our laws to favor legislation, similar again to Cannabis.
