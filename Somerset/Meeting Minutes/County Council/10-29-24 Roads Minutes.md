# **SOMERSET COUNTY ROADS BOARD TUESDAY OCTOBER 29, 2024** 

At 2:00 p.m., the Somerset County Roads Board met in regular session with President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig N. Mathies, Sr., Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Darryl Webster. 

Also present for today’s meeting were Deputy County Administrator Ernie Leatherbury, County Roads Director Woody Barnes, County Roads Superintendent Gary Beauchamp, and Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez. 

President Charles Laird called the meeting to order and asked Commissioner Eldon Willing to lead those present in Devotion and the Pledge of Allegiance. 

Minutes from the September 3, 2024 open meeting were presented for approval. A motion was made by Vice President Randy Laird and seconded by Commissioner Eldon Willing to approve the minutes as presented. By a show of hands in favor by President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig Mathies, Commissioner Eldon Willing, Commissioner Darryl Willing, the motion carried. 

# **ROADS DIVISION** 

An update was provided on the FY25 Road Resurfacing Project. Mr. Barnes advised that 32 miles of tar and chip have been completed successfully. Work on Smith Island continues; however, the Roads Department has faced challenges with the barge transportation due to unfavorable weather conditions. Mr. Barnes then advised that funds saved from the asphalt overlay program will be used to address urgent repairs on Sign Post Road. These repairs, originally part of the five-year plan, will be completed sooner, allowing the road to settle before further work. 

Mr. Barnes advised that the Roads Department has scheduled the painting of approximately 24 miles of road in the county. These roads are a mix of newly paved surfaces and older roads that have worn due to traffic. The work is expected to take place either later this week or next, depending on weather conditions. 

Next, Mr. Barnes presented an issue with the 10,000-gallon fuel tank at the Roads Department, which has developed a leak. The spill was contained and 6,100 gallons of fuel were safely removed, but the tank is now unusable. After considering options, replacing the tank was determined to be the best solution, with a quote of $57,313 for the replacement. With this being

A discussion was held regarding the FY25 capital purchases. Mr. Barnes advised that the Roads Department budgeted $200,000 for two new dump trucks and received bids from International Delmarva and Ford. The Ford bid, at $147,000, was $50,000 less than International Delmarva’s, with both trucks meeting the same specifications. Mr. Barnes recommended purchasing the Ford truck, noting the option to buy through a piggyback contract with Worcester County and Ocean City. 

A motion was made by Vice President Randy Laird and seconded by Commissioner Darryl Webster to authorize the purchase of the two Ford dump trucks as requested. By a show of hands in favor by President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig Mathies, Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Darryl Webster, the motion carried. 

Next, a discussion was held regarding Ducks Unlimited Phragmites Eradication Project, (in partnership with the Maryland Department of Agriculture and DNR). Mr. Barnes advised that this three-year project is aimed at addressing the spread of phragmites on 2,000 acres of private land in Somerset County. Ducks Unlimited will be heading the entire project with the Roads department's role being to identify affected areas. 

A second program regarding Ducks Unlimited is the Wetland Restoration Program, which seeks to reduce pollution flowing into the Chesapeake Bay. Ducks Unlimited has applied for a $1 million grant to fund ditch maintenance and wetland restoration on 20-30 acres in Somerset County. They requested the county's support for in-kind services, including the use of an amphibious excavator, at no financial cost. Mr. Barnes requested approval to provide a letter of support for the grant. 

A motion was made by Commissioner Craig Mathies and seconded by Commissioner Darryl Webster to approve the letter of support and authorize the president’s signature. By a show of hands in favor by President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig Mathies, Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Darryl Webster, the motion carried.

A discussion was then held regarding surplus vehicles. Mr. Barnes requested approval to salvage several unusable older vehicles, with a combined estimated value of $3,524 (pricing provided by Route 30). 

A motion was made by Commissioner Craig Mathies and seconded by Commissioner Eldon Willing to approve the request to salvage the requested surplus vehicles. By a show of hands in favor by President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig Mathies, Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Darryl Webster, the motion carried. 

Next, Mr. Barnes advised of upcoming road detours and right of way utility work, with SHA requiring a detour through Stockyard Road and Allen Road due to a project at the north end of the county. Utility installation work will begin on College Backbone Road in early November. The Wastewater Treatment Plant is also set to perform a tie-in at South Smith Island Road and Marsh Road in the coming weeks, with the necessary excavation and backfilling work being handled by the Roads Department. Charter Spectrum are installing fiber along Charles Canon Road and Rumbly Road. Lastly, Chesapeake Utilities is wrapping up the gas line project on Linden Avenue. 

Mr. Barnes then advised of the Roads Department recent support of community events, including: lighting to the City of Crisfield Youth Basketball Program, the Deal Island School Fall Festival, and Deal Island Christmas Lighting Event; traffic cones to the Haunted Drive-Thru at the Somerset County Fairgrounds. Mr. Barnes advised that these services were typically handled by SHA in the past, but with their shift in priorities, the Roads Department has taken on more of this support in recent years. 

# **WATERWAYS** 

A discussion was held regarding the Waterway Facilities Portable Toilet Services. Mr. Barnes advised that the contract for ADA-compliant portable toilets at various county locations will expire on December 1. The Roads Department has negotiated an agreement with Atlantic Pumping to continue the service for $1,400 per month. Mr. Barnes requested approval to renew the contract to ensure portable toilet availability at the county waterways. 

A motion was made by Commissioner Eldon Willing and seconded by Commissioner Darryl Webster to renew the contract with Atlantic Pumping as presented. By a show of hands in favor by President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig Mathies, Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Darryl Webster, the motion carried.

# **DISCUSSION** 

Elaine Ortega (11644 Long Point Road), addressed the Board and asked to start by presenting a letter from her elderly neighbor, Mr. Larry Antonick. Mr. Antonick’s letter expressed concerns about road flooding. He noted that when the road floods, he is unable to access lifesustaining services, as his Verizon landline service goes out and his medical alert system doesn’t function properly due to poor cell service. He highlighted that while flooding may be an inconvenience for some, it is life-threatening for him. He urges the county to allocate funds to address road flooding and keep roads passable. Following this, Ms. Ortega advised that flooding has become a frequent issue, especially from September 21 to October 4, 2024, when the road flooded daily during high tides. The road was impassable during high tide and only passable at low tide for trucks, but the water didn’t drain properly. This flooding, worse than any prior storm, led to 11 days without mail delivery. Ms. Ortega requested two improvements: Raising sections

Next, Audra Grant (11640 Long Point Road) advised that she shares many of Ms. Elaine Ortega’s concerns about flooding on Long Point Road. She described being stranded at home on Christmas Eve due to flooding, forced to stay in a hotel while transporting a trunk full of Christmas dinner. She also mentioned the salt damage to vehicles, which leads to costly repairs and shortened vehicle lifespans. One neighbor's car was totaled after getting stuck in floodwaters. More importantly, she highlighted the life-threatening risks of flooding, especially in emergencies when residents cannot access medical care, food, or work. She urged the board to consider permanent solutions, such as raising the road and improving drainage, to ensure safe and reliable access in the future as climate change may worsen flooding. 

Mr. Bob McIntyre (28101 Mount Vernon Road) inquired about the work being done on Smith Island Road, specifically whether the flooding was causing delays and if materials were waiting to be delivered. In response, it was explained that flooding had impacted Smith Island, and the delivery of materials was further complicated by the need to transport them by barge, which was limited by weather conditions and scheduling conflicts. Mr. McIntyre also requested that some asphalt be added to smooth out rough areas on Polks Road, where pipes have caused road damage, making the road less problematic. 

With no further business to discuss, a motion was made by Commissioner Craig Mathies, seconded by Commissioner Darryl Webster, and carried on a vote in favor by a show of hands by President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig Mathies, Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Darryl Webster to adjourn the Somerset County Roads Board meeting at 2:44 p.m. and enter into Somerset County Board of Health.
