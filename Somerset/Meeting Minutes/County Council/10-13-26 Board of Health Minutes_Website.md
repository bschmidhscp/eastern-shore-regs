## BOARD OF HEALTH FOR SOMERSET COUNTY **REGULAR SESSION TUESDAY OCTOBER 13, 2026** 

At 2:00 pm, President Charles Laird called the Board of Commissioners to open in Special Session. Present for today’s meeting were President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig Mathies, Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Darryl Webster. 

Also in attendance were County Administrator Ernest Leatherbury and Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez. 

President Charles Laird called the meeting to order and asked Commissioner Eldon Willing to lead those present in devotion and the pledge of allegiance. 

The meeting began with the introduction of Christina Gray, the Health Department’s new Deputy Health Officer. Ms. Gray shared that she previously worked with the Wicomico Health Department and had experience serving in Anne Arundel and Prince George’s counties. Having lived in the area for many years, she expressed how happy she was to have the opportunity to serve her home community. 

The meeting then moved into the FY 2026 Annual Report, presented by Ms. Colbert.  Ms. Colbert reviewed the department’s accomplishments and progress toward its strategic priorities, including health promotion, communication, employee morale, and workforce development. The report also highlighted the Community Health Needs Assessment, which focuses on access to healthcare, behavioral health, and chronic disease and wellness. 

Several accomplishments from the past year were shared, including 70 community visits through the mobile wellness van and the distribution of 60 items to families in need through the Surveillance Quality Improvement (SQI) Committee. The Health Equity Resource Communities (HERC) initiative showed significant progress, increasing its closed referral rate from 41% in the first quarter to 95% in the fourth quarter while serving 349 individuals. The Local Behavioral Health Authority (LBHA) provided vital Recovery Support Services to 380 individuals, the Continuum of Care (COC) Housing Program approved 100 percent of eligible individuals, and the comprehensive re-entry program served 121 individuals returning to the community after incarceration. 

Additional accomplishments included enrolling 75 participants in the Healthy Heart Ambassador (HHA) Program. There was a reported 26% reduction in emergency department visits through HERC initiatives. The Maryland Children’s Health Program (MCHP) assisted 519 clients, an increase of 150 from the previous fiscal year, while the Oral Health Program (CHC) referred 709 children for dental services. Ms. Colbert also discussed the department’s redesigned website,

new employee intranet, monthly newsletter, and Communications Committee, all of which were established to improve communication and access to information. 

The annual report also highlighted the department’s first Employee Enrichment Day, which was attended by nearly all staff and included workshops on communication, nutrition, stress management, and employee wellness. Other activities included a migrant health fair serving 63 individuals, back-to-school immunization clinics, a gun safety and suicide awareness program, and the department’s first dementia conversations program for caregivers. 

Following the presentation, Ms. Weber commended those present for their work and noted that many of the accomplishments were achieved directly by department employees rather than contracted vendors. Ms. Weber explained that the programs reflect both the department’s strategic plan and needs identified by community members and partner organizations. The strategic plan is expected to be reviewed and updated over the coming months. Board members expressed appreciation for the department’s efforts and had no additional questions regarding the annual report. 

Ms. Weber then provided an update on the Rural Health Transformation Fund. The state approved funding for two prevention initiatives focused on nutritional support and healthy lifestyles, along with $1.2 million to expand mobile health services. Planned efforts include strengthening the mobile wellness program, improving transportation support, expanding services in underserved communities, and increasing access to oral health screenings. The department will issue requests for proposals to identify providers who can help deliver these services. 

The board was also informed that an additional $300,000 had been secured through the Maryland Affordable Housing Trust (MAHT) program to support new wells and repairs to existing wells. She explained that the Rural Health Transformation Fund could also help support legal deed reconciliation and environmental health staff working with affected residents. The department plans to connect residents with well-related needs to the HERC program, where community health workers can help address additional barriers such as transportation, food insecurity, and access to healthcare. 

During the discussion, Commissioner Craig Mathies asked whether wells and septic systems were required to be installed in the front yard of a property. Ms. Weber explained that placement depends on property size, required setbacks, neighboring wells, and site evaluations. Residents were encouraged to consult the Environmental Health Department before purchasing property or making changes that could affect existing systems. She also emphasized that staff are available to review individual properties and help residents understand potential concerns.

President Charles Laird asked whether the well-related work would be competitively bid. Ms. Weber confirmed that projects exceeding $10,000 must follow county procurement procedures. 

The discussion then turned to the Community Reinvestment and Repair Fund, supported by cannabis-related revenues. Ms. Weber reported that a request for proposals had been released to solicit projects from community organizations, with applications due November 2, 2026, and awards anticipated by December 1, 2026. Awards of up to $100,000 may be available to eligible community partners serving designated portions of 21853 and 21817. 

Commissioner Eldon Willing asked how residents needing well assistance would learn about available resources. Ms. Weber explained that the department would focus on referrals through the HERC program and expand outreach efforts to make residents aware of available services. A suggestion was made regarding working with local school guidance counselors to identify families who may need assistance. Ms. Weber agreed that schools could be valuable partners and discussed coordinating with community school leadership to help connect families with community health workers and other resources. 

At the conclusion of the discussion, the board thanked Ms. Weber and attending staff for their continued work and recognized their dedication. 

Ms. Weber was then asked about her official final date and confirmed that it would be December 31, 2026. Board members expressed their appreciation and thanked her for her service. 

With no public comments or further business needed, the board was advised for the need of a Somerset County Special Session to enter into Closed Session. 

Therefore, at 2:22 p.m., a motion was made by Commissioner Craig Mathies and seconded by Vice President Randy Laird to adjourn Somerset County Board of Health and enter into Somerset County Commissioner Special Session.  By a show of hands in favor by all present members of the board, the motion carried.
