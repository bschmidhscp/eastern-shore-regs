Page 27-28 — Statement of Activities This is the income statement of activities, noting that the County ended the year with a decrease of $3.2 million, related to the proposed landfill closure and post closure costs (estimated to be a $2 million expenditure).

First, Mr. Polidore presented a Service Grant Agreement Grant Award between the Somerset County Health Department and Somerset County Emergency Services. He explained that in August 2025, EMS took over the Automated Extemal Defibrillation (AED) machine program. The purpose of<sup>theprogramwasto</sup> replace and add access to AED’s and training for use of the equipment. Mr. Polidore stated he has been voicing his support of this program, and the need to expand it and someone listened. The agreement provides up to $18,000, from the Board of Health Department, to add to the program and purchase AED’s and supplies to place in each road patrol car of the Somerset County Sheriff's Office. Mr, Polidore advised 14 AED’s plus supplies will be purchased. Currently there are 12 deputy units on the road. He explained how the deputy units also respond to calls, and often times are there first. If there is a cardiac arrest the deputy will have the AED with them to use if needed, hopefully reducing cardiac arrest deaths. There are up to 16 units when fully staffed. The AED program will be at no cost to the county.

Next, Mr. Polidore presented the Mobile Integrated Health Team Service Agreement for the Boards consideration and approval. This agreement is optional protocol for EMS, in which a

partnership between the County, Somerset County Health Department and Tidal Health provides medical resources, assessments, and other needs determined by the program, and those individuals working in it, to help the community (such as transportation to appointments, installation of a handicap ramp, etc.). Staffing is thru the Health Department and Tidal Health and is grant funded. Wicomico County has this program in place, as well as other counties throughout the State. Initially the program will go through the Electronic Maryland EMS Data System (Emeds) reporting process, with EMS providers giving referrals to the program, after which it will be expanded and opened up to other health care providers should they chose to join, with it noted that joining is an option, and not mandatory.
