# BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS FOR SOMERSET COUNTY **WORK SESSION TUESDAY JULY 22, 2025** 

At 2:00 pm, President Charles Laird called the Board of Commissioners to open in Work Session. Present for today’s meeting were President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, and Commissioner Darryl Webster. Commissioner Craig Mathies and Commissioner Eldon Willing were not in attendance. 

Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez was also in attendance. 

President Charles Laird led those present in devotion and the Pledge of Allegiance. 

Mr. Keith Lackey of the Maryland Department of Planning and Dr. Richard Benedetto of UMES School of Pharmacy were now before the Board for a presentation on Drone and Medical Delivery on the Eastern Shore. Mr. Lackey thanked the Board for the opportunity to present and advised that the project’s lead presenter, Mr. Jim Slaughter, was unfortunately unable to attend today’s meeting. 

Mr. Lackey began by advising that the drone delivery pilot project is funded through a $1.7 million U.S. Department of Transportation SMART grant. Phase one of the pilot project focuses on using drones to deliver prescription medications to remote communities, particularly Smith Island and the greater Crisfield area, with an emphasis on hypertension medications and select over-the-counter supplies. The goal of phase one is to prove the concept and gather data, including community perceptions before and after receiving drone-delivered medications. Mr. Lackey expressed that the hope is to apply for a second-phase grant of approximately $8 million next year, which would expand services and capabilities, and potentially allowing for the transport of additional medical supplies such as lab samples (pending FAA approval). 

The drone delivery project addresses challenges faced by residents in isolated areas, where transportation barriers can delay access to critical medications. One example shared was of a patient (with no means to travel) unable to monitor his blood pressure due to a dead battery in his monitor. In such cases, drones could offer a timely solution. Drone delivery is also intended as a cost-effective alternative during ferry service interruptions, which currently require expensive medevac helicopter transport. 

Mr. Lackey advised that at this stage, the drones will not land at delivery sites but will lower packages via tether. Safety and privacy are priorities, with FAA-certified pilots, flight paths

designed to avoid structures and roads, and cameras used only for navigation (looking forward and downward without zoom capability). Noise is minimal and is expected to be unobtrusive once the drone reaches altitude. He further advised that recent test flights have taken place near Crisfield, with coordination from local law enforcement, and continued by advising that demonstration flights are planned to and from Smith Island, with mapping flights scheduled before any actual medication deliveries occur. FAA waivers have been obtained for beyond-visual-line-of-sight flights, with additional waivers required for crossing state lines or transporting certain medical samples. 

Mr. Lackey then advised that the outreach team has been distributing flyers, placing surveys at local pharmacies, posting sign-up information on community social media pages, and encouraging residents to volunteer for the pilot program. He also clarified that while most mainland deliveries will go directly to patients, deliveries to Smith Island will be received by a TidalHealth employee, who will then distribute them in compliance with Board of Pharmacy regulations. 

Looking ahead, Mr. Lackey advised that a potential second phase could expand services to include emergency equipment such as automated external defibrillators (AEDs), which could be dispatched by drone more quickly than ambulances in some cases. The project also has economic development potential, creating opportunities for trained drone pilots and maintenance providers on the Eastern Shore. 

Mr. Lackey and Dr. Benedetto then concluded with encouragement for attendees to visit a demonstration flight scheduled later that day in Crisfield. Flyers were left for distribution, and contact information was provided for residents or organizations interested in participating or learning more. 

At the conclusion of their presentation, the Board thanked Mr. Lackey and Dr. Benedetto for coming in today and the for the information they provided. 

With no further business to discuss open session, a motion was made by Commissioner Darryl Webster and seconded by Vice President Randy Laird to adjourn the Work Session at 2:30 p.m. and enter into Special Session for the need of a Closed Session. By a vote in favor of all present Board members, the motion carried.
