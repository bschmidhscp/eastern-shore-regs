# **SOMERSET COUNTY ROADS BOARD TUESDAY SEPTEMBER 30 2025** 

At 2:00 p.m., the Somerset County Roads Board met in regular session with President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, and Commissioner Craig Mathies. Commissioner Darryl Webster was absent for the start of the meeting. Commissioner Eldon Willing was absent for the entire meeting. 

Also present for today’s meeting were Interim County Administrator Ernest Leatherbury, Public Works Director Woody Barnes, County Roads Superintendent Mike Marvel, County Roads Assistant Superintendent Gary Beauchamp, and Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez. 

President Charles Laird called the meeting to order and asked Commissioner Craig Mathies to lead those present in Devotion and the Pledge of Allegiance. 

Minutes from the July 8, 2025 and August 5, 2025 meeting were presented for approval. 

A motion was made by Commissioner Craig Mathies and seconded by Vice President Randy Laird to approve the minutes as presented. By a vote in favor by all present members of the Board, the motion carried.

# **ROADS DIVISION** 

Mr. David Peterson (engineer with Bowman Consulting) joined the meeting via teleconference at this time to discuss the proposed Somerset Grove Development (a modular home subdivision to be located in Somerset County). Mr. Peterson began by advising that Clayton Homes (the developer) is seeking the County's approval to accept the roads within the subdivision into the County's road inventory for management and maintenance. He continued by providing a brief background of the project, advising that the development is planned to consist of 79 lots situated near Washington High School.

Following Mr. Peterson’s initial introduction to the project, Mr. Barnes continued by advising that the development will feature roads that are approximately 4,500 feet in total length, 26 feet wide, and a 50-foot right-of-way in a curb and guttering style. Utilities such as water and sewer placed within the roadbed and electricity installed outside the roadbed but within the rightof-way. Mr. Barnes also advised of an extensive stormwater management drainage plan that will cross over and move in between homes. 

Mr. Barnes then addressed some concerns with this request regarding the development’s infrastructure and its potential impact on long-term maintenance. One primary concern was the extensive utility infrastructure (particularly the stormwater drainage system) which would require ongoing maintenance and could present challenges due to its location within the roadbed. Additionally, there are concerns about the limited parking within the subdivision, which could lead to street parking (especially for visitors) potentially causing congestion and increasing the risk of towing enforcement by the HOA. Another concern was snow removal. With utility infrastructure like manholes and stormwater facilities in the streets, there were questions about where snow would be placed during winter months, given the limited space for snow disposal. Lastly, there was concern about the adequacy of the 20-foot easements between homes for stormwater maintenance access. Mr. Barnes expressed doubt that the available space would allow for the proper servicing of utilities, particularly given the need for large equipment. 

In response to these concerns, Mr. Peterson addressed each issue. Regarding the easement size, he explained that while a 30-foot easement was initially proposed, the developer had opted for 20-foot easements to accommodate the size of the lots. He assured the Board that the stormwater pipes were shallow (around 4 to 5 feet deep) and should be accessible for maintenance with small equipment. On the parking issue, Mr. Peterson confirmed that the HOA would enforce a no-street parking policy, meaning any vehicles parked on the street would be subject to towing in accordance with the HOA's covenants. Regarding the stormwater infrastructure, Mr. Peterson emphasized that the pipes would be made of a durable material that has a long lifespan and minimal maintenance needs. He did not foresee any significant issues with the stormwater system in the next 20-30 years, barring unforeseen circumstances.

Mr. Peterson ended his call, leaving the meeting. 

Commissioner Darryl Webster joined the meeting at this time. 

Mr. Barnes presented a FY26 Capital Purchase request for three new trucks for the County Roads Department. Mr. Barnes advised that $465,000.00 was allocated for the purchase of these trucks during the budgetary discussions, however, the final bid for the trucks has come in slightly above the budgeted amount, totaling $466,942.80 (due to mid-season manufacturing price hikes). Mr. Barnes continued by advising that the trucks would be purchased under a piggybacked state contract. 

A motion was made by Commissioner Craig Mathies and seconded by Vice President Randy Laird to approve the purchase (despite the $1,942 overage) due to the necessity of the trucks and the availability of the units. By a vote in favor by all present members of the Board, the motion carried. 

Next, a discussion was held regarding fuel site upgrades. Mr. Barnes advised that $95,000 was allocated for upgrades to the gasoline tank and fuel dispensers at the Roads Department. The vendor responsible for emergency repairs to the diesel tank, Delmarva Petroleum Services, has provided a quote of $91,000, which is below the allocated budget. Upgrades to the fuel site include: replacing a 10,000-gallon gasoline tank and installing two new dispensers (one for gasoline and one for diesel) to ensure the system remains operational and in compliance. 

A motion was made by Commissioner Craig Mathies and seconded by Vice President Randy Laird to approve the upgrades and associated cost for fuel site upgrades at the Roads Department. By a vote in favor by all present members of the Board, the motion carried. 

Mr. Barnes then provided an update on compliance-related upgrades to the fuel system. During an inspection, it was revealed that the current underground fuel lines at the facility do not meet the Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) standards. To address this, the lines need to be replaced with double-wall lines and the sump pumps must be upgraded to meet MDE requirements. The cost for these upgrades is quoted at $48,000, which was not initially budgeted for. Mr. Barnes proposed to take the necessary funds from the Smith Island bridge project, which was allocated $200,000 in FY26.

A motion was made by Commissioner Craig Mathies and seconded by Commissioner Darryl Webster to approve the reallocation of funds and to move forward with the compliance upgrades. By a vote in favor by all present members of the Board, the motion carried. 

Next, Mr. Barnes presented a request made by Choptank Fiber and Electric to attach their utilities to Bridge 11 on Sign Post Road. Currently, Verizon utilities are attached to the west side of the bridge, and Choptank is seeking permission to move to the east side. It was noted that this is a standard request, and establishing prior rights is essential to avoid disputes in the future when bridge repairs or replacements are required. 

A motion was made by Vice President Randy Laird and seconded by Commissioner Craig Mathies to approve the request as presented. By a vote in favor by all present members of the Board, the motion carried. 

Mr. Barnes provided an update regarding a recent accident involving the gradall. The incident occurred on Route 413, where a car (with non-working tail lights) abruptly stopped and turned into the transfer station, leading the gradall driver to attempt to avoid a collision. Unfortunately, the gradall struck the back of a Somerset commuter bus and overturned on the shoulder. The driver of the gradall, who was not injured but suffered a minor concussion, did everything possible to avoid the collision. The commuter bus supervisor commended the driver for his efforts. However, the incident raised safety concerns about that area of Route 413, where there is no dedicated turn lane for vehicles entering the transfer station. Mr. Barnes advised that he would like to request the State Highway Administration’s consideration for a turn lane at this location. 

The Board agreed and gave a consensus for Mr. Barnes to move forward with the request to SHA. The Board also suggested that this be included in the upcoming Capital Transportation Program (CTP) or prioritized for future action if necessary. 

# **WATERWAYS** 

An update was provided on the FY27 Waterways Improvement Program funding. Mr. Barnes advised that, initially, the department applied for $350,000 in FY26 to replace the Ewell County Dock. However, the program's redesign now allows up to $500,000 in funding, with a 50/50 match beyond that amount. The total project cost is estimated at $700,000, meaning the department could receive a significant funding increase, reducing the county's contribution from $350,000 to approximately $225,000. # **DISCUSSION** 

Vice President Randy Laird inquired about backside mowing. Mr. Beauchamp advised that one piece of equipment was still under repair, requiring extensive work on the hydraulic pump. A second unit is being used to continue mowing operations. It was noted that the repair of the Kubota mower was covered under warranty, and the service provider has assured that the equipment will be fully operational once repairs are completed. 

# **PUBLIC COMMENT** 

Robert J. McIntyre addressed the board with suggestions regarding the ongoing safety concerns at Route 413. He proposed that a stoplight or other traffic control measures be implemented at the transfer station entrance to prevent future accidents. Additionally, he inquired about the discrepancy in funding for the breakwater project, asking whether additional measures were being taken to ensure the longevity of the breakwater. 

At the conclusion of the meeting, a motion was made by Commissioner Craig Mathies and seconded by Vice President Randy Laird to adjourn Roads Board and enter in County Commissioner Special Session. Therefore, at 2:42 p.m., the motion carried on a vote in favor by all present members of the Board to adjourn Roads Board and enter in County Commissioner Special Session.
