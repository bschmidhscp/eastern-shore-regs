Somerset County Sanitary Commission Minutes of June 5, 2025 

A regular session of the Somerset County Sanitary Commission was held on Thursday, June 5, 2025 at 1:00 p.m. in Room 111 of the Somerset County Office Complex. Members present were Charles Bagley, Interim Chairman, Dennis Williams, SecretaryTreasurer, Maxine Landon, Member, and Barbara Somers, Member. Also in attendance were Josh Taylor, General Manager, Richard Padgette, Superintendent, Cody Bradshaw, Assistant-Superintendent, Starr Mister, Office Manager, Jennie Marshall, Senior Administrative Aide, Trish Raab, Administrative Assistant, Heather Konyar, Legal Counsel, Cockey, Brennan, & Maloney and Richard Crumbacker, Somerset County Times. The Crisfield (Peripheral Area) Board position is currently vacant. Gene Hawke, Crisfield Resident, was present to speak during Public Input portion of the Public Hearing. 

Mr. Bagley called the meeting to order at 1:00 p.m. 

#### General Business — Information 

Mr. Taylor informed the Board of the Wastewater Treatment Plant (WWTP) Operator vacancy at the Princess Anne WWTP. The consensus of the Board was to move forward with advertising the position of WWTP Operator in the Somerset County Times and on the Sanitary District's page under Government on the Somerset County Website, www.somersetmd.us. 

Mr. Taylor informed the Board, the request for services agreement needs updating. The agreement is a mutual legal binding document where the owner requests for the District to perform preliminary review services related to a project. Management, with guidance from Legal Counsel, drafted agreement, which includes but not limited to: Owner certification, scope of services, and reimbursement. The consensus of the Board was to move forward with the proposed revision to the request for services agreement as presented. The agreement will be brought before the Board for approval during the July meeting. 

### Princess Anne - Information 

Mr. Taylor updated the Commission regarding the Princess Anne Wastewater Treatment Plant Enhanced Nutrient Removal (ENR) Upgrade by stating the District are holding monthly progress meetings with Davis, Bowen & Friedel (DBF) to discuss this project as it develops. DBF will be meeting with the Maryland Department of Environment to discuss design, the next phase in the project. 

Mr. Taylor stated to the Commission, there has been no movement with the Princess Anne Color Removal Project since last month’s commission meeting. 

Mr. Taylor updated the Commission regarding the Princess Anne Wastewater Treatment Plant Lab Project by stating the District is currently outsourcing lab services until the new

# Debt Modification Agreement —- HSB LOC 

During the June 15'" 2023 Regular Session, the Board approved a Resolution and Taxable Note with Lender, Hebron Savings Bank, in the amount of $5,000,000. This Line of Credit (LOC) has a maturity date of June 20" 2025. The District intends to continue to use this LOC as interim financing for capital projects (which shall include, without limitation: design, engineering, planning, fiscal, and legal expenses related thereto) and for other public purposes of the District permitted by the Enabling Act. The interim financing will be repaid using monthly grant reimbursements and subsequently secured through long-term financing. Management expressed the need for this extension as the LOC continues to be utilized for the Smith Island Clean Water Project and future projects. They are anticipating reimbursements for these projects from Governmental Agencies such as MDE, USDA, and EPA. Ms. Landon made a motion to approve the Debt Modification Agreement, extending the Hebron Savings Bank, $5,000,000 Line of Credit for an additional three years, June 20" 2028. Ms. Somers seconded the motion which carried to approval unanimously. The Debt Modification Agreement will be presented to the Somerset County Commissioners, Guarantor, for their consent and approval at their Board Meeting which is anticipated to be held on June 17" 2025.

## Septage & Hauled Waste Disposal Policy

## Advanced Payment Agreement 

Management, with guidance from Legal Counsel, drafted and presented to the Board during the May 8" meeting, an advanced payment agreement for septage haulers who have lost their disposal privileges and would like to have their permit reinstated with the District. By entering into this advanced payment agreement, a bill will be issued to the permit holder every 30 days which outlines their disposal usage at the Princess Anne Wastewater Treatment Plant. The monthly invoice will be due upon receipt, any advanced funds in their account will automatically apply to the monthly bill. The agreement lists the Applicant’s responsibility to maintain advanced payment, those who do not abide by the agreement will have their permit suspended. Ms. Somers made a motion to approve the advanced payment agreement as presented. Ms. Landon seconded the motion which carried unanimously to approval.

### Public Input 

Gene Hawke, Crisfield resident, was present to state his concerns with the rate increase, specifically the Crisfield Peripheral Area, which currently pay 1.2% of the rate set by the City of Crisfield for water and sewer. Mr. Hawke explained he attended the Public Hearing held by the City for their water and sewer rates where he expressed his concerns with their rate increase. Mr. Taylor explained the 1977 agreement with the City of Crisfield, expiring in 2027, which constitutes the rate of 1.2% to be charged through the District to all Crisfield residents in the Peripheral Area (outside city limits). The District is optimistic to discuss and negotiate the renewal and terms of the 1977 agreement. Mr. Taylor stated, the District is unaware of how the City calculates their rates, but assured Mr. Hawke, the District, as a governmental not for profit entity , bases their proposed rates on actual costs to the District. Mr. Taylor also explained the roles of the District and the City for maintaining the lines in the peripheral area. 

Richard Crumbacker, Crisfield resident, suggested analyzing the actual costs to the District to maintain Crisfield. He believes the Crisfield residents are being billed for expenses they are not directly responsible for. Ms. Mister explained, the District is unaware of what the 1.2% upcharge covers for the City but assured Mr. Crumbacker, the District does budget the water and sewer rate separately based on actual costs. 

Mr. Crumbacker also requested the District consider having a rate reduction for people who pay their bill in advance at the beginning of the year, saving the District money on printing and mailing bills for the remainder of the year.
