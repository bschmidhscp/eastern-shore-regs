# 8-6-24 Roads Minutes_Signed

**SOMERSET COUNTY ROADS BOARD TUESDAY AUGUST 6, 2024** 

At 2:00 p.m., the Somerset County Roads Board met in regular session with President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig N. Mathies, Sr., Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Darryl Webster. 

Also present for today’s meeting were Deputy County Administrator Ernie Leatherbury, County Roads Director Woody Barnes, County Roads Superintendent Gary Beauchamp, and Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez. 

President Charles Laird called the meeting to order and asked Commissioner Eldon Willing to lead those present in Devotion and the Pledge of Allegiance. 

Minutes from the June 11, 2024 open meeting and July 9, 2024 open and closed meetings were presented for approval. A motion was made by Vice President Randy Laird and seconded by Commissioner Eldon Willing to approve the minutes as presented. By a show of hands in favor by President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig Mathies, Commissioner Eldon Willing, Commissioner Darryl Willing, the motion carried. 

# **ROADS DIVISION** 

An update was provided on the FY25 Road Resurfacing Project. Mr. Barnes advised that the asphalt overlay portion is tentatively scheduled to begin on or around August 19<sup>th</sup> (weather pending), with the single surface treatment tentatively scheduled to begin on or around September 9<sup>th</sup> . Mr. Barnes also advised that material has been transported to Rhodes Point and the prep work is scheduled to begin next week. Mr. Barnes then commended Roads staff on the planning portion of this project, advising of the tremendous cost savings to the County through their scheduling and utilization of rented machinery. 

Mr. Barnes provided an update on the budget for the Whitehaven Ferry Overhaul project; advising that, during budget planning for FY24, Wicomico County estimated the overhaul to cost $300k with the final cost of the project coming in at $416k (due mainly to rising inflation of cost on parts, materials, and labor and project change orders). While the ferry overhaul is over budget, the Roads Department will still be under their total operating budget for FY24 by an estimated $500k (+/-). Mr. Barnes stated that he fully anticipated the Ferry Budget to fall back in line with the FY25, sharing the cost of labor and normal operating expense with Wicomico County. 

# **WATERWAYS**

Mr. Barnes provided information on the three types of funding available through the Waterway Improvement Program. In regards to the Tylerton Breakwater, the County can apply for a 100% fully State funded grant ($400k) through the dredging grant. In regards to the Ewell County Dock, the County can apply for a construction grant ($700k) that will be a 50/50 split between the County and DNR. In regards to the Ewell Basin, the County can apply for the FY26 shoreline protection grant ($100k) to cover engineering costs and consultant costs; the County can also apply for the FY26 basin rehabilitation grant ($250k) to create transient slips as well as rehabilitate mooring piles and decking. 

A discussion was held regarding transient slips and ferry boats with the Board stating their concerns for ensuring the privately-owned ferry boats are being tied up properly and not hindering the public from accessing the docks. A discussion was also held regarding dredging. 

Mr. Barnes advised that his list of priorities of these three items are: Ewell Dock, Tylerton Breakwater, and Ewell Basin. 

A motion was made by Vice President Randy Laird and seconded by Commissioner Darryl Webster to move forward with the grant applications as presented. By a show of hands in favor by President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig Mathies, Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Darryl Webster, the motion carried. 

Vice President Randy Laird inquired about possibly raising Smith Island Road in the area of Rhodes Point (as was done in other areas of roads on the island). Mr. Barnes advised that they are unable to raise that portion of road, as it would potentially trap water on residents’ properties when we experience tidal flooding.
