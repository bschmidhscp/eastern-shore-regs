**SOMERSET COUNTY ROADS BOARD TUESDAY JANUARY 20, 2026** 

At 2:00 p.m., the Somerset County Roads Board met in regular session with President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, and Commissioner Craig Mathies, Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Darryl Webster. 

Also present for today’s meeting were Interim County Administrator Ernest Leatherbury, Public Works Director Woody Barnes, County Roads Superintendent Mike Marvel, County Roads Assistant Superintendent Gary Beauchamp, and Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez. 

President Charles Laird called the meeting to order and asked Commissioner Craig Mathies to lead those present in Devotion and the Pledge of Allegiance. 

Minutes from the December 23, 2025 meeting were presented for approval. A motion was made by Commissioner Darryl Webster and seconded by Vice President Randy Laird to approve the minutes as presented. By a show of hands in favor by all present members of the board, the motion carried. 

# **ROADS DIVISION** 

Mr. Marvel provided a quick update on the fuel site upgrades at the Roads Department, advising that the old tank will be unhooked and pumped out on February 23<sup>rd</sup> and the replacement of the new tank should be complete by March 6<sup>th</sup> . 

An update was provided on the Choptank fiber and residential electric service project on Sign Post Road. Mr. Marvel advised that the layout is complete and the flags are in. The next step is getting the fiber buried underground; however, that is dependent on the weather. 

Next, Mr. Marvel presented a letter of support request for the City of Crisfield in regards to the BUILD Grant. The object of this is to raise Byrd Road, South Somerset Road, and Hall Highway. He also advised that they are attempting to get the State Highway Association involved in this proposed plan as well. The board presented the question of how high they are planning to raise the road, to which Mr. Marvel advised five feet above elevation. The board expressed concerns about this, stating opposition to the proposed idea as raising the roads that high would result in flooding everywhere else. Mr. Barnes then advised that this grant has a minimum of $25M and, therefore, the City of Crisfield needed to bring additional roads into the project (County and State) in order to meet that requirement.
