##### NOTICE OF PUBLIC HEARING 

### BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS FOR SOMERSET COUNTY AMENDMENTS TO THE SOMERSET COUNTY NUISANCE ORDINANCE 

Tuesday, March 12, 2024 at 2:05 PM County Commissioners’ Meeting Room, Room 111 11916 Somerset Avenue Princess Anne, Maryland 

In accordance with the Public Local Laws ofSomerset County Section 2-410, the Somerset County Commissioners are empowered to enact an ordinance for the purpose of establishing property standards for the maintenance of real property known as Nuisances. 

The Somerset County Commissioners are proposing amendments to the current Nuisance Ordinance #1050 to include language for the regulation of fowl/backyard birds/nonmigratory birds, 

Members of the public are invited to attend the Public Hearing and comment on the draft ordinance. The County Commissioners will consider all public comment prior to making a decision on the draft Ordinance. 

###### Fair Summary 

The proposed amendments to the Nuisance Ordinance include the clarifying the definitions in Section 1 Definitions to include fowl/backyard birds/non-migratory birds and Section 2, subsection ‘R’ about containment of fowl/backyard birds/non-migratory birds to the property through fencing and/or a coop. 

For further information about the proposed Ordinance, please contact the Somerset County Department of Technical and Community Services at 410-651-1424 or view the proposed ordinance on the DTCS website at: 

huips://www.somersetmd.us/departments/departments_-_n_- zplanning_and_zoning/index.php 

##### Board of County Commissioners for Somerset County 

Ralph D. Taylor, County Administrator 

Mr. Drewer was asked to provide the staff report. He explained that Non-Migratory Birds, 

such as chickens, roosters, fowl and other such animals are not currently regulated by our Nuisance Code. Recently, these animals have become a nuisance issue to surrounding property owners. In order to address these complaints, an amendment to the Somerset County Nuisance Code is required. Mr. Drewer read the two amendments being proposed to add within our current local code as follows: 

First, under Section I: Definitions:Bird/Backyard Bird/Fowl- A non-migratory bird to include chickens or roosters.

And secondly, Section II: The following conditions shall constitute a nuisance: R. F‘owl/Backyard Birds/Non-Migratory Birds shall not occupy a residential property without an attempt to confine to the property throughfencing and/or a coop. 

Commissioner Mathies clarified that the language being added to the ordinance will require their fowl to be maintained in an area, so that they can’t roam. Commissioner Webster questioned violations being imposed for non-compliance. Mr. Drewer stated, yes that was correct, and violations will be able to be addressed as they occur. 

With there being no further questions from the Board, Vice President Randy Laird asked if there were any proponents present to speak. 

Ms. Shelley Somers, Crisfield, Maryland came forward. Ms. Somers explained she has learned a great deal about backyard chickens over the last year that she has no desire to learn, after finding that there is no ordinance in place to address her neighbor’s backyard chickens who said they had a permit to have them. Stating her neighbor's backyard chickens are her backyard chickens (as they are on her property frequently) and upon calling law enforcement, Ms. Somers ‘was told to trap and return the chickens, She discussed the environmental health conditions created by the birds as they roam, eat and leave feces in her yard, noting that environmental health can’t impose restrictions because there is no ordinance in place. Being a nurse for 42 years, she knows the illnesses and infections that chickens can carry. She also stated that roosters can be aggressive and are not allowed to be in flocks in other counties. She closed in saying she has no problem with her neighbors having chickens, just as long has she doesn’t have to have them in her own yard. 

Vice President Randy Laird asked if there were any opponents present to speak. There were none. Mr. Taylor advised that written public comment would be taken for 10 days, or until Friday, March 22, 2024 at 4:30 p.m. The public hearing was declared closed at 2:13 p.m.

Next, Ms, Sonja White, CEO Helpme2Helpyou came forward to introduce her organization to the Board, and express her interest in the Lower Shore Shelter for her program. She explained that her purpose would be to use the shelter as a transitional group home for initially men and then women recently out of incarceration. It would be the first program of this type on this side of the bridge. She explained that personally she has 22 years of corrections experience, as well as being a Maryland State Police Certified Trainer, and is currently retired. She stated her belief in that everyone deserves a second chance, discussing how moving from incarceration into society can be overwhelming, with many not knowing how to handle the obstacles of the conversion. Her program would address physical, emotional and social needs, and she discussed how her program would work to assist the residents with the transition. Her interest in the shelter is due to its location not being too far from the Detention Center. Commission Webster noted that it would be the intention that residents housed there be Somerset County residents previous to their incarceration to be housed at the shelter. Ms, White agreed. It was noted that this would be an 18-month program, with a staff of approximately 20 (24 x 7 facility), which would include nursing and therapist staff, Ms. White also advised she is applying for grants to secure funding; also discussing partnerships she is currently working on. But in order to be approved for grants (no specified amount given), they are required to have a set address in order to be approved for funding; anticipating it will cost about $300,000 a year to run her program. Currently, there is no funding in place; an important need for her project. Ms. White was advised that the Board is currently reviewing available options, but was commended on her program, and her commitment to what she is trying to accomplish.
