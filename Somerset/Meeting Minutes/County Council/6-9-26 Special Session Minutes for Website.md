## BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS FOR SOMERSET COUNTY **SPECIAL SESSION TUESDAY JUNE 9, 2026** 

At 2:00 pm, Vice President Rnady Laird called the Board of Commissioners to open in Special Session. Present for today’s meeting were Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Darryl Webster. President Charles Laird and Commissioner Craig Mathies were absent. 

Also in attendance were County Administrator Ernest Leatherbury and Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez. 

# **<u>Closed Session Summary Report for June 9, 2026</u>** 

On June 9, 2026, from 3:00 p.m. until 3:30 p.m., at 11916 Somerset Avenue, Room 111, upon a motion made by Commissioner Darryl Webster, seconded by Commissioner Eldon Willing, and carried on a vote in favor by all present members of the board to enter into Closed Session by Authority of the General Provisions Article of the Annotated Code of Maryland § 3-305 (b)(1) the appointment, employment, assignment, promotion, discipline, demotion, compensation, removal, resignation, or performance evaluation of an appointee, employee, or official over whom it has jurisdiction; (7) to consult with counsel for legal advice. 

Present for the meeting were President Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Craig Mathies, Commissioner Eldon Willing, and Commissioner Darryl Webster. County Administrator Ernest Leatherbury, County Attorney Kirk Simpkins, DTCS Director Jesse Drewer, Emergency Services Director Yvette Cross, EMS Deputy Chief Cory Polidore, Director Jamie Manaia, and Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez were also present. Topics discussed were: (1) Topic: Legal; Action Taken: Mr. Simpkins to prepare and file brief pertaining to the legal matter discussed; (2) Topic: Personnel; Action Taken: Employee from Probationary Status to Permanent Status: (3) Topic: Personnel; Action Taken: None. 

Vice President Randy Laird called the meeting to order and asked Commissioner Eldon Willing to lead those present in devotion and the pledge of allegiance. 

Minutes from the April 28, 2026, Work Session, Special Session, and Closed Session were presented for approval. 

A motion was made by Commissioner Eldon Willing and seconded by Commissioner Darryl Webster to approve the minutes as presented. By a vote in favor by all present members of the board, the motion carried. 

The board then welcomed Superintendent of Schools, Mr. David Bromwell, and Mr. Jon Hill to provide an overview of the school system's budget and current initiatives. Mr. Bromwell began by

<!-- page 3 -->
reviewing several legislative changes that had taken place since his previous meeting with the Commissioners. He reported that legislation supported by Delegate Anderson and Senator Carozza had passed, allowing the Board of Education to enter closed session under certain circumstances. He also noted legislation recognizing student board members and discussed changes allowing the Commissioners to appoint or replace members of the Board of Education when necessary. Mr. Bromwell then advised that he has been meeting regularly with Mr. Leatherbury to maintain communication between the County and the Board of Education and is open to continuing monthly discussions on budget and related matters. A PowerPoint presentation was shared with the budgetary items for the Board’s review. 

The primary focus of the presentation was the school system’s budget. Mr. Bromwell explained that, since beginning his position in November, he has worked to better understand previous budget practices and funding structures. He discussed concerns regarding the reporting of federal restricted funds and stated that he believes complete transparency regarding all funding sources is necessary so that the Board of Education, County Commissioners, and community have a full understanding of the resources available when making financial decisions. 

Mr. Bromwell explained that the school system’s funding consists of local, state, and federal revenue sources. He noted that the local contribution from the County remained consistent at approximately $13 million and stated that he did not intend to request additional local funding for the current budget year. He referenced the significant maintenance of effort increase provided by the County in the prior year and stated that requesting additional funding immediately afterward would not be appropriate unless circumstances changed. 

Mr. Hill reviewed budget figures and explained the differences between unrestricted and restricted funding sources. He noted that interest revenue from the school system’s money market account contributes additional revenue and that restricted funds, including certain grant and federal funds, must be used for designated purposes. Mr. Bromwell highlighted that federal restricted funding had decreased significantly from the prior year, contributing to an overall reduction in projected revenue. He explained that additional federal funding was anticipated but had not yet been released. 

Mr. Bromwell emphasized the importance of sharing complete financial information with county officials. He stated that while prior practices regarding federal restricted funding were unclear, he intends to provide this information moving forward to strengthen trust and ensure that all parties are making decisions based on the complete financial picture. 

The discussion continued regarding employee compensation and the use of previous funding provided by the County. Mr. Bromwell reviewed salary increases implemented through prior funding, including efforts to move the beginning teacher salary toward $60,000. He noted that Somerset County Public Schools experienced some of the largest salary increases in the state during the previous year, with increases varying by employee group and salary level.

<!-- page 4 -->
The board questioned whether previous salary funding had been targeted toward employees at the lower end of the salary scale as originally intended. Mr. Bromwell acknowledged the concern and agreed to review the history of those allocations. He explained that collective bargaining practices traditionally result in salary adjustments being applied across employee groups, but he recognized the importance of examining whether future compensation strategies should better address recruitment and retention needs at specific points on the salary scale. 

Mr. Bromwell discussed employee retention challenges, particularly among experienced teachers. He noted that some employees leave between eight and fifteen years of service to seek opportunities in neighboring counties, often due to salary schedules and retirement-related considerations. He stated that the school system must continue examining compensation, benefits, and working conditions to improve retention. 

The meeting also included discussion of healthcare costs and participation in the Eastern Shore of Maryland Educational Consortium. Mr. Bromwell explained that participation in the consortium allows multiple school systems to negotiate more favorable healthcare and energy costs. He noted that healthcare expenses are projected to increase and that the anticipated increase has been included in the proposed budget. 

Mr. Bromwell reviewed additional financial pressures resulting from state requirements, including the addition of mathematics coaches and National Board Certified teacher incentives. He explained that while the goals of these initiatives are educationally valuable, they create challenges when mandates are introduced without corresponding funding. He noted that National Board Certification provides professional growth opportunities and recognizes teacher expertise but also creates significant salary obligations for the school system. 

The group discussed the purpose and impact of National Board Certification. Mr. Bromwell explained that certification provides teachers with advanced training and leadership opportunities but does not automatically guarantee improved classroom performance. He emphasized the importance of strong evaluation systems, teacher support, and professional development. He also discussed the challenge of balancing the role of National Board Certified teachers as classroom instructors and mentors to other educators. 

Mr. Bromwell provided an overview of proposed budget initiatives intended to address school safety, student support, and operational needs. He discussed the Board’s interest in adding student support monitors, explaining that he prefers this term rather than security guards because the goal is not simply enforcement but relationship-building with students. He stated that effective school safety involves knowing students, understanding concerns before they escalate, and creating a positive school environment.

<!-- page 5 -->
He also discussed implementation of the Evolve Weapons Detection System through grant funding at the secondary schools and intermediate school. Mr. Bromwell clarified that the system is not a traditional metal detector but instead identifies prohibited items and provides location information to staff monitoring the system. Student support monitors would assist with operating the system and helping students safely enter school buildings. 

Mr. Bromwell provided additional updates on operational improvements, including efforts to digitize personnel records within Human Resources. He explained that moving historical employee files into a digital system would improve efficiency, allow employees quicker access to records, and provide better documentation when employment matters require review. 

He also discussed the possibility of implementing the Advancement Via Individual Determination (AVID) program. He explained that AVID is designed to prepare students for increased academic rigor by teaching organizational skills, note-taking strategies, study habits, and approaches to succeeding in advanced coursework. He stated that the goal is to better prepare students for high school, college, and career pathways. The program remains dependent on final budget considerations. 

Discussion continued regarding the proposed expansion of sixth-grade programming at Deal Island Elementary School. Mr. Bromwell explained that the idea developed after conversations with the school community regarding the future of the small school. He stated that he values the benefits of smaller learning environments but also recognizes statewide trends toward larger school structures. After reviewing available space, transportation considerations, and community feedback, the school system began exploring the possibility of transitioning sixth grade to Deal Island, followed by seventh grade in a future year. He reported that the community response has been positive and that the plan is being considered as a way to expand opportunities while maintaining the school’s presence. 

Mr. Bromwell also discussed transportation needs and the continued effort to increase funding for bus contractors. He noted that even with previous increases, Somerset County remains below neighboring counties in transportation compensation and that additional adjustments may be needed. 

Facility needs were also reviewed. Mr. Bromwell explained that many school buildings are aging and that the system has historically lacked a dedicated reserve fund for unexpected repairs. He stated that the proposed budget includes setting aside significant funds for emergency facility needs, such as major equipment failures, to provide greater financial stability. 

Mr. Bromwell reported that a balanced budget had been submitted to the Board of Education for review and that additional discussion would occur before final approval. He expressed support for establishing a more predictable budget timeline, potentially allowing earlier review by the County Council, and emphasized the importance of a cooperative and transparent budget process between the school system and county government.

Following the budget presentation, discussion shifted to school climate, student behavior, academic performance, special education services, and strategies for improving outcomes throughout Somerset County Public Schools. 

The board raised concerns regarding teacher retention, reports of educators leaving the school system, and challenges related to student behavior in classrooms. Concerns were expressed that disruptive student behavior is affecting teachers’ ability to provide instruction and impacting the learning environment for other students. Examples were shared regarding incidents involving student aggression toward staff and concerns that students who engage in serious disruptions are sometimes returned to classrooms without sufficient intervention. 

Mr. Bromwell acknowledged these concerns and stated that addressing student behavior and supporting teachers must be a priority. He explained that the school system previously attempted to establish an internal alternative program for students with significant behavioral challenges; however, implementation was unsuccessful due to staffing limitations. He stated that the proposed budget includes consideration of a contracted alternative education program that could serve approximately 20 to 30 students who require a different educational setting due to significant behavioral needs. Mr. Bromwell then explained that contracting for alternative services may be more effective than creating a fully staffed internal program. He noted that developing an internal program would require redirecting employees from existing vacancies and priorities, whereas a contracted program could provide specialized staffing and support. He emphasized that an alternative program would not solve every discipline issue but would provide an additional option for students who require a different environment while protecting the learning environment for other students. 

The discussion also included the importance of supporting school administrators and teachers. Mr. Bromwell stated that principals must have the training, resources, and support necessary to help teachers manage challenging situations. He noted that school leaders are often managing numerous competing responsibilities and that the school system must continue strengthening instructional leadership and behavioral support systems. 

The board also raised concerns regarding special education services and whether students with significant needs are receiving appropriate support. Discussion focused on the balance between inclusion practices and ensuring that all students receive meaningful educational opportunities. Concerns were expressed that some students may not be receiving the level of specialized support they need while also affecting the ability of other students to learn. Mr. Bromwell provided background on changes in special education practices across Maryland. He explained that the state has moved toward inclusive classroom models after concerns that some students were historically overidentified for special education services. He noted that while inclusion has been successful for many students, schools are now evaluating whether additional specialized settings may be needed for certain students based on individual needs. Mr. Bromwell

<!-- page 7 -->
emphasized that Individualized Education Programs (IEPs) are designed to meet the specific needs of each student and that educational placement decisions should be based on those individual circumstances. He stated that the school system must ensure compliance with IEP requirements while also considering the needs of teachers and other students in the classroom. He indicated that the school system would continue reviewing special education practices and support structures. 

The conversation then turned to academic achievement and improving student outcomes. Mr. Bromwell acknowledged concerns regarding student performance and stated that improving academic results will require focused effort, consistent expectations, and stronger accountability throughout the organization. He shared an example of discussions he held with administrators and instructional leaders regarding student achievement data. He explained that when asked what specific actions would be taken to improve performance, responses were often general. He challenged leaders to develop more specific plans and timelines, including identifying the number of instructional days remaining before state assessments and creating detailed strategies for maximizing student preparation. 

Mr. Bromwell discussed the importance of using instructional time effectively and aligning classroom practices with state standards. He provided an example of reviewing instructional pacing and questioned whether excessive time was sometimes spent on individual standards when assessments contained limited questions on specific topics. He stated that small adjustments in instructional planning, focus, and accountability could contribute to improved student performance. 

Mr. Bromwell also discussed concerns regarding state assessment timelines and the delay in receiving testing results. He noted that although assessments are now completed electronically, results are not immediately available for schools to use in improvement planning. He stated that the delay creates challenges because schools must begin planning for improvement without having access to the most current assessment data. 

Mr. Bromwell emphasized that improving academic performance will require a stronger culture focused on safety, learning, and collaboration. He stated that building a positive organizational culture begins with leadership and must extend throughout schools, staff, students, families, and the broader community. He expressed confidence that the school system has dedicated employees who want to improve outcomes and stated that his goal is to provide the support and direction necessary for staff to be successful. 

He shared an example of efforts to improve organizational culture within the school system, describing early meetings with members and staff where employees expressed appreciation for increased collaboration and communication. He stated that creating an environment where employees feel supported, valued, and able to contribute is essential to improving morale and effectiveness. The board agreed that building the appropriate culture is critical and expressed support for the Superintendent’s focus on safety and learning. Discussion emphasized that improving schools requires partnership among educators, administrators, families, community members, and local government.
