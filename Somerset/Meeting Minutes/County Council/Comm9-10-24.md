BOARD OF COUNTY COMMISSIONERS FOR SOMERSET COUNTY September 10, 2024 

At 2:00 p.m., President Charles Laird called the Board of County Commissioners for Somerset County to open in Regular Session. Also present for today’s meeting Vice President Randy Laird, Commissioner Eldon Willing, Commissioner Craig N. Mathies Sr., and Commissioner Webster. 

Deputy Administrator Emest J. Leatherbury Jr., and Executive Aide Lory E. Ebron were also present. 

## Closed Session Summary Report 

On Tuesday, September 10, 2024 from 3:25 p.m., until 3:52 p.m., President Charles Laird, upon a motion made by Commissioner Eldon Willing, and seconded by Commissioner Darryl Webster opened in Closed Session by Authority of the General Provisions Article of the Annotated Code of Maryland §3-305 (b)(1) the appointment, employment, assignment, promotion, discipline, demotion, compensation, removal, resignation, or performance evaluation of an appointee, employee, or official over whom it has jurisdiction; and (3) consider the acquisition of real property for a public purpose and matters directly related to the acquisition. Craig N. Mathies, Sr., and Vice President Randy Laird voted in favor, therefore the motion carried 5-0. 

Deputy County Administrator Ernest J. Leatherbury Jr., Ms. Lory Ebron, Executive Aide, Mr. Scott Sturges, Dog Control Director, Ms. Jamie Manaia, HR Director, Charles Cavanaugh, Director of Solid Waste and Maintenance, and Warden Muir were also present. 

Topics discussed: (1) Probationary to Permanent Employee Status, (2) Hire Dog Control Officer I 3) Mitigation. Topics Approved: (1) Approval to move promotional probationary employee to permanent employee status (2) Approval to hire Dog Control Officer I.

Ms. Yvette Cross, Emergency Services Director presented 5-year Memorandums of Agreement (MOA) between Somerset County and the following pharmacy’s and veterinary offices in the county listed below to provide prescription needs for evacuees who do not have an adequate supply of medication to last the duration of their or their pets stay at a shelter during an event. Previously approved in 2019, the MOA’s need to be renewed for another 5-year period. She noted that Somerset County agrees to reimburse should they be unable to collect payment from the 

evacuee. 

- Layfield Veterinary 

- - Somerset Animal Hospital Veterinary - Crisfield Discount Pharmacy - Chesapeake Health Pharmacy - Apple Drugs - Marion Pharmacy - Karemore Pharmacy 

Ms. Cross advised that Attorney Simpkins has reviewed the MOA’s and found them legally 

sufficient. 

Commissioner Mathies made a motion to authorize the President’s signature on the MOA’s as presented. Commissioner Webster seconded the motion. By show of hands, the motion carried 5-0. 

Next, Warden Keith Muir came forward to inform the Board of the approval of the Somerset County Detention Center and University of Maryland Opioid Use Disorder Telemedicine Treatment Initiative Grant in the amount of $195,474.00 that will be used to support the ongoing clinical service program for screening, referral, and treatment for opioid use disorder-diagnosed individuals incarcerated in the Somerset County Detention Center. Warden Muir noted this is the second year into the program. 

Mr. Ronnie Dryden, Senator Marybeth Carozza, Delegate Charles Otto and Retired Captain Paul Bames (formally in charge of Intelligence Department) came forward to discuss concerns occurring at Eastern Correctional Institute, a State institution, that are being brought to the Boards attention. 

Mr. Dryden began by noting concerns discussed with Secretary Moyer in 2016, which have not gotten any better. He explained that he is hearing continued concerns from correctional officers, who fear retaliation. His main objective for being here today is to bring attention to issues regarding shortage of staffing (going from 800 to 550 currently, which includes understaffing on the tiers/3 officers), overly stringent hiring procedures (regarding polygraph questions and credit reporting), forced overtime, the inability to retain Correctional Officers and retention bonuses, and

President Charles Laird stated the biggest goal of the institution was to create jobs, and expressed his support to address the concerns expressed today to change this mindset today of privilege. He asked if any of the commissioners would like to speak.

The amount of excessive overtime officers is working was discussed, with safety concerns noted using overtime practices resulting in over worked officers. 

Mr. Dryden advised three things need to happen; legislation to end centralized hiring, less stringent polygraph testing, noting intelligence is doing the job and there is a one-year probationary period upon hiring, and retention bonuses. 

Senator Carozza stated that due to the severity of the situation, she would first like to address the direct crisis, and then move towards legislative fixes that are targeted specifically so that it is successful. 

Commissioner Darryl Webster discussed hiring back log numbers at Jessup, noting the influx of legal immigrants that have no background history information that are getting the jobs in Baltimore. He feels that the officer’s think that the County Commissioners and the Secretary of Safety don’t care. He was also disturbed to hear that there are only three (3) CO’s staffed when it should be six (6) for safety reasons with the exception of the midnight shift. Sick leave usage was in 2023 was 55,000 hours. Sick leave and FMLA is being abused. He questioned 2024 numbers already at 38,400 hours noting they will probably easily go over last year’s number. There were 83 CO vacancies now, 26 were hired, but 37 resigned so that leaves negative 11. The system is not working. Polygraph and background check are too stringent and it’s taking one year to go through the hiring process which is too long. Overtime pay cap a pay for entire jail was $100,000 and is now at $500,000 a pay. Job fairs look great, but don’t work if no one is hired because it stops when the applicant gets to the polygraph and back ground checks.
