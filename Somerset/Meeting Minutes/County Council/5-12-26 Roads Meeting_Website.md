# **SOMERSET COUNTY ROADS BOARD TUESDAY MAY 12, 2026** 

At 2:00 p.m., the Somerset County Roads Board met in regular session with President 

Charles Laird, Vice President Randy Laird, and Commissioner Craig Mathies, and Commissioner Eldon Willing. Commissioner Darryl Webster was absent today. 

Also present for today’s meeting were County Administrator Ernest Leatherbury, Public Works Director Woody Barnes, County Roads Superintendent Mike Marvel, County Roads Assistant Superintendent Gary Beauchamp, and Executive Secretary Kimberly Lopez. 

President Charles Laird called the meeting to order and asked Commissioner Eldon Willing to lead those present in Devotion and the Pledge of Allegiance. 

Minutes from the March 17, 2026 meetings were presented for approval. A motion was 

made by Commissioner Craig Mathies and seconded by Commissioner Eldon Willing to approve

the minutes as presented. By a vote in favor by all present members of the board, the motion carried. 

# **ROADS DIVISION** 

The meeting began with a discussion regarding the Health Department parking lot project. The Board was advised that several months earlier an agreement had been approved between the Health Department and the County to construct a new parking lot to address overflow parking and provide additional space for fleet vehicles previously stored at other locations. The Roads Department has overseen the project, and the remaining work consists of paving the completed site. After reviewing the bid tabulation included in the meeting packet, Mr. Marvel recommended awarding the paving contract to Pruitt's Resurfacing Solutions ($10,500 bid). 

A motion was made by Vice President Randy Laird and seconded by Commissioner Craig Mathies to approve the recommendation and award the bid to Pruitt's Resurfacing Solutions. By a show of hands in favor by all present members of the board, the motion carried. 

Next, a request from Sheriff Howard was presented to reduce the posted speed limit on Stewart Neck Road and Revells Neck Road from 50 miles per hour to 40 miles per hour in response to concerns expressed by area residents. Mr. Marvel presented traffic data collected over an elevenday period that supported the proposed reduction. The Board discussed the traffic counts and the importance of continued enforcement to ensure compliance with the new speed limit. 

Following this, a motion was made by Commissioner Craig Mathies and seconded by Vice President Randy Laird to approve the speed limit reduction as requested. By a show of hands in favor by all present members of the board, the motion carried. Commissioner Craig Mathies also requested that the Sheriff be advised that enforcement would be necessary to encourage motorists to observe the reduced speed limit. 

Mr. Marvel then presented the FY27 Maryland Department of Transportation State Highway Administration Highway User Revenue Certification of Use Agreement. He explained that the agreement is required annually in order for the County to receive approximately $900,000 in Highway User Revenue funding and certifies that the funds will be expended in accordance with 

applicable state and federal requirements.

<!-- page 3 -->
A motion was made by Commissioner Craig Mathies and seconded by Commissioner 

Eldon Willing to approve the FY27 Maryland Department of Transportation State Highway Administration Highway User Revenue Certification of Use Agreement as presented. By a show of hands in favor by all present members of the board, the motion carried. 

Next, the Board considered a request from GMB Consulting Group on behalf of the University of Maryland Eastern Shore to allow the installation of raised crosswalks along College Backbone Road as part of the Agricultural Research Building construction project. It was explained that the raised crosswalks would also serve as traffic-calming measures. 

Following a brief discussion, a motion was made by Commissioner Craig Mathies and seconded by Vice President Randy Laird to approve the request. By a show of hands in favor by all present members of the board, the motion carried. 

An update was provided on the Smith Island Bridges 21 and 22 rehabilitation project , noting that the County continues to partner with Smith Island United in pursuing a congressional grant to fund the bridge improvements. As part of the grant review process, the committee requested a letter of support from the Maryland Department of Transportation State Highway Administration (MDOT SHA) because the bridges are included in the Federal Highway Bridge Inventory. The meeting packet included the County's request for support, submitted by Mr. Barnes, along with the MDOT SHA Administrator's letter endorsing the project (for the board’s review). 

Mr. Marvel then reported that work continues with M2 Construction and GMB Consulting Engineers to address damage to the County dock in Crisfield and a section of Smith Island Road resulting from construction of the new wastewater treatment facility. M2 Construction has accepted a proposal to repair a damaged mooring pile at the County dock. In addition, the Roads Department is reviewing a proposal to repair approximately 200 feet of Smith Island Road, which GMB Consulting Engineers has determined to be fair and reasonable. 

A follow-up report was given regarding the River Road tree replanting project. Mr. Marvel advised that the agreement with the affected property owner had been fulfilled through the planting of approximately 250 evergreen seedlings to replace vegetation impacted during roadside mowing

operations in October 2024. The work has been completed in accordance with the agreement, and the matter is considered closed by the Roads Department. 

Mr. Marvel then presented the FY26 centerline striping schedule, noting that the list of roads included in the meeting packet are tentatively scheduled for completion during the first week of June, although scheduling remains subject to contractor availability. 

# **WATERWAYS** 

An update was provided on county waterway maintenance activities, with Mr. Marvel reporting that winter and spring weather had resulted in numerous maintenance needs at County waterway facilities. Correspondence with the Maryland Department of Natural Resources confirmed that eligible repairs will be funded through the County's DNR Maintenance Grant. 

Mr. Marvel then advised that plans and bid documents for the St. Peter's Creek projects are still pending from the project engineer and will be presented to the Board once they are ready for advertisement.

# **DISCUSSION** 

Commissioner Craig Mathies relayed concerns from a constituent regarding the need for a stop sign within a residential development near the University due to vehicles exiting the apartment complex without stopping. Mr. Barnes explained that the roads within the development remain privately owned and are not part of the County road system. However, he agreed to discuss the 

concern with the property's developer during future conversations.

<!-- page 5 -->
Commissioner Eldon Willing addressed dredging and navigation issues affecting St. Peter's 

Creek and other County waterways. Mr. Barnes explained that the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers no longer has funding available for routine dredging of many smaller channels, requiring local jurisdictions to seek alternative funding sources. Additional discussion focused on challenges associated with spoil disposal sites, coordination among state agencies, and the limited progress on previously funded dredging initiatives despite available engineering funding. Mr. Barnes continued by emphasizing that suitable disposal locations continue to be the primary obstacle to advancing dredging projects throughout the County. 

Mr. Richard Crumbacker, present in the audience, requested that Freedomtown Road be added to the upcoming centerline striping schedule due to fading pavement markings. The request was acknowledged. 

# **PUBLIC COMMENT** 

There were no public comments today. 

At the conclusion of the meeting, a motion was made by Commissioner Craig Mathies and seconded by Vice President Randy Laird to adjourn Roads Board and enter in County Commissioner Special Session. Therefore, at 2:23 p.m., the motion carried on a vote in favor by all present members of the Board.
