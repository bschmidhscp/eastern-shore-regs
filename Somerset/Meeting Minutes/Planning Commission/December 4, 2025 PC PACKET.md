## **STAFF REPORT** 

##### **Meeting of December 04, 2025** 

**Case No.: REZ 25-193 Applicant: Chesapeake Renewables Energy LLC Property Owner: Chesapeake Renewables Energy LLC Location: 33001 Costen Rd (Portion of property closest to Ocean Highway) Tax Map: Map 42, Grid 13, Parcel 017, Lot 4 Zoning: Agricultural Residential-AR Property Size: Approximately 28.52 Acres (of a 106.18 acre lot) Request: I-2 General Industrial** 

- **A.** **<u>OVERVIEW:</u>** On October 2, 2025, the Department of Technical and Community Services received an application from Chesapeake Renewables Energy LLC, requesting a rezoning of sections of the property (Tax Map 42, Grid 13, Parcel 017, Lot 4) from Agricultural Residential (‘AR’) to General Industrial (‘I-2’) citing a mistake in the existing zoning classification and a substantial change in the character of the neighborhood. The lots were recently subdivided to better distinguish the solar farm boundaries from Lot 4. The area to be rezoned is approximately 28.52 Acres in size, is located near Pocomoke, on the Eastern side of Ocean Highway. The applicant has indicated that they would like to use the site in an industrial manner to facilitate economic growth in Somerset County. **A public hearing on the rezoning request has been scheduled and noticed for the Planning Commission’s December 4, 2025 meeting.** 

_*For rezoning applications, the Planning Commission will need to hold a Public Hearing on the request and make a recommendation to the County Commissioners. The County Commissioners must also hold a Public Hearing before approving or denying the request._ 

##### **B.** **<u>RELEVENT BACKGROUND INFORMATION:</u>** 

**1.** **<u>Existing and Proposed Land Use:</u>** The current land use is an undeveloped wooded lot. There are some wetlands on the property and to the west is Ocean Highway. The applicant proposes to use the land for an industrial use that complements the neighboring solar powerplant. 

**2.** **<u>Neighborhood:</u>** For rezoning applications, Maryland Case Law requires that a ‘neighborhood’ be identified. Neighborhoods are usually bordered by geographic features such as roads or streams, and in rural areas, can be larger than neighborhoods in urban or suburban areas. For this request, staff identified a neighborhood generally defined by: Costen

Road to the North, and East, Ocean Highway to the Southwest, and Davis Store Road to the Southeast. 

**3.** **<u>Zoning & Character of Surrounding Neighborhood:</u>** The property directly adjacent to the site to the West and South is zoned General Industrial (‘I-2’). Land on all three other sides of the property is zoned Agricultural Residential (‘AR’). The surrounding neighborhood consists of farmland, woodland, residential, and industrial land uses. 

<u>For the Planning Commission’s reference, the Zoning Ordinance provides the following guidance on both the General Industrial (‘I-2’) and Agricultural Residential (‘AR’) Zoning Districts:</u> 

_‘I-2 General Industrial District – In this district are permitted those manufacturing, processing, and storage uses which would be separated from other uses by reasons of characteristics, which may harmfully affect other uses. The exclusion of other uses is intended to promote the economic welfare of the county by reserving especially suited sites for industry and by controlling the mingling of incompatible uses. To also provide for selected commercial uses._ 

_<u>Agricultural Residential District (AR) - To provide for a full range of agricultural</u> activities a nd to protect agricultural land from encroachment by incompatible land uses, thereby encouraging a stable agricultural economy. The agricultural district also permits low density residential and community service uses. This district also provides a land reserve for future development._ 

There are 45 land uses permitted by-right in the ‘AR’ zoning district and 32 land uses permitted by Special Exception (Special Exceptions need a public hearing with the Board of Zoning Appeals (BZA) with subsequent approval or denial). 

There are 100 land uses permitted by-right in the ‘I-2’ zoning district and 28 land uses permitted by Special Exception. For a full list of the permitted land uses, please refer to Section 5.7 of the Zoning Ordinance. 

**If the rezoning is approved, please note that the property may be developed with all of the Industrial land uses allowed in the ‘I-2’ zoning district.** 

4. **<u>Environmental Considerations:</u>** There is no water and sewer service on the property or surrounding parcels. There are some wetlands adjacent to the property (southern boundary near the river). A portion of the property is designated as a Forest Conservation Area. 

5. **<u>Comprehensive Plan Guidance:</u>** The area is located in the Priority funding area, and the Future Land Use Map suggests that this area be ‘Industrial’.

- **C.** **<u>REZONING CRITERIA:</u>** The Maryland Land Use Article provides criteria to be considered for rezoning applications.  These items are listed below and are also addressed in the attached recommendation. 

   1. <u>Population Change:  In 2010, the total population of Somerset County was 26,470 persons.</u> By 2020, this had decreased to 24,620 according to the United States Census Bureau. The most recent American Community Survey (ACS) estimates that the total population is roughly 24,910 representing an approximate 6 % change in population from 2000 to 2018. Within the identified neighborhood, population change is estimated to be minimal since the last comprehensive rezoning in 2019. 

   2. <u>Availability of Public Facilities</u> Sewer and water are not available for hook-up on this property. Transportation access is available via Costen Road and possibly Ocean Highway. 

   3. <u>Present and Future Transportation Patterns: Traffic volumes on Ocean Highway in the</u> vicinity of the applicant’s property have increased since the last County Comprehensive rezoning in 2019. 

   4. <u>Compatibility with Existing and Proposed Development for the Area: Located in the</u> vicinity of this lot is an already improved industrially zoned property (i.e. Sysco).    5. <u>Relationship of the Proposed Amendment to the Comprehensive Plan: The Future Land</u> Use Map suggests that this parcel be industrial in nature. 

   6. <u>Change in the Character of the Neighborhood:  In accordance with State Law, a rezoning</u> can only be approved by a finding that there has been a “substantial change” in the character of the neighborhood since the last comprehensive rezoning, or that there is a “mistake” in the existing zoning (see #7 below). **The last comprehensive rezoning occurred in 2019, so any changes to justify the rezoning will need to have occurred since that date.** There has been no change in character since the last rezoning, or the comprehensive plan update. <u>Mistake in the Existing Zoning: This property was zoned AR during the County’s</u> comprehensive rezoning in 2006. It is surrounded by the AR zoning district on two sides while being immediately east of the rest of the parcel zoned I-2. Since then there has been little to no change in the development of the neighborhood. That said the applicant makes the case that the rest of the parcel was zoned I-2 to facilitate industrial development, and that at the time of the rezoning the county and applicant where unaware of the environmental constraints that the rest of the parcel would face. Therefore, to better match the county’s intention. The I-2 zoning should also apply to the parcel’s uplands.

##### Narrative in Support of Chesapeake Renewable Energy's Rezoning Request 

Chesapeake Renewable Energy (CRE) respectfully submits this narrative argument in support of its request to amend the zoning classification of a portion of its property, specifically a segment of existing Lot 4 on Route 13 and Costen Road, from Agricultural Residential (AR) to Heavy Industrial (I-2). This request, which follows the subdivision of Lot 4 into four large parcels, is warranted both by the established legal criteria for rezoning under Article 4, Section 204 of the Land Use Article of the Annotated Code of Maryland and by the facts on the ground. 

The Maryland statute outlines a clear, two-step process for zoning amendments. The first step, detailed in §4-204(b)(1), requires findings of fact related to community planning, which we address below based on the most recent adopted 1996 Somerset County Comprehensive Plan ("1996 Plan"). The second step, outlined in §4-204(2), requires a finding of either a substantial change in the character of the neighborhood or a mistake in the existing zoning classification. It is on the basis of a clear mistake in the original zoning and a demonstrable change in the character of the neighborhood that this request is supported. 

###### I. Findings of Fact (Land Use Article, §204(b)(1) 

The 1996 Plan provides the appropriate planning context for this decision, and the proposed rezoning is consistent with the Plan's underlying goals, particularly the promotion of economic activity within a primary growth area. (See Attached 10-23 of Comprehensive Plan). The legislative body is required to make findings of fact concerning the following matters: 

1. Population Change: While the 1996 Plan projected a population of 28,055 residents by 2010, the anticipated job creation from a change in rezoning is beneficial for economic development in Somerset County. It will allow for the Applicant to develop the property with industrial uses, thereby creating new jobs and contributing positively to local employment. The potential new jobs may attract new residents, aligning with any underlying goal of stable economic and population growth. 

2. The Availability of Public Facilities: The CRE parcel is strategically located within a Primary Growth Area as defined by the 1996 Plan. These areas are explicitly designated as having access to central water and sewer systems and are deemed "suitable for a broad spectrum of land uses." The parcel's designation as a Primary Growth Area strongly suggests the necessary capacity or the suitability for necessary upgrades to support industrial use, consistent with the 1996 Plan's goal of implementing central water and sewer systems. 

3. Present and Future Transportation Patterns: The site's location directly on Route 13—a major state highway, which makes it ideally suited for industrial operations reliant on truck traffic. The 1996 Plan recognized the need for improvements to Rt. 13 traffic flow. Establishing new industrial activity in a location with existing major transportation infrastructure is sensible planning.

<!-- page 14 -->
4. Compatibility with Existing and Proposed Development for the Area (Addressed in Section II): As will be discussed below, the immediate neighborhood character has substantially shifted toward an industrial-like use. 

5, The Recommendation of the Planning Commission: This finding will be addressed following the Planning Commission's review. 

6. The Relationship of the Proposed Amendment to the Local Jurisdiction’s Plan (Addressed in Section II): The rezoning is necessary to realize the intended economic development goals of the County, as evidenced by the original zoning action. 

Il. The Statutory Basis for Approval (Land Use Article, §204(b)(2) 

The request for rezoning is justified by compelling evidence demonstrating both a substantial change in the character of the neighborhood and a mistake in the existing zoning classification. The legislative body may grant the amendment based on a finding of either of these two conditions. 

1. A Substantial Change in the Character of the Neighborhood 

While the area is split zoned, with the requested rezoning within the AR zone, the surrounding neighborhood is now defined by the Applicant's existing operations. Since 2006, CRE has developed two substantial solar facilities on approximately 100 acres of its property. Though located in the AR zone, the use of roughly 100 acres for commercial energy production is fundamentally industrial in nature, not agricultural. This solar development has effectively converted the immediate area’s character away from traditional agriculture. Furthermore, the remaining AR-zoned portions of the property—the subject of this request—are now environmentally constrained by wetlands and functionally surrounded by industrial-like solar use, rendering them unsuitable for their nominal agricultural purpose. The neighborhood’s character is no longer purely agricultural; it is now defined by energy production and associated industrial potential. 

###### 2. A Mistake in the Existing Zoning Classification 

There was a mistake in the 2006 zoning action. In 2006, the County rezoned approximately 85 acres from AR to I-2, intending to provide a viable area for industrial use that would generate significant economic benefit. However, neither the Applicant nor the County Approving Authority was aware of the full extent of the wetlands on the site at the time. 

The consequence of this oversight is that most of the land successfully rezoned to I-2 is comprised of unusable wetlands, while a significant portion of the most upland ground remains locked in the AR classification.

<!-- page 15 -->
The practical effect of this mistake is that the County's intended goal, to facilitate industrial development and economic benefit, was inadvertently undermined by environmental realities. This application seeks only to correct this deficiency by shifting the I-2 designation to the usable uplands of Lot 4, allowing the County’s original, well-intentioned economic development goal to be realized. Granting this rezoning is not a departure from the Plan, but rather an essential corrective action to align the zoning map with the physical geography, thus fulfilling the original objective of attracting industrial economic activity to a designated Primary Growth Area. 

Conclusion: 

Chesapeake Renewable Energy’s request meets all planning prerequisites and is overwhelmingly supported by the need to correct a past zoning mistake under the requirements of Land Use Article, §4-204. We respectfully request the rezoning from AR to I-2, thereby enabling sustainable economic development on the most suitable portion of the property and realizing the County's long-standing goals for this Primary Growth Area, as well as eliminating an undesirable split zoning for the property.
