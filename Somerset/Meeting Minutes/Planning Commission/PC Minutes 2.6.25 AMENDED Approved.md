# **SOMERSET COUNTY PLANNING COMMISSION MEETING MINUTES** _February 6, 2025_ 

The following are the minutes of the Somerset County Planning Commission Public Hearing and meeting held on February 6, 2025, at 6:00 p.m. in the meeting room of the Somerset County Commissioners, Room 111, in the Somerset County Office Complex, 11916 Somerset Avenue, Princess Anne, Maryland. 

**<u>Planning Commissioners Present: Somerset County DTCS Staff Present:</u>** Matt Webster Jesse Drewer, Director Janet McIntyre Will Cornish, Assistant Director and Zoning Admin. Skip Colborn Catherine Skeeter, Planner Steve Raab Nelson Brice Laverne Stewart **<u>Planning Commission Legal Counsel Present:</u>** Andrew Illuminati, Esq. 

Mr. Matt Webster opened the meeting at 6:00 p.m. 

## **A.** **<u>APPROVAL OF MINUTES:</u>** 

January 9, 2025 Meeting Minutes: motion to approve by Mr. Steve Raab and seconded by Mr. Skip Colborn. 

## **B.** **<u>NEW BUSINESS:</u>** 

**a. Site Plan:** SP24-248 Skipjack Seafood Market (Robert P. Evans III, Skipjack Circle/Market Land (Tax Map 23C, Grid 17, Parcel 270) 

## Mr. Jesse Drewer read the staff report for the record: 

_“The Department of Technical and Community Services received a submittal from Mr. Evans of on June 3, 2024. Mr. Evans is seeking site plan approval for the development of a seafood market. The Site Plan was reviewed at a Technical Advisory Committee (TAC) meeting on June 10, 2024. A revised Site Plan was submitted on January 10, 2025 for review and comment.  The proposed Skipjack Seafood Market will be located on the corner of Skipjack Circle and Market Lane in Princess Anne. Market Lane runs parallel to Rt. 13, and the site of the proposed is very visible from Rt. 13. The lot of concern is 1.11 acres, and is zoned C-2 (General Commercial).”_ 

Mr. Brock Parker was present, on behalf of the applicant, to discuss the proposal.  Mr. Parker provided a synopsis of the project, highlighting that the business will be a market, not a restaurant, retail seafood establishment focusing on preparation and selling for off-site consumption.  Mr. Parker mentioned the presence on the plan of the proposed future building, and the second access onto Skipjack Circle.  Mr. Parker stated that other offices/agencies of concern (SHA, County Engineer for Stormwater Management, Sediment and Erosion Control) have reviewed the project and are ready to sign-off.  Mr. Parker also stated that the proposed initial structure will be a pole building.

Mr. Colborn questioned the degree of landscaping proposed on the site plan, wondering if greater density of plantings could be provided adjacent to the EconoLodge, and along Skipjack Circle. Mr. Parker agreed with the requests for expanding the landscaping to include placing additional rose bushes between the crepe myrtles along Skipjack, and the expansion of the corner landscaping in the NW corner of the lot. 

On a motion by Mr. Brice, seconded by Mrs. McIntyre, the Planning Commission voted unanimously to approve the Site Plan, with standard Staff Recommendations//Conditions, as well as the addition of Planning Commission-requested landscape upgrades. 

**b. Major Subdivision Approval:** SA24-565 Somerset County Industrial Park (Board of County Commissioners for Somerset County), Revells Neck Road (Tax Map 32, Grid 4, Parcel 149, Lot B) 

Ms. Catherine Skeeter read a summary of the staff report for the record: 

_“The Department of Technical and Community Services received a submittal from Mr. Brock Parker of Parker & Associates, Inc on December 19, 2024, on behalf of the Somerset County Commissioners. The applicant is seeking major subdivision approval for the creation of a new Somerset County Industrial Park, comprised of 20 lots for individual sale. The Site Plan was reviewed at a Technical Advisory Committee (TAC) meeting on January 13, 2025. A revised plat was submitted on January 22, 2025 for Planning Commission consideration. The parcel has a storied past, once planned for the development of a Wal-Mart distribution facility, it has been farmed since that fell through. The northern portion of the property has been strip mined, and a notable elevation difference bisects the parcel. Directly to the east is ECI, and another County-owned parcel is to the west, which is utilized as a borrow site for County projects. The property is ideally zoned as Light Industrial, is not adjacent to any extensive residential communities, and the future development will not impact the sensitive environmental features on the northern portion of the parcel.”_ 

Mr. Brock Parker was present, on behalf of the applicant, to discuss the proposal.  Mr. Parker described the diversification of the lot sizes provided on the plan, and the future flexibility to resubdivide, making the project attractive to developers.  Landscaping on the individual lots is to be addressed on a case by case basis, and Forest Conservation is to be addressed in the same way in a designated area in the rear of the lot.  The Stormwater Management is to be handled on a modified existing pond for quantity and for quality management on a lot by lot basis. 

Mr. Webster asked about whether the plan was to construct the three streets from the start.  Mr. Parker stated that this would be up to County.  Mr. Colborn inquired about the perimeter landscape buffer, stating that the perimeter buffer should be installed prior to the lots being sold and developed.  Mr. Parker agreed that planting the front buffer should be done. 

On a motion by Mr. Colborn, seconded by Mr. Raab, the Planning Commission voted to provide preliminary final approval of the proposed Major Subdivision, with conditions that a 10’ landscape buffer be added to the western property boundary, and a 15’ landscape buffer be added to the property frontage along Revells Neck Rd. (note: conditions were amended at the March 6, 2025 meeting of the Planning Commission to correct the following: with conditions that a 10’ landscape buffer be added to the property frontage)

## **C.** **<u>OTHER BUISINESS:</u>** 

**a. Comprehensive Plan Update** – Smith Planning and Design (Priority Preservation Element draft chapter) 

Virginia Smith and Michele King of Smith Planning and Design were present (virtually via Teams) to provide the Planning Commission and public a status update on the progress of the new Comprehensive Plan.  A PowerPoint slide deck was displayed for review.  Discussion points: 

- NEW Chapter 11 “Priority Preservation Area” 

   - Somerset can now become a “Certified” county and retain 75% of locally generated agricultural transfer tax revenue, rather than the current 33% 

   - Priority preservation areas have been identified, based on development, zoning, and soils, within which preservation will be concentrated 

   - 40% of land in Somerset is already within a preservation program 

   - Set goal of 50% total area of land in a preservation program – 20,742 acres are needed to reach this goal 

- Next Steps 

   - Technical & Community Service staff review of Growth Areas and PPA 

   - Finalize Future Land Use Map, including PPA. 

   - In-Person Technical Committee Meeting and Planning Commission – March 6th 

   - Cohesive Draft Comprehensive Plan
