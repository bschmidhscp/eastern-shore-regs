A. OVERVIEW: 
On April 4, 2025, the Department of Technical and Community Services received an application 
from Theodore Miller, requesting a rezoning of his property (Tax Map 23, Grid 3, Parcel 2) from 
Residential (‘R-2’) to Agricultural Residential (‘AR’) citing a mistake in the existing Zoning 
Classification. After the public hearing on the 10th of July, 2025 it was decided that the applicant 
should pull their case, and reapply after the publication of the current county comprehensive plan. 
The applicant has now decided to move forward with the request once more, citing, however, a 
change in character rather than a mistake. The updated County Comprehensive Plan now includes 
a component for “Priority Preservation Ares” and the Miller property is identified as such. The 
parcel is approximately 72.47 acres in size, is located near Princess Anne, on the southern side of 
Brownstone Rd. Mr. Miller has indicated that he would like to continue his farming operation by 
applying to the Maryland Agricultural Land Preservation Foundation (MALPF) for a conservation 
easement. A public hearing on the rezoning request has been scheduled and noticed for the 
Planning Commission’s April 02, 2026 meeting.  
*For rezoning applications, the Planning Commission will need to hold a Public Hearing on the 
request and make a recommendation to the County Commissioners. The County Commissioners 
must also hold a Public Hearing before approving or denying the request.

 Neighborhood: For rezoning applications, Maryland Case Law requires that a 
‘neighborhood’ be identified. Neighborhoods are usually bordered by geographic features

suburban areas. For this request, staff identified a neighborhood generally defined by: Deal

For the Planning Commission’s reference, the Zoning Ordinance provides the following

environments at a moderate density. Quiet, hazard free, uncongested residential 
neighborhoods are encouraged. A mix of housing types are provided, with multi-
family dwellings needing special exception approval. To provide for compatible uses, 
while prohibiting incompatible uses.’ 
 
• Agricultural Residential District (AR) - To provide for a full range of agricultural

C. REZONING CRITERIA: The Maryland Land Use Article provides criteria to be considered 
for rezoning applications.  These items are listed below and are also addressed in the attached 
recommendation.  
 
1. Population Change:  In 2010, the total population of Somerset County was 26,470 
persons. By 2020, this had decreased to 24,620 according to the United States Census 
Bureau., population change is estimated to be minimal since the last comprehensive 
rezoning in 2019. 
 
2. Availability of Public Facilities: Sewer and water are not available on this property. 
Transportation access is available via Deal Island Road (State Route 363).   
 
3. Present and Future Transportation Patterns: Traffic volumes on Deal Island Road in 
the vicinity of the applicant’s property have decreased since the last County 
Comprehensive rezoning in 2019. The most recent numbers available shown from the 
AADT range from 2014 to 2022. Those numbers decreased from 3,471 vehicles per day in 
2014, to 3,461 vehicles per day in 2022. Currently, the site sees minimal traffic on 
Brownstone as it serves as an access/exit road for a small subdivision and a residential 
street, with direct access to Deal Island Road.  
 
4. Compatibility with Existing and Proposed Development for the Area: In general, 
rezoning’s can occur with the express purpose of using the land for another type of 
development. For example, a rezoning from Agricultural Residential to Commercial might 
occur in order to allow for the development of a grocery store. With Mr. Miller’s request, 
he intends to continue using the land in its current capacity, but just under a different zoning 
designation. As the current land use is agricultural and the proposed use is agricultural, the 
Planning Commission should find that the proposed land use is compatible with the 
existing land uses in the neighborhood. 
 
5. Relationship of the Proposed Amendment to the Comprehensive Plan: The Future 
Land Use Map suggests that this parcel is “undeveloped” and is classified within the newly 
established priority preservation area.

6. Change in the Character of the Neighborhood or Mistake in the Existing Zoning:  In 
accordance with State Law, a rezoning can only be approved by a finding that there has 
been a “substantial change” in the character of the neighborhood since the last 
comprehensive rezoning, or that there is a “mistake” in the existing zoning (see #7 below). 
The last comprehensive rezoning occurred in 2019, so any changes to justify the 
rezoning will need to have occurred since that date.  
 
Change in Character: Staff found that since 2019 the property immediately to the north of 
this property has been rezoned Agricultural Residential. The recently adopted 2025 
Comprehensive plan has placed the parcel in the priority preservation area – a new 
component of the Comprehensive Plan. Also, the future land use in the area has been 
changed from residential to undeveloped.  Per the Comprehensive Plan: “Establishing a 
PPA does not explicitly hinder or restrict development but rather encourages preservation. 
An initial step in the process of establishing the PPA for Somerset County included review 
of areas outside of existing and future growth.”  Staff note that the identification of the 
subject parcel as a Priority Preservation Area highlights the change in character of the area, 
as it is no longer viewed as a place for future development or residential growth. 
 
Mistake: This property was zoned R-2 during the County’s last comprehensive rezoning in 
2019, having been originally rezoned as R-2 from A-R in 2001. During the 2019 rezoning, 
the argument could be made that the parcel incorrectly identified as an opportunity for 
residential development, despite its agricultural nature and historical use. 
The 2019 rezoning wrongfully relied on outdated future land use maps from the 1996 
Comprehensive Plan, which planned for residential growth that ultimately did not occur. 
Further, the 2001 rezoning, likely occurred due to projected and desired residential growth 
in the county. With the 2008 recession along with other factors, residential growth never 
materialized in the area, while agricultural use persisted as the primary use of land in this 
area. Currently, staff believes there was an error in the methodology used for the 2019 
rezoning, but feel the argument for a change is character is stronger than the argument for 
a mistake. 
 
D. ANALYSIS & STAFF RECOMMENDATION: In staff’s opinion, there is merit in 
considering the request, based on a change in the character of the area, supported by the 
identification of the Miller parcel on the Priority Preservation Area map in the 2025 
Comprehensive Plan. The current land use is agricultural with all future plans provided by Mr. 
Miller indicating that the property will continue to be farmed along with an application for a 
conservation easement on the property. Staff is confident that Mr. Miller anticipates applying 
for the MALPF program (if approved for the rezoning). Based on current population estimates 
for the County (suggesting loss of population), it is unlikely to be developed residentially for

E. POINTS TO CONSIDER: 
 
• In any instance of a rezoning request, the Planning Commission is encouraged to look at the 
surrounding land uses and zoning. The Planning Commission should always be aware 
that if approved, the property may be developed with any of the listed land uses in the 
zoning ordinance.  
• The Planning Commission is asked to consider the long-term appropriateness of the region 
as a whole to determine if a rezoning is warranted at this time.
