###### **SOMERSET COUNTY PLANNING COMMISSION MEETING MINUTES** _May 7, 2026_ 

**B. Site Plan Review:** SP-26-267 The Trilogy Group LLC – 4381 Crisfield Highway (Tax Map 64, Grid 02, Parcel 120, Lot 2) 

Mr. Drewer read a summary of the Staff Report, for the record. 

Mrs. Sara Matthews & Mr. Cliff Matthews, approached the table with Mr. Scyhuler Fogle and Mr. David Batiste to give a summary of their request, stating this site was temporary with their intentions to relocate to the new County Industrial Park once the park is ready. 

Mr. Colborn asked about the office trailer and if a ramp would be needed for handicap accessibility. The Matthews stated at this time they did not have intentions to install a ramp however they would if their employee situation were to change and it be needed. 

A short discussion ensued among the Planning Commission members and the Matthews about the facility and that it was not open to the public, would be securely fenced in per Maryland Cannabis Administration (MCA) requirements, and would have approximately 20 cameras on site with security to monitor the facility. 

Mr. Colborn asked about stormwater management, which Mr. Drewer stated that because the project has less than 5,000sqft of disturbance, stormwater management is not required. 

Mr. Drewer read the recommended conditions and staff recommendation. 

Mr. Brice asked how long their ‘temporary’ intentions would be, Mr. Illuminati stated it would be good to put a time limit on the proposal and after such time, if the use remained, the owners would be required to resubmit for Planning Commission approvals. 

Mr. Drewer recommended at 36-month timeframe, which after a short discussion among the Planning Commission was found to be agreeable. 

On a motion my Mrs. McIntyre, seconded by Mr. Brice, the Planning Commission unanimously moved to approve the Trilogy Group Site Plan with the following conditions: 

1. All required revisions to the plat be confirmed prior to final approval and signatures. 

2. Application for Building Permits may not be submitted before May 17, 2026 (30-day appeal period after BZA Approval) 

3. All local, State, and Federal regulations shall be adhered to. 

4. This site plan is approved as a temporary use, and should the owners continue to utilize it for more than 36 months following issuance of a building permit, owners will be required to submit an updated site plan for the Planning Commission to review.

###### **A.** **<u>BACKGROUND:</u>** 

The Department of Technical and Community Services received a Site Plan submittal from Mr. Nathan Noble of Wilkins-Noble, LLC on January 30, 2026, on behalf of Southern Connection Seafood. The applicant is seeking Site Plan approval for the addition of two new 40’x120’ standalone storage structures, to support the already-existing business operations of Southern Connection Seafood.  The Zoning Code requires site plan approval for all new <u>or expanded</u> commercial uses, per section 8.15b(3). The Site Plan was reviewed at a Technical Advisory Committee (TAC) meeting on February 10, 2026.  A revised Site Plan was submitted on May 20, 2026 for Planning Commission consideration. 

###### **B.** **<u>PROJECT OVERVIEW:</u>** 

The proposed two storage buildings will be located adjacent to the existing cold storage building, and be utilized for dry storage.  To note: the existing cold storage building did not come before the Planning Commission prior to its construction, though this should have been a requirement due to its nature of expanding a commercial operation.  Concrete was already poured for the storage building nearest the cold storage building, but the process was halted by this office to ensure proper approvals were secured prior to the continuation of construction.  Also, of note: prior to coming through for Planning Commission review, the applicant was required to go through a lot consolidation to eliminate lot lines that previously cut through the site.  The Lot Reconfiguration Plat was recorded in the land records on May 22<sup>nd</sup> .

###### **C.** **<u>PROJECTS SPECIFICS:</u>** 

- a. Access: The parcel is accessed via a driveway off of Crisfield Hwy. 

- b. Stormwater Management: Proposed SWM measures must undergo full review and approval from County Engineer.

###### **MEMORANDUM** 

**TO:** Somerset County Planning Commission **FROM:** Department of Technical and Community Services (P&Z) **DATE:** June 4, 2026 

**SUBJECT:** Overview of Applicants/Recommendation to County Commissioners – Maryland Agricultural Land Preservation Foundation (MALPF) Easement Properties – Twenty (20) Properties Pending 

###### **<u>Introduction</u>** 

Since 1977, the Maryland Agricultural Land Preservation Foundation (MALPF) has purchased agricultural preservation easements throughout the State that restrict development on prime farmland and woodland.  The MALPF program, which is entirely voluntary on the part of landowners, is considered one of the most successful agricultural land preservation programs in the nation.  In Somerset County, MALPF has purchased 71 easements preserving nearly 7,000 acres through May 2026; three more were/are pending since May 2026. 

The deadline for submitting Fiscal Year 2027 applications and ranking to MALPF in Annapolis is July 1, 2026.  Please note that this deadline refers to the actual application form; each application needs to be reviewed by both the County’s Agricultural Land Preservation Board and the Planning Commission, but both of these board’s reviews, along with approval by the County Commissioners, can occur after July 1<sup>st</sup> , but within 60 days following July 1<sup>st</sup> .  Ranking must occur prior to July 1<sup>st</sup> .  Only 16 applications may be submitted to the State, but we have 20 interested applicants, so unfortunately, upon ranking, 5 will not be turned in to the State. 

###### **<u>Discussion</u>** 

Twenty FY27 MALPF applications have been drafted and are nearly ready to be submitted to the State. According to State Law, the local Ag Board and Planning Commission’s role is to recommend to the County Commissioners whether properties qualify for the State Program based on the following: 

1. Soils Criteria – 50% or more of the soils consist of U.S.D.A. Capability Classes I, II, or III; 

2. Size – Property must be at least 50 acres in size or adjoin an already-established easement property; 

3. Location – Property can be located within a 10-year water and sewer service district if the property is of “significant size” and has “outstanding productivity,” which is defined as having an approved soil conservation plan, is in an area designated by the County for agricultural preservation, and meets the soils criteria as listed above. 

Each of the 20 applications has at least 50% of the property soils classified as either Class I, II, or III.  For eligibility, if needed, wetlands (tidal and non-tidal) can be deducted from the calculations. Excluding the wetlands on the Windsor (115.84 ac. on Charles Cannon Rd) application allowed it

<!-- page 17 -->
to exceed the 50% threshold.  All properties are either 50 acres or more in size, or adjacent to already preserved land. 

The William Miller property on Wallace Taylor Rd and the Tull Farm Holding LLC 87.37 ac Polks Rd property did not initially qualify based on soil quality.  However, due to the productive nature of the land, the mapped soil classification was questioned and prompted a site analysis by Mr. Mark Carey of the Somerset Soil Conservation District office and USDA soil scientist Dr. Ann Rossi-Gill.  Their site analysis confirmed that the mapped soil classification was not accurate due to the drained nature of the land from adequate ditching.  Their new classification designated the soil such that it would qualify. 

The Zacierka property on Charles Cannon Rd also faced a qualifying issue due to soil quality.  In this case, a 25-acre portion of the property is being excluded from the paid easement acreage in order for at least 50% of the property soils to qualify. 

After the initial review of all applications, the Ag Board will be asked to rank all of the applications based on a number of criteria, including farm size, amount of land in cropland, ownership and operation, soils, etc.  The Board will be asked to approve a ranking at a future date.  We have scheduled the County Commissioners MALPF Public Hearing for June 30<sup>th</sup> .  The date for the next Ag Board meeting should be arranged prior to this Public Hearing, so that the Commissioners only have to review the 16 ranked applications, and not the full 20. 

In addition to the local Ag Board’s review, the Planning Commission also reviews and makes a recommendation on the applications to the County Commissioners.  However, the Planning Commission is not tasked with the ranking process, but rather a secondary overview and general recommendation. 

###### **<u>Board Action</u>** 

At this time, PC has the following action to take: 

- Whether to forward a favorable recommendation to the County Commissioners for the submittal of the soon-to-be-ranked top 16 applications to MALPF 

At their upcoming June 17<sup>th</sup> meeting, the Ag Board will approve a ranking of the applications based on soils, size, and location.  In the future, it is the goal of this office to move the date by which applicants express interest in applying to MALFP up to February 1<sup>st</sup> , rather than the current deadline of April 1<sup>st</sup> , in order to allow more time for application completion and ranking prior to bringing the applicant list before the Ag Board and Planning Commission.

### ADU Bill (HB 1466) FAQ 

###### **1. What is the purpose of Accessory Dwelling Units – Requirements and Prohibitions (HB 1466)?** 

Maryland’s General Assembly passed HB 1466 during its 2025 legislative session. HB 1466 makes it the policy of Maryland to promote and encourage the creation of Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) on land with a single-family detached dwelling unit as the primary dwelling unit in order to help solve the state’s housing shortage and meet its housing needs. To accommodate all who want to live in the state, Maryland will need a mix of quality housing types and affordability options, and ADUs are an important part of that mix.

###### **2. How does HB 1466 define an ADU?** 

HB 1466 defines ADUs as a secondary unit (1) on the same lot, parcel, or tract as a primary single-family detached dwelling unit and (2) that is not greater than 75% of the size of the primary single-family detached dwelling unit. The bill further states that ADUs can be a separate structure from the primary unit or attached as an addition to the primary unit. While not explicitly defined in the bill, jurisdictions may also permit ADUs that are wholly enclosed within the existing single-family dwelling. 

###### **3. When does HB 1466 go into effect?** 

The act became effective on October 1, 2025. The act includes a separate requirement that, by October 1, 2026, local governments adopt a local law authorizing development of ADUs on land with an existing single-family detached dwelling unit. 

###### **4. What does HB 1466 do?** 

The act defines ADUs, creates a statewide policy governing ADUs, requires that local governments adopt local laws authorizing development of ADUs, and prohibits documents such as deeds, bylaws of homeowner associations, and contracts from imposing unreasonable limitations on a property owner’s ability to develop an ADU or offer an ADU for rent. 

###### **5. How does HB 1466 modify local zoning?** 

HB 1466 modifies local zoning through its requirements for local laws authorizing the development of ADUs. See below for more information on those requirements. 

###### **6. What are the requirements for local laws governing ADUs?** 

Local laws must follow the intent of HB 1466, which is to promote and encourage ADUs. Such a law must: 

- Provide for the development of ADUs as defined in the bill and described in question two above 

- Provide for construction of ADUs that meet public health, safety, and welfare standards (such as building codes and adequate public facilities ordinances) 

- Exclude ADUs from density calculations 

- Exclude the development of an ADU from any measures that limit residential growth on the property on which the unit is proposed 

The act states that a local law governing ADUs may not: 

- Impose additional parking requirements on ADUs without first completing a parking study to determine the jurisdiction’s parking needs 

- Establish side and rear setback requirements that exceed existing accessory structure setback requirements 

The act states that a local law governing ADUs may: 

Establish standards for ADU safety 

- Prohibit conversion of an accessory structure as an ADU if the only access to the structure by car is from an alley 

###### **7. Does HB 1466 require local laws to permit ADUs by right?** 

No, but it does require that local laws authorize ADUs in some manner. A local government may establish a process that allows ADUs by right, or a local government may establish a process that requires a conditional use or special exception to confirm local requirements have been met. The Maryland ADU Policy Task Force recommended permitting “one internal or attached ADU by right on any lot in which a single-family residential unit is similarly permitted by right” as a best practice to support ADU development. 

###### **8. Can local laws be more permissive of ADUs than the requirements of HB 1466?** 

Yes, existing and future local laws governing ADUs can be more permissive than the requirements of HB 1466. This could include by-right approval, reduced or eliminated impact fees, or Adequate Public Facilities Ordinance exemptions. The ADU <u>Policy Task Force report</u> includes many best practices to support ADU development that are more permissive than the requirements of HB 1466. 

###### **9. Does HB 1466 impose any requirements regarding lot size or residential density?** 

HB 1466 does not limit the size of the lot on which an ADU can be located, but it does limit the size of the ADU to no more than 75% of the size of the primary single-family dwelling unit. Although the act does not identify how to measure the size of the primary dwelling for purposes of the 75% limitation, MDP recommends using the square footage of the finished living area above grade.  See ADU Policy Task Force report page 16 (ADU sizes should be "subordinate to the principal dwelling to support affordability and limit the impact on community facilities"). The act also requires that local laws governing ADUs exclude them from density calculations.

###### **10. What limit can my jurisdiction impose on ADU size?** 

HB 1466 defines an ADU as no greater than 75% of the size of its primary single-family detached dwelling unit. Because local jurisdictions are required to authorize ADUs consistent with the state definition, local jurisdictions may not limit the size of ADUs to anything below the 75% limit. Not all ADUs will be this size: a homeowner who wishes to develop a smaller ADU may do so under state law. See question #9 above for more information on ADU size requirements. 

###### **11. How does HB 1466 address facility capacity?** 

HB 1466 requires that local laws governing ADUs “provide for construction of accessory dwelling units that meet public health, safety, and welfare standards, including relevant building codes and adequate public facilities provisions.” For municipalities that do not determine their own facility capacities, MDP recommends consulting with their county government. 

The ADU Policy Task Force report recommends that local laws continue to follow existing environmental laws and laws regarding facility capacity. For by-right development of ADUs, local laws can include language allowing their development “as available utilities permit” (p. 12). 

###### **1 2. What is a parking study?** 

HB 1466 does not define the elements of a parking study, but it does require that any additional off-street parking requirements established for ADUs consider the following factors: 

- The cost to construct off-street parking spaces 

- Whether sufficient curb area exists along the front line of properties to accommodate on-street parking 

- The increase in impervious surfaces due to the creation of new off-street parking and its relation to stormwater management 

- Variability in parking due to the size of the ADU’s lot. 

HB 1466 requires that any local law requiring additional parking for ADUs shall provide a waiver process from the parking requirements. 

The Maryland ADU Policy Task Force report includes several best-practice recommendations for parking policies related to ADUs so that parking requirements do not become an unreasonable barrier to ADU development. 

###### **13. Is my jurisdiction required to do a parking study to comply with HB 1466?** 

The act requires that local jurisdictions complete a parking study only if their local laws impose off-street parking requirements on ADUs. If a jurisdiction’s local law does not require that an off-street parking space be constructed in order to develop an ADU, the jurisdiction does not need to complete a parking study. 

###### **14. How does HB 1466 apply to historic districts and historic properties?** 

The act’s prohibition against restrictive covenants (such as deeds or bylaws) specifically exempts Maryland properties listed in or determined to be eligible for listing in the National Register of Historic Places. Historic neighborhoods often include housing types similar to what we now call ADUs, accommodating multi-family and multi-generational living configurations. In many cases, ADUs can be compatible with historic neighborhoods. The Maryland Historical Trust recommends that local governments utilize the Secretary of the Interior's Standards for Rehabilitation (particularly Standards 9 and 10) and consider historic precedents and local architectural styles and approaches when increasing density in historic neighborhoods. If you have additional questions regarding historic properties, please contact Nell Ziehl, Chief of the Office of Planning, Education, and Outreach at the Maryland Historical Trust, at nell.ziehl@maryland.gov. 

###### **15. My jurisdiction already has an ADU law. Does HB 1466 require that the law be changed?** 

If a jurisdiction’s existing ADU law complies with all of HB 1466’s requirements, it does not need to be changed. Local governments should confirm with their legal counsel whether their ADU laws comply with HB 1466. Any new legislation that a local jurisdiction must adopt to comply with HB 1466 must be adopted by October 1, 2026. 

###### **16. How does HB 1466 define “unreasonable limitations”?** 

HB 1466 addresses unreasonable limitations in its amendment of the Real Property Article rather than in its amendment of the Land Use Article. It defines them as any restriction on use that prohibits, either explicitly or by effect, a property owner from developing or offering an ADU for rent. The act’s definition of unreasonable limitations applies to covenants, deeds, or similar private legal documents.

HB 1466 prohibits HOA bylaws from imposing unreasonable limitations (defined above) on the ability of a property owner to develop an ADU or offer an ADU for rent. HOAs should consult with their legal counsel to determine whether their bylaws may impose an unreasonable limitation on ADUs. 

###### **18. Does HB 1466 require that HOAs allow the use of ADUs as short-term rentals?** 

HB 1466 specifies that, for the purposes of its amendment of the Real Property Article, an unreasonable limitation does not include a limitation on the short-term rental of an accessory dwelling unit. 

###### **19. Does HB 1466 allow local governments to regulate the use of ADUs as short-term rentals?** 

HB 1466 does not alter a local government’s powers to regulate short-term rentals. 

###### **20. How does HB 1466 affect existing ADUs?** 

HB 1466 is silent on the topic of existing ADUs. Once a county or municipality adopts a new ADU law meeting the requirements of HB 1466, some existing ADUs within its jurisdiction may not conform with the new requirements. To avoid confusion and burden for existing ADU owners, in consultation with the jurisdiction’s legal counsel, MDP recommends that local legislation recognize the legally existing ADUs and avoid deeming them as non-conforming. The ADU Policy Task Force addressed best practices for nonconforming ADUs on page 23 of its report. 

###### **21. Does HB 1466 govern ADU structure or construction type?** 

HB 1466 does not identify permitted ADU structure types. It states, broadly, that local laws governing ADUs may "provide for construction of accessory dwelling units that meet public health, safety, and welfare standards, including relevant building codes." However, § 4-504 of Maryland’s Land Use Article separately provides that local governments may not prohibit the placement of a new manufactured home or modular dwelling in a zone that allows single-family residential uses. 

The ADU Policy Task Force report recommends that “detached ADUs, including both those involving the conversion of an existing accessory structure (such as a garage) or the construction of a new structure, should only be required to meet the same standards and lot requirements for any other accessory structure that is not a dwelling unit” (p. 15). 

###### **22. Does Maryland state law require fire sprinklers to be added to ADUs?** 

State law requires residential fire sprinkler systems in all new single- and two-family residential dwellings, which would include newly constructed ADUs (Section 313.2 of the International Residential Code as referenced by COMAR 09.12.51.04A). While Maryland’s Attorney General has provided an opinion stating that no provision of state law currently requires fire sprinklers in manufactured homes (see Maryland’s Attorney General Opinion No. 107 <u>OAG 74), local county or municipal building codes, or</u> local laws governing ADUs, may have separate requirements regarding fire sprinklers.  The State of Maryland Department of <u>State Police, Offce of the Fire Marshalli , is responsible for licensing and regulating residential sprinkler systems. Contact the</u> local government agency that administers the local building code, or the Office of the Fire Marshall, to determine how fire sprinkler requirements apply to a proposed ADU.
