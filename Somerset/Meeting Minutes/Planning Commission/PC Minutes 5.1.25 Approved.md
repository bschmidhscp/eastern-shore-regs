# **SOMERSET COUNTY PLANNING COMMISSION MEETING MINUTES** _May 1, 2025_ 

The following are the minutes of the Somerset County Planning Commission Public Hearing and meeting held on May 1, 2025, at 5:00 p.m. in the meeting room of the Somerset County Commissioners, Room 111, in the Somerset County Office Complex, 11916 Somerset Avenue, Princess Anne, Maryland. 

**<u>Planning Commissioners Present: Somerset County DTCS Staff Present:</u>** Jeremy Mason (via phone) Jesse Drewer, Director Janet McIntyre Will Cornish, Assistant Director and Zoning Admin. Skip Colborn Catherine Skeeter, Planner Nelson Brice 

**<u>Planning Commission Legal Counsel Present:</u>** Andrew Illuminati, Esq. 

In the absence of the Chairman, Vice-Chair Mr. Jeremy Mason opened the meeting at 5:00 p.m. 

## **A. APPROVAL OF MINUTES** 

April 3, 2025 Meeting Minutes: motion to approve by Mr. Skip Colborn and seconded by Mrs. Janet McIntyre. 

In the physical absence of the Vice-Chair, to ensure an expedient meeting, members moved to appoint Mrs. McIntyre as temporary chair to facilitate the meeting. 

## **B. NEW BUSINESS** 

**a.** **<u>Revised Major Subdivision Approval:</u>** SA25-566 Somerset Grove/Clayton Homes, Old Princess Anne Road / S. School Road / US Route 13 (Tax Map 23, Grid 12, Parcel 45, Lots 1-79) 

## Mr. Jesse Drewer read the Staff Report into the record. 

_“The Department of Technical and Community Services received a submittal from Bowman Consulting Group, Ltd on March 28, 2025, on behalf of the property owners. The applicant is seeking revised major subdivision approval for the extensive modification of the previously named and platted Somerset Gardens subdivision. The newly named Somerset Grove is comprised of 79 lots for individual sale. The Subdivision Ordinance requires major subdivisions to come before the planning commission for approval, per section III C.(1). The Plat and Site Development Plan were reviewed at a Technical Advisory Committee (TAC) meeting on April 7, 2025 (preliminary plat was previously presented at the September 12, 2024 TAC). Based on TAC comments the plat was not modified prior to Planning Commission, but a further detailed staff review letter was provided to the applicant on April 21, 2025 for edits to the subdivision plat. Review comments have been acknowledged by the applicant, and edits are pending._

Ms. Watkowski presented on the history of the property development, and the updated version of the subdivision, citing the improvements associated with higher standards due to updated codes and regulations.  Ms. Watkowski cited the consistency of the project with the Comprehensive Plan, as well as the general need for growth in the County and the ideal location of this site, particularly for those looking for smaller lots, such as young new homeowners or the retired community. Mr. Peterson mentioned the constraints the Forest Conservation area and stormwater management pond place on the property when it comes to creating a park. 

Mr. Mason inquired about the fees of the HOA, Mrs. McIntyre seconded this concern.  Ms. Watkowski stated that this is not established yet, but assured that would be known prior to selling properties. 

Mr. Colborn also asked about the lack of sidewalks, siting their benefits to safety.  Mr. Peterson shared that the group could look further into providing this.  Mr. Mason echoed the sidewalk concern.

**b. Site Plan Approval:** SP25-256 Somerset Grove/Clayton Homes, Old Princess Anne Road / S. School Road / US Route 13 (Tax Map 23, Grid 12, Parcel 45, Lots 1-79) 

Mr. Drewer read a summary of the Staff Report for the record. 

_“The proposed residential development plans for a total of 79 modular homes. There is an area to south of the proposed neighborhood that will be used to fulfill the Forest Conservation requirements. The property is located between US Rt. 13 and Old Princess Anne Road to the south of Howard H Anderson Road. There are two proposed entrances to the development, one on Old Princess Road and the other on Howard H. Anderson Road. The property is ideally zoned as R-2. There is an existing stormwater management pond on the site dating back to the first proposed development of the site in the early 2000s, which will be rehabilitated and upgraded to meet current regulations.”_ 

Mr. Rosebush spoke about the suggested sidewalk and playground amenities, stating that cost to the buyers would increase were these to be a condition of approval.  Mrs. McIntyre stated that not having sidewalks would not make the community unsafe explicitly.  Mr. Rosebush stressed that their proposal has emphasized a “gated feel” with the extensive landscaping.  Mr. Colborn asked about the need for overflow parking, as the HOA/County Roads will not allow on-street parking. 

Mr. Colborn stated that there could be flexibility in the ask for sidewalks, but that a tot lot is a must.  Mr. Peterson outlined the challenge with placing a tot lot, with Mr. Rosebush proposing 20’ rather than 30’ easements to allow for greater area on the lots and to sacrifice a lot for the creation of a tot lot. 

Mr. Drewer read Staff Recommendations for approval: 

- Revised subdivision plat be recorded prior to site plan signing. 

- Stormwater Management and Sediment and Erosion Control Approval shall be obtained.

**c.** **<u>Solar Energy Floating (SEF) Zone Approval:</u>** REZ-25-191 Nexamp, Inc., 10960 Kemps Nursery Road (Tax Map 22, Grid 06, Parcel 284) 

Mr. Drewer read a summary of the Staff Report and case history for the record. 

_“The Somerset County Zoning ordinance requires all solar energy facilities generating more than two (2) megawatts in electricity go through the SEF Floating Zone process. Pending a recommendation by the Planning Commission, a public hearing will be held by the County Commissioners on the request. If the floating zone is granted, the applicant would then be permitted to submit for the standard site plan review process by staff and the Planning Commission._ 

_The applicant proposes a solar energy facility that generates 5 megawatts of electricity. It is proposed to be 3 independent systems with 1 1MW and 2 2MW facilities. The proposed solar energy facility will be located on approximately 30 of the 136 acres that comprise the site. Located approximately 0.4 miles down Kemps Nursery Road, the property and surrounding area are primarily agricultural in nature. The property is zoned as Agricultural Residential and complies with the conditions outlined in section 7.5c of the zoning code.”_ 

Attorney Ms. Sharon Van Emberg and Mr. Erick Alves de Sa were present to discuss the proposal. Ms. Van Emberg explained that since their first Planning Commission appearance, the property had been surveyed to reveal that the southern portion suspected to belong to the property owner in fact was a part of the property.  As such, in the revised proposal the panels have been moved south on the parcel, further from the road, more out of sight, and on less productive farm land.  Mr. Alves de Sa went through a quick PowerPoint presentation further outlining Nexamp and the project specifics.  The panels will now jut into the Chesapeake Bay Critical Area, Mr. Alves de Sa confirmed that he has been in contact with Critical Area Commission staff to ensure the development is in compliance with any Critical Area requirements.  Also, the parnel area will be within the floodplain, which will require additional standards be met.  Mr. Alves de Sa outlined the proposed landscaping on the eastern boundaries of the site, and highlighted the existing wooded buffers surrounding most of the project area. 

Mr. Jimmy Beauchamp voiced his general support for the new location of the panels, but still wanted to share that farm land being lost would be a negative, with the new siting leading to a greater area of farm land lost than the original.  Mr. Beauchamp also inquired about the maintenance of the grassed areas outside of the project fences.  Mr. Alves de Sa stated that the area outside of the fences would be maintained by Nexamp for this particular site.

**d. Critical Area Growth Allocation Applications:** GA25-32 Kingsbay Mansion, LLC/David Carlin King & Gerald Philpott 27999 Coulbourn Creek Road, (Tax Map 57, Grid 08, Parcel 119) 

Mr. Drewer read the Staff Report into the record. 

“ _The Department of Technical and Community Services received an application from David King and Gerald Philpott on April 16, 2025, following several months of discussions on expansion of uses for the property, which is known as Kingsbay Mansion. The applicants are seeking Chesapeake Bay Critical Area (CBCA) Growth Allocation approval. Pursuant to the Somerset County Critical Area Ordinance, all applications for Growth Allocation are to be forwarded to the Planning Commission for review, a public hearing, comments and a recommendation._ 

_The Planning Commission recommendation is forwarded to the Somerset County Commissioners who will hold a second public hearing, provide comment, and decide whether to utilize a portion of the growth allocation acreage allocated to the county. If recommended by the County Commissioners, the growth allocation request will be forwarded to the Critical Area Commission for review and confirmation._ 

The applicant proposes no physical increase in the structure, rather the growth allocation from Resource Conservation Area (RCA) to Limited Development Area (LDA), which will allow the creation of a public serving restaurant within the primary structure on the property _”_ 

Mr. Jerry Philpott was present as the applicant.  Mr. Philpott shared with the Planning Commission members the intent to establish a restaurant within their existing dwelling/bed and breakfast, noting that no physical modifications to the site/footprint would be necessary as part of the request.  The need in the County for a restaurant, as well as the prime location of Kingsbay, was stressed by Mr. Philpott.
