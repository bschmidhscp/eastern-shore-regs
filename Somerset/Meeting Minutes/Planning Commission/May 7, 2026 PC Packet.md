DECISION AND FINDINGS OF FACT 
Theodore Miller (“Applicant”) is the owner of a certain real property 
identified as Somerset County Tax Map 23, Grid 03, Parcel 002 (the “Property”). 
The Property is 72.47 acres +/- and is located at 11105 Brownstone Road, Somerset 
County, Maryland. The Applicant seeks to rezone the property from Residential 
(“R2”) to Agricultural Residential (“AR”) citing a substantial change in the 
character of the neighborhood. The Applicant currently farms the land and seeks 
to apply for a Maryland Agricultural Land Preservation Foundation (“MALPF”) 
conservation easement. The Property is located near Princess Anne on the 
southern side of Brownstone Road.  
 
On April 4, 2025, the Department of Technical and Community Services 
received an application for the rezoning of the Property. The application was held 
in abeyance pending the completion of the Comprehensive Plan. On Thursday, 
April 2, 2026, the Somerset County Planning and Zoning Commission (“Planning 
Commission”) met to consider the rezoning request. The Planning Commission 
received evidence from the Applicant through exhibits and testimony. The 
Applicant’s evidence centered around the six criteria set forth in the Annotated 
Code of Maryland, Land Use Article, § 4-204(b)(1), which must be addressed to 
amend a zoning classification. Those provisions are: 
(1) Population change; 
(2) The availability of public facilities; 
(3) Present and future transportation patterns; 
(4) Compatibility with existing and proposed development for the 
area; 
(5) The recommendation of the planning commission; and 
(6) The relationship of the proposed amendment to the local 
jurisdiction’s plan;

<!-- page 8 -->
After receiving the evidence, the Planning Commission asked questions of 
the Applicant in relation to the requested rezoning. The County staff presented the 
Staff Report. After the presentation of the Staff Report, the matter was opened for 
public comment. There was no public present in favor or against the rezoning 
request; however, an opposition letter was received and noted as a part of the 
record. The Planning Commission then closed the evidentiary portion of the 
hearing and began deliberations on the request.1 The Planning Commission voted 
to make a preliminarily favorable recommendation due a substantial change in the 
character of the neighborhood. The Planning Commission requested that a 
decision and finding of fact be prepared for the next meeting to finalize the 
recommendation. 
 
Md. Ann. Code, Land Use Article, § 4-204(b)(2) allows a legislative body to 
change the zoning classification of a property based on a finding that there was “a 
substantial change in the character of the neighborhood where the property is 
located; or a mistake in the existing zoning classification.” The specific finding of 
the Planning Commission is that the Applicant’s rezoning request is not in conflict 
with the criteria set forth in MD Code, Land Use Article, § 4-204(b)(1). Applying 
the criteria, the Planning Commission finds: 
 
(1) Population change. 
Per the Somerset County Comprehensive Plan, in 2010, the total 
population of Somerset County was 26,470 people. By 2020, the 
population had decreased to 24,620 people. The most recent 
American Community Survey (ACS) estimates that the total county 
population is roughly 24,910 people representing an approximate 6 
% change in population from 2000 to 2018. The last comprehensive 
rezoning occurred in 2019. The Planning Commission finds that 
there has been a population change in favor of rezoning. 
 
(2) The availability of public facilities; 
Sewer and water are not available for hook-up on this Property. The 
Staff Report noted that transportation access is available via Deal

Island Road (State Route 363). Other services, such as fire and police 
are able to meet the needs as there is not a change in the proposed 
use and the Applicant intends to continue farming. The Planning 
Commission finds that there are available public facilities that could 
accommodate the proposed rezoning. 
 
(3) Present and future transportation patterns; 
The Property is located on Deal Island. Traffic volumes on Deal 
Island Road in the vicinity of the Property have slightly decreased 
since the last County comprehensive rezoning in 2019. The most 
recent numbers available show from the AADDT range from 2014 to 
2022. Those numbers decrease from 3,471 vehicles per day in 2014 to 
3,461 vehicles per day in 2022. Currently, the Property sees minimal 
traffic on Brownstone, as it serves as an access/exit road for a small 
subdivision and residential street with direct access to Deal Island 
Road. The Planning Commission finds that the transportation 
network can accommodate the proposed rezoning. 
 
(4) Compatibility with existing and proposed development for the 
area; 
The Applicant’s request is both compatible with existing and 
proposed development for the area. The Applicant’s request intents 
to continue using the land in its current capacity (as farm land), but 
just under a different zoning designation. As the current land use is 
agricultural and the proposed use is agricultural, the Planning 
Commission finds that the proposed rezoning intended use are 
consistent and compatible with the existing and proposed 
development.  
 
(5) The relationship of the proposed amendment to the local 
jurisdiction’s plan; 
The future land use maps suggest that this parcel is “undeveloped” 
and is classified within the newly established priority preservation 
area. The priority preservation area is new to the County 
Comprehensive Plan. The goal of the priority preservation area is to 
“support and sustain a strong, diversified agricultural community

<!-- page 10 -->
through implementation of preservation and development mechanisms that are 
balanced and maintains the rural character of Somerset County.” See Chapter 11, 
Somerset County Comprehensive Plan.  
 
In accordance with State Law, a rezoning can only be approved by a finding 
that there has been a “substantial change” in the character of the neighborhood 
since the last comprehensive rezoning, or that there is a “mistake” in the existing 
zoning. The last comprehensive rezoning occurred in December 2018, so any 
changes to justify the rezoning will need to have occurred since that date. 
 
Substantial Change in the Character of the Neighborhood 
Since the last rezoning, the neighborhood has changed. Since 2019 the 
property immediately to the north of this property has been rezoned Agricultural 
Residential. The recently adopted 2025 Comprehensive Plan places the Property 
in the priority preservation area, which is a new component to the Comprehensive 
Plan. Also, the future land use in the area has changed from residential to 
undeveloped. Per the Comprehensive Plan, “establishing a priority preservation 
area does not explicitly hinder or restrict development, but rather encourages 
preservation. An initial step in the process of establishing the priority preservation 
area for Somerset County includes review of areas outside of existing and future 
growth.” The identification of the Property as a priority preservation area 
highlights the change in character of the area, as it is no longer viewed as a place 
for future development or residential growth. The Planning Commission 
recommends a rezoning based on a substantial change in the character of the 
neighborhood. 
 
Mistake in the Existing Zoning 
The Applicant is not proceeding on a claim of mistake in existing zoning. 
The Planning Commission does not recommend a rezoning based on a mistake in 
existing zoning.

change in the character of the neighborhood.

A. BACKGROUND:  
The Department of Technical and Community Services received a Site Plan submittal from Mr. 
Cliff Matthews on March 30, 2026, on behalf of The Trilogy Group LLC. The applicant is seeking 
Site Plan approval for the establishment of a Cannabis Cultivation Facility.  The Zoning Code 
requires site plan approval for all commercial development, per section 8.15b(3). The Site Plan 
was reviewed at a Technical Advisory Committee (TAC) meeting on April 6, 2026.  A revised 
Site Plan was submitted on April 23, 2026 for Planning Commission consideration. The applicant 
was required to get Board of Zoning Appeals special exception approval for the use, which was 
approved at the April 17, 2026 Meeting. 
 
B. PROJECT OVERVIEW:  
The proposed cultivation facility will be located on Crisfield Highway, near the city limits of 
Crisfield.  The lot is currently unimproved, having historically been used for the storage of boats, 
vehicles, and trailers.  The lot has preexisting impervious cover (gravel) over 37.6% of the lot.  
The lot is within the Critical Area (Limited Development Area), which would typically only allow 
for 15% impervious area.  P&Z Staff discussed at length with Critical Area Commission Staff for 
the last proposed redevelopment project on this site.  Concerns for lot coverage have been taken 
into consideration on the revised Site Plan, by placing all new development on already-impervious 
areas. Also, revised plans reflect efforts to preserve the existing wooded areas - no tree clearance 
is proposed.  Additional improvements to the existing conditions will include on-site landscaping 
and fencing around the entire facility.  Additionally, the parcel is ideally located near the City, and 
adjacent to the Route 413 Hiker/Biker Trail.

Re: Local Jurisdiction Annual Reports for Calendar Year 2025 (CY2025)  
   
Dear Planning Director and Planning Commission/Board Chairs,   
   
I would like to thank all jurisdictions who submitted a Local Jurisdiction Annual Report for CY 
2024. The information you provided is essential for the Maryland Department of Planning’s 
(MDP’s) annual Summary of Local Government Annual Reporting, submitted to the General 
Assembly. MDP compiles and analyzes this information to measure Maryland’s progress toward 
meeting its sustainable growth goals, such as concentrating new development in Priority 
Funding Areas (PFAs).   
  
As required by the General Assembly since 1952, under Land Use Article §1-207 (basic 
requirements), §7-104 (adequate facilities reporting requirements), and §1-208 (measures and 
indicators), all planning commissions and planning boards with planning and zoning authority 
are required to prepare and submit an annual report for CY2025 by July 1, 2026.  
 
New for CY2025 is the adoption of Maryland’s Sustainable Growth Planning Principles 
(Principles). The Principles were signed into law by Governor Moore on April 8, 2025, following 
the Maryland General Assembly approval of House Bill (HB) 286. The Principles are now 
required to be implemented through all comprehensive plans and comprehensive plan updates. 
The planning commission should review these new Principles and the Sustainable Growth 
Implementation Guide prior to approving this year’s annual report.  
 
Also new for the CY2025 Local Jurisdictional Annual Report, §20-125, Local Government Article, 
requires charter, code and commission counties to identify the amount and location of the 
collection and the expenditures of development impact fees, surcharges, or excise taxes 
collected for the prior calendar year. MDP added Section VII to the Local Jurisdictional Annual 
Report Worksheet, County Collection and Expenditure of Development Impact Fees, Surcharges, 
or Excise Taxes. As this is a new requirement, MDP will host a question-and-answer session this 
spring and invite staff from those counties which need to submit this report. If you would like to 
attend, please let MDP know and review Section VII prior to the meeting. 
 
The annual reporting process is a perfect time to review and discuss land use and development 
trends, evaluate public facility capacities and needs, and address new planning legislation, such

<!-- page 37 -->
as HB 538’s impact on manufactured housing in single-family zoning districts and density 
increases for qualified projects, HB 1466’s requirement to adopt a local law that permits 
accessory dwelling units on properties with an existing single family detached home, and the 
new Principles. MDP’s reporting forms include questions that are designed to help spark 
conversations between planning staff and local officials about development in their community, 
by posing such questions as: Did your jurisdiction identify any recommendations for improving 
the planning and development process within the jurisdiction? Is this what our plan intended? 
How are our growth areas coming along? These types of discussions are important catalysts to 
promote the development of great communities.  
 
MDP has prepared Annual Report templates to assist with organizing the requirements, 
organized by type of jurisdiction, described below: 
 
Jurisdictions issuing fewer than 50 new residential permits are encouraged to use the annual 
report short form (For Municipalities or Counties) template.  
  
Jurisdictions issuing more than 50 new residential permits must report the additional 
measures and indicators requirements of §1-208(c). The requirements are summarized in 
the annual report long form template. As always, the template forms are guides and may be 
adjusted to meet local permitting practices.  
 
As an additional reminder, MDP has streamlined our submission process. Annual reports, five-
year mid cycle reports, comprehensive plans, annexations, PFA certifications, water and sewer 
plans, and APFO reports can now be submitted to mdp.planreview@maryland.gov. Hardcopy 
submissions to MDP are no longer required. For additional information and guidance on 
submissions to MDP, visit MDP’s submissions webpage, which is designed to help local 
governments with this process.   
 
Our department is always available to help you with the annual report so please do not hesitate 
to contact David Dahlstrom, AICP at david.dahlstrom@maryland.gov, if you have any questions 
or need assistance. We appreciate your participation.
