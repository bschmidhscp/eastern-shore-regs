# **SOMERSET COUNTY PLANNING COMMISSION MEETING MINUTES** _November 7, 2024_ 

The following are the minutes of the Somerset County Planning Commission Public Hearing and meeting held on November 7, 2024, at 5:00 p.m. in the meeting room of the Somerset County Commissioners, Room 111, in the Somerset County Office Complex, 11916 Somerset Avenue, Princess Anne, Maryland. 

**<u>Planning Commissioners Present: Somerset County DTCS Staff Present:</u>** Matt Webster Jesse Drewer, Director Jeremy Mason Will Cornish, Assistant Director and Zoning Skip Colborn Administrator Janet McIntyre Catherine Skeeter, Planner Steve Raab Laverne Stewart **<u>Planning Commission Legal Counsel Present:</u>** Nelson Brice Andrew Illuminati, Esq. 

- **1) 5:00 PM – Call to Order:** Mr. Matt Webster opened the Public Hearing at 5:00 p.m. 

- **2) 5:01PM – Public Hearing on Proposed Text Amendments for Wireless Telecommunication Facilities** – Section 5.7(d) Commercial Uses, Section 5.7 Supplemental Use Regulations, and Section 17 Definitions. 

Mr. Jesse Drewer read the public hearing advertisement into the record.  Mr. Webster offered any proponents or opponents the chance to voice their stance.  With no public comment, the options were outlined by Mr. Will Cornish. 

- _Option #1:  Amend 5.7(d) Commercial Uses #3 to Permitted by Right Use to include Wireless Telecommunication Facilities and the Addition of Supplemental Use Regulations for Wireless Telecommunication Facilities to Section 5.8(n) on pages 5- 34 through 5-36_ 

- _Option #2: Amend Section 8.4 Essential Services to include Wireless Communication Towers as an Essential Service._ 

The Public Hearing closed at 5:05 PM. 

- **3) 5:05PM – Public Hearing on Proposed Text Amendments for Cannabis** – Section 5.7(d) Commercial Uses, 5.7(e) Industrial Uses, Section 5.7 Supplemental Use Regulations, and Section 17 Definitions. 

Mr. Drewer read the public hearing advertisement into the record.  Mr. Drewer then presented a Staff Report for the record.

<!-- page 2 -->
_After discussion among staff and with the Planning Commission Attorney, to come into compliance with Maryland State Law in regards to cannabis zoning, we are proposing the following text amendment to Section 5.7(d) Commercial Uses to include Cannabis. Adding Cannabis to Commercial Uses would also allow for its’ deletion from Section 5.7(e) Industrial Uses._ 

_It is also recommended to add Supplemental Use Regulations for Cannabis to Section 5.8(m) which can be seen on pages 5-33 through 5-34._ 

_Furthermore, after researching alcohol dispensary regulations in Somerset County, is has been determined that the minimum distance for dispensaries was lowered to 200ft. This is the minimum distance a dispensary (and proposed cannabis operation) could be located to a Place of Worship, School, Library, or Youth Center._ 

Mr. Webster offered any proponents the opportunity to voice their stance.  Hearing none, opponents were offered the chance to speak. 

Mr. Richard Schneider approached to speak as an opponent to the proposed zoning text amendment for cannabis.  Mr. Schneider voiced his opinion that zero benefit would come to the County were the cannabis regulations, as guided by the State, be moved forward.  Mr. Schneider mentioned the importance of a pending Feasibility Study, being conducted by UMES, and taking its points into consideration prior to amending the code. 

Mr. Robert Hajir came up to speak as an opponent to the proposed cannabis zoning text amendment, and the pending Community Impact Study, called for by the Town of Princess Anne, and supported by the Community & Family Safety Alliance.  UMES, as an unbiased actor, is conducting the study, with staff from the Pharmacy, Business, and Criminal Justice Departments involved.  The timeframe, as stated by Mr. Hajir, may be a few months out to completion. 

Mr. Robert Davis, pharmacist and owner of High Tide Dispensary, approached to speak as a proponent of the proposed cannabis zoning text amendment.  Mr. Davis mentioned the wealth of information available in support of legalizing cannabis production and sale – stating the point that the black market essentially disappears in areas with the highly regulated legal sale of cannabis through dispensaries.  Mr. Davis noted the importance of cannabis for a wide range of medical treatments.  Mr. Davis also referenced the value to be brought back to the County from the Cannabis Public Health Fund and the Community Reinvestment and Repair Fund. Mr. Davis also mentioned that it would be unlikely that the State would issue another license, and that a guiding County Ordinance with cannabis appropriately addressed is of great importance.  Mr. Davis also added that UMES has a program specifically created for Cannabis Management. 

Mr. Hajir re-approached to expand.  Mr. Hajir mentioned that 10% of Somerset County middle schoolers are actively using marijuana, as referenced in a report by the State.  Mr. Hajir expressed that a County-specific cannabis study would be far more valuable than any existing study.

Mrs. Janice Jones-Dale approached to speak as an opponent of the proposed zoning text amendment.  Mrs. Jones Dale spoke of her concern for the image a retail cannabis shop would portray about the County. 

The Planning Commission took a brief recess until 5:55 PM. 

The Planning Commission re-opened the Public Hearing at 5:55 PM. 

Mr. Schneider re-approached to offer his clarification of a figure previously referenced by Mr. Davis – 5% of the state 9% tax on cannabis comes back to the County.  Also, he encouraged the County to take time to pass any text amendments on cannabis, pointing out that the State has not set any hard deadline by which this must be accomplished. 

Mr. Hajir re-approached to state that the licensee in the County has until January of 2026 to open or the license will be rescinded.  Decisions should not be expedited to favor a business man’s interest, but rather the County’s best interest. 

The Planning Commission closed the Public Hearing at 6:00 PM. 

- **4) 6:00PM - APPROVAL OF MINUTES:** October 3, 2024 Meeting Minutes: motion to approve by Mr. Mason and seconded by Mr. Raab. 

- **5) NEW BUSINESS:** 

   - **a. Discussion and Recommendation: Somerset County Zoning Code Text Amendments** 

_Wireless Telecommunications Facilities Text Amendment Discussion:_ 

Mr. Cornish again outlined the 2 options for code changes.  Mrs. Janet McIntyre favored the option 2, stating that the service seems essential, but expressed need for clarification of both options.  Mr. Cornish stated the benefit of option 1 relating to control over siting, and for size concerns.  Mr. Nelson Brice asked if this code would include the large telecommunications towers, with Mr. Cornish stating this would apply to the 50’ and under telecommunications towers, more akin to standard utility poles.  Mr. Webster voiced his favoring of option 1, as it would allow for some oversight from the County, which option 2 would not. 

On a motion by Mrs. Laverne Stewart, seconded by Mr. Mason, the Planning Commission voted to move forward with option 1, with only Mrs. McIntyre voting against. 

_Cannabis Text Amendment Discussion:_ 

Mr. Drewer referenced State guidance, stating that cannabis shall not be regulated more strictly than alcohol, referencing the maximum 200’ setback from a place of

worship school, public library, or youth center.  The current proposal is to allow by Special Exception in AR, MRC, I1, and 12; and permitted in C1 and C2. 

Mr. Skip Colborn spoke of the logical motive to allow for the production of cannabis in the proposed zones, but spoke negatively of the health implications of allowing for the sale of cannabis in the County.  Recommended a two-year moratorium or a referendum, guided by citizen input, to slow down the process. 

Mr. Brice asked about the location of industrial zones where cannabis would now be allowed. 

Mrs. McIntyre voiced her support for the staff-recommended cannabis zoning text amendment.  Mrs. McIntyre stated that the concern for youth access would not be increased by having regulated sale, nor would the appearances of the County be impacted by cannabis.  Mrs. McIntyre stated that compliance with State guidance is best, and having an appropriate ordinance in place for the County would be wise. Mrs. McIntyre referenced the medicinal benefits of cannabis, and potential benefits to regulated sale access. 

Mr. Colborn voiced concern for a perceived lack of public input for the sale of cannabis.  Mr. Illuminati referenced instances where the public did, in fact, have input in the passing of laws relating to the expanding of cannabis sale. 

Mrs. Stewart voiced her distaste for the proposed cannabis text amendment, sighting her desire for the results of the pending Community Impact Study. 

Mr. Illuminati recommended a potential modification of the Staff’s proposed amendment, instead changing from Permitted to Special Exception for growing and processing in C1 and C2, as this would go along with the degree in which brewpubs, micro-breweries, and distilleries are regulated.  Also, this would not impact the sale of cannabis, which would remain permitted in C1 and C2. 

Mr. Mason voiced his concern for the selling aspect.  Mrs. McIntyre stated that the County Commissioners are where the final decision lies, and that the Planning Commission should focus on coming to a consensus to get a decision to them. 

Mr. Raab made a motion to accept the amended staff recommended cannabis zoning text amendment, seconded by Mr. McIntyre.  This motion failed with the following votes: 2 for, 4 against (Raab-yes, McIntyre-yes, Colborn-no, Mason-no, Stewart-no, Brice-no). 

Mr. Mason made a motion to accept an altered cannabis zoning text amendment (sale not allowed), seconded by Mr. Colborn.  This motion failed with the following votes:

- **b. Site Plan:** SP24-250 Network Towers LLC, Bardwell Drive/Ocean Highway (Tax Map 23, Grid 18, Parcel 136, Lot 1B)

Mr. Golden presented a PowerPoint to the Planning Commission, highlighting the need for greater coverage in the proposed location, and the compliance with County Standards across the board. No Planning Commission members had any concerns about the proposal.

## **6) OTHER BUSINESS** 

Mr. Drewer gave the date for the upcoming Planning Commission meeting: December 5, 2024, during which the only item so far slated is the further discussion of the cannabis code update.
