B. NEW BUSINESS: 
a. Proposed Zoning Code Text Amendment: Cannabis – Section 5.7(d) Commercial Uses, 
5.7(e) Industrial Uses, Section 5.7 Supplemental Use Regulations, and Section 17 Definitions. 
 
Mr. Jesse Drewer read the legal memo prepared by legal counsel, Mr. Andrew Illuminati, Esq., for 
the record. 
 
Discussion commenced among Planning Commission members.  Mr. Skip Colborn expressed his 
desire for a compromise, stating that many within the County are not in favor of the change.  Mr. 
Colborn brought up the issue of liquor vs. beer and wine, referencing the licensing process for their 
sale.  Mr. Colborn questioned whether the County could support a cannabis dispensary, as well as 
mentioned the stigma associated with locals patronizing such a business, citing the likelihood that 
a potential cannabis dispensary would more-so serve populations from outside of the County.  
Traffic/parking concerns were raised by Mr. Colborn, as well.   Mr. Colborn stated his belief that 
a cannabis dispensary would not be appropriate in the C-1 commercial district, as well as within 
MRC.  
 
Mr. Steve Raab asked for a renewed synopsis of the Staff’s recommendations.  Mr. Drewer 
provided the following summary of the proposed amendment to the table of permitted uses and 
supplemental use regulations:

<!-- page 3 -->
2 
 
Growing permitted by special exception in C-1, C-2, OC, I-1, and I-2 zones; processing would be 
by special exception in the C-1, C-2, OC, I-1, and I-2 zones; and selling would be permitted by 
special exception in AR and MRC, permitted in C-1 and C-2, and not permitted in the I-1 and I-2 
zones; on-site consumption would not be permitted in any zoning district. 
 
A cannabis business may not locate within: 500 feet from a pre-existing primary or secondary 
school; 500 feet from a pre-existing childcare center, registered family childcare home, 
playground, recreation center, library, place of assembly, or public park; 2,640 feet from another 
pre-existing cannabis business or 100 feet from a pre-existing residence. 
 
On a motion by Mr. Raab, seconded by Mr. Brice, the Planning Commission voted to approve the 
proposed zoning code text amendment to modify, as staff and legal counsel recommended, the 
table of permitted uses and supplemental use regulations for cannabis businesses.

A. BACKGROUND:
The Department of Technical and Community Services received a submittal from Mr.
Evans of on June 3, 2024.  Mr. Evans is seeking site plan approval for the development of
a seafood market. The Zoning Code requires site plan approval for all new commercial
development, per section 8.15b(3).  The Site Plan was reviewed at a Technical Advisory
Committee (TAC) meeting on June 10, 2024.  A revised Site Plan was submitted on
January 10, 2025 for review and comment.
B. PROJECT OVERVIEW:
The proposed Skipjack Seafood Market will be located on the corner of Skipjack Circle
and Market Lane in Princess Anne.  Market Lane runs parallel to Rt. 13, and the site of the
proposed is very visible from Rt. 13. The lot of concern is 1.11 acres, and is zoned C-2
(General Commercial).
C. PROJECTS SPECIFICS:
a.
Access: Two ingress/egress points, one on Market Lane and one on Skipjack Circle
are proposed.
b. Parking: 9 parking spaces are proposed, with the minimum requirement being 9 (8
+ 1 employee).
c.
Signage: No signage has been proposed at this time.
d. Stormwater Management: This project will result in greater than 5,000 square feet
of disturbance and therefore requires stormwater management plan approval
through the County Engineer.

<!-- page 7 -->
e. Water and Sewer: The site is already served by the Somerset County Sanitary 
District and reconnection to their services will require approval through SCSD.  
f. Landscaping: Commercial landscaping required, per sec. 8.9(c) of the zoning code.  
Landscape buffers are proposed on the front and sides of the lot, using a mix of 
screening trees, canopy trees, and shrubs.   
g. Lighting: The plan submitted includes the photometric lighting plan. There are 12 
wall pack lights on the front, sides, and rear of the building. 
 
D. DISCUSSION POINTS:  
a. Building Aesthetics: Considering the proposal does not include a façade plan, it 
may be worthwhile to discuss the aesthetics of the proposed development.  
 
E. RECOMMENDATION/CONDITIONS: 
a. Staff recommends approval of the Site Plan for Skipjack Seafood Market

A. BACKGROUND:  
The Department of Technical and Community Services received a submittal from Mr. 
Brock Parker of Parker & Associates, Inc on December 19, 2024, on behalf of the Somerset 
County Commissioners. The applicant is seeking major subdivision approval for the 
creation of a new Somerset County Industrial Park, comprised of 20 lots for individual sale. 
The Subdivision Ordinance requires major subdivisions to come before the planning 
commission for approval, per section III C.(1). The Site Plan was reviewed at a Technical 
Advisory Committee (TAC) meeting on January 13, 2025. A revised plat was submitted 
on January 22, 2025 for Planning Commission consideration. 
 
B. PROJECT OVERVIEW:  
This County-owned lot is slated for subdivision in order to establish a new industrial park, 
with a collection of lots available for purchase and development.  The parcel has a storied 
past, once planned for the development of a Wal-Mart distribution facility, it has been 
farmed since that fell through.  The northern portion of the property has been strip mined, 
and a notable elevation difference bisects the parcel.  Directly to the east is ECI, and another 
County-owned parcel is to the west, which is utilized as a borrow site for County projects.  
The property is ideally zoned as Light Industrial, is not adjacent to any extensive residential 
communities, and the future development will not impact the sensitive environmental 
features on the northern portion of the parcel. 
 
C. PROJECTS SPECIFICS: 
a. Access: 3 new roads with cul de sacs are being proposed off of Revells Neck Rd.  
Access to the northern-most lot 21, which is not at this time intended for sale and 
development, is available via an existing 50’ access and drainage easement with a

<!-- page 24 -->
private entrance from Revells Neck. Construction plans for the proposed public 
roads must be approved by the county engineer and roads department. These roads 
must be constructed to county standards. 
b. Signage: the location of a proposed Somerset Industrial Park sign is shown on 
newly proposed parcel A, adjacent to the planned lot 2. 
c. Stormwater Management: This project requires stormwater management plan 
approval through the County Engineer.  Drainage and maintenance easements have 
been shown on the plat. Sediment and erosion control review needed through the 
Soil Conservation Office. 
d. Water and Sewer: Sewer and water service are available, though the water is non-
potable.  A pump station will be needed, and is planned to be placed on proposed 
parcel A.  
e. Landscaping: Landscaping shall be addressed on a case-by-case basis as the 
commercial entities develop each individual lot.  Forest Conservation shall also be 
addressed upon lot development, though an area has been established in the 
northern portion of the parcel to accommodate the future planting. 
 
D. DISCUSSION POINTS:  
a. Landscaping: Though landscaping shall be addressed as each lot is developed, 
should there be landscaping along the whole of Revells Neck Rd at the creation of 
the subdivision? What about landscaping around the proposed sign on parcel A. 
b. Details to be added to the plat: Somerset County Planning & Zoning signature 
block, note regarding landscaping and forest conservation, note regarding Critical 
Area designation. 
c. What is parcel A?  Should this be renamed to parcel 149 revised lot B?  
 
E. RECOMMENDATION/CONDITIONS: 
a. It is recommended that the Planning Commission consider approval of the proposed 
major subdivision to establish the Somerset County Industrial Park, with the 
following conditions: 
• All required revisions to the plat be confirmed prior to recording. 
• Stormwater Management and Sediment and Erosion Control Approval shall 
be obtained. 
• Additional recommendations as applied by the Planning Commission, be 
addressed on the site plan.

Dear Mr. Parker, 
 
On January 13, 2025 the Somerset County Technical Advisory Committee met to garner comments 
on the proposed major subdivision of County owned land for the purpose of establishing a new 
County Industrial Park at their Revells Neck Rd property.  The following items were discussed by 
members at the meeting, and are summarized below, in no particular order.  Since these comments 
represent a summary of what was discussed, please contact the person listed for each item for 
further information as needed. 
 
Planning Department Comments: 
Contact: Jesse Drewer, jdrewer@somersetmd.us or Catherine Skeeter cskeeter@somersetmd.us 
(410) 651-1424 
• See marked up plat for comments in red. 
• Declaration of Intent for Forest Conservation must be completed. 
• Resubmit updated plat by January 27th to be brought to the Planning Commission for 
review at their February 6th meeting.  
 
Office of the County Engineer Comments (sent via email): 
Contact: John Redden, jredden@somersetmd.us (410) 621-9188 
• The existing site sign and wire fence/gate should be shown. Any existing utility or 
drainage easements should be shown. The proposed building should be labeled. 
• Construction plans for the proposed public roads must be approved by the county 
engineer and roads department. These roads must be constructed to county standards. 
• Stormwater management plans must be approved by the county engineer.  The standard 
review checklist shall be used. 
• A business owners association must be established to own and maintain the stormwater 
management facilities, and common areas. 
• Drainage and maintenance easements must be provided throughout the industrial park. 
• Common areas will be required for the stormwater management facilities, and industrial 
park sign. 
• The proposed lots shall have denied access to prison Road, which is a private road.
