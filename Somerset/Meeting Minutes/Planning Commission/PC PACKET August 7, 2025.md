**a. Public Hearing – Rezoning:** REZ-25-192 Theodore Miller 11105 Brownstone Road, (Tax Map 23, Grid 03, Parcel 002) 

Mr. Will Cornish read a summary of the Staff Report for the record: 

_“On April 4, 2025, the Department of Technical and Community Services received an application from Theodore Miller, requesting a rezoning of his property (Tax Map 23, Grid 3, Parcel 2) from Residential (‘R-2’) to Agricultural Residential (‘AR’) citing a mistake in the existing Zoning Classification. The parcel is approximately 72.47 Acres in size, is located near Princess Anne, on the southern side of Brownstone Rd. Mr. Miller has indicated that he would like to continue his farming operation by applying to the Maryland Agricultural Land Preservation Foundation (MALPF) for a conservation easement._ 

_This property was zoned R-2 during the County’s last comprehensive rezoning in 2019, having been originally rezoned as R-2 from A R in 2001. During the 2019 rezoning, the argument could be made that the parcel incorrectly identified as an opportunity for residential development, despite its agricultural nature and historical use. The 2019 rezoning wrongfully relied on outdated future land use maps from the_

Mr. Theodore Miller came up to speak on his request.  Mr. Webster asked what Mr. Miller believes was the mistake that could warrant a rezoning.  Mr. Miller shared the long agricultural history of the property, citing the size change from an original 300+ acres, and a potential development of the property falling through.  Mr. Miller stated that since his involvement with the property in the 60’s that it has always been agricultural.  Mr. Miller stated that his intent is to keep the property in agricultural nature in perpetuity, hopefully securing a MALPF easement offer.  Mr. Webster stated his concern for the lack of a clear mistake to justify the rezoning. 

Mr. Webster asked if there were any opponents or proponents present to speak.  One proponent was present to speak in favor of the rezoning request.  Neighboring property owner Mr. Gary King voiced his support. 

PC members and Staff further reviewed the zoning map to get an idea of the adjacent area zoning designations.  Mr. Webster asked if there could be a better argument for a rezoning once the Draft Comprehensive Plan is officially adopted.  Mr. Andrew Illuminati shared that the Staff argument for the rezoning is based on the idea that the 2019 comprehensive rezoning relied on a flawed methodology.  Mr. Illuminati stated that the Planning Commission will simply make a recommendation to the County Commissioners for the rezoning.  Also, the applicant claimed a mistake took place, but if the applicant is able to wait until the updated Comprehensive Plan is adopted, a change in character of the area could become the basis for the rezoning. 

Mr. Raab moved to close the Public Hearing, with a second from Mrs. Stewart.

Ms. Skeeter brought up the Tier 3 Water and Sewer service designation, based on the 2012 Tier map, without any movement toward service.  Mr. Drewer stated that a Tier 4 designation would be more likely if mapped now. 

Mr. Colborn inquired about the setbacks impacting the parcel.  Ms. Skeeter spoke about the Critical Area 100’ Buffer, extending from tidal influence.  Mr. Colborn asked if the MALPF program would encompass the entirety of the parcel – Ms. Skeeter confirmed that the whole parcel would be encumbered by a MALPF easement.

### **A.** **<u>BACKGROUND:</u>** 

The Department of Technical and Community Services received a Site Plan submittal from Dr. Howard Haft on April 11, 2025. The applicant is seeking Site Plan approval for the creation of a new Primary Care Medical Office. The Zoning Code requires site plan approval for all new commercial development, per section 8.15b(3). The Site Plan was reviewed at a Technical Advisory Committee (TAC) meeting on May 5, 2025.  A revised Site Plan was submitted on July 24, 2025 for Planning Commission consideration. 

### **B.** **<u>PROJECT OVERVIEW:</u>** 

The proposed Somerset Primary Care will be located on Crisfield Hwy, very near the city limits of Crisfield.  The lot is currently unimproved, having historically been used for the storage of boats, vehicles, and trailers.  The lot has preexisting impervious cover (gravel) over 37.6% of the lot.  The lot is within the Critical Area (Limited Development Area), which would typically only allow for 15% impervious area.  Due to the historic use, and the proposed new use leading to the reduction of impervious to only 24.4%, the exceeding of the typical 15% has been deemed permissible (P&Z Staff discussed at length with Critical Area Commission Staff).  Concerns for lot coverage have been taken into consideration on the revised Site Plan by reducing the sprawl of the building and parking area.  Also, revised plans reflect efforts to preserve the existing wooded areas - no tree clearance is proposed.  Additional improvements to the existing conditions will include Storm Water Management on-site and landscaping.  Additionally, the parcel is ideally located near the City, and adjacent to the Rt. 413 Hiker/Biker Trail.

### **C.** **<u>PROJECTS SPECIFICS:</u>** 

- a. Access: One access is proposed, in the relative location of the existing access point. 

- b. Signage: A proposed sign of 6’ in height is shown on the plan, with landscaping surrounding it. 

- c. Stormwater Management: Proposed SWM BMP area is shown on the plan – must undergo full review and approval from County Engineer. 

- d. Water and Sewer: Public water and sewer are available for this property – coordination with the County Sanitary District is ongoing. 

- e. General Landscaping: 15’ front and 10’ side/rear landscaped buffers required – appear to be sufficiently accommodated by the existing trees. 

- f. Parking and Parking Landscaping: The parking aisles, as proposed, consist of greater than 10 uninterrupted spaces.  These, per code, should be broken up by 6’ parking dividers – placing at least one within each aisle. 

- g. Dumpster: A proposed 10’ x 10’ screened dumpster pad is shown at the northern end of the parking lot. 

- h. Lighting: The location of lighting, whether on the building façade or along sidewalks, has not been addressed on the Site Plan. 

### **D.** **<u>DISCUSSION POINTS:</u>** 

- a. Parking Aisles: Segments of parking spaces over 10 must be broken up by 6’ wide parking dividers, to be either paved for pedestrian connectivity, or perhaps landscaped. The divider should be centralized within the aisle, and ideally provide connection to a sidewalk.  Dividers could be accommodated by losing 1 space from each aisle, leaving a total of 28 spaces, which is still greater than the minimum required 23. 

- b. Landscaping: No new landscaping is proposed along the long edge of the parking aisle adjacent to Rt. 413.  Though this area mostly falls under tree canopy, there is an opportunity for improved curb appeal, and parking screening, if this space were to be landscaped further with shrubs or understory plants. 

- c. Lighting should be addressed on the Site Plan. 

- d. Due to its location directly adjacent to the Rt. 413 Hiker/Biker Trail, a bike rack is recommended to accommodate greater accessibility for individuals not travelling via vehicle. 

### **E.** **<u>RECOMMENDATION/CONDIITIONS:</u>** 

- a. It is recommended that the Planning Commission consider approval of the proposed Site Plan, with the following conditions: 

   - All required revisions to the plat be confirmed prior to final approval and signatures 

   - Stormwater Management and Sediment and Erosion Control Approval shall be obtained. 

   - Additional recommendations as applied by the Planning Commission, be addressed on the Site Plan.

### **RULES OF PROCEDURE OF THE** 

### **SOMERSET COUNTY PLANNING AND ZONING COMMISSION** 

### **<u>Foreword</u>** 

These standard procedures are issued as a guide to assist the Somerset County Planning and Zoning Commission, its staff and other County agencies in the orderly and efficient conduct of all matter, which the Commission is concerned. 

### **<u>Objective</u>** 

The Somerset County Planning and Zoning Commission seeks to conduct all business, which may properly come before it by law or custom to secure the harmonious development of the entire County for the benefit of its present and future inhabitants. 

### **SECTION ONE** 

### **<u>Definitions</u>** 

The word “Commission” shall refer to the County Planning and Zoning Commission 

### **SECTION TWO** 

### **<u>Officers and Committees</u>** 

- 2.1 The Commission shall include five (5) citizens appointed by the Board of County Commissioners. In addition, the Commission shall contain one member appointed by the Board of Commissioners representing the City of Crisfield and one member appointed by the Board of County Commissioners representing the Town of Princess Anne. The Commission shall organize in the month of July. A Chairman and Vice Chairman shall be elected on an annual basis (see Section 2.5). The term of the members shall be five (5) years. The presiding officers may be re-elected for an additional one-year term upon receiving a majority vote from Commission members. 

- 2.2 The Chairman, or in his absence, the Vice Chairman, shall preside at all meetings and hearings of the Commission. The presiding officer shall decide on all points of order and procedure, subject to these rules, unless otherwise directed by a majority of the Commission members present. In the absences of both the Chairman and Vice Chairman, the executive secretary shall appoint a member of the Commission to serve as temporary Chairman who will be responsible for presiding over the meeting. 

- 2.3 Special committees may be appointed for special purposes or study.

### **SECTION THREE** 

### **<u>Meetings</u>** 

- 3.1 **<u>Regular Meetings</u>** shall be held on the first Thursday of each month. If additional time is needed, the following Thursday shall also be set aside as a regular meeting date. The staff shall give each member 48 hours or more notice of change of date of the meeting by mail or telephone. 

- 3.2 **<u>Special Meetings</u>** may be called by the Chairman or in his absence, the Vice Chairman. Notice of such meeting shall be given each member by mail or telephone 48 hours or more before said meeting. This does not apply to meetings requiring advertisement or other notice. 

- 3.3 **<u>Attendance</u>** It shall be the duty of each member to attend all meetings. Should any member be absent from one-half or more of all meetings held during the year, the Commission shall recommend to the Board of County Commissioners that the member’s resignation be requested, unless determined by vote of a majority of Commission members that there is a sufficient excuse for nonattendance. 

### **SECTION FOUR** 

### **<u>Voting</u>** 

- 4.1 A quorum shall consist of a majority of the existing membership. 

- 4.2 The order of business shall be in accordance with the agenda prepared by the staff in consultation with the Chairman. Members of the Commission who wish to add to the agenda shall first notify the Chairman of their request. 

- 4.3 Questions put to a vote are decided by a majority of the members present and voting, assuming a quorum is present. No decision shall be made in the absence of a quorum. Chairman shall vote at the same time as members. 

- 4.4 A tie vote by the Commission shall be interpreted as a defeat of the motion upon which the vote was taken.
