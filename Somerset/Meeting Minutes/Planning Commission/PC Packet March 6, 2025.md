onto Skipjack Circle.  Mr. Parker stated that other offices/agencies of concern (SHA, County 
Engineer for Stormwater Management, Sediment and Erosion Control) have reviewed the project 
and are ready to sign-off.  Mr. Parker also stated that the proposed initial structure will be a pole 
building.

Ms. Catherine Skeeter read a summary of the staff report for the record: 
 
“The Department of Technical and Community Services received a submittal from Mr. Brock Parker of Parker & 
Associates, Inc on December 19, 2024, on behalf of the Somerset County Commissioners. The applicant is seeking 
major subdivision approval for the creation of a new Somerset County Industrial Park, comprised of 20 lots for

A. BACKGROUND:  
 
The Department of Technical and Community Services received an application from 
Bohler Engineering on November 26, 2024, on behalf of the applicant, Nexamp Inc. The 
applicant is seeking Utility Scale Solar Energy Facility (SEF) Floating Zone Approval for 
property located at 10960 Kemps Nursery Road. Following the procedure set forth in the 
Somerset County Zoning Code section 7.7, an application is submitted to the Board of 
County Commissioners who, following a vote, refer the application to the Planning 
Commission. The request was initially heard by the County Commissioners on December 
3rd where it was denied. Following discussions over the next month with staff, the matter 
was brought back up to the County Commissioners for a referral recommendation on 
January 28, 2025, where it was forwarded to the Planning Commission for a public hearing 
and discussion. 
 
The Somerset County Zoning ordinance requires all solar energy facilities generating more 
than two (2) megawatts in electricity go through the SEF Floating Zone process. Pending 
a recommendation by the Planning Commission, a public hearing will be held by the 
County Commissioners on the request. If the floating zone is granted, the applicant would 
then be permitted to submit for the standard site plan review process by staff and the 
Planning Commission.

B. PROJECT OVERVIEW:  
 
The applicant proposes a solar energy facility that generates 5 megawatts of electricity. It 
is proposed to be 3 independent systems with 1 1MW and 2 2MW facilities. The proposed 
solar energy facility will be located on approximately 30 of the 136 acres that comprise the 
site. Located approximately 0.4 miles down Kemps Nursery Road, the property and 
surrounding area are primarily agricultural in nature. The property is zoned as Agricultural-
Residential and complies with the conditions outlined in section 7.5c of the zoning code 
[Staff Exhibit #1] which states; 
 
I. 
Utility scale solar energy facilities will only be considered on parcels 
designated as ‘Agricultural Residential’ (AR) or ‘General Industrial’ (I-2). 
II. 
Siting of utility scale solar energy facilities will not be considered for 
placement within 1,500 feet of the right-of-way for U.S. Route 13, Ocean 
Highway or M.D. Route 413, Crisfield Highway. 
III. 
Utility scale solar energy facilities may not be considered for parcels 
consisting of 50% or more prime agricultural soils as determined by the 
Natural Resources Conservation Service Soil Service Soil Survey as Class 
I, II, or III, and as further advised by the Somerset County Soil Conservation 
District. 
IV. 
Development Cap. No more than 1,800 acres of utility scale solar will be 
considered within the County as defined by the limits of development for 
each utility scale solar energy facility developed or proposed. 
 
It should be noted that the above condition III, after consulting with legal counsel, is not 
explicitly met, as the site contains greater than 50% prime agricultural soils. Per the 
Somerset County Soil Conservation District [Staff Exhibit #2], the site is fully comprised 
of Class I-III Soils.  
 
Section 7.5(A), which is the statement of intent for the SEF states, “it is intended that utility 
scale solar energy facilities are not placed on prime agricultural lands, are aesthetically 
attractive, are placed so as to protect the commercial viability of the U.S. Route 13 and 
M.D. Route 413 corridors, and are compatible with the surrounding neighborhood.” The 
Commission must determine whether, in light of the prime soils analysis, a 
recommendation for an SEF is appropriate based on the conceptual site plan. [Applicant 
Exhibit #2].

7.5  Utility Scale Solar Energy Facility Floating Zone (SEF) 
a.
Statement of Intent.
The purpose of the utility scale solar floating zone is to allow for the orderly development of
utility scale solar energy facilities typically generating more than two (2) megawatts in
electricity (AC) that are appropriately sited and sized. Furthermore, it is intended that utility
scale solar energy facilities are not placed on prime agricultural lands, are aesthetically
attractive, are placed so as to protect the commercial viability of the U.S. Route 13 and M.D.
Route 413 corridors, and are compatible with the surrounding neighborhood.
b. Application for Floating Zone.
Refer to Section 7.7 ‘Procedure for Floating Zone District Approval.’
c.
Applicability.
(1) Utility scale solar energy facilities will only be considered on parcels designated as
‘Agricultural Residential’ (AR) or ‘General Industrial’ (I-2).
(2) Siting of utility scale solar energy facilities will not be considered for placement within
1,500 feet of the right-of-way for U.S. Route 13, Ocean Highway or M.D. Route 413,
Crisfield Highway.
(3) Utility scale solar energy facilities may not be considered for parcels consisting of 50%
or more prime agricultural soils as determined by the Natural Resources Conservation
Service Soil Service Soil Survey as Class I, II, or III, and as further advised by the
Somerset County Soil Conservation District.
(4) Development Cap. No more than 1,800 acres of utility scale solar will be considered
within the County as defined by the limits of development for each utility scale solar
energy facility developed or proposed.

(1) Visual Shield.  
i. 
A full visual shield must be provided on all sides of the solar energy facility

of vegetation, fencing, berms, or other materials may be used in accomplishing

i.

i. 
Low growth grass consisting of native and/or salt tolerant species is required.

parcel developed with a utility scale solar energy facility.

i. 
If landscaping is proposed to meet the visual shield standard, solar energy facility 
owners are encouraged to plant at the beginning of construction. Care should be 
given to the timing and placement of vegetation on-site.   
ii. 
No more than 50% non-native species may be used.

Map Unit Description (Brief, Generated)
The map units delineated on the detailed soil maps in a soil survey represent the 
soils or miscellaneous areas in the survey area. The map unit descriptions in this 
report, along with the maps, provide information on the composition of map units 
and properties of their components.
A map unit delineation on a soil map represents an area dominated by one or 
more major kinds of soil or miscellaneous areas. A map unit is identified and 
named according to the taxonomic classification of the dominant soils. Within a 
taxonomic class there are precisely defined limits for the properties of the soils. 
On the landscape, however, the soils are natural phenomena, and they have the 
characteristic variability of all natural phenomena. Thus, the range of some 
observed properties may extend beyond the limits defined for a taxonomic class. 
Areas of soils of a single taxonomic class rarely, if ever, can be mapped without 
including areas of other taxonomic classes. Consequently, every map unit is 
made up of the soils or miscellaneous areas for which it is named and some 
minor components that belong to taxonomic classes other than those of the 
major soils.
The Map Unit Description (Brief, Generated) report displays a generated 
description of the major soils that occur in a map unit. Descriptions of non-soil 
(miscellaneous areas) and minor map unit components are not included. This 
description is generated from the underlying soil attribute data.
Additional information about the map units described in this report is available in 
other Soil Data Mart reports, which give properties of the soils and the limitations, 
capabilities, and potentials for many uses. Also, the narratives that accompany 
the Soil Data Mart reports define some of the properties included in the map unit 
descriptions.
