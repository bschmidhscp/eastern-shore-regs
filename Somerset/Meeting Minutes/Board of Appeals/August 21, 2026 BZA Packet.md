### **Board of Zoning Appeals** 

**Special Exception for Tull Farm Holdings, LLC** 

#### **<u>DECISION AND FINDINGS OF FACT</u>** 

Tull Farm Holdings, LLC, (“Applicant”) is the owner of certain real property identified as Somerset County Tax Map 0004, Grid 0017, Parcel 30, Lot C (+/- 69.32 acres) and Lot A (+/- 66.15 acres), ID # 01-032615 (the “Property”), located at 9773 Stewart Neck Road in Somerset County, Maryland. The Property is located within Somerset County’s Agricultural Residential (“AR”) Zoning District. The Applicant seeks to establish a surface mine/ borrow pit area on approximately +/- 33.7 acres. The property is located on the north side of Polks Road approximately 4,380 feet from the intersection with Allen Road and 1,800 feet south from the intersection of U.S. Route 13 and Old Princess Anne Road. The location of the proposed borrow pit is shown as approximately 1,500 feet from Polks Road, 25 feet from adjacent side property lines and 100 feet from tidal wetlands in the rear. A special exception for surface mining is required per Section 5.7(e)(13) of the Somerset County Zoning Ordinance. 

On June 26, 2026, the Somerset County Board of Zoning Appeals (“Board”) held a hearing on the special exception request. The Board received evidence, both testimonial and documentary, from the Applicant, the County and a number of individuals who spoke in opposition to the special exception request<sup>1</sup> . The evidence presented to the Board centered around the eight criteria set forth in the Somerset County Zoning Ordinance Section 11.2(a). Those criteria are: 

> `1` The Board finds the testimony of the Applicant’s witnesses and the County to be reliable and credible. The exhibits received as part of the hearing are relied upon, without citation, in this Decision and Findings of Fact.

<!-- page 8 -->
- _(1) The proposed use conforms in all respects to minimum requirements of the district in which it is located;_ 

- _(2) The proposed use will not adversely affect the health, safety, and general welfare of residents of the area;_ 

- _(3) The proposed use will not interfere with the adequate and orderly provision of public facilities necessary to service the area or the proposed Special Exceptions;_ 

- _(4) The proposed use will not create congestion in the road or undue traffic hazards and that adequate ingress and egress is provided;_ 

- _(5) The proposed use will not adversely affect the area and surrounding property due to adverse environmental characteristics including undue smoke, odor, noise, improper drainage inadequate access;_ 

- _(6) The proposed use will not adversely affect the established character of the area; and_ 

- _(7) The proposed use is consistent with the guidelines established for such uses in the County’s Critical Area Program, where applicable._ 

- _(8) The proposed use is consistent with the Comprehensive Plan or a local Area Plan adopted by the Board of County Commissioners._ 

After receiving the evidence, the Board voted to continue discussion until the next 

meeting. 

On July 17, 2026, the Board unanimously voted to approve the special exception and directed legal counsel to prepare a Finding of Fact. Based on the evidence received, the Board finds that the Applicant’s request satisfies the criteria for a special exception. Applying the criteria, the Board finds: 

(1) _The proposed use conforms in all respects to minimum requirements of the district in which it is located._ The Property is located within an unincorporated area of Somerset County. The AR Zoning District is predominantly agricultural in nature. The primary land use in this Zoning 

2

<!-- page 9 -->
District is agriculture, forestry and related activities. These resource based industries must locate near their source of raw material. There are multiple borrow pits within the immediate vicinity. The Board finds the Applicant has satisfied this provision. 

_(2) The proposed use will not adversely affect the health, safety, and general welfare of residents of the area._ Within the immediate vicinity of the Property there is one residential structure to the west of the proposed borrow pit. That structure is 600 feet from the property line and is the closest structure to the proposed borrow pit. There are two residential lots on the easterly side of the abutting properties. The Applicant articulated how he will address dust due to truck traffic on the haul road. The Applicant articulates that he will water the road as necessary to minimize any disturbance. Additionally, the general area has heavy truck traffic due to the surrounding borrow pits and poultry farms. There is no evidence that an additional borrow pit will adversely affect the health, safety, or general welfare of the residents in the area. The Board finds the Applicant has satisfied this provision. 

_(3) The proposed use will not interfere with the adequate and orderly provision of public facilities necessary to service the area or the proposed Special Exceptions._ This area does not have access to public water or sewer. Further, there is no belief that a borrow pit will cause the need for police to respond. As a result, there is zero burden on public facilities by allowing for a borrow pit. The Board finds the Applicant has satisfied this provision. 

_(4) The proposed use will not create congestion in the road or undue traffic hazards and that adequate ingress and egress is provided._ Polks Road, off which the proposed borrow pit will be located, is an improved County road. It is approximately a half mile from Allen Road, which is also an improved County road. The area is rural in nature and there is no pedestrian traffic. 

3

<!-- page 10 -->
The addition of a borrow pit will not create congestion or undue traffic hazards. Based on the Applicant’s site plan, the Board finds there is adequate ingress and egress for the proposed borrow pit. The Board finds the Applicant has satisfied this provision. 

_(5) The proposed use will not adversely affect the area and surrounding property due to adverse environmental characteristics including undue smoke, odor, noise, improper drainage inadequate access._ The area is already populated with multiple borrow pits. Borrow pits do not create smoke or odor. To the extent that there is noise, the Board has the authority to limit hours of operation. There is no evidence that the borrow pit will create a drainage issue. Further, two thirds of the remaining property will be undeveloped. The Board finds the Applicant has satisfied this provision. 

_(6) The proposed use will not adversely affect the established character of the area._ As previously mentioned, the character of the area is rural in nature. There are multiple borrow pits in the immediate vicinity, less than a mile to a mile and a half away. The character of the area is well suited for borrow pits. Previously, the Property had a borrow pit which was deactivated and the land was successfully restored to its original state. The Board finds the Applicant has satisfied this provision. 

_(7) The proposed use is consistent with the guidelines established for such uses in the County’s Critical Area Program, where applicable._ Somerset County Critical Area Ordinance, Section 1-122(E), governs surface mining in the critical area. Section 1-122(E) provides that “surface mining is permitted in agricultural districts and only on sites of five or more acres.” Further, Section 1-122(E) provides that “proposed new surface mining sites or expanding operations of existing sites shall comply with additional criteria.” The additional criteria are:

**_1._** _New surface mining operations are permitted within the Critical Area provided that identification of appropriate post-excavation uses for this land such as recreation, habitat restoration, open space use, or development are accomplished according to the appropriate land management classification (IDA, LDA or RCA) and other applicable County and State codes and ordinances._ 

**_2._** _Areas such as the following shall not be used for surface mining:_ 

- **_a._** _Habitat Protection Areas and other important natural resource areas such as those of scientific value or areas where assemblages of rare species occur;_ **_b._** _Areas where highly erodible soils exist;_ 

**_c._** _Areas where the use of renewable resource lands would result in the substantial loss of long-range (25 years or more) productivity of forest and agriculture, or would result in a degrading of water quality or a loss of vital habitat;_ 

   - **_d._** _Lands that are within 100 feet of the mean high water line of tidal waters, tidal wetlands, or the edge of streams._ 

**_3._** _Surface mining operations shall operate under the following conditions:_ 

   - **_a._** _Future wash plants including ponds, spoil piles, and equipment may not be located within the Buffer as defined in this Ordinance;_ 

   - **_b._** _Existing wash ponds shall be reclaimed as soon as possible after the cessation of a sand and gravel operation;_ 

**_c._** _To the fullest extent possible, existing sand and gravel operations shall conduct their extraction activities so as to provide, at a minimum 100-foot Buffer of natural vegetation between the operation and the mean high water line of tidal waters or the edges of streams and tidal wetlands, whichever is further inland._ 

The Board in reviewing the criteria for future borrow pits, finds that 

the Applicant has satisfied the criteria of Section 1-122(E) of the Critical Area 

5

<!-- page 12 -->
Ordinance. Specifically, the Board finds that the Property is not a designated habitat protection area or a similar important natural resource area such as those of scientific value or areas where assemblages of rare species occur. There is no evidence that the Property has highly erodible soils. There is no evidence that there would be a substantial loss of long range productivity or forest and agricultural use, or result in degrading of water quality or loss of a vital habitat if a borrow pit was operational on the Property. Finally, there is no evidence that the land is within 100 feet of the mean high water line of tidal wetland or the edges of streams. For these reasons, the Board finds that the Applicant has satisfied this provision. 

_(8) The proposed use is consistent with the Comprehensive Plan or a local Area Plan adopted by the Board of County Commissioners._ On September 23, 2025, by Resolution No. 1214, the Board of County Commissioners for Somerset County, Maryland, adopted the 2025 Somerset County Comprehensive Plan.<sup>2</sup> The parcel is not located within the Primary Growth Area as enumerated on Map 10-12<sup>3</sup> of the Comprehensive Plan. The Board finds the Applicant has satisfied this provision as the proposed use is not inconsistent with the Comprehensive Plan. 

In accordance with Section 11.2(d) of the Zoning Ordinance, the Board of Appeals finds that it is empowered to grant the special exception, and that granting the special exception will not adversely affect the public health, safety, security, morals, or general welfare, or would result in dangerous traffic conditions, or would jeopardize the lives or property of the people living in the neighborhood.

FOR THESE REASONS, a special exception for the Property, in accordance with the special exception application on the real property identified as Somerset County Tax Map 0004, Grid 0017, Parcel 30, Lot C and Lot A, ID # 01-032615, located at 9773 Stewart Neck Road in Somerset County, Maryland, is hereby GRANTED. 

FURTHER, in accordance with Section 11.2(d)(1), in granting the special exception the Board prescribes the following appropriate conditions and safe guards to protect the community:

The land shall be restored to a safe and usable condition, as approved by the Department of Technical and Community Services, upon closure of the borrow pit;**
