June 26, 2026 

The following are the minutes of the Somerset County Board of Zoning Appeals meeting held on the 26<sup>th</sup> day of June 2026 at 7:30 PM in the meeting room of the Somerset County Commissioners, Room 111, in the Somerset County Office Complex, 11916 Somerset Avenue, Princess Anne, Maryland. 

**<u>Board of Appeals Members Present: Somerset County DTCS Staff Present:</u>** Isaac Ballard, Acting-Chairman Jesse Drewer, Director Paul Emely Will Cornish, Assistant Director and Zoning Administrator William Cottman Donna Cannon, Administrative Assistant III Andrew Baines Catherine Skeeter, Senior Planner – by Phone 

**<u>Board of Appeals Legal Counsel Present</u>** Andrew Illuminati, Esq. 

Vice Chair Ballard opened the Meeting at 7:30 P.M. 

Chairman Ballard called the meeting to order and gave an explanation on how the meeting will proceed. 

Chairman Laird asked if there were any changes to the minutes from the April 17<sup>th</sup> , meeting. there being none Mr. Ballard asked for a vote to approve the meeting minutes. The motion was made by Mr. Cottman and seconded by Mr. Baines 

**<u>Subject of Appeal:</u>** SE 2026-2556 Tull Farms Holdings LLC Polks Road 

**VAR 26-1066:** The applicant is seeking the establishment of a Borrow pit/ Surface mine on tax map 4, grid 17, parcel 30, lot A & C on Polks Rd. 

**Testimony:** Mr. Drewer read the ad into the record. Richard Douglass Reynolds of 2611 Plover Drive unit 301 Ocean City Maryland & James Thomas Nelson III of 28311 Revells Neck Rd Westover Maryland came forward and were sworn in. 

**_11916 Somerset Avenue, Suite 211 • Princess Anne, Maryland 21853_** Telephone (410) 651-1424 • Fax (410) 651-2597 Email: dtcs@somersetmd.us

Mr. Illuminati also had all who wished to speak at the hearing sworn in ahead of time to expediate the process. 

Chairman Ballard asked the applicant to explain their request. 

- Mr. Nelson began his testimony by explaining that he simply wished to move the dirt to help with his irrigation and make a bit of money on the side through the pit. Mr. Reynolds provided a copy of a map of the proposed site. The map was made Staff exhibit one. It was noted that the exhibit was already contained within the provided County Staff report. 

- Mr. Ballard inquired about the existing stakes on the property and whether they indicated where the applicants wished to dig the pit. Mr. Reynolds confirmed this was indeed the case. 

- Mr. Cottman asked if moving the dirt was primarily for irrigation and landfill. Mr. nelson confirmed this was indeed the case. 

- Mr. Illuminati noted the 8 requirements needed for a Special exception to be approved, and inquired if the applicants could address how they met them. 

   - (1) The proposed use conforms in all respects to minimum requirements of the district in which it is located 

   - (2) The proposed use will not adversely affect the health, safety, and general welfare of residents of the area 

The applicants pointed out there were numerous borrow pits already in the area 

- (3) The proposed use will not interfere with the adequate and orderly provision of public facilities necessary to service the area or the proposed special exception 

The applicants claimed that hours would typically be 7 to 5 Monday to Friday, but during emergencies the may have to operate outside of the proposed hours. Mr. Reynolds gave the example of a water main break at 10 pm. 

- (4) The proposed use will not create congestion in the road or undue traffic hazards and that adequate ingress and egress is provided 

The applicants claimed that Polks Rd would be adequate for the increased traffic. 

- (5) The proposed use will not adversely affect the area and surrounding property due to adverse environmental characteristics including undue smoke, odor, noise, improper drainage inadequate access; 

The applicants pointed out there were numerous borrow pits already in the area.

- (6) The proposed use will not adversely affect the established character of the area; 

The applicants pointed out there were numerous borrow pits already in the area 

- (7) The proposed use is consistent with the guidelines established for such uses in the County’s Critical Area Program, where applicable 

Burrow pits are allowed in the critical area as long as state standards are met. The applicant noted that even after they received the boards approval they would be required to receive the state permits, and at that time state standards would be addressed. 

- (8) The proposed use is consistent with the Comprehensive Plan or a local Area Plan adopted by the Board of County Commissioners. 

Mr. Nelson noted that he is a farmer and his main goal is irrigation and he is intending to place the rest of the property into an agricultural preservation program. 

Mr. Cornish read the staff report into the record. The staff report was made staff exhibit two. 

- Mr. Drewer read the letter from the State of Maryland Critical Area Commission into the Record this was made staff exhibit two. One of the matters the state expressed concern over was water access. The Applicants noted that they would not be seeking any water access with their application. 

- Mr. Drewer also noted that an amendment to the staff report would have to be made as a 200-foot buffer from the mean high-tide water line would have to be established per the Critical Area Commission. 

- Mr. Drewer than read a letter from the Chesapeake Bay Foundation into the record. The letter expressed their opposition to the project on environmental grounds and was made opposition exhibit one. 

- Mr. Cornish read a personal letter from Ms. Luis Harman of 1 Cumberland court Annapolis Maryland into the record. The letter expressed opposition on historical grounds and was made opposition Exhibit two. 

- Chairman Ballard asked if there was anyone in attendance who wanted to speak in support of the request. 

No one came forward. 

- Chairman Laird asked if there was anyone in attendance who wanted to speak in opposition of the request.

- Alex Votaw of the Law office of G. Macy Nelson came forward and questioned whether surface mining was an appropriate use in the RCA, and brought up the applicants lack of details in the application. She pointed out that there was a lot of information relevant to the neighbors of the property that were not addressed by the applicants. 

- After that Ms. Votaw began to call the opposition up one by one. The first to speak was Mr. Carl Petty of 30230 Polks Rd. 

- Mr. Petty presented an aerial of his property confirming his home was roughly 300’ from the projects property line, this was made Opposition Exhibit 4. 

- MS Votaw presented a map showing the property was zoned AR-Agricultural Residential, this was made Opposition Exhibit 5. 

- Mr. Petty had concerns about the silica dust from the project causing health risks. He also had concerns about the numerous hard to see around sharp turns on Polks road that had the potential to be dangerous with dump trucks on them. opposition exhibit 6 was then added to the record depicting how hard the turns were to see around 

- Mr. Petty continued that he also had concerns about the noise and dust from the project. He also pointed out the no one knew the exact size of the project as the applicants said they wanted a 5- acre pit but were asking for nearly 40 acres with the project. 

- He was also even more concerned with the noise if the applicant were to operate at 10 P.M. as Mr. Reynolds had provided an example of. 

- He also expressed concerns over the depth of the pit as if it was below the water table he worried that wells. Could run dry. 

- Ms. Votaw then added Opposition exhibit seven showing the parcel was in ground water management area 1. 

- Mr. Petty then listed several environmental and historical reasons he disapproved of the pit. Including the historic ownership of the parcel by a Mr. Horsey who had significant history in Somerset county. - Mr. petty particularly took issue to what he saw as a violation of the Resource Conservation Area as according to him there was a habitat protection area near the pit. As there were several bald eagle nests in the immediate vicinity. Exhibit eight included an aerial depicting bald eagle nests on the property. 

- Opposition exhibit nine was then presented that depicts bald eagle nest on the subject property in what the applicant considered the general vicinity of the property, 

- Opposition exhibits ten and eleven depicted different angles of various eagle nests on the property.

- a. There is a substantial landscape buffer between their proposed pit and Mr. Petty’s property and they intend to extend said buffer for the rest of the length of the project. 

- b. MDE regulates dusts and a water truck is required by state law if the operation is substantial enough 

- c. 90% of the dirt will be leaving the property and immediately heading to Allen road to reach the highway. If the trucks are heading to Allen road they will only have to navigate one turn to get there. The only time the trucks should be traveling the length of Polks road in the opposite direction is if someone on Polks road orders dirt. And a dump truck would have to bring the dirt to them regardless of where the pit was located. 

- d. Water does not sit perfectly laterally under the ground, it pools in certain places. The reason ponds and such went dry in the past was because at those times they just dug the cheapest pits possible without modern technology, also the size of the well and its depth determine if a well runs dry, not the nearness of borrow pits. 

- e. According to Mr. Reynolds there were two pits dug on the farm in the past in response to those who oppose the pit on historic grounds. 

- f. The water quality is regulated by the state, and he is not allowed to let water flow from his pit into the adjacent water in the first place. 

- g. Mr. Reynolds claim that the eagles enjoy his existing pits as they are filled with water and make nice hunting grounds.

- i. The Resource Conservation area is regulated by the state

- k. They admit they do not have a timeline for the lifetime of the mines operations as it will all depend on economic factors.
