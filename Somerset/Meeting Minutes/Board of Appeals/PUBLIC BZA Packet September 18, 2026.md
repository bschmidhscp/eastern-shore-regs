###### **RULES OF PROCEDURE SOMERSET COUNTY BOARD OF APPEALS** 

###### **I. Rules of General Application** 

###### **Section 1. References, Definitions.** 

- A. Administrative Agency. References in these rules to an “Administrative Agency” do not include the Board. 

- B. Appeal on the Record. An appeal from an administrative agency, board or commission, which is based on the record created by the initial agency's determination. 

- C. Appellant. A party who seeks review of an administrative agency decision through the Board. 

- D. Applicant. A party who files an application requesting certain relief which is in the powers of the Board to determine. 

- E. Board. The Board of Appeals of Somerset County is hereinafter referred to as “the Board.” 

- F. Board Members. Members including alternate members of the Board of Appeals of Somerset County. References to “Board Members” shall include alternate members. 

- G. Convene. A hearing is “convened” for the purposes of these Rules when the Chair opens the hearing and begins to administer the Chair’s announcements. 

- H. De Novo Appeal. A determination by the Board, based on evidence presented for the first time before them, of an application requesting certain relief from or modification of existing zoning regulations. 

- I. Identification. For the purposes of these Rules, to become identified in a particular matter means to sign the sign-in form provided by the Board or administrative agency at the time the hearing is convened or to identify themselves before the Board or administrative agency prior to testifying in connection with the particular matter before the Board or administrative agency. 

- J. Interested Party. For the purposes of these rules, any abutting property owner and all adjacent property owners within two hundred (200) feet of the subject property’s property line can file to be an interested party. In filing with the County, the interested party shall make specific reference to the matter in which the person is interested and shall provide the person’s name and mailing address. Interested party (“party/parties”) in appeals on the record refers to 1) the appellant; 2) the administrative agency from which the appeal is taken; or 3) any other person in interest that, no later than thirty days after the appeal is filed, notifies the Secretary in writing that he/she/it desires to be made a party. Interested party (“party” or “parties”) with regards to appeals de novo means the applicant and every person identified as an interested party. 

- K. Person. The term “Person” refers to a natural person or artificial entity, including a corporation,

partnership, unincorporated association, civic or citizen’s organization or trust. 

- L. Person Requesting Notice. A “person requesting notice” means any person, not a party, who makes a written request to the Secretary for notice of the date, time and place of a public hearing. ###### **Section 10. Conduct of Hearings.** 

- A. Order and Rules of Procedure. After opening announcements by the Chair, the Board may act on minutes of the previous meeting(s). Each party and witnesses will be identified and the pre-published notice as well as the Staff Report will be entered into the record. A reasonable time limit for the presentation of evidence, testimony (oral or written) and/or argument of each case may be established by the Chair as agreed upon by the Board. 

- B. Opening statements are an introduction to the case proposed to be presented. Therein, the party may give a brief overview of the evidence they intend to present and the issues they intend to prove. Closing statements are a summary and finalized argument of what has been presented and how the evidence supports their claim(s). Ordinarily, only the applicant/appellant and one (1) party opponent shall be permitted to make an opening and closing statement. The Board, in its discretion, may permit more than one party opponent to make an opening and closing statement. Opening and closing statements are not evidence and no evidence shall be introduced during opening and closing statements. 

- C. Testimony. Once a party has completed their testimony or direct or cross examination, they will not be granted leave to give additional testimony or to conduct additional direct examination except that the applicant may testify and offer witnesses and evidence, in rebuttal. The Chair has discretion to permit additional testimony, or re-direct or re-cross examination in extraordinary circumstances. To cross-examine a witness, a party must request and be granted permission from the Chair.

- E. Burden of Proof. The Burden of Proof is on the applicant to establish that the applicant is entitled to the relief requested.

Failure to appear for a scheduled hearing at the time scheduled in the Board’s agenda for that meeting will result in the matter being dismissed and all relevant fees paid shall be forfeited. ###### **Section 11. Evidence.** 

- A. Admissibility. The Board will conduct hearings in a manner best calculated to afford all parties an opportunity to present their positions and to serve the ends of justice and fairness. Any evidence which would be admissible under the rules of evidence applicable in judicial proceedings in the State of Maryland shall be admissible in hearings before the Board. 

- B. Technical Rules of Evidence Not Applicable. The Board will not be bound by the technical rules of evidence but will apply such rules to the end that substantive evidence may be conveniently provided. The Board may exclude immaterial or unduly repetitious evidence, and may limit the number of witnesses when testimony is cumulative in nature. 

- C. Expert Witnesses. The Board will not recognize “expert witnesses" but will hear witnesses as may be compelled by the Chair and give weight to their testimony as the Board deems appropriate. An expert opinion, alone, may be insufficient evidence to support an error in zoning. Nothing contained in this Rule is intended to prohibit any person from expressing an opinion on any matter about which he or she may testify. 

- D. Administrative Notice. All public records, previous Board cases, public and local laws and regulations of Somerset County which pertain to the particular case may be received by reference by the Board for its consideration.

   - (1) Any interested party or the applicant may request that a subpoena be issued.

   - (5) All requests for subpoenas shall clearly state the name and address of the party being subpoenaed, the case in which the subpoena applies, and the purpose of the subpoena for either requiring a witness to testify or to deliver documents for the record of the Board.

   - (1) A statement of the grounds and findings of fact forming the basis for the decision, and the final order of the Board.

   - (3) Approval by the affirmative vote of a majority of those members present.

   - (4) The agency shall notify the appellant in writing upon completion of the transcript. (5) Within ten (10) days after the appellant is sent notice of the completion of the transcript, the appellant shall pay the balance of the cost of the transcript. 

- B. Transmission and Copies. Upon receipt of the cost of preparation of the transcript, the agency shall transmit to the Secretary the record of the administrative agency proceeding, including the transcript, and shall mail to the appellant and all parties a copy of the transcript. The administrative agency determines what should be included in the record. 

- C. Time for Transmission. The record, including the transcript, shall be transmitted to the Secretary within sixty (60) days after the appeal is noted unless an extension is granted by the Board for due cause. If the record is not transmitted to the Secretary within sixty (60) days after the appeal is noted because of the appellant’s failure to promptly request, or pay for, the transcript, the appeal shall be dismissed. 

###### **Section 4. Memoranda - Appeal from an Administrative Agency Decision.** 

- A. Memorandum of Appellant and Parties Supporting Appellant. In an appeal on the record from an action of an administrative agency, the appellant and each party supporting the appellant, shall file with the Secretary a memorandum within fifteen (15) days of the record and transcript being completed. The memorandum should also be filed in the proceedings and served upon the administrative agency at that time. 

- B. Memorandum of Agency and Party Opponents. Within fifteen (15) days after the filing of the memorandum by the appellant and any party supporting the appellant, each party opponent, shall file a reply memorandum. 

- C. Content of Memoranda. Each memorandum shall set forth a concise statement of all points on which the appeal is based, with an argument, including the following, in support of each point: 

   - (1) Procedural History; 

   - (2) Facts of Case; 

   - (3) Points of Law; 

   - (4) References to legal authority;

   - (6) Exhibits by number. 

- D. Length of Memoranda. A Memorandum shall not exceed ten (10) pages in length exclusive of exhibits. 

- E. Service of Memoranda. A party filing a memorandum shall, at the time the memorandum is filed, mail to all other parties a copy of the memorandum and shall attach to the memorandum an affidavit or certificate of mailing. 

###### **Section 5. Hearing.** 

- A. Notice of Hearing. At least fifteen (15) days before the Board hearing to consider an appeal on the record, the Secretary will mail to all parties notice of the date, time and place of the hearing. The notice shall give the name and address of each party to the appeal. 

- B. Argument. The Board will entertain oral argument based on the record and memoranda of the parties. The Board may reasonably limit the amount of time for each speaker testifying at the hearing. 

- C. Effect of Failure to File Memorandum. A party that has failed to file a memorandum shall not be permitted to present argument to the Board. 

- D. Order of Presentation at Hearing. The order of presentation shall be governed by the following: 

   - (1) Presentation of staff report; 

   - (2) Presentation of administrative agency; 

   - (3) Presentation by appellant; 

   - (4) Presentation by parties supporting the appellant; 

   - (5) Presentation by party opponents. 

- E. Burden of Proof. The appellant shall have the burden of proving that the decision of the administrative agency was clearly erroneous, arbitrary, capricious and/or contrary to law. 

- F. Disposition. The Board may affirm, modify or reverse the decision of the administrative agency or may remand the matter to the administrative agency with directions. 

- G. Decision. The Decision of the Board shall conform to Part I, Section 5, (Quorum and Vote Requirement) of these Rules of Procedure in lieu of making its own findings or fact, the Board may adopt the findings of fact of the administrative agency.
