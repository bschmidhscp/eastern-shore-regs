- **A.** Chapter 1-115 of the County’s Critical Area Ordinance addresses variances to the requirements contained in the County’s Critical Area Program (which includes the Critical Area Ordinance and the County’s Critical Area Maps). 

   - **a.** Subsection ‘B’ of Chapter 1-115 contains ten standards that must be met to justify the granting of a variance by either the Director of DTCS (in the case of Administrative Variances) or by the County’s Board of Zoning Appeals (in the case of appeals to decisions made by the Director).  The Applicant has the burden of proof when making a justification for the granting of a variance.  They are as follows: 

- **(1) Due to special features of the site or special conditions or circumstances peculiar to the applicant’s land or structure, a literal enforcement of the Somerset County Critical Area program would result in an unwarranted hardship to the applicant;** 

_A literal enforcement of the program would_ **_<u>not</u>_** _lead to an unwarranted hardship upon the applicant, because the prior permitted structures allowed for adequate utilization of the property._ 

- **(2) A literal interpretation of the Somerset County Critical Area program would deprive the applicant of a use of land or a structure permitted to others in accordance with the provisions of this Ordinance;** 

_The proposed use and increase of impervious in the Buffer would_ **_<u>not</u>_** _be permitted on any RCA lot._ 

- **(3) The granting of the variance would not confer upon the applicant any special privilege that would be denied by this Ordinance to other lands or structures in accordance with the provisions of the Somerset County Critical Area program;** 

_Granting of the variance would confer special privilege that would otherwise not be granted to others._ 

### **(4) The variance request is not based upon conditions or circumstances that are the result of actions by the applicant;** 

_The variance request is based upon the requested expansion of impervious area within the Buffer. The footprint of the previously approved structures would be allowed to be replaced in-kind._

### **SOMERSET COUNTY ADMINISTRATIVE VARIANCE REQUEST DEPARTMENT OF TECHNICAL AND COMMUNITY SERVICES PRINCESS ANNE, MARYLAND** 

### **Public Hearing of May 15, 2025 with Continuance Hearing on May 27, 2025 for final decision** 

**APPLICANT: Island Sunset, Inc c/o Frank Parks of Fisher of Men Foundation CASE #: CA VAR 25-016** 

**REQUEST: Critical Area Buffer Variance – Encroachment into the Critical Area 100’ Buffer for the construction of a “dwelling,” associated decking, and a shed** 

**LOCATION: 11190 Roland Parks Rd, Deal Island, MD 21821 [Tax Map 20, Grid 2, Parcel 25]** 

Upon the above Application for a Critical Area Buffer Variance relating to the property described therein, located on Tax Map 20, Grid 2, Parcel 25, the Director of the Somerset County Department of Technical and Community Services (DTCS) held a Public Hearing on May 15, 2025, and considered the Application and the testimony and other evidence presented at the Hearing in reaching a decision on May 27, 2025 during a Continuance Hearing.  The following is a summary of all evidence presented and of the Director’s findings. 

### **<u>HISTORY OF THE CASE</u>** 

The Applicants’ property recently experienced a fire which destroyed a preexisting dwelling, shed, storage building, and associated decking.  The applicant is seeking approval to rebuild in an expanded manner.  The lot is impacted by the 100’ Buffer from all directions. The property is 20 acres in size (±871,200 s.f.), is zoned CO Conservation, and is located within the Resource Conservation Area (RCA) of the Critical Area. The parcel contains substantial natural vegetation. 

The proposed lot coverage for the entire property (discounting the area of decks) is 4,464 s.f. (0.51%) of which 3,863 s.f. is within the 100’ Buffer (though the accuracy of the buffer delineation is under question based on the comment letter from the Critical Area Commission). Of the total lot coverage, 629 s.f. will be _new_ lot coverage placed within the 100’ Buffer. Outside of the Buffer 601 s.f. is proposed. 

The Applicant applied for an Administrative Variance under the requirements of Section 1- 115D of the County’s Critical Area Ordinance, as a variance would be needed for the encroachment into the Buffer. 

The Critical Area Commission (CAC) was notified of this Application on April 16, 2025, and provided a comment letter dated May 13, 2025.

### **<u>FINDINGS OF FACT</u>** 

The Director, after due consideration, does hereby make the following Findings of Fact and Decision in this matter in accordance with the provisions of the Somerset County Critical Area Ordinance.  The Director reviewed the criteria for the granting of a Variance and found that Approval was not warranted in this situation. The Director finds, from a preponderance of the evidence presented at the Public Hearing, that the Applicant has not met the burden of proof to warrant an encroachment into the 100’ Buffer in accordance with the provisions of Chapter 1-115B of the Somerset County Critical Area Ordinance, as more fully described below. 

**Code Criteria for Administrative Variances – Chapter 1-115.B of the Somerset County Critical Area Ordinance:** Chapter 1-115 of the County’s Critical Area Ordinance addresses variances to the requirements contained in the County’s Critical Area Program (which includes the Critical Area Ordinance and the County’s Critical Area Maps).  Chapter 1-115.B contains ten standards that must be met to justify the granting of a Variance by the Director of DTCS in the case of Administrative Variances.  The following lists the standards that must be met and the Findings of the Director in this case, based on evidence and testimony presented at the Public Hearing: 

- **(1) Due to special features of the site or special conditions or circumstances peculiar to the applicant’s land or structure, a literal enforcement of the Somerset County Critical Area program would result in an unwarranted hardship to the applicant;** 

<u>Finding:  The Director finds that a literal enforcement of the program would not lead to</u> an unwarranted hardship upon the applicant, because the prior permitted structures allowed for adequate utilization of the property, and in-kind replacement would be permissible without a variance.

### **Request for Postponement of Variance Hearing** 

We request that the County postpone this variance hearing until more information is provided by the applicant to both County staff and Commission staff for review. Below, we outline the reasons for this request. 

### <u>Inaccurate Site Plan</u> 

It appears that the Critical Area Buffer was not correctly delineated on the plan. Per COMAR 27.01.09.01.E(7), the Buffer must be expanded for steep slopes, nontidal wetlands, hydric soils, and highly erodible soils. Due to the presence of hydric soils, the Critical Area Buffer encompasses the entire upland portion of the site (Attachment 1A). Thus, the applicants are proposing a 2,395 square foot increase of lot coverage within the expanded Buffer. Additionally, each improvement (existing and proposed) will need to be labeled on the plan indicating the use. 

### <u>RCA Density</u> 

Based on the information provided, it is unclear the proposed use for each building, and if the proposal is consistent with the RCA 1 dwelling unit per 20-acre density requirement. Per Natural Resource Article Section 8-1802(a)(11)(i)(ii), the term “Dwelling Unit” means a single unit providing complete, independent living facilities for at least one person, including provisions for sanitation, cooking, eating, sleeping, and other activities routinely associated with daily life. A dwelling unit includes a living quarter for a domestic or other employee or tenant, an in-law or accessory apartment, a guest house, or a caretaker residence. Given that this property is 20 acres in size, only one dwelling unit is permitted, and a variance to the RCA density provision cannot be obtained. 

Prior to the fire, the original buildings were two separate structures, one of which was a singlefamily dwelling. The proposed plan shows structures that appear to be attached, but they are labeled on the site plan as two separate buildings. This office is requesting the applicant to provide floor plans for the previous buildings as well as for the proposed improvements to verify that the proposal is consistent with the density provisions. 

Given the inaccurate Buffer delineation and lack of information to determine if RCA density is met, we request that the County postpone this hearing until this information is provided. 

### **Critical Area Variance Comments** 

If the Planning Director decides to hear the case, this office opposes this variance request as it fails to meet each and every one of the Critical Area variance standards. While we empathize with the applicant’s situation, they have every ability for an in-kind replacement of the improvements that were damaged from the fire without requiring a variance. 

Maryland’s Critical Area law provides that variances to a local jurisdiction’s Critical Area program may be granted only if the County’s Planning Director finds that an applicant has satisfied the burden to prove that the request meets each and every one of the variance standards under COMAR 27.01.12, including the standard of unwarranted hardship. Furthermore, State law establishes the presumption that a proposed activity for which a Critical Area variance is

requested does not conform to the purpose and intent of the Critical Area law and County’s Critical Area Program. The Planning Director must make an affirmative finding that the applicant has overcome this presumption, based on the competent and substantial evidence presented from the applicant. 

Based on the information provided, the decision to deny this request would not result in unwarranted hardship. State law defined “unwarranted hardship” to mean that without the requested variance, an applicant shall be denied reasonable and significant use of the entire parcel. The property was already improved with a single-family dwelling, an additional building, sheds, and outdoor amenities. The applicant has the ability to replace these buildings and amenities in a manner that is in-kind, which would not necessitate a variance request. However, the applicant is requesting a variance to allow for disturbance to the Critical Area Buffer so that they may redevelop out-of-kind improvements, which will result in an additional 2,395 square feet of lot coverage within the Buffer. Furthermore, this office does not consider, and has not previously considered, accessory structures to meet the standard of unwarranted hardship, as it is not within the limits of reasonable and significant use of the lot. Therefore, denying this variance request would not result in an unwarranted hardship. 

Critical Area law and regulations are designed to foster more sensitive development for shoreline areas as to minimize adverse impacts water quality and wildlife and plant habitat. Ecologically sensitive areas such as the Critical Area Buffer within the RCA are purposefully protected within the Critical Area regulations and the County’s Critical Area program because of their importance in meeting the goals of the Critical Area law.

- (2) Conserve fish, wildlife, and plant habitat, and 

- (3) Establish land use policies that accommodate development while recognizing that development adversely affects the first two goals. 

Granting a variance to allow for the redevelopment of the property with out-of-kind improvements that increase lot coverage within the Critical Area Buffer when the applicants previously had reasonable and significant use of their lot would not be in harmony with the spirit and intent of the Critical Area law and would be contrary to the goals of the Critical Area law.
