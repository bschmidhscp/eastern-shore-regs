Brian Laird, Chairman Paul Emely A.C. Alrey William Cottman Andrew Baines

**<u>Subject of Appeal:</u>** SE 2025-2554 Matthews Sara – 4630 Crisfield Hwy 

**SE 25-2554:** The applicant is seeking approval to conduct a Cannabis growing facility in C2 zoning 

**Testimony:** Mr. Laird read the ad into the record. Sara Matthews of 2401 Queen Mitchel Rd Gambrills Maryland came forward and was sworn in. 

Chairman Laird asked the applicant to explain her request.

<!-- page 2 -->
- Mrs. Matthews represents the trilogy group who intend to purchase a building located at 4630 Crisfield Hwy, which belongs to John and Annette Humphreys. She testified that she intends to open an indoor cannabis growing operation at the location. She intends for the facility to bring sustainable agricultural jobs to Somerset County. 

- She continued, the facility will meet all state requirements for security, odor mitigation, and incident reporting. The project is fully licensed by the Maryland Cannabis Commission. And that the project is not a dispensary, and there will be no on-site consumption of cannabis. 

- In addressing concerns that have come up before Mrs. Matthews focused on the problem of odors. she mentioned the use of negative pressure rooms and carbon filters for odor mitigation. She also mentioned there would be no walk-in customers, and there would be a fence around the perimeter. The only ones allowed on-site would-be employees and the delivery drivers taking their product to licensed dispensaries. 

Mr. Emely questioned the logistics of their negative pressure system. 

- Mrs. Matthews called one of her partners up to address the question as he would be in charge of the facility’s design. 

- Mr. Schuyler Fogle of 2185 Reese Rd, Westminster Md came forward and was sworn in. 

- Mr. Fogle testified that the negative wouldn’t be vented outside, instead the individual rooms would have their air vented into the interior hallway of the facility. So even though the grow rooms would have negative pressure, the facility as a whole would act somewhat like a selfcontained bubble. 

- Mr. Baines then stated that the hospital had used a similar system during the covid-19 pandemic, so he was familiar with the principle. 

- Mr. Fogle than continued that if designed correctly, you could stand outside the building and you wouldn’t smell anything, the only way someone could be exposed to the smell is if they were to walk into one of the grow rooms, and shoved your face into a plant. 

- Mr. Cottman inquired about where the cannabis was shipped after being grown. 

- Mr. Fogle answered after the cannabis was grown and processed it would be sent to retail dispensaries throughout the state. 

- Mr. Cottman then asked if it would be sold locally or if it would leave the county. 

- The applicant explained that there were no restrictions on which dispensaries in the state could receive the cannabis. 

- Mr. Cottman questioned the security around the building. 

The applicant answered there would be fencing, 24-hour surveillance and on-site security.

- Mr. Emely then asked where the closest similar project was located, and if there were any on the Eastern Shore. 

- The applicant answered SunMed, a facility located in Cecil county. That follows a similar business model is not technically on the Eastern Shore. 

Mr. Drewer read the staff report into the record. This was made exhibit one. 

- During the staff report Mr. Drewer noted that the distance to the Coastal Community Church property line was 463 feet, and the distance to Highway Holiness Church was 398 feet. The zoning code required a minimum distance of 500 feet to any churches so the board would also have to grant as a condition of the special exception for the applicant to locate within the 500foot buffers around the churches. 

Mr. Illuminati than informed the board that there were 8 criteria for special exceptions that the applicant must conform with, they are: 

- 1.The proposed use conforms in all respects to minimum requirements of the district in which it is located; 

- 2.The proposed use will not adversely affect the health, safety, and general welfare of residents of the area; 

- 3.The proposed use will not interfere with the adequate and orderly provision of public facilities necessary to service the area or the proposed Special Exceptions; 

- 4.The proposed use will not create congestion in the road or undue traffic hazards and that adequate ingress and egress is provided; 

- 5.The proposed use will not adversely affect the area and surrounding property due to adverse environmental characteristics including undue smoke, odor, noise, improper drainage inadequate access; 

- 6.The proposed use will not adversely affect the established character of the area; and 

- 7.The proposed use is consistent with the guidelines established for such uses in the County’s Critical Area Program, where applicable. 

- 8.The proposed use is consistent with the Comprehensive Plan or a local Area Plan adopted by the Board of County Commissioners. 

Mr. Laird than asked if there were any other questions 

Mr. Laird then asked if there was anyone who came to speak in favor of the request 

Mr. Laird asked if anybody wanted to speak in opposition of the request

Alex Johnston of 26948 Plantation Rd Crisfield Md came forward. 

- His concerns were, the smell. Alex pointed out the applicant said odor “mitigation” not elimination. He would need the applicant to guarantee the lack of odor in order to be in favor of the request. 

- His next concern was the fencing to be used at the facility. Mr. Johnston mentioned he had billboards on his property and he was worried about the new fencing obscuring. 

- His final concern was future use of the site. He did not want the facility to ever be able to expand into outdoor cannabis production. 

- Mr. Laird addressed the odor issue by pointing out they would be required to follow state regulations regarding the maintenance of their filtration system. 

- There was brief discussion before Mr. Drewer pointed out that the BZA was simply voting on the approval of the use of the site. Any details about the site plan, including fencing would be worked out by the planning commission. 

- Mr. Laird asked if the board had any questions 

There were none. 

- Mr. Fogle, and Mrs. Matthews then stepped forward to try to address Mr. Johnston’s concerns. Mrs. Matthews stated that in order to maintain their state license they are stipulated to ensure that no odors emanate past their property boundaries. 

- They also mentioned setting up a hotline for anyone to all if the odor goes where it is not supposed to. 

- Mr. Fogle than state that regarding mitigation vs elimination, he was willing to say that his practices were closer to elimination. 

- Mr. Emely than enquired about the number of employees they could expect within the next 5 years. 

- The applicant than replied they expected anywhere from 15 to 30 employees within the next 5 years
