**<u>Subject of Appeal:</u>** SE 2026-2555 Matthews Sara – 4381 Crisfield Hwy 

**SE 26-2555:** The applicant is seeking approval to conduct a Cannabis growing facility in I1 zoning **Testimony:** Mr. Laird read the ad into the record. Sara Matthews of 2401 Queen Mitchel Rd Gambrills Maryland came forward and was sworn in. 

Chairman Laird asked the applicants to explain their request. 

Mrs. Sara Matthews, representative of Trilogy Group LLC, began her testimony. They are Maryland licensed Cannabis grower who intend to open a facility on the property of off Crisfield Hwy. She continued, the property is meant to be a temporary site. As their license actually allows the cultivation of up to 300,000 square feet, and the current property is well below that. 

They have had to find a temporary site, due to the circumstances involving the wait for the permanent site at the Somerset County Industrial Park. They have to have plants growing soon in order to keep their license, but as soon as the industrial park property is ready, they intend to move the operation over there. 

Chairman Laird asked the board whether they had any questions for Mrs. Matthews. Mr. Cottman asked to confirm that this was intended to be a temporary site.

Mrs. Matthews confirmed it was. 

Mr. Baines then asked about the type of structure to be built there. 

Mrs. Matthews clarified they would be bringing in Greenhouses and grow containers. 

Mr. Drewer read the staff report into the record. It was made exhibit one. 

Mr. Baines asked how long they intended to stay at the current location. 

Mrs. Matthews replied as soon as the industrial park site was ready they intended to move there. The current site had no where enough space to have a fully functioning facility. 

Mr. Cottman asked for clarification about what had happened at the industrial park site. 

It was then explained to Mr. Cottman that even though they had been approved to put their facility in the industrial park. The industrial park itself was still in the process of being finalized. 

Mr. Baines then questioned the security of the greenhouses. 

Mrs. Matthews told him they would have to meet the same requirements as a normal cannabis growing facility. This would include fencing, 24-hour security, motion detectors, cameras, and lighting. 

Chairman Laird then inquired about why the first two sites they had been approved for had fallen through. 

The applicant explained the industrial park was still in the process of being completed, and the other property had fallen through after negotiation with the property owner. Due to their license needing them to have plants growing, they were unable to wait for the industrial park to be complete. 

Chairman Laird then asked if anyone in the crowd would like to speak in support of the facility. 

Mr. Schuyler Fogle of 2185 Reese Rd, Westminster Md came forward and was sworn in. 

Mr. Fogle testified that the facility was meant to be temporary, and that they had gone through great lengths to insure the temporary facilities would match the same odor blocking performance as a permanent structure. 

The greenhouse would act as a sealed container. The walls are to be built to be roll up walls, but they are never intended to actually roll up. The facility will use active HVAC. And the vents are only intended to be used during active emergencies. 

In the case that the vents do have to be used. They have a fog-based filter to filter out any older during any emergency air exchange.

Mr. Emely asked if the applicant had witnessed this air filtration system in person. 

Mr. Fogle replied he had seen them used in California, and Oregon. 

Mr. Emely questioned the logistics of their negative pressure system, including how much air it would discharge. 

Mr. Fogle answered they typically discharged 600 CFM per filter. 

Chairman Laird then asked if there was any one there in opposition 

Mr. Drewer than read a notarized letter of opposition into the record. Said letter was then made exhibit two. 

In response to the letter the applicant answered. That the site will only vent during emergencies, and said vented air will be filtered to remove odors. They also added that due to the fencing people on the bike path will not be able to directly see the facility anyway. 

To finalize their point, the applicant noted that cannabis growing was highly regulated by the state of Maryland. So even if the complainer did not trust them to properly build their facility, the state could be trusted to come down on them to force them to build it right. 

Mr. Baines then stated that the hospital had used a similar system during the covid-19 pandemic, so he was familiar with the principle of their filtration system. 

There was brief discussion before Mr. Drewer pointed out that the BZA was simply voting on the approval of the use of the site. Any details about the site plan, including filtration would be worked out by the planning commission. 

Chairman Laird then asked if Legal wanted to add anything. 

Mr. Illuminati than informed the board that they should put an additional condition in their approval. This condition would take away the boards approval to grow cannabis on the lot if the use was abandoned for one year. This would protect the community, as once the applicant left the location for their permanent facility, the property could not be used to grow cannabis by another group without them also having to come before the board of appeals. 

The board agreed with Mr. Illuminati’s decision. 

Mr. Laird asked if the board had any questions 

Mr. Baines inquired about what the applicants plan would be if the industrial park was not ready any time soon. 

Mrs. Matthews made the point that they were a master of pivoting at this point. They have already had to revise plans twice so they are used to pivoting by now.
