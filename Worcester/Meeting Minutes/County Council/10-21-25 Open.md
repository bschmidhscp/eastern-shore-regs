# **Minutes of the County Commissioners of Worcester County, Maryland** 

October 21, 2025 

Theodore J. Elder, president Eric J. Fiori, vice president Caryn G. Abbott Anthony W. Bertino, Jr. Madison J. Bunting, Jr. Joseph M. Mitrecic Diana Purnell 

Following a motion by Commissioner Purnell, seconded by Commissioner Fiori, with Commissioner Bertino momentarily out of the room and Commissioner Bunting temporarily absent, the commissioners unanimously voted to meet in closed session at 9:00 a.m. in the Commissioners’ Conference Room to discuss legal and personnel matters permitted under the provisions of Section 3-305(b)(1) and (7) of the General Provisions (GP) Article of the Annotated Code of Maryland and to perform administrative functions permitted under the provisions of Section GP 3-104. Also present at the closed session were Chief Administrative Officer Weston Young, Deputy Chief Administrative Officer Candace Savage, County Attorney Roscoe Leslie, Public Information Officer Kim Moses, Human Resources Director Stacey Norton and Deputy Director Pat Walls; and Assistant Finance Officer Ondrea Starzhevskiy. Topics discussed and actions taken included the following: hiring Zach Edwards as a part-time landfill operator I within the Solid Waste Division; promoting Vehicle and Equipment Mechanic II Ronald White to vehicle and equipment mechanic III within the Roads Division and Ms. Starzhevskiy to finance officer; receiving legal advice from counsel; and performing administrative functions, including discussing potential board appointments. 

Following a motion by Commissioner Abbott, seconded by Commissioner Fiori, the commissioners unanimously voted to adjourn their closed session at 10:04 a.m. 

After the closed session, the commissioners reconvened in open session. Commissioner Elder called the meeting to order, and following a morning prayer by Tara Handy and pledge of allegiance, announced the topics discussed during the morning closed session. 

The commissioners reviewed and approved the open and closed session minutes of their regularly scheduled meeting on October 7, 2025. 

Upon a motion by Commissioner Mitrecic, the commissioners unanimously agreed to table Item 12 - Private School SRD Program Request. Commissioner Mitrecic stated that, based upon legal advice, the commissioners have concurred that the following issues need to be addressed before they consider this request. Upon a motion by Commissioner Mitrecic, the commissioners unanimously approved by consent agenda item numbers 2-11 as follows: scheduling the next Worcester County Fair for August 7-9, 2026; out-of-state travel for four STAR Team members in the Sheriff’s Office to attend Leadership training in Reading, PA; granting 100% County tax credits totaling $12,553.18 for the three Ocean City Chamber of Commerce properties; an extra work order of $3,500 for Mead & Hunt for work on the Strategic Highway Safety Plan; $56,879.28 to purchase two replacement lagoon aerators for the Assateague Point Wastewater Treatment Plant from AquaAerobics Systems; an over-expenditure of $2,000 to clean and paint the exterior of the Newark Water Plant; a change order of $23,500 for the KCI contract for the design of the Riddle Farm emergency force main bypass; waiving the stormwater bonding requirements for the Runway 1432 RSA Runway Rehab Program; scheduling a public hearing to consider a request to expand the West Ocean City SSA and connect certain properties identified on Tax Map 26 to the sewer system; and increasing the Sheriff’s salary to $117,392 effective November 2026. 

The commissioners conducted a public hearing on Rezoning Case No. 451, which was filed by Attorney Hugh Cropper on behalf of Richard and Elizabeth Smithson, property owners, for an amendment to the Official Zoning Maps to change approximately 150 acres of land located on the north side of Public Landing Road (MD Route 365), west of Outten Road, Snow Hill, in the Second Tax District of Worcester County, Maryland, from E-1 Estate District to A-1 Agricultural District. County Attorney Roscoe Leslie swore in those individuals who planned to give testimony during the hearing. Development Review and Permitting (DRP) Director Jennifer Keener reviewed the application, which received a favorable recommendation from the County Planning Commission. 

Commissioner Elder opened the floor to receive public comment. 

Mr. Cropper reviewed the request, which was based on a mistake. He concurred with the Planning Commission’s findings and asked the commissioners to accept the findings and Ms. Keener’s testimony as his testimony as well. 

Chris McCabe of Coastal Compliance reviewed the terms of the easement on the property and concurred that it had been a mistake for the property to be zoned E-1 District, as it has historically been used for agriculture, is designated for agriculture in the Comprehensive Plan’s Land Use Map, and should be similarly zoned. 

There being no further public comment, Commissioner Elder closed the floor. 

Upon a motion by Commissioner Bunting, the commissioners conceptually adopted the Planning Commission’s Findings of Fact as their own and approved the rezoning from E-1 to A- 1.

The commissioners conducted a public hearing on Bill 25-05 (Taxation – Homestead Property Tax Credit), which was introduced by Commissioners Abbott, Bertino, Bunting, Elder, Fiori, Mitrecic, and Purnell on September 2, 2025. Chief Administrative Officer Weston Young stated that this bill, if approved, would reduce the Homestead Property Tax Credit from three percent to zero percent effective July 1, 2026. 

Commissioner Elder opened the floor to receive public comments. 

Vince Gisriel of Ocean City applauded the commissioners for considering this action, stating that he would be hard pressed to imagine a better action that they could take to help County residents. 

There being no further public comment, Commissioner Elder closed the floor. 

Following some discussion and upon a motion by Commissioner Bertino, the commissioners unanimously adopted Bill 25-05 as presented. 

The commissioners conducted a public hearing to obtain the views of citizens on community, economic development, and housing needs to be considered for submission of an application to the Maryland Community Development Block Grant Program (CDBG). Housing Program Coordinator Davida Washington reviewed the application for a $300,000 grant to support the Countywide housing rehabilitation program. To date 338 County homeowners have received assistance through the County’s program, and the requested funds will be leveraged with the Maryland Affordable Trust Indoor Outdoor Plumbing Grant, State Special Loans Program, Shore Up, and the Bay Restoration Fund to improve seven or more owner-occupied homes. 

Commissioner Elder opened the floor to receive public comments. 

There being no public comments, Commissioner Elder closed the floor. 

Upon a motion by Commissioner Purnell, the commissioners unanimously adopted Resolution No. 25-24 authorizing the filing of a CDBG application to fund the County’s housing rehabilitation program in SFY26. 

Pursuant to the request of Recreation and Parks Director Kelly Rados and upon a motion by Commissioner Bertino, the commissioners unanimously accepted the proposal from Gripe Associates for mechanical and electrical engineering services for the HVAC systems located at the Worcester County Recreation Center. 

Pursuant to the request of Fire Marshal Matt Owens and upon a motion by Commissioner Abbott, the commissioners unanimously awarded the best responsive bid for the Fire Training Center vehicle storage construction project to Whayland Company for $3,361,500. 

Pursuant to the request of Public Works Director Dallas Baker and upon a motion by Commissioner Abbott, the commissioners unanimously awarded design, permitting, construction administration, and inspection services for Cells 6 and 7 at the Central Landfill to EA Engineering, Science & Technology for $899,404.

Following some discussion, Commissioners Abbott, Elder, Fiori, Mitrecic, and Purnell introduced the aforementioned text amendment as an emergency bill and agreed to schedule a public hearing .

Upon nominations by Commissioner Bunting, the commissioners unanimously agreed to reappoint Debbie Hielman to the Housing Review Board and William Gabeler to the Recreation Advisory Board. 

Pursuant to a request from Downtown Snow Hill, Inc. and upon a motion by Commissioner Bertino, the commissioners unanimously approved a one-time grant of $16,000 to Downtown Snow Hill, Inc. for a destination assessment. 

The commissioners recessed for 10 minutes. 

The commissioners conducted a third work session with Development Review and Permitting Director Jennifer Keener and Consultant Michael Bayer on updates to the Comprehensive Plan. Michael Bear presented a general overview of the Water Resources Element. 

In response to questions from Commissioner Bertino asking why electricians are the only service professionals required to pay a registration fee, Ms. Keener stated that electricians were licensed locally until 2021 when the State overhauled the licensing regulations to ensure consistency, but allowed local jurisdictions to continue registering. 

In response to questions by Commissioner Elder, Ms. Keener stated that registration fees generate about $36,000 in year one and $3,000 in year two for two-year revenues of $39,000, and these funds cover program costs. In response to a question by Commissioner Mitrecic, Ms. Keener stated that the registration fee is $100 for a Master Electrician and $40 for unlimited. Commissioner Mitrecic stated that licensing fees are a cost of doing business, this registration fee is very minimal, and he opposed eliminating the fees. 

Commissioner Mitrecic discussed emails sent to the commissioners concerning the issuance of a proclamation on October 7, 2025 to members of the Turning Point USA chapter at Stephen Decatur High School. He stated that all clubs have the right to be recognized. 

The commissioners answered questions from the press, after which they adjourned to meet again on November 4, 2025.
