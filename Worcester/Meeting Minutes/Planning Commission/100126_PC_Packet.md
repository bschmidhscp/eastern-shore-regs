FY27/28 MALPF Easement Applications Review
The Planning Commission met with Katherine Munson, Planning Manager, Worcester County 
Environmental Programs, to review the following eleven (11) applications to sell an easement to 
the Maryland Agricultural Land Preservation Foundation (MALPF) in the FY27/28 application 
cycle: 
1) Beauchamp, Map 99, Parcel 6, 2007 New Bridge Road, 55 acres
2) Beauchamp, Map 99, Parcel 85, Stockton Road, 75 acres
3) Carmean, TM 54, Parcel 18, 6083 Snow Hill Road, 144.5 acres
4) Deuberry, TM 48, Parcel 51, Porters Crossing Road, 82.42 acres
5) Glad Mar Farms, Inc., Map 69, Parcel 15 and Map 77, Parcel 6A (3 acres withheld),
Whitesburg Road, 129.71 acres
6) Gravenor, Map 63, Parcel 84, 4915 Pennewell Road, 20.306 acres
7) Maddux, Map 91, Parcel 59, New Bridge Road, 144.92 acres (re-application)
8) Sterling, Map 100, Parcel 30, Payne/Johnson Roads; 73.52 acres
9) Wallace/Hargis, Map 72, Parcel 50, 4308 Paw Paw Creek Road, 205.8 acres
10) Watson Powell Land Holdings, Map 56, Parcel 18, Blake Road, 275.74 acres
11) Watson Powell Land Holdings, Map 56, Parcel 83, Blake Road, 163.02 acres
Ms. Munson explained as background that the MALPF program is a state program that purchases 
permanent agricultural easements on farmland across the state. The county works with the program 
by managing applications, monitoring easements and providing matching funds (general fund). 
Funding is mainly generated by the real estate transfer tax and the agricultural transfer tax. 
Certified counties retain more of the agricultural transfer tax to use for easement purchases. She 
noted that funding is limited due to the state’s diversion of $25 million from Program Open Space 
annually through 2029 to address other state budget shortfalls. She provided the Planning 
Commission with a recent newsletter that is sent to participating and interested landowners. 
Maps were provided to the Planning Commission showing the location of the applicant properties 
in relation to protected lands, zoning and the draft growth areas. Individual aerial maps of each 
property were provided. In response to a question, Ms. Munson stated that funding is likely 
available for purchase of one or possibly two easements on the applicant properties. 
Mrs. Wimbrow made the motion to endorse all eleven (11) applications presented, as the 
applications meet all program requirements and are consistent with the comprehensive plan, and 
recommend approval of all applications to the Worcester County Commissioners. Mrs. Knight 
seconded the motion, and the motion carried unanimously (6-0).

Project Description and Site Conditions: 
The subject property, identified as Tax Map 0026, Parcel 211, consists of approximately 2.42 acres and is 
located on the west side of Maryland Route 611 (Stephen Decatur Highway), across from Mystic Harbor in 
Worcester County. The parcel also fronts Sinepuxent Road, which will provide access to the proposed gated 
storage yard. The site is currently undeveloped, primarily wooded, and generally flat, with existing land 
cover consisting of open grass area, existing woods, non-tidal wetlands, and wetland buffer. 
 
The project proposes a gated, protected gravel storage yard for boats on trailers, with two paved 
commercial entrances from Sinepuxent Road to provide pull-through access for towing vehicles and 
trailers. The storage yard will include compacted gravel storage and circulation areas, paved entrances, 
open grass area, and retained existing woods. An 8-foot-high chain-link fence with black privacy slats will 
enclose the storage area, control access, and serve as the primary visual barrier. 
 
Stormwater management requirements will be met through the use of two M-2 Submerged Gravel 
Wetland facilities. Forest Conservation requirements will be addressed by retaining the northern portion of 
the site, which consists of existing woods, wetlands, and wetland buffer, and by mitigating the remaining 
Forest Conservation requirements at a 2:1 ratio offsite at an existing nearby mitigation bank.

Waiver Requests 
The proposed site plan incorporates paved commercial entrances, a compacted gravel surface for the gated 
boat storage yard, stormwater management facilities, retained natural resource areas, and an 8-foot-high 
perimeter fence with black privacy slats. The Applicant respectfully requests Planning Commission approval 
of the following waivers to allow the project to function safely and efficiently while maintaining appropriate 
screening, stormwater management, and environmental protection measures. 
 
1. Gravel Boat Storage Yard Surface - §ZS1-320(f)(1) 
§ZS1-320(f)(1) requires parking areas and vehicular travelways to have a hard and durable surface 
and identifies concrete, asphalt, tar and chip, brick, and interlocking paving systems as acceptable 
materials. The section allows gravel or similar materials when approved by the Planning Commission 
and supplied and maintained with a binding agent. 
 
The storage yard is proposed as a low-speed, low-volume gated facility for boats on trailers. The 
commercial entrances and public-road interface will be paved, while the proposed gravel surface 
will be limited to the secured interior storage and circulation area. 
 
Requiring asphalt or concrete over the entire storage yard would add substantial construction cost 
and create a more intensive paved condition while providing little operational benefit for this use. A 
properly compacted gravel surface provides durable support for towing vehicles and boat trailers, is 
appropriate for low-speed storage-yard traffic, and can be readily maintained. 
 
The gravel lot will be properly compacted, graded, and regularly maintained to provide a stable, 
safe, and functional surface. Ongoing maintenance will include periodic regrading and material 
replenishment as necessary. 
 
The Applicant respectfully requests a waiver from §ZS1-320(f)(1) to permit the proposed 
compacted gravel surface for the storage yard, subject to regular maintenance of the gravel lot.

2. Landscape Screening - §ZS1-322(e) 
§ZS1-209(c)(14) establishes the landscape screening requirement for the proposed storage yard, and 
§ZS1-322(e) establishes the applicable screening standards and expressly authorizes the Planning 
Commission to modify or waive those standards due to special circumstances or conditions. 
 
The site plan proposes an 8-foot-high chain-link fence with black privacy slats around the storage 
yard. The privacy slats will form a continuous visual barrier that screens the boats on trailers from 
adjacent roads and properties, thereby satisfying the visual purpose of the screening requirement. 
 
Along Maryland Route 611, only a 5-foot setback is available in front of the proposed fence. This 
narrow area does not provide adequate planting width for large screening trees or shrubs without 
conflicts with the fence, the road right-of-way, and long-term plant establishment and 
maintenance. 
 
Along Sinepuxent Road, a drainage swale is located between the proposed fence and the road. 
Planting large screening material within the swale would interfere with the swale's drainage 
conveyance and routine maintenance and could reduce its functional capacity. 
 
Because the available roadside areas cannot support effective landscape screening without creating 
access, maintenance, or drainage conflicts, the slatted privacy fence provides a practical and 
durable alternative. The requested waiver will keep the roadside swale clear and functional while 
providing an effective year-round visual barrier. 
 
Pursuant to §ZS1-322(e), the Applicant respectfully requests that the Planning Commission waive 
the landscape screening standards applicable under §ZS1-209(c)(14) to permit the proposed 8-
foot-high chain-link fence with black privacy slats to serve as the visual screening barrier in lieu of 
large landscape screening material along Maryland Route 611 and Sinepuxent Road, based on the 
special circumstances and conditions created by setback limitations, drainage infrastructure, and 
long-term maintenance considerations. 
 
 
Summary of Requested Planning Commission Waivers: 
1. A waiver from §ZS1-320(f)(1) to allow a compacted gravel surface for the gated storage yard, 
subject to regular maintenance of the gravel lot. 
2. A waiver under §ZS1-322(e) of the landscape screening standards applicable to the requirement 
established by §ZS1-209(c)(14), allowing the 8-foot-high chain-link fence with black privacy slats to 
provide visual screening in lieu of large landscape material along Maryland Route 611 and 
Sinepuxent Road due to the site's special circumstances and conditions.

INSPECTION CRITERIA:
REGULAR INSPECTIONS SHALL BE MADE DURING THE FOLLOWING STAGES OF CONSTRUCTION:
1.
DURING EXCAVATION TO SUBGRADE.
2. DURING PLACEMENT OF BACKFILL OR PERFORATED INLET PIPE AND OBSERVATION WELLS.
3.
DURING PLACEMENT OF GEOTEXTILES AND ALL FILTER MEDIA.
4. DURING CONSTRUCTION OF ANY APPURTENANT CONVEYANCE SYSTEMS SUCH AS
DIVERSION STRUCTURES, INLETS, OUTLETS, AND FLOW DISTRIBUTION STRUCTURES.
5. UPON COMPLETION OF FINAL GRADING AND ESTABLISHMENT OF PERMANENT
STABILIZATION, AND BEFORE ALLOWING RUNOFF TO ENTER THE WETLAND.
THE FOLLOWING ITEMS SHOULD BE ADDRESSED TO ENSURE PROPER MAINTENANCE AND
LONG-TERM PERFORMANCE OF SUBMERGED GRAVEL WETLANDS:
1.
PRIVATELY OWNED PRACTICES SHALL HAVE A MAINTENANCE PLAN AND SHALL BE
PROTECTED BY EASEMENT, DEED RESTRICTION, ORDINANCE, OR OTHER LEGAL MEASURES
PREVENTING ITS NEGLECT, ADVERSE ALTERATION, AND REMOVAL.
2. DURING THE FIRST YEAR OF OPERATION, INSPECTIONS SHOULD BE CONDUCTED AFTER
EVERY MAJOR STORM AND POORLY ESTABLISHED AREAS REVEGETATED.
3.
SEDIMENT ACCUMULATION IN THE PRETREATMENT ARES SHOULD BE REMOVED AS
NECESSARY.
4.
SIGNS OF UNEVEN FLOW DISTRIBUTION WITHIN THE WETLAND MAY MEAN THAT THE
GRAVEL OR UNDERDRAIN IS CLOGGED.  THE GRAVEL AND/OR UNDERDRAIN MAY NEED TO
BE REMOVED, CLEANED, AND REPLACED.
5.
A DENSE STAND OF WETLAND VEGETATION SHOULD BE MAINTAINED THROUGH THE LIFE OF
THE FACILITY WITH PLANTINGS REPLACED AS NEEDED.
6. INLETS AND OUTLETS TO EACH SUBMERGED GRAVEL WETLAND CELL SHOULD BE FREE
FROM DEBRIS TO PREVENT CLOGGING.
7.
EROSION AT INFLOW POINTS SHOULD BE REPAIRED.  FLOW SPLITTERS SHOULD BE
FUNCTIONAL TO PREVENT BYPASSING OF THE FACILITY.

Amazon Web Services (AWS) and Jeremey Sweeney, board member of the Maryland Broadband 
Cooperative, Inc., have submitted a text amendment application to permit a Power Feeding Equipment 
(PFE) building in the CA Commercial Airport District. A copy of the draft bill language, the application, 
and supplemental information is attached. 
 
The text amendment process 
A text amendment is the mechanism by which the county revises its zoning standards relative to a 
particular regulation or zoning district, not a specific property nor a specific project. Applications are 
routinely submitted by interested parties, usually to address a specific use or modify a code requirement. 
This application is specifically designed to address a use that is not currently defined in the county code. 
It does not authorize the broader development prohibited by the moratorium on data centers and cable 
landing stations, which a future text amendment will address. 
 
This application is unique because only one property carries a CA District floating zone designation. 
Since the request is not property specific, the Mayor and City Council of Ocean City are not required to 
participate in the text amendment application; however, consent will be required during the site plan 
review and permitting processes.  
 
Amendments to the zoning code must be submitted to the Planning Commission. The Commission will 
review each proposal and provide a recommendation to the Worcester County Commissioners 
(favorable or unfavorable). It may also suggest changes to the proposed language. If at least one County 
Commissioner introduces the amendment as a bill, the Commissioners will schedule a public hearing to 
gather public input before acting. If the bill is not introduced, it will fail; however, no deadline applies to 
the resubmission of another text amendment application. 
 
What is a Power Feed Equipment (PFE) building? 
According to the application and background information provided by the applicants, this structure is 
not a data center, cable landing station or other public utility structure. The intent is to provide electrical 
power to the subsea cable line such that the information can be sent further inland to a data center in 
another jurisdiction without requiring a cable landing station or data center in Worcester County.

<!-- page 82 -->
Where is the use proposed? 
This bill limits applicability to the CA Commercial Airport District floating zone, currently located only 
at Ocean City Municipal Airport. The attached regulations identify eight permitted principal uses, which 
are not limited to airport-related operations.  
What is the size of the building proposed? 
As initially drafted, the bill would allow one building at a maximum gross floor area of 1,200 square 
feet. Additionally, there could be an outdoor fenced enclosure of up to 3,500 square feet in area.  
How much power does this type of facility demand? 
The text amendment limits the power usage to no more than 350kW. According to the background 
information, this is equivalent to a fast EV charger. 
What are the setbacks and other lot requirements? 
In the Commercial Airport District, uses do not have defined lot area, setback, lot width, parking or 
landscaping standards. The Planning Commission shall approve the standards as part of the site plan 
review process. The Planning Commission may also attach other conditions or restrictions on the 
property to protect the public’s health, safety and general welfare (https://ecode360.com/14021307). 
What are the noise impacts of this facility? 
The definition of a PFE building states that backup batteries and generators may be located in the 
outdoor enclosure. The background information indicates that the noise impact will be “negligible”, with 
levels of 30 dB to 45 dB measured at 100 yards (300 feet) from the facility. The applicant should explain 
whether the anticipated noise impacts are primarily a result of the exterior generators or other 
infrastructure, and how many generators and the size that would be proposed. Detailed plans and 
equipment specifications would be required to be submitted during the site plan review process. 
The applicant has proposed conducting a noise impact study, as described on the attached website. 
Section ZS 1-345 sets no daytime or nighttime noise limits for properties in an A District. Because 
Ocean City Airport is zoned A-2 Agricultural District with a CA District floating zone, the Planning 
Commission and/or County Commissioners may want to establish a use-specific noise limit to give staff 
a standard for evaluating the study’s results. 
If adopted: 
If adopted, the project will require site plan review by the Technical Review Committee, with 
approval from the Planning Commission. The Federal Aviation Administration will also participate 
to ensure the project does not endanger air navigation or airspace safety under § ZS 1-302 AP Airport 
Protection District.

Background 
The PFE Building and Amazon’s Fastnet System 
Fastnet is a submarine cable system that Amazon is developing between the United States and 
Ireland.1 In the United States, the Fastnet cable will land at the Ocean City Municipal Airport and 
require a small onshore facility called a Power Feed Equipment (PFE) building.  
A PFE building is not a cable landing station (CLS) or a data center, and Amazon does not intend 
to build either in Worcester County. Rather, a PFE building is a small, unmanned equipment shelter—
about the size of two shipping containers—that supplies electrical power to the cable. It stores no 
data and does not use the type of networking equipment typical of a CLS, resulting in signiﬁcantly 
less power usage. It uses no water and has negligible noise impact. The land-based segment of the 
connected cable is no diﬀerent than ordinary telecommunications and internet cables that 
commonly run underground along roads within the County. 
Amazon intends to lease airport land and leverage our existing partnership with Maryland 
Broadband Cooperative, Inc. to build and operate the PFE building. To ensure zoning clarity, we are 
requesting a zoning text amendment to the County Code to add a PFE building and international 
telecommunications cable as permitted uses on Commercial Airport (CA) zoned land only.  
Previous plans for a cable landing station (CLS) 
Amazon previously intended to terminate the Fastnet cable at a CLS on Route 611 using a Globalinx 
development. In July 2026, Worcester County issued a one-year moratorium on all data centers and 
cable landing stations within the County. Amazon understands and respects the community 
concerns that motivated the moratorium and is no longer pursuing development of a CLS. In 
pursuing the PFE building as an alternative, Amazon is committed to working with the County to 
address the community’s concerns while developing this critical infrastructure. 
Maryland Broadband Cooperative (MdBC) partnership 
Amazon intends to build and operate the PFE building in partnership with MdBC, a Maryland-based 
membership cooperative that has built and maintained ﬁber infrastructure across the state for two 
decades. MdBC’s network keeps essential connectivity running for healthcare and 911 systems, 
schools, and local internet providers. Amazon has worked with MdBC since 2025 on the Fastnet 
project and is a member of the cooperative (as is Worcester County). MdBC’s local roots and 
operational experience make it a natural partner to build and maintain the proposed PFE building.2

FAQ 
How is a PFE building diﬀerent than a CLS?  
Cable landing stations and PFE buildings both provide power to a subsea cable system and require 
generators and batteries in case of grid power outages. A PFE building, however, typically uses 
signiﬁcantly less power because it does not terminate and regenerate optical signals using 
sophisticated networking equipment; it simply passes the data through to another facility. 
 
Are you building a data center?   
No. Amazon has no plans to build a data center in Worcester County. Fastnet will support existing 
Amazon infrastructure in the Washington, DC region. 
  
Will you continue to pursue a CLS on Route 611 or in Worcester County?    
If Amazon can build a PFE building at the airport, then we will look to place the additional necessary 
equipment (which might have otherwise been in a nearby CLS) in an existing Amazon facility in the 
greater Washington, DC region.  
  
What about batteries?   
Fastnet will be a critical international telecommunications link designed to operate 24x7x365. To 
achieve that, the PFE facility will include Direct Current (DC) batteries and backup generators. In the 
event of a power grid outage, backup batteries will take the load for a short duration (5-10 minutes) 
before the generator starts. The facility will be equipped with an industry-standard ﬁre suppression 
system and will not place additional burdens on local ﬁre and emergency services. This conﬁguration 
is consistent with the design MdBC has deployed throughout its extensive Maryland network without 
issue for over 20 years. 
 
Why do you have to build it here?     
We are working closely with the Ocean City Municipal Airport to choose a site that does not impact 
the surrounding community, is complementary to future airport development, and meets technical 
project requirements. The structure will be a small, single-story building relative to the nearby 
residential properties with natural tree buﬀering in between.

foundation of the international cable landing network is approved in this piecemeal fashion, it establishes
