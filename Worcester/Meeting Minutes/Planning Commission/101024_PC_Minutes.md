- I. Code Requirements Waiver Request — Landscaping Irrigation and Gravel Travelway As the first item of business, the Planning Commission reviewed a request to waive the landscaping irrigation and surface treatment requirements for a proposed project by Fritz Eisenbrandt to allow the accessory use of a principal agricultural structure or use of land for the commercial hosting of non-agricultural functions and events in the A-1 Agricultural District located at 11930 Ocean Gateway. The applicant and his attorney, Kristina Watkowski presented the request. Mr. Church recused himself from the vote. 

Also known as the Harry Kelly Farm (near Taylor Marine), Ms. Watkowski asserted that asphalt would not be in alignment with the surroundings and has asked that the Planning Commission would waive this requirement and allow for gravel with a millings top. Ms. Watkowski provided examples of other Agritourism sites within the County that also do not have asphalt. 

Additionally, the applicant requested a waiver of the automatic landscaping irrigation requirement stating that the landscaping will be bonded and that a landscaping maintenance installation and agreement would be signed and recorded with the Clerk of Court, which would ensure that the long-term maintenance of the plants would be properly managed. ] 

Ms. Wimbrow asked how wastewater would be managed, and was informed by Mr. Mitchell that they would be using portable facilities which would be used at every event.

On a motion made by Ms. Knight and seconded by Ms. Wimbrow, the Planning Commission approved the waiver requests for the automatic irrigation and surface treatment to be gravel with a millings top, with Mr. Church abstaining. 

# Ill. |Comprehensive Plan Work Session 

As the next item of business, the Planning Commission met with Michael Bayer and Angela Fleck from Wallace Montgomery to discuss the draft Communities Facility chapter of the proposed Worcester County Comprehensive Plan. 

Mr. Bayer provided a status of the overall project schedule. He then explained that they are currently working on the development capacity analysis, which will use the existing land use map and assumptions to determine whether the existing land uses can absorb the growth during this planning cycle, or whether additional areas will need to be set aside by the Planning Commission. He advised that the analysis will also be input into the Water Resources Element (WRE), which involves infrastructure planning and capacity. Then we will meet with the municipalities to obtain their feedback on the existing and proposed growth areas to ensure that we are working together to plan for the County’s growth. The Planning Commission will see that in the early part of 2025. 

Mr. Bayer stated that the draft Housing Element will be presented for the next work session in November. There are significantly more requirements for the housing element than what is in our existing comprehensive plan, and it is to be strongly data driven and intensive. We are required to address issues surrounding housing availability, affordability, workforce, and fair housing. Ms. Ott stated that the Coastal Association of Realtors could provide data resources to inform this chapter. Mrs. Keener advised that the County would be issuing an RFP for a Housing Study to update the 2004 documents relative to substandard housing in the next few weeks. This study will overlap with our comprehensive plan and may inform the housing chapter. 

The Planning Commission then reviewed the draft Community Facilities Chapter. Mr. Bayer noted that it is essentially an inventory of existing facilities in the county. The Planning Commission made the following recommendations: 

- e Under Goals and Objectives No. 2, it was recommended to revise the term “demanded” to better reflect the intent (“needed” was suggested). 

- e Consider adding a sentence about the private school system in Worcester County. 

- e Map 3-1 Public Safety, both Ocean Pines and Ocean City have two fire stations. Only one is documented for each. There was also discussion about overlap of fire and EMS services
