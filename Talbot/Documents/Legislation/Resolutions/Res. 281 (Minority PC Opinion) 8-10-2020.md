SUMMARY OF THE TALBOT COUNTY PLANNING COMMISSION’S
MINORITY OPINION CONCERNING
THE TRAPPE LAKESIDE DEVELOPMENT
August 10, 2020

Please note, Background, an Abbreviations Key and list of important
document references start on p. 4, as well as an expanded discussion of other
Minority Opinion points.

Deliberations of Planning Commissioners. On June 10, after 4 meetings’ and
nearly 12 hours of public hearings and deliberations, the Talbot County Planning
Commission (“Commission”), voted 3:2 that Amendment No. 1 Resolution 281
(“Res 281”) is consistent with the Talbot County Comprehensive Plan (“Plan”); if
the Talbot County Council (“Council”) approves Res 281, the Trappe Lakeside
Development (“Lakeside”) will be reclassified to “immediate priority” status,
allowing initial homes built at Lakeside access to hookup to Trappe’s existing
sewer, and for the development of a new sewer plant that would serve ultimately,
the planned 2,500 homes. The fact that this decision required 4 meetings and 12
hours hearing from Talbot County staff, Lakeside representatives and concerned
Talbot County residents and discussing various issues, indicates the complexity
of this matter, and the necessity to understand the concerns raised.

Designated Growth Area. We recognize and appreciate that Lakeside’s 865
acres in Trappe are part of a Designated Growth Area established by the County.
The County has identified such areas to focus growth in the town centers and
reduce the impact of sprawl on the farmlands and sensitive areas. Annexation
and development within the incorporated towns is consistent with smart growth.

ENR Technology. Furthermore, we recognize and appreciate Lakeside’s
commitment to invest in and use ENR technology, a technology contributing
improvements treating sewage over BNR technology, where only 3 milligrams
per liter (mg/l) of Nitrogen is released, versus 8 mg/l of Nitrogen.

Minority Opinion. Commissioners Boicourt and Ghezzi voted against
consistency for the reasons indicated below:

1. Our Comprehensive Plan states “Annexation and development
within growth areas should occur incrementally.... The County
supports orderly town expansion and discourages premature
development at the growth area’s extremities”.“ Passage of Res 281
promotes early town expansion and allows for two-thirds of the
development to proceed without further review of amendment of the
Talbot County Comprehensive Water and Sewerage Plan. Though it is
desirable to support growth in the Designated Growth Areas rather than
outlying agricultural land, the intent of such designation was to have a

Attachment A

Background. With the Talbot County Planning Commission (“Commission”)
having adopted a shorter version of the Decision Summary following meetings,
some of the Commissioners were of the opinion that relying on transcripts was
an inadequate means of communicating the essence of deliberations to the
Talbot County County Council (“Council”) and to the public at large, especially for
decisions that had significant potential impact to the County. For this reason, the
Commission decided to provide a summary report of the deliberations on the
proposed Lakeside Development in Trappe (“Lakeside”, formerly known as
Trappe East), where the Town of Trappe and the developer requested change in
the water and sewer status. Specifically, the request was to allow Lakeside
immediate sewer access across its 865 acres. The attempt herein is to abide by
the 1998 SEC rule for disclosure documents and provide a description in plain
English.

Decision at Hand. The proposed reclassification and remapping of the Lakeside
Planned Unit Development in the Town of Trappe from S-2 and W-2 status to S-1
and W-1 (Resolution 281 Amendment 1, or “Res 281”) was considered at the
Planning Commission’s February 5 meeting. At that time, the Commission
decided to return the Res 281 to the County Council with comments. A letter
dated February 9 was sent to the Council explaining the deliberations. There
were a few arguments laid out, but the main points are summarized in quotes
from this letter:

« “Two primary concerns remain: the pace and scale of growth within the
Lakeside Planned Unit Development (and the resulting impacts on County
infrastructure) and possible degradation of the environmentally sensitive
Miles Creek...

« _...the Planning Commission determined that the proposal to reclassify the
entire Lakeside Planned Unit Development to S-1/W-1 status would be
premature, and would neither ensure an orderly expansion of the Town
nor act to protect the environmentally sensitive Miles Creek. If, however,
the reclassification were to be done in phases, in concert with
development phasing, there would then be time to monitor and evaluate
the impacts on the County and the environment.”

Charge. The Planning Commission was charged with finding whether the
proposed reclassification was consistent with Talbot County's Comprehensive
Plan (“Plan” or “Comprehensive Plan”). Among other aspects, the primary issues
were rural, character, protection of environmentally sensitive, and ensuring that
the pace of development can be handled by County infrastructure. The following
selections from the Comprehensive Plan were referred to during the discussions:

Expanded discussion on each concern of the Minority Opinion, with four
additional concerns included:

1.

Chapter 2.II.A.1.a of Comprehensive Plan states “Annexation and
development within growth areas should occur incrementally.... The
County supports orderly town expansion and discourages premature
development at the growth area’s extremities”. Furthermore, there is
no data supporting demand for the planned 2,500 homes over any period,
and little ability to manage growth at any rate once Res 281 is passed.
Roads, replacing valuable agricultural land, would be developed well in
advance of use, and possibly for decades. Over the last decade Talbot
County’s housing market has suffered. Demand for Talbot County
housing has begun to pick most recently due to new concerns for people
to move away from more densely populated areas to seemingly safer,
rural areas, but even now rural areas are facing new concerns as Covid-
19 is spiking in rural areas across the country — there is great uncertainty
— we cannot rely on an immediate improvement of the housing market with
no reliable track record to support it.
