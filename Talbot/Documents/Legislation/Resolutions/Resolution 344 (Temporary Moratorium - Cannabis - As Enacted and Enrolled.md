A RESOLUTION TO ADOPT A TEMPORARY MORATORIUM ON THE

PERMITS, AND OTHER AUTHORIZATIONS FOR THE LOCATION,
CONSTRUCTION, AND/OR OPERATION OF BUSINESSES ENGAGED IN

NINE (9) MONTHS TO ALLOW THE COUNTY SUFFICIENT TIME TO ENACT
REASONABLE, THOUGHTFUL, AND LAWFUL ZONING REQUIREMENTS AND
OTHER REGULATIONS PERTAINING THERETO AS DEEMED NECESSARY TO
PROTECT THE PUBLIC HEALTH, SAFETY, AND WELFARE

WHEREAS, pursuant to Md. Code Ann., Local Gov’t § 10-324, Talbot County,
Maryland (the “County”), as a Charter county, may enact local laws relating to zoning and
planning to protect and promote public safety, health, morals, and welfare; and

WHEREAS, following a State referendum and ballot measure approved in the 2022
Statewide election, the Maryland General Assembly enacted comprehensive cannabis reform
statutes during the 2023 legislative session pursuant to House Bill 556 and Senate Bill 516; and

WHEREAS, on May 3, 2023, the Governor of Maryland signed into law the Maryland
Cannabis Reform Act, Chapter 254, Acts of Maryland (“Chapter 254”), effective July 1, 2023,
which regulates the legalization of non-medical adult use cannabis and, inter alia, provides
political subdivisions with the power to establish zoning requirements and otherwise regulate
cannabis subject to certain limitations; and

WHEREAS, the County Council of Talbot County (the “County Council”) recognizes
the significant planning, zoning, property, economic, and secondary effects arising from or
related to the location, construction, and operation of businesses engaged in growing, processing,
and/or selling cannabis; and

WHEREAS, the County Council considers the public interest to be best served by
sufficiently deliberated regulatory schemes regarding businesses engaged in growing,
processing, and/or selling cannabis; and

WHEREAS, the County currently lacks regulations regarding businesses engaged in
growing, processing, and/or selling cannabis; and

WHEREAS, the County Council recognizes the potential negative impacts of regulations
regarding businesses engaged in growing, processing, and/or selling cannabis use stemming from
inadequate and hurried deliberations; and

WHEREAS, the County Council has determined that enacting hastily prepared
regulations prior to the full understanding of Chapter 254 and the State regulations promulgated
pursuant thereto, including the elements of social equity distribution and the Community
Reinvestment and Repair Fund (“CRRF”), would fail to meet the spirit and intent of Chapter 254
while potentially creating confusion, unintended consequences, unreasonable and inequitable

<!-- page 3 -->
outcomes, and litigation, and would otherwise be prejudicial to the public health, safety, and
welfare; and

WHEREAS, the County Council requires sufficient time for appropriate study and
public input to ensure the establishment of effective and durable zoning text amendments and
other necessary regulations applicable to businesses engaged in growing, processing, and/or
selling cannabis; and

WHEREAS, the County Council has determined that a temporary moratorium, for a term
of (9) nine months, to be a reasonable length of time for the due consideration of zoning text
amendments and other regulations for the safe, practical, and legal implementation of Chapter
254 in the County.

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED by the County Council for Talbot County,
Maryland, that:

1. Moratorium established. A temporary moratorium is hereby established on the
processing, review, and approval of applications, site plans, permits, and other authorizations,
including but not limited to zoning, occupancy, and building certificates and permits and
approvals, for businesses engaged in growing, processing, and/or selling cannabis for a period of
nine (9) months.

2. Recommendations and legislation. This temporary moratorium is established to allow
a reasonable time within which County staff, with the assistance of subject matter experts where
appropriate, can prepare recommendations for review by the Planning Commission; the Planning
Commission can prepare recommendations for the County Council; and the County Council can
consider and enact legislation to:

(a) Efficiently and fairly establish zoning requirements and other regulations for
cannabis;

(b) Promote the policies articulated in the State’s cannabis legalization statutes as
approved by Governor Wes Moore on May 3, 2023; and

(c) Consider such other related matters as the County Council, the Planning Commission,
and County staff deem appropriate.

3. Applications. During the duration of this temporary moratorium, the County shall not
process, review, or approve any application, site plan, permit, or other authorization, including
but not limited to any zoning, occupancy, or building certificate or permit within the County’s
jurisdiction, for a business engaged in growing, processing, and/or selling cannabis. This
temporary moratorium shall apply to applications currently pending before the County as well as
applications filed after the effective date.

4. Effective date. This temporary moratorium shall be effective immediately upon
adoption of this Resolution.

<!-- page 4 -->
5. Extension. The County Council reserves the right to extend or modify this
temporary moratorium for such additional period(s) and upon such terms and conditions as it
deems necessary to protect the public health, safety, and welfare.

6. Severability. If any section, subsection, sentence, clause, or phrase of this
Resolution is, for any reason, held to be unconstitutional, such decision shall not affect the
validity of the remaining portions of this Resolution, which can be given effect without the
invalid section, subsection, sentence, clause, or phrase, and to that end, all provisions of this
Resolution are hereby declared to be severable.
