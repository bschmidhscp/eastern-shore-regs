# STATEMENT OF THE CASE 

The Applicant requested approval for a Special Exception to demolish an existing 2,580 SF commercial retail building and construct a new 2-story building with 5.310 SF of Gross Floor Area in the Village Hamlet zoning district. The Applicant also requested a Non-Critical Area variance of the 50’ setback from the state highway to construct the new building at IT from the front property line. 

On September 2, 2026, the Planning Commission unanimously voted to provide a positive recommendation to the Board of Appeals for approval. 

# SUMMARY OF TESTIMONY 

The Applicant was represented by Adam Ruf of Rauch, Inc., accompanied by the Applicant, Julie Kilnion Andrews. 

Mr. Rut’ testified that the existing structure is an established retail antique store. The Applicant seeks to redevelop the structure to bring merchandise that has historically been stored outdoors into the indoor structure, thereby substantially improving the curb appeal and aesthetic presentation of the Property. 

Mr. Ruf further stated that the project will reduce impervious surface coverage within proximity of the 100-foot shoreline development buffer. The project will also improve site access, traffic circulation, and parking. Specifically. the new site plan eliminates the hazardous condition wherein vehicles must back out directly onto the adjacent state highway to leave the Property. An existing gravel area will be removed. A variance is necessary because of the physical constraints of the Property, which include a 50-foot state highway setback and a 100foot shoreline buffer.

<!-- page 2 -->
Mr. Ruf demonstrated on the site and utility plan submitted with the Application that the requested variance will allow parking to be maintained to the right of the structure and distant from the shoreline buffer. The resulting distance from the state highway will also be consistent with surrounding properties. 

Mrs. Andrews testified that her intent is to clean up merchandise on the property across the road by moving it indoors, which will enhance the visual character of the surrounding area. Mrs. Andrews further testified that she operates without employees and that her antique shop does not host special events that generate increased traffic. 

Mr. Ruf noted that the proposed structure minimizes its ground footprint by utilizing a two-story design. He acknowledged that the VH district limits gross floor area to 2,000 square feet but contended that the proposed building design aligns with the intent and spirit of the zoning code. 

The State Highway Administration (“SI-IA”) issued no comments in opposition to the state highway variance. Planner Andrew Nixon testified that final site plan approval will be contingent upon SI-IA approval.

The Board generally finds that the requested special exception and variance will improve traffic and safety by eliminating vehicles from having to back onto a state highway. While not criteria for these applications, it will also improve environmental impacts of the Property by eliminating impervious cover within the 100’ shoreline development buffer, improving the septic system, and stormwater runoff. 

The Critical Area and state highway setbacks were not in existence when the Property was developed.
