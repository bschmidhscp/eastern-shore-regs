# STATEMENT OF THE CASE 

The Applicants requested a Critical Area variance of the 100’ Shoreline Devcloprnent Buffer (“Buffer”) to construct a two-story addition attaching an existing dwelling and existing detached garage over an existing brick patio and porch. The Applicants also requested a Non Critical Area variance of the side yard building restriction line to construct a detached garage. 

# SUMMARY OF TESTIMONY 

The Applicants were represented at the hearing by legal counsel. Brendan Mullaney. who appeared alongside Applicant Jamie Smith. Brett Ewing. a land planner with Lane Engineering, also appeared and testified on behalf of the Applicants as an expert witness. 

The Board first acknowledged the A’pplicants written responses and accepted them as testimony. 

Mr. Mullaney provided an overview of the application, noting that the 4.736-acre Property was first improved around W20. With the exception of a swimming pool added in 2015, all existing site improvements predate the enactment of Critical Area laws. He explained that these pre-existing improvements, apart from the pool, are non-conforming. Mr. Mullaney noted that the existing garage does not function effectively for vehicle storage because it directly faces the primary residence. The Applicants seek to replace it with a new detached garage situated over an existing parking pad. 

Mr. Mullaney explained that the proposed development will result in an 1 88-square-foot reduction of lot coverage within the Buffer. He contended that the Applicants analyzed the site and removed as much coverage within the Critical Area as feasible, including the removal of a

<!-- page 2 -->
brick walkway leading to the pier, a reduction not required under Critical Area law. While the new garage will add lot coverage overall, none of that additional coverage will be located within the Buffer. 

Mr. Mullaney emphasized that the unwarranted hardship arises from the narrow shape of the Property combined with the non-conforming status of its historic improvements. The Buffer encumbers three of the four sides of the home, while the remaining portion of the narrow parcel is constrained by dense trees and vegetation, a septic tank, a sewage disposal area, and required setbacks. The new garage is designed to avoid Buffer impacts while improving ingress and egress to and from the Property. Characterizing the proposed house improvements as reasonable, Mr. Mullaney argued that a strict interpretation of the Critical Area law would deprive the Smiths of the ability to make reasonable home improvements enjoyed by other property owners within the same zoning district. 

Mr. Smith testified that the Applicants purchased the Property approximately 30 years ago when raising their four young children. Now that their children are adults with families of their own, the Applicants need to expand the primary living space and garage to accommodate frequent gatherings with extended family. He explained that the principal objectives of the variance request are to create a functional kitchen and dining area and to construct a garage capable of accommodating parked vehicles. 

Brett Ewing testified as an expert witness in Critical Area law and land development. He testified that total lot coverage following the project will be 9.8%, well within the 15% maximum permitted under Critical Area regulations and noted that all current coverage within the Buffer prcdatcs Critical Area legislation. Mr. Ewing concurred that unique site conditions justif3’ the requested variances, specifically citing the parcel’s narrow configuration, existing driveway and circular parking areas, septic system and sewage disposal area, and surrounding vegetation. He highlighted that the new construction will be situated on an existing gravel area and that placing the improvements anywhere else on the lot would result in greater adverse environmental impacts. 

Andrcw Nixon, Talbot County Planner, testified that mitigation will not be required because the Applicants are decreasing total lot coverage within the Buffer.
