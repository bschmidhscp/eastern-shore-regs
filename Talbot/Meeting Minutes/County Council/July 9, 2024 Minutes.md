She stated that funding recommendations varied from 20% to 100% of the requests made and outlined why those projects not receiving funding were not recommended. ### VII. <u>Public Hearings:</u> 

Resolution No. 360, A RESOLUTION TO AMEND THE TALBOT COUNTY COMPREHENSIVE WATER AND SEWER PLAN FOR THE PURPOSES OF APPROVING AN ALTERNATE LOCATION FOR THE PUMP STATION AND EXPANDING THE POTENTIAL ROUTE FOR THE FORCE MAIN TO PROVIDE SANITARY SEWER SERVICE TO THE UNIVERSITY OF MARYLAND MEDICAL CENTER SHORE REGIONAL MEDICAL CENTER, TO BE LOCATED AT 10000 LONGWOODS ROAD, EASTON, MARYLAND, AND THE TALBOT COUNTY COMMUNITY CENTER, LOCATED AT 10028 OCEAN GATEWAY, EASTON, MARYLAND, AS INITIALLY SET FORTH IN RESOLUTION NO. 184, ADOPTED ON SEPTEMBER 13, 2011, was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for public hearing.  Prior to the public hearing, County Attorney, Patrick Thomas, stated that the purpose of the proposed legislation is to amend the County’s Comprehensive Water and Sewer Plan to provide for alternative sites for the pump station and the force main route to those outlined in Resolution No. 184, adopted by the Council in 2011 to provide maximum flexibility for the planning and construction of the new regional medical center.  Ray Clarke, County Engineer, stated that Resolution No. 184 provided for extension of water and sewer to the new hospital site and the Talbot County Community Center, and included a pump station on Hiners Lane and MD Rt. 662, as well as a force main.  Mr. Clarke stated that Resolution No. 360, if approved, will provide for modification of the location of both the pump station and the force main.  He stated that the Public Works Advisory Board (at its meeting on Thursday, June 20, 2024) had recommended by a vote of 4 – 0 that the Council adopt Resolution No. 360.  Brennan Tarleton, Planning Officer, stated that the Planning Commission, at its meeting on Wednesday, July 3, 2024, voted 5 – 0 to recommend that the Council adopt Resolution No. 360. Council discussion ensued with Mr. Tarleton who, at Mr. Lesher’s request, emphasized that the sewer line would be a restricted access line specifically for serving the hospital and the Talbot County Community Center; any properties that are within the county that are not already annexed into the town and classified as S-1/W-1, would not be afforded the option to connect to the line, depending upon where it goes or is located in the future, unless at such time they are annexed and reclassified through a Comprehensive Water and Sewer Plan Amendment.  Members of the public were afforded an opportunity to comment on the legislation.  The Council approved Resolution No. 360 by voting 4 – 0 as follows: 

Mr. Callahan – Aye Mr. Stepp – Aye Mr. Lesher – Aye Ms. Mielke – Aye 

Resolution No. 360 is effective immediately.

X. <u>Work Session on Bike and Pedestrian Trails – Cassandra Vanhooser, Director, Department of</u> Economic Development and Tourism; Heather Grant Executive Director, Talbot Thrive; Owen Bailey, Director, Land Use and Policy, Eastern Shore Land Conservancy; Thomas Mackay, Rail Program & Policy Manager, Rail & Intermodal Freight Section, Office of Planning and Capital Programming, Maryland Department of Transportation; John Thomas, Assistant Director, Rail and Intermodal Freight, Maryland Department of Transportation; Town of Queen Anne; Brennan Tarleton, Director, Department of Planning and Zoning; Ray Clarke, County Engineer – The Council held a work session on bike and pedestrian trails at 2:30 p.m. on Tuesday, July 9, 2024 in the Meeting Room of the Talbot County Free Library in Easton.  Prior to presentations on proposed bike and pedestrian trail initiatives, Ms. Vanhooser provided background information on the proposed initiatives by various groups, including the Maryland Department of Natural Resources (DNR), to create a network of bike and pedestrian trails throughout the region, including Talbot County, to improve safety for both bikers and pedestrians, improve bike and pedestrian infrastructure, encourage “active” transportation, and promote community investment. She stated that a group of local citizens has formed an organization, Talbot Thrive, to advocate for healthy, safe outdoor mobility and to take action on their goals, and that Eastern Shore Land Conservancy is leading an effort, with its partners, to create an Eastern Shore regional bike trail network around the state, and that the Maryland Department of Transportation (MDOT) and DNR are exploring options for developing a rail trail from Easton to Queen Anne.  Ms. Vanhooser stated that in 2020, the Talbot County Council issued a letter of support for a technical assistance grant from the National Park Service which she and her counterparts in the Departments of Parks and Recreation and Planning and Zoning had worked on for several years; the grant was awarded and County, State, and federal representatives worked to create an assessment of the opportunities for developing the rail corridor from Easton through Cordova to the town of Queen Anne. Ms. Vanhooser emphasized the positive economic impact that bike and pedestrian trails, considered “community amenities” has on communities.  Heather Grant utilized a PowerPoint to provide an overview of the goals of Talbot Thrive:  safer outdoor places for individuals to enjoy from a recreational perspective, to advocate

for systemwide changes in how infrastructure is prioritized, so that no matter what method individuals choose to get from one location to another, individuals can do so safely, more directly, and with dignity.  She provided information on Talbot Thrive’s initiatives to date with regard to safety and education, legislative advocacy, trails and infrastructure, both completed and ongoing, a proposal to establish a Talbot County Bike and Pedestrian Advisory Committee which would both work independently and in coordination with County staff to identify, evaluate and seek grants and other financial resources available to the County for the development and maintenance of bicycle and pedestrian facilities, and to complete a bike and pedestrian master plan for Talbot County as had recently been completed in Queen Anne’s County.  Ms. Grant then provided an update on the Easton to Oxford Oyster Trail which is currently underway.  Owen Bailey provided an update on the work of the Eastern Shore Regional Trails Steering Committee, led by Eastern Shore Land Conservancy through support from a National Parks Planning Grant.  He stated that the Steering Committee advocates for the benefits of connectivity of parks and trails to the landscape of our region, including getting cars off the roads, economic development, allowing individuals to connect with their families, etc. He stated that the Steering Committee is seeking input from the public as to where they would like to see trails, connections they want to make, and infrastructure which may already be in place and to incorporate same into a master plan or the comprehensive plans of local jurisdictions.  Further information may be found in the near future on the soon-to-be-established website:  MESTN.org.  Mr. Thomas stated that the State of Maryland owns approximately 140 miles of railroad lines in Caroline, Dorchester, Kent, Queen Anne’s and Talbot counties, approximately 70 miles of which are still active lines.  He stated that the primary goal of the railroad team at Maryland Department of Transportation (MDOT) is to preserve future use of the lines if needed, while still allowing interim use now that they are no longer in service. Mr. Mackay utilized a PowerPoint to outline the history of the State of Maryland’s purchase of railroad lines throughout the state in order to preserve the lines, a process which is authorized by the Surface Transportation Board (STB), a federal agency. He stated that abandonment of any lines and interim use of lines for a purpose other than railroad activity must also be authorized by the STB and explained the differences in the terms “Abandoned Lines” – whereby active rail service is ended and the rail property abandoned;  “Interim Use” – whereby the rail line property, with approval of the STB can by utilized for another purpose;  and “Railbanking”  - whereby the railroad corridor is “banked” for resumption of rail services at a future point in time if needed; in the meantime, rail infrastructure can be removed and recreational trail development can be permitted on the property. Mr. Mackay provided information on the general guidelines for the “railbanking” process and noted several projects completed, in progress or planned, including the Cambridge Rail-Trail in Dorchester County, and provided information on the railroad corridors owned by MDOT, the history of the Rail-Trail within the Easton town limits which began in 1993, and outlined work being done by the Maryland Transit Administration to manage the rail corridors, emphasizing that better utilizing of the property would permit MTA to employ more resources to other areas.  Lastly, Mr. Mackay stated that his office continues to work with DNR regarding projects currently being planned and is looking for local input and partnerships related to planning and design of projects, prioritization of segments to development into trails as well as actual construction of the trails and agreements required to support any projects.  Phil Starkey, Mayor of the Town of Queen Anne, spoke of development of a section of rail corridor within the town limits, noting that Town officials had met State officials and officials of surrounding jurisdictions regarding the proposal.  Members of the public were offered an opportunity to comment on the proposal for a rail-trail from Easton to the town of Queen Anne.  Mr. Callahan assured those in attendance that much consideration would be given, and Council approval would be needed prior to proceeding with any rail-trail in Talbot County.
