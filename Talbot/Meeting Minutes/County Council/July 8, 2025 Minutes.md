# TALBOT COUNTY, MARYLAND 

County Council **MINUTES** July 8, 2025 

Present – President Chuck Callahan, Vice President Pete Lesher, Keasha N. Haythe, Lynn L. Mielke, Dave Stepp, County Manager Clay Stamp, and County Attorney, Patrick Thomas. 

- I. <u>Agenda – Mr. Callahan requested and received unanimous consent for approval of the Agenda of</u> Tuesday, July 8, 2025. 

- II. <u>Disbursements – Mr. Callahan requested and received unanimous consent for approval of the</u> Disbursements of Tuesday, July 8, 2025. 

III. <u>Introduction of Andrew Kitzrow, Town Manager, Town of Easton – Megan Cook, Mayor, Town of</u> Easton, introduced Andrew Kitzrow, the new Easton Town Manager.  Mr. Kitzrow comes to Talbot County having previously worked for the past 20 years in Wicomico County in the Department of Recreation and Parks, and for the last eight years with the City of Salisbury.  Mr. Kitzrow stated that he is very familiar with the relationships which need to take place between municipalities and the County.  Council members welcomed Mr. Kitzrow to Talbot County. 

IV. <u>Presentation of Proclamation:  National Parks and Recreation Month – July 2025 – Prior to</u> presentation of the proclamation, Emily Gilmer, Director, Department of Parks and Recreation, expressed appreciation on behalf of her department for Council’s support of the various programs of the Department of Parks and Recreation.  Council members expressed their appreciation for the many benefits Parks and Recreation programs provide to the Talbot County community. The Clerk read a proclamation into the record in recognition of July as National Parks and Recreation Month. The proclamation spoke of various recreational and leisure activities provided to communities by departments of parks and recreation throughout the country, the positive impacts the programs bring to the families and communities they serve and encouraged individuals to participate in the many activities offered by the Talbot County Department of Parks and Recreation.  Upon motion by Mr. Stepp, seconded by Mr. Lesher, the Council approved the proclamation by voting 5 – 0 as follows: 

Mr. Callahan – Aye Mr. Stepp – Aye Mr. Lesher – Aye Ms. Mielke – Aye Ms. Haythe – Aye 

Mr. Stepp presented the Proclamation to Ms. Gilmer and members of the staff of the Department of Parks and Recreation in attendance.

Bill No. 1617, A BILL TO AMEND CHAPTER 190 (ZONING, SUBDIVISION, AND LAND DEVELOPMENT) OF THE TALBOT COUNTY CODE FOR THE PURPOSES OF ENACTING REGULATIONS REGARDING DISSOLVED AIR FLOTATION (DAF) RESIDUALS, FOOD PROCESSING RESIDUALS, AND LIQUID ORGANIC SOIL AMENDMENTS, PROVIDING THAT THE STORAGE OF SUCH MATERIALS IS A PERMITTED USE IN THE AGRICULTURAL CONSERVATION (“AC”), COUNTRYSIDE PRESERVATION (“CP”), WESTERN RURAL CONSERVATION (“WRC”), AND RURAL CONSERVATION (“RC”) ZONING DISTRICTS, AND DEFINING TERMS ASSOCIATED THEREWITH, was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for public hearing.  Prior to the public hearing,  Bryce Yelton, Assistant Planning Officer, stated that the legislation was initiated by the Planning Commission after comments were received from Talbot County farmers and others involved with agriculture in the county following a matter which came before the Agricultural Resolution Board a couple of years ago involving DAF materials.  Mr. Yelton stated that the purpose of the legislation is to hopefully resolve any future issues related to DAF residuals in Talbot County; he then provided a detailed explanation of the materials included in DAF residuals.  Members of the public were then afforded an opportunity to comment on the legislation.  The public hearing on Bill No. 1617 will remain open.  Bill No. 1617 is eligible for vote on Tuesday, July 22, 2025. 

Bill No. 1618, A BILL TO AMEND CHAPTER 190 (ZONING, SUBDIVISION, AND LAND DEVELOPMENT) OF THE TALBOT COUNTY CODE FOR THE PURPOSES OF PROVIDING THAT CANNABIS GROWING AND THE PROCESSING OF CANNABIS GROWN ON-SITE SHALL BE PERMITTED USES IN THE AGRICULTURAL CONSERVATION (“AC”), COUNTRYSIDE PRESERVATION (“CP”), AND WESTERN RURAL CONSERVATION (“WRC”) ZONING DISTRICTS, PROVIDING THAT THE PROCESSING OF CANNABIS NOT GROWN ONSITE SHALL BE PERMITTED BY SPECIAL EXCEPTION IN THE AC, CP, AND WRC ZONING DISTRICTS, AND PROVIDING THAT CANNABIS DISPENSARIES SHALL BE PERMITTED BY SPECIAL EXCEPTION IN THE LIMITED COMMERCIAL (“LC”), GENERAL COMMERCIAL (“GC”), AND LIMITED INDUSTRIAL (“LI”) ZONING DISTRICTS, EACH SUBJECT TO CERTAIN CONDITIONS, AND DEFINING TERMS ASSOCIATED THEREWITH, was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for public hearing.  Prior to the public hearing, Brennan Tarleton, Planning Officer, stated that the legislation before the Council was drafted by Planning and Zoning staff at Council’s direction to address all facets of the operational standards of cannabis as outlined in the State’s requirements related to the growing, processing, and sales of cannabis in the state of Maryland.  Local legislation follows the passage of HB805 by the Maryland General Assembly, effective June 1, 2024.  Mr. Tarleton stated that staff utilized legislation recently enacted by surrounding jurisdictions as a guide in drafting proposed legislation for Talbot County.  He stated that the draft legislation was then forwarded to the Planning Commission for review, consideration and revision; following the Planning Commission’s review, the Council held a work session with the Planning Commission to discuss the draft legislation, revisions proposed by the Planning Commission and to receive further direction from the Council. Members of the public were then afforded an opportunity to comment on the legislation.  Council discussion ensued with Mr. Tarleton and County Attorney, Patrick Thomas, regarding various provisions
