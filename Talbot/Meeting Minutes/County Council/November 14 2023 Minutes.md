- IV. <u>Proclamation:  Talbot Arts - The Clerk read a proclamation into the record regarding Talbot Arts,</u> the official arts agency in Talbot County, funded annually by the Maryland State Arts Council and supplemented by grants from Talbot County and the Towns of Easton and St. Michaels.  The proclamation recognized the contributions of Talbot Arts to the culture, quality of life, and economic development in Talbot County for the benefit of residents and visitors. 

<mark>Mr. Lesher presented the Proclamation to Talbot Arts President Amy Steward and Past President Nancy Larson.  Ms. Mielke noted that the County Council has also prepared Certificates of Recognition for Don Buxton, former Executive Director of Chesapeake Music, and James Redman, retired Title 1 Coordinator and Curriculum Supervisor for Talbot County Public Schools, for their contributions to the Talbot County arts community.  The Certificates will be presented at the Talbot Arts showcase to be held on November 15, 2023 at the Academy Art Museum.</mark>

### VI. <u>Introduction of Numbered Resolutions:</u> 

A RESOLUTION TO ESTABLISH A VEHICLE WEIGHT LIMIT ON BLACK DOG ALLEY PURSUANT TO MD. CODE ANN., TRANSPORTATION § 25-102(A)(11) AND CHAPTER 134 OF THE TALBOT COUNTY CODE (ROADS ORDINANCE) IN ORDER TO REROUTE HEAVY TRUCK “THROUGH TRAFFIC” was read into the record and brought forward for introduction. XII. <u>Consolidated Transportation Plan (CTP) Meeting with Maryland Department of Transportation Representatives</u> <mark>– The Council met with representatives of the Maryland Department of Transportation (MDOT) and State Highway Administration (SHA) on Tuesday, November 7, 2023 at 3:00 p.m. in the Meeting Room of the Talbot County Free Library in Easton.</mark> Representatives of the local delegation were also in attendance.  Secretary of Transportation Paul Wiedefeld stated that his office is committed to _Vision Zero_ – no motor vehicle related fatalities by 2030, and he outlined challenges faced by the Department of Transportation including increasing costs of labor and materials, and the loss of gas tax revenue as cars become increasingly more efficient.  SHA Administrator, William Pines, stated that his department will continue to advance infrastructure projects while placing a greater focus on safety. The representatives, and Ken Fender, District 2 Engineer, Maryland State Highway Administration, provided an update on several projects in Talbot County including new lights at U.S. Rt. 50 at MD Rt. 322 (Easton Bypass); assessment of MD Rt. 328 safety and capacity improvements between U.S. Rt. 50 and Black Dog Alley; bids for a “rail-trail” link on MD Rt. 322 at Glenwood Avenue; new signal at U.S. Rt. 50 and Chapel Road to be activated by the end of November 2023; signal reconfiguration at U.S. Rt. 50 and MD Rt. 309; and change of the flashing signal to a full signal at MD 331 and Black Dog Alley.  Travis Johnson, Maryland Transit Administration Local Transit Support Director, stated that funding in the sum of $123,000 had been appropriated to Delmarva Community Transit for propane filling stations for their propane buses.  Melissa Williams, Maryland Transportation Authority Planning and Program Development Director, briefed the group on the status of toll revenues in the state and the status of the Tier II NEPA (National Environmental Policy Act) Study for the proposed third bridge crossing of the Chesapeake Bay. Chrissy Nizer, Motor Vehicle Administrator, provided an update on average wait times at MVA offices (4 minutes), stated that 75% of transactions are now completed outside branch offices, and advised that driver’s license manuals are now available in 17 languages plus sign language.  She stated that of the 600 vehicle and pedestrian deaths in Maryland in 2022, the majority were caused by the lack of seatbelt use, and impaired or distracted drivers.  She stated that $24,000 in safety grants had been provided to the Talbot County Sheriff’s Office and Easton Police Department. Ashish Solanki, Maryland Aviation Administration (MAA) Regional Aviation Director, stated that BWI Airport has now exceeded 90% of its pre-Pandemic passenger numbers and contributed $9 billion to the State’s economy in 2022; MAA is providing $525,000 toward Easton Airport’s runway extension project.  Bob Munroe, Maryland Port Administration and Environment Deputy Executive Director, stated that Maryland handled a record amount of cargo in FY23 and that cruise ships are leaving the Port of Baltimore at full capacity.   Ray Clarke, County Engineer, called attention to several items listed on the County’s 2023 Priority Listing forwarded to the State in April 2023 and stated that the County needs to work with the Maryland Department of Transportation on various projects including, Black Dog Alley and Goldsborough Neck Road. Representatives of the municipalities in attendance also outlined several projects on which they had partnered with the State or for which they were requesting assistance from the State.  Senator John Mautz and Delegates Christopher Adams and Tom Hutchinson discussed projects or concerns affecting the citizens of Talbot County, including evacuation access out of the Bay Hundred peninsula; substandard work for ADA ramps completed by a contractor in the Town of St. Michaels, how a proposed third Bay Bridge will affect U.S. Rt. 50 traffic in Talbot County, and roadwork required for access to the site of the new Regional Medical Center north of Easton. Council discussion ensued with the representatives as various items were brought forward, particularly those relevant to Talbot County and the Eastern Shore.  The draft CTP budget for FY24 – FY29 totals $21.2 billion.
