VII. <u>Update to Board of Health – Dr. Fahmi Fahmi, Talbot County Health Officer – Dr. Fahmi utilized a</u> PowerPoint presentation to update the Council on various matters related to services provided by the Talbot County Health Department and health-related topics.  He provided statistical information on rabies clinics conducted by the Office of Environmental Health which showed an increase of 278% in vaccinations from 2023 to 2025; vaccination data for August 2024 through June 2025 showing a total of 875 immunizations were administered; and stated that the Health Department held 28 outreach events for CPR, AED and NARCAN training. He briefed the Council on the first Global Vision Clinic for Talbot County, during which 29 Talbot County Public School students received comprehensive eye exams by optometrists and an ophthalmologist and necessary prescriptions were filled free of charge to the students.  Dr. Fahmi stated that the ability of students

to see well not only impacts their academic performance in a positive way but has a positive effect on their social well-being as well. He stated that it is his hope that the clinic will serve as a model for future clinics. He provided an overview of the Mobile Integrated Health Unit (MIH) program whereby personnel from the Health Department and Department of Emergency Services, including paramedics, nurses, and community health professionals, provide in-home healthcare services to senior citizens and those with complex medical conditions. X. <u>Introduction of Legislation:</u> 

A BILL TO ENACT A NEW CHAPTER 35 OF THE TALBOT COUNTY CODE, ENTITLED “COMMUNITY REINVESTMENT AND REPAIR SPECIAL REVENUE FUND,” FOR THE PURPOSES OF ESTABLISHING A COMMUNITY REINVESTMENT AND REPAIR FUND TO RECEIVE MONIES RECEIVED FROM THE STATE CANNABIS COMMUNITY REINVESTMENT AND REPAIR FUND ESTABLISHED PURSUANT TO § 1-3A-03 OF THE ALCOHOLIC BEVERAGES AND CANNABIS ARTICLE OF THE ANNOTATED CODE OF MARYLAND AND ESTABLISHING A TALBOT COUNTY REINVESTMENT AND REPAIR COMMISSION TO ADMINISTER THE SAME was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for introduction.  Prior to introduction, Patrick Thomas, County Attorney, stated that the Cannabis Article establishes a community repair and reinvestment fund which receives its revenue from the sale of cannabis, conversion fees for medical licenses that were converted to “adult use” licenses, and donations, among others.  He stated that a percentage of the revenue received is distributed to each county; the counties are required to enact legislation establishing the purposes for which the funds will be utilized. Mr. Thomas stated that Talbot County currently has received approximately $731,000 in revenue; the proposed legislation requires that the funding be used for community-based initiatives which benefit lowincome communities or serve disproportionately impacted areas, as defined by State law; and administrative expenses the County incurs in administering the fund; the monies cannot be used for law enforcement.  Mr. Thomas cited several examples for possible use of the funding, including after-school programs, behavioral health, housing and homeless prevention initiatives.  He stated that the legislation authorizes use of the funds for the purposes outlined in State law and would also establish a commission to administer the funds; in Talbot County, entity administering the grant application process for the funding would be the members of the Talbot Family Network Board of Directors.  The legislation was introduced by Mr. Callahan, Ms. Haythe, Mr. Lesher, Ms. Mielke, and Mr. Stepp as Bill No. 1620.  A public hearing was scheduled for Tuesday, October 14, 2025, at 5:30 p.m. in the Bradley Meeting Room, South Wing, Talbot County Courthouse, 11 North Washington Street, Easton, Maryland 21601.

- XV. <u>Work Session on Claiborne Water System – The Council met with representatives of the Claiborne</u> community and Brennan Greene, L.E.H.S., Director, Talbot County Office of Environmental Health on Tuesday, September 9, 2025, at 2:30 p.m. in the Bradley Meeting Room to discuss the water system in the village of Claiborne.  Ray Clarke, County Engineer, briefed the Council on the history of the water system, a private two-well system begun over 100 years ago when the village was a stopping off point for steam operated rail cars and ferries and both needed a source of water to continue their journeys.  Once the railroad ceased operations in the community, two water companies, the Bay Hundred Water Company and the Claiborne Water Company began to serve 14 and 28 properties, respectively, both of which have been operated and maintained by several village residents.  Mr. Clarke stated that Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) enforcement actions have become more stringent over the past several years and they now require that any private water system serving 15 or more residential structures is required to have a licensed operator.  Mr. Clarke stated that when the County looked into possibly taking over the water systems, it was determined that however, because of the low number of houses served, the cost to hire a licensed operator and the needed removal of arsenic from the systems would be cost prohibitive to the residents. Mr. Clarke outlined various options which could be considered but stated that, in his opinion, it is his recommendation that Talbot County assist the residents of Claiborne by providing funding for the completion of a Preliminary Engineering Report (PER) under  USDA Rural Development and MDE guidelines at an estimated cost of $35,000 to $50,000;
