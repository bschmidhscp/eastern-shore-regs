## VI. <u>Introduction of Administrative Resolution:</u> 

ADMINISTRATIVE RESOLUTION ESTABLISHING THE OFFICE OF COMMUNITY PARTNERSHIPS FOR TALBOT COUNTY, MARYLAND was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for introduction.  Prior to introduction, Mr. Stamp stated that the administrative resolution is being brought forward for Council’s consideration as a potential solution to growing challenges by non-profits in the Talbot County community to serve individuals experiencing homelessness, hunger and/or other needs.  He stated that nonprofits are seeing an increasing need for services and a decrease in funding from grants, the federal government and the State government, and the County receives numerous requests for funding each year from nonprofits so they can provide services to those in need in the community.  He stated that Council requested that staff devise a process to ensure that funding provided by the County gets to the right place and the right people.  Mr. Stamp then outlined the framework by which the Office of Community Partnerships, if approved, would be structured to provide efficient and effective oversight, alignment, and sustainable community impact of County funding provided to various community nonprofits and agencies.  He stated that the County would benefit from a budgetary standpoint as budget requests to Council would be prioritized and coordinated by the proposed Office of Community Partnerships prior to coming to Council.  Nancy Andrew, Executive Director, Talbot Family Network, Andy Hollis, Executive Director, Neighborhood Service Center, the County’s Community Action Agency, and Linda Webb, Director, Talbot County Department of Social Services provided Council with a snapshot of what they see happening in the community with the individuals served by their organizations, concurring with Mr. Stamp’s statements that the need is becoming greater, while funding is remaining flat or decreasing.  Each of the representatives expressed their support for the establishment of a coordinating office that all the nonprofits can work through collectively to ensure that they are not competing with each other, not duplicating services, and are providing the best services they can to the citizens of Talbot County.  Upon motion by Mr. Stepp, seconded by Ms. Mielke, the Council approved the administrative resolution by voting 4 – 0 – 1 as follows: 

Mr. Callahan – Aye (via telephone) Mr. Stepp – Aye Mr. Lesher – Aye

## VIII. <u>Public Hearing:</u> 

Bill No. 1624, A BILL TO AMEND CHAPTER 190 (ZONING, SUBDIVISION, AND LAND DEVELOPMENT) OF THE TALBOT COUNTY CODE FOR THE PURPOSES OF PROVIDING THAT CANNABIS GROWING AND THE PROCESSING OF CANNABIS GROWN ON-SITE SHALL BE PERMITTED BY SPECIAL EXCEPTION IN THE AGRICULTURAL CONSERVATION, COUNTRYSIDE PRESERVATION, AND WESTERN RURAL CONSERVATION ZONING DISTRICTS, PROVIDING THAT THE PROCESSING OF CANNABIS GROWN OFF-SITE SHALL BE PERMITTED BY SPECIAL EXCEPTION IN THE VILLAGE

MIXED, GENERAL COMMERCIAL, AND LIMITED INDUSTRIAL ZONING DISTRICTS, AND PROVIDING THAT CANNABIS DISPENSARIES SHALL BE PERMITTED BY SPECIAL EXCEPTION IN THE LIMITED COMMERCIAL, GENERAL COMMERCIAL, AND LIMITED INDUSTRIAL ZONING DISTRICTS, EACH SUBJECT TO CERTAIN CONDITIONS, AND DEFINING TERMS ASSOCIATED THEREWITH, was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for public hearing.  Prior to the public hearing, County Attorney, Patrick Thomas, stated that the purpose of the legislation is to regulate cannabis growing, processing, and dispensaries in Talbot County. He stated that the legislation proposes 1,000-foot setbacks from residential and institutional uses, including schools; increases the minimum lot size requirement to 75 acres, both of which are a significant increase over the previous legislation requiring 500-foot setbacks and a minimum lot size of 20 acres, respectively. Mr. Thomas stated that the dispensary requirements are as strict as they can be under current State law; the legislation provides that growing, processing and dispensaries are only permitted by Special Exception through the Board of Appeals.  Council discussion ensued with Mr. Thomas who clarified that should the County choose not to enact cannabis legislation State law would control and is much more liberal than the provisions in Bill No. 1624.  Members of the public were afforded an opportunity to comment on the legislation.  Bill No. 1624 is eligible for vote on Tuesday, March 24, 2026.
