VII. <u>Update on Leeds Creek (Tunis Mills) Bridge Project – Ray Clarke, County Engineer; Mike</u> Mertaugh, Assistant County Engineer – Mr. Clarke provided a brief history of the project to replace the Leeds Creek (Tunis Mills) Bridge.  He stated that the County held a public meeting on August 7, 2024, at which time County staff and the representatives of consultant for the project, Wallace Montgomery & Associates, utilized a PowerPoint presentation to provide information and displays of the proposed new bridge and addressed questions, comments and concerns from citizens in attendance.  He stated that the State of Maryland normally inspects bridges on a biennial basis (every two years); however, the Leeds Creek (Tunis Mills) Bridge is now being inspected on an annual basis as it has been deemed to be structurally deficient.  Mr. Clarke stated that since the public meeting, County staff has been working with York Bridge Concepts, the company contacted by citizens in the Tunis Mills area who are interested in replacing the bridge with an all-wooden structure, including the pilings.  Mr. Clarke stated that County staff met with the engineer for York Bridge Concepts to specifically discuss the pilings for the proposed new bridge.  He stated that Maryland State Highway Administration regulations prohibit utilizing spliced timber piles; in addition, no federal funding would be forthcoming if timber piles were used.  Mr. Clarke stated that the information had been provided to the interested residents of Tunis Mills and surrounding vicinity.  The current design includes using steel pilings filled with reinforced concrete with a concrete cap as the base of the bridge, with a laminated deck and a timber wearing surface. Utilizing the current design, the new bridge is estimated to cost $6.5M, with 80% of funding coming from federal aid.  Based on confirmation with the State Highway Administration, if the design is switched to an all-timber bridge where the timbe piles would need to be spiced, the project would no longer be eligible for federal funding.  The County would be burdened with the full price of the project, which would be an estimated $5.9M.  Mr. Clarke then showed a short video his office had received of the timber decking of the current bridge and outlined his concerns about using the same material for the replacement bridge and his recommendations for materials for same: steel pilings, concrete caps, a laminated deck, timber wearing surface, and timber rails.  He stated that the concrete could be stamped and colored to appear as if it were wood.  Council discussion ensued with Mr. Clarke and Mr. Mertaugh regarding specifics of the proposed design of the new bridge.  Mr. Clarke and Mr. Mertaugh then reviewed the proposed costs for the new bridge, with and without 80% federal funding.  Council members expressed their appreciation to Mr. Clarke, Mr. Mertaugh, and to County staff for their work on the proposed project and for their outreach to county citizens.  Upon motion by Mr. Lesher, seconded by Ms. Haythe, the Council approved moving forward with the current design and the materials as outlined by Mr. Clarke by voting 5 – 0 as follows: 

Mr. Callahan – Aye Mr. Stepp – Aye Mr. Lesher – Aye Ms. Mielke – Aye Ms. Haythe - Aye
