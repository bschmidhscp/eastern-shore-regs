## VIII. <u>Introduction of Legislation:</u> 

A BILL TO AMEND CHAPTER 190 (ZONING, SUBDIVISION, AND LAND DEVELOPMENT) OF THE TALBOT COUNTY CODE FOR THE PURPOSE OF PROVIDING THAT NOTICE OF PUBLIC HEARINGS ON NEW AND RENEWAL SHORT-TERM RENTAL LICENSES SHALL BE PROVIDED TO OWNERS OF ALL PROPERTIES WITH A PROPERTY LINE WITHIN 1,000 FEET, INCLUDING IN LINE OF SIGHT WITHIN 1,000 FEET ACROSS WATERWAYS OF THE SHORT-TERM RENTAL DWELLING, OR THE 50 PROPERTIES NEAREST TO THE SHORTTERM RENTAL DWELLING, WHICHEVER IS LESS was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for introduction.  Prior to introduction, County Attorney, Patrick Thomas, stated that the proposed legislation had been requested by Mr. Stepp.  Mr. Thomas stated that currently, public hearing requirements for new and renewal short-term rental license applications are to be sent to owners of all properties with a property line within 1,000 feet, including a line of sight within 1,000 feet across waterways, of the proposed short-term rental dwelling.  He stated that the proposed text amendment would permit notices to be sent to those properties or to the 50 properties nearest to the dwelling, whichever is less.  Bryce Yelton, Assistant Planning Officer, stated that similar legislation had been introduced in May 2026 and the Planning Commission had reviewed it, as well as other short-term rental legislation in the past calendar year.  Mr. Yelton stated that it was the Planning Commission’s recommendation to do a distance calculation, by restricting the distance to which notices have to be sent to avoid having to send out so many notices; the current distance is 1,000 feet; it was the Planning Commission’s recommendation to consider 500 or 750 feet versus a strict cap on the number of individuals to which notices would be sent. Council discussion ensued with Mr. Yelton and Mr. Thomas.  Mr. Lesher requested that the Office of Law draft amendments to the proposed legislation which would incorporate the Planning Commission’s previous recommendations on the standard of review, composition of the Short-Term Rental Review Board, etc. The legislation was introduced by Mr. Callahan, Ms. Haythe, Mr. Lesher, Ms. Mielke, and Mr. Stepp as Bill No. 1653.  A public hearing was scheduled for Tuesday, October 13, 2026, at 5:30 p.m.

## IX. <u>Public Hearings:</u> 

TALBOT COUNTY COMMUNITY REPAIR AND REINVESTMENT FUND (CRRF) DISTRIBUTION PLAN was brought forward for public hearing.  Prior to the public hearing, Nancy Andrew, Director, Office of Community Partnerships, and Executive Director, Talbot Family Network, the Local Management Board, stated that Bill No. 1620, approved by the Council in October 2025, tasked the Local Management Board, the Talbot Family Network Board of Directors, with administration of monies received from the Talbot County Community Repair and Reinvestment Fund (CRRF).  Ms. Andrew stated that the CRRF was created by the Maryland General Assembly in 2023 for the purpose of reinvesting cannabis revenue in communities disproportionately impacted by past cannabis enforcement and related disparities.  She stated that in 2025, the Maryland General Assembly approved legislation requesting that local jurisdictions develop a distribution plan, in consultation with their communities, and instituted a deadline of October 1, 2026, to have the Distribution Plans in place.  Ms. Andrew outlined efforts to date toward the drafting of a Distribution Plan which identifies three initial priority areas:  economic stability and mobility, housing stability and child development and educational opportunity, while recognizing behavioral health, transportation, food security, and access to services as important contributing factors supported by data.  Council discussion ensued with Ms. Andrew.  Members of the public were then afforded an opportunity to comment on the Draft Distribution Plan.  Written comments on the Draft Plan will be accepted until Friday, September 18, 2026.  The final Distribution Plan will be scheduled for approval at the Council meeting on Tuesday, September 22, 2026.

XIV. <u>Work Session with Talbot County Agricultural Community – The Council met with Shannon Dill,</u> D.B.A., Assistant Director of Operations and Principal Agent, University of Maryland Extension – Talbot County; John Swaine, III, Vice President, Talbot County Farm Bureau and Chairman, Talbot Soil Conservation District; Shawn Smith, District Manager, Soil Conservation District.  Also in attendance were Nicole Walker, Area Extension Director for Talbot, Dorchester and Wicomico counties and Extension Advisory Council members, Ryan Snow and Laura Heikes.  Ms. Dill briefed the Council on current vacancies at the Extension Office for an Agricultural Agent and Nutrient Management and stated that agents from other offices on the Eastern Shore are currently filling in until the positions are filled.  She updated the Council on activities of the Talbot County Corn Club, started through 4-H, and now in its 75<sup>th</sup> year and the annual Corn Club Banquet which is scheduled for January 6, 2027.  Mr. Swaine stated that Farm Bureau continues to seek support for its noxious weed program to keep weeds such as Johnson Grass and Thistle under control.  Mr. Lesher inquired about new interest in farming as a vocation – Mr. Swaine stated that a new generation seems interested in pursuing farming; Ms. Heikes stated that the Easton High School FFA (Future Farmers of America) is very active.  Mr. Smith stated that the Talbot Soil Conservation District began in 1958 and each District in the state is tasked with agricultural conservation, urban and erosion control and education outreach.  He provided statistical information as to the acreage under cover crop in Talbot County, excluding harvest grains, in 2025 (36,000); stated that 46 conservation plans were written for approximately 7,000 acres in 2025; stated that his office had worked with 21 farmers to implement 37 practices; and outlined the criteria under which the completion an erosion sediment control plan is required and is approved by the Soil Conservation Office; after which the Maryland Department of the Environment inspects the projects.  Mr. Smith stated that 160 erosion sediment control plans were reviewed and completed in 2025 and outlined the Soil Conservation District’s partnership with the Talbot County Department of Public Works for approval of stormwater management plans for agricultural structures. With regard to education outreach, Mr. Smith stated that the Soil Conservation District office is supporting the Easton High School FFA chapter and is hoping to reinstitute the Talbot Envirothon which provides an opportunity for high school students to meet with and learn from professionals in the industry, including the Maryland Department of Natural Resources, Maryland Department of the Environment, and soil and wetland scientists, and to participate in a local competition.  He stated that the winner would move on to the State competition; the winner of the State competition would participate in the national competition.  Mr. Smith will provide more information to the Council as the program progresses.  Mr. Smith stated that Kyle Hutchison will be recognized in the near future for his 20 years of volunteer service on the Talbot Soil Conservation District Board of Supervisors and Mildred Bullock will be recognized as the 2025 Cooperator of the Year on Wednesday, October 14, 2026.  Mr. Smith concluded his presentation by briefing the Council on the Whole Watershed Act, passed by the Maryland General Assembly in 2024.  He stated that the five-year pilot program targets five watersheds throughout the state which focus on bringing positive change to their particular watershed in a short period of time.  He stated that the Talbot Soil Conservation District has partnered with ShoreRivers and Envision the Choptank; Envision the Choptank’s application for the Upper Choptank Watershed was selected for additional funding with a focus on seeing what milestones can be met which provide a meaningful impact for reducing nitrogen and phosphorus in the watershed which can then be utilized throughout the state.  Council discussion ensued with the representatives as various items were brought forward.
