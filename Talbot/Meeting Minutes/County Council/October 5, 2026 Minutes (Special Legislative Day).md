# TALBOT COUNTY, MARYLAND 

County Council **MINUTES** October 5, 2026 

_SPECIAL LEGISLATIVE DAY_ 

Present – President Chuck Callahan, Vice President Pete Lesher, Keasha N. Haythe, Lynn L. Mielke, Dave Stepp, County Manager Clay Stamp and County Attorney Patrick Thomas. 

- I. <u>Agenda – Mr. Callahan requested and received unanimous consent for approval of the Agenda for</u> Monday, October 5, 2026. 

- II. <u>Disbursements – Mr. Callahan requested and received unanimous consent for approval of the</u> Disbursements of Tuesday, September 29, 2026. 

- III.      Introduction of Legislation: 

A BILL TO AMEND CHAPTER 24 (BOARDS, COMMITTEES AND COMMISSIONS) OF THE TALBOT COUNTY CODE FOR THE PURPOSE OF REPEALING AND REENACTING ARTICLE II (BOARD OF TOURISM) TO ESTABLISH A NEW BOARD OF ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT AND TOURISM AND SET FORTH THE MEMBERSHIP, DUTIES, AND FUNCTIONS THEREOF was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for introduction.  Prior to introduction, County Attorney Patrick Thomas, stated that the purpose of the legislation is to consolidate the existing Economic Development Commission and the Tourism Board into a single board, the Board of Economic Development and Tourism.  He stated that if approved, the legislation will take effect on March 1, 2027.  Ms. Haythe stated that the Departments of Economic Development and the Tourism Office were combined in 2016; however, the boards were not combined at that time.  She stated that since we the County has a new director, she had requested that the Office of Law draft legislation which would combine the boards to align with the guidelines of the IEDC (International Economic Development Council).  She stated that if approved, the legislation would not become effective until March 1, 2027, in order to allow time for the new combined board to be in place.  Ms. Mervine, read the following statement into the record: 

_"Good evening, Council members and County leadership. Before I begin, I wanted to be very clear that I am deeply grateful for the dedicated service both boards have provided. Their commitment, experience, and longstanding contributions have laid the foundation for the work we are discussing tonight_ **_,_** _and we sincerely appreciate every member who serves and continues to serve.  Their efforts have brought Talbot County to where it is today, and this proposed structure builds on their important work, not replacing it. I’m also here to talk about what the forward-looking steps for our county look like. The creation of this unified board will help us modernize our approach, align with the practices as nationally recognized standards from IEDC, the International Economic Development Council.  We went by their recommendations and the industry sectors quite closely, and I believe it will strengthen our ability to compete, collaborate, and grow."_

## <u>Update from Talbot County:</u> 

Clay Stamp introduced Stephanie Mervine, the new Director of the Talbot County Department of Economic Development and Tourism.  Mr. Stamp stated that Ms. Mervine replaces Cassandra Vanhooser who recently accepted a position with the State as the Managing Director of the Maryland Department of Commerce Office of Tourism.  He expressed his appreciation to Ms. Vanhooser for her work with the Economic Development Commission and the Tourism Board on developing a Strategic Plan to move the County forward and to Kristen Goller for stepping up and doing a great job after Ms. Vanhooser left.  He  stated Ms. Mervine is a native of Wicomico County who has lived throughout the country and is an accomplished economic development professional who brings a wealth of experience to Talbot County.  Ms. Mervine stated that during her first 30 days in the job she will be on a listening tour, incorporating some of the best practices she has learned throughout her career in order to adapt them to Talbot County’s needs based on the Strategic Plan.  Ms. Mervine stated that the Department of Economic Development and Tourism’s BRE (Business, Retention and Expansion) Program will go live on September 30, 2026; the Program, which she utilized as the Economic Development Director for three counties when she worked in Oklahoma, involves working with the businesses in each community to learn how they have done in the past, how they are doing at present and what they hope to be doing in the future, to understand the uniqueness of Talbot County, and to work with elected officials of the towns and other partners, including the Chamber of Commerce, to learn where the attraction to that particular community is and to learn where there are gaps; the information gathered will be entered into a database. She stated that it will take approximately a year to meet with everyone and to begin to see trends which can then be correlated to the Strategic Plan.  Ms. Mervine invited everyone to an event the Department of Economic Development and Tourism will be hosting in recognition of Economic Development Week in Maryland.  The event will be held at The Ivy in Easton on Thursday, October 1, 2026, from 7:30 a.m. to 9:30 a.m. Mr. Stamp stated that economic development and tourism is all of us and the County wants to collaborate with the municipalities on projects we can do together.

## <u>Updates from Towns:</u> 

<u>Easton – Mayor Cook provided an update on downtown Easton traffic light upgrades and</u> pedestrian crossing signals.  She stated that work should be completed at the corner of Washington St. and Goldsborough Streets prior to the Waterfowl Festival  (November 13 – 15, 2026); paving has been completed on the Rails-to-Trails extension through Moton Park; and work continues on the West Glenwood Avenue stream restoration project.  She encouraged everyone to attend Fall Kick-off activities in Easton on October 3 – 4, 2026 – pumpkins will be on display and there are events for everyone.  She stated that the Comprehensive Plan will go before the Town Council in November and is anticipated to be completed by the end of 2026; the ribbon-cutting for Vetagro in Mistletoe Hall Commerce Business Park is scheduled for the week of October 5<sup>th</sup> and a recent ribbon-cutting was held for Easton Utilities’ new well.  Mayor Cook stated that the Brookletts building had been sold, the Town will be moving to OpenGov in approximately six months, the Easton Fire Department had held its first training at its Emergency Services Training Campus and encouraged everyone to stop by the _Who Framed You_ shop on Harrison Street to view the work of the  artist who is painting the crab for the New Year’s Eve Crab Crawl, a new event for the town. Mr. Abbatiello expressed his appreciation to Ms. Mielke for her service to the citizens of Talbot County over the past four years and for her support and wished Mr. Lesher and Mr. Stepp good luck in the November elections.  He expressed his appreciation to the County for their consideration to increase the LOSAP (Lenth of Service Award Program) for volunteer firefighters, which, in his opinion, is important to helping the firefighters in Talbot County remain an all-volunteer force; tax incentives, home purchase assistance, college tuition reimbursement have previously been established.

<u>Queen Anne – Mayor Starkey stated that the Town is hoping to purchase a 2-acre waterfront</u> property on Main Street in Queen Anne for use as a public access point and kayak launch.  He stated that 5,000 square feet of the property is in Queen Anne’s County with the remainder in Talbot County; the Town has submitted a grant application to the Department of Natural Resources (DNR) but stated that the process can be long and the Town will be requesting Council’s consideration to use $75,000 in County Program Open Space (POS) funding for the project.  Mr. Starkey expressed his appreciation to Talbot County for their assistance with the paving of potholes on Chairs Alley in Queen Anne.  He stated that the Town has had several issues with new residents doing major renovations without the Town having plans for the renovations; several stop-work orders have been issued. 

<u>Oxford – Ms. Wahl stated that the Commissioners adopted the Town’s 2025 Comprehensive Plan</u> in August.  She then provided updates on the following projects:  the Oxford Inn is anticipated to complete work on the building in the next several months - the owners have requested to renew their permit for another six months due to substantial issues with the foundation of the building; once work is completed, the Inn will be used to provide a boutique hospitality experience; Robert Morris Inn – Ms. Wahl stated that the building remains in foreclosure and bankruptcy proceedings, but the bank recently authorized funding for stabilization of the building; Critical Infrastructure Upgrades – Ms. Wahl stated that the Town is prioritizing in-house staff capability versus contracting out work; the Town is constructing a test well to provide a redundant water source in order to increase the town’s water capacity while also reducing water loss, completing arsenic removal, water system upgrades and addressing longstanding stormwater projects.  She expressed her appreciation to the County Council and the County Manger for their support to remedy the flooding issues on the Oxford Causeway, the only ingress and egress to the town.  Ms. Wahl stated that the Town is working on requesting a three-year $1.6 million NOAA (National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration) earmark to fund a study on coastal resiliency; she stated that the Town is continually assessing its risk and exposure through several public/private partnerships to ensure that all agreements are serving the public interest. 

<u>St. Michaels – Mr. Richardson stated that the town of St. Michaels had approximately 10,500</u> visitors per week this past summer.  He stated that a 29-unit townhouse development project is in the planning stages and the Town is working closely with the developer and architectural team to ensure that the architecture is a good fit for St. Michaels; the development project will be located at the entrance to the town, adjacent to the former Suburban Propane property.  He stated that the developer requested that the Town waive the fee and in lieu of requirements for trees which need to be removed; however, the Town did not do so and will now be working with the developer to plant approximately 500 trees throughout the town.  Mr. Richardson expressed his appreciation to Brian Moore, Superintendent of Roads and County Facilities, and the Talbot County Sheriff’s Office for their assistance with moving a boat off the road which had been at that location for a good while. Mr. Richardson provided an update on several Town projects to protect housing and the community, including a third well to ensure the town has enough availability of water during its busiest months; he stated that the Town has submitted grant requests for federal and State funding for four shovel-ready projects to combat sea level rise and climate change; the Town has also received support from the Department of Natural Resources for bulkheads and public boatslips; he stated that the Rails-to-Trails extension will be completed in the near future and the Town is in the final stages of completing the NEPA (National Environmental Policy Act) process; the Town is working with the Public County Land Conservancy and Eastern Shore Land Conservancy on the San Domingo Creek Park project – which includes a new pier, installation of meadows and reforesting a portion of the property while still maintaining open space; the entrance will be off West Chew Avenue and parking will be located off Boundary Lane.
