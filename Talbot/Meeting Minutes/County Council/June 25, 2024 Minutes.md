Certificate of Recognition to Talbot Agriculture and Education Center for 60<sup>th</sup> <u>Anniversary – The</u> Clerk read a certificate of recognition into the record which spoke of the establishment of the Talbot Agriculture and Education Center in 1964 for the purpose of strengthening and preserving Talbot County’s rural culture through the hosting of events for agriculturally oriented organizations

FEE SIMPLE OWNERS, was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for public hearing.  Prior to the public hearing, County Attorney, Patrick Thomas, stated that the Segars Corner Road is a very short road which is an alternate connector between Lewistown Road and Coveys Landing Road.  He stated that the roadway is in very bad condition and would require significant expense to upgrade and maintain to County road standards; the resolution proposes to abandon the road and convey the underlying land to the property owners on each side. Resolution No 359, A RESOLUTION TO PLACE A QUESTION ON THE BALLOT AT THE NOVEMBER 2024 GENERAL ELECTION TO AMEND § 614 OF THE CHARTER OF TALBOT COUNTY TO ALLOW, BUT NOT REQUIRE, THE COUNTY COUNCIL OF TALBOT COUNTY TO ADD UP TO ONE CENT (1¢) PER ONE HUNDRED DOLLARS OF ASSESSED VALUE ABOVE THE REVENUE CAP FOR AN ADDITIONAL FIVE (5) YEARS BEGINNING JULY 1, 2026, was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for public hearing.  Prior to the public hearing, County Attorney, Patrick Thomas, stated that the purpose of the proposed Charter amendment outlined in Resolution No. 359 would extend the provisions of the Charter amendment outlined in Resolution No. 288 approved by the voters of Talbot County at the 2020 General Election for another five (5) years, beginning July 1, 2026; revenues raised would be used for Public Safety.  Mr. Thomas stated that Resolution No. 288 permitted, but did not require, the Council to add up to one cent ($.01) per $100 of assessed value above the revenue cap for a period of five (5) years, beginning July 1, 2021.  Mr. Lesher stated that he wished to introduce an amendment to Resolution No. 359, originally offered by Mr. Stepp, which would extend the provisions for seven (7) years instead of five (5) so that the provisions would expire in a presidential election year when the ballot is less crowded, instead of a Councilmanic election year, when the ballot is crowded.  The amendment, known as Amendment No. 1, was introduced by Mr. Callahan, Ms. Haythe, Mr. Lesher, and Ms. Mielke.  Members of the public were then afforded an opportunity to comment on Resolution No. 359 and Amendment No. 1.  Martha Sparks, Finance Director, stated that the additional one cent ($.01) has augmented funding to provide 26 positions to date, including paramedics, School Resource Officers (SROs) EMTs, 9-1-1 Operators, and supplemental retirement programs - Law Enforcement Officers Pension System (LEOPS) and Correctional Officers Retirement System (CORS), vehicles and increased funding for the county’s volunteer fire departments.  The Council approved Amendment No. 1 by voting 5 – 0 as follows: 

Mr. Callahan – Aye Mr. Stepp – Aye (via absentee ballot) Mr. Lesher – Aye Ms. Mielke – Aye Ms. Haythe - Aye 

Resolution No. 359, as amended, was then brought forward for vote.  The Council approved Resolution No. 359, as amended, by voting 5 – 0 as follows:
