IV. <u>Council Discussion with Trappe Town Attorney Lyndsey Ryan Regarding Allegations Involving Paris Foods’ Municipal Sewer Connection – Lyndsey Ryan, Attorney for the Town of Trappe, and</u> Brian Schmidt, President, Trappe Town Council, briefed the Council on the process and timeframe by which Paris Foods, a long-standing business in the town of Trappe, came to be connected to the Town of Trappe wastewater treatment plant.  Ms. Ryan stated that Paris Foods approached the Town of Trappe in January 2021 to discuss the possibility of connecting to the Town’s wastewater treatment plant as well as other options for providing sewer service to the property.  She stated that at the time of the request, Paris Foods had a discharge permit from the Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) for their own septic system which was due to be renewed in 2022.   She stated that following the Town Engineer’s review of data relating to the discharge from Paris Foods, it was determined that the discharge was of a level and magnitude capable of being treated by the Town’s wastewater treatment plant.  She stated that the MDE permit for the force main showing the connection of Paris Foods to the Town of Trappe wastewater treatment plant was made in accordance with the maps outlined in Resolution No. 281, as amended and approved by the Council in August 2020, which, in the Town’s opinion, showed the Paris Foods property as “S-1”.  She stated that in August 2021, the Town entered into an agreement to provide 16 equivalent dwelling units (EDUs) to Paris Foods for connection to the Town’s wastewater treatment plant.  Ms. Ryan stated that it has been brought to the Town’s attention that following corrective measures to Resolution No. 281, the Paris Foods property is shown as “unprogrammed”.  As such, the Town has submitted an application to amend the County’s Comprehensive Water and Sewer Plan to reclassify the subject property to “S-1”.  Council discussion ensued with Ms. Ryan and Mr. Schmidt regarding various facets related to the connection of Paris Foods to the Town’s wastewater treatment plant, including the timeframe between learning of the connection of the “unprogrammed” property and notification to the County, any MDE permit violations since connection of Paris Foods to the wastewater treatment plant and the process moving forward in order to avoid connection inconsistencies from happening in the future.

- IX. <u>Introduction of Administrative Resolutions:</u> 

An ADMINISTRATIVE RESOLUTION ADOPTING AND APPROVING A GENERAL FUND RESERVE POLICY FOR TALBOT COUNTY, MARYLAND was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for introduction.  Martha Sparks, Finance Director, stated that the purpose of a Fund Balance is to provide financial resilience to mitigate the impacts of unanticipated revenue shortfalls and/or unexpected expenditures.  She stated that historically, Talbot County has budgeted very conservatively by underestimating revenues and overestimating expenditures and thus has been able to maintain a Fund Balance although the County does not currently have a formal reserve policy.  She stated that maintaining and adhering to a reserve policy is a best practice recognized by the Government Finance Officers Association (GFOA) and is also a factor by which rating agencies determine an entity’s creditworthiness. She stated that having a formal reserve policy also enables the County to obtain lower interest rates on borrowed funds.   Ms. Sparks outlined the process by which the proposed policy was developed in conjunction with the County’s financial advisor, Davenport & Company, and reviewed by the County’s bond counsel. Council discussion ensued with Ms. Sparks.  Upon motion by Mr. Lesher, seconded by Ms. Haythe, the Council approved the administrative resolution by voting 5 – 0 as follows:
