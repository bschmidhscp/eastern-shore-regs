- IV. <u>Update to Board of Health – Dr. Fahmi Fahmi, Talbot County Health Officer – Dr. Fahmi utilized a</u> PowerPoint presentation to update the Council, sitting as the Board of Health, on the following services provided by, and initiatives of, the Talbot County Health Department:  (1) Senior Services – Dr. Fahmi provided statistical information on the number of senior citizens in Talbot County who receive various services from the Health Department, including through the Senior Care program, nurse monitoring, screenings and evaluations.  He noted various partnerships to help enable expansion of senior services, including Health Department employees’ participation with the Maryland Department of Health’s Office of Long Term Supports and Services to facilitate the employees’ certification as statewide trainers for the Inter-Resident Assessment Instrument Home Care and Pediatric (interRAI – HC) assessment tools, a standardized system used to evaluate and coordinate care for individuals with complex health needs; coordination with Talbot County Emergency Medical Services on the Mobile Integrated Health program; the participation of Senior Services Director, Carrie Ottey, on the Talbot County Senior Citizen Task Force, and the purchase of a van currently being modified to accommodate transport of handicapped individuals; (2) Sustainable Transportation – receipt of an electric vehicle from Baltimore to provide expanded delivery of services and outreach in the county in a more efficient, eco-friendly manner; (3) Family Services Program – training of 23 doulas in the community for the Maternal Child Health Program which has provided 784 home visits for the Healthy Families Program; the Early Head Start Program currently serves 28 children and families; Easton High School is in the process of developing a vegetable garden which will be located on the Easton Elementary School campus to both support nutrition and education on healthier foods for the Healthy Families Program; the

- VII. <u>Presentation by Easton Utilities on Talbot County Rural Broadband Project,</u> _<u>Connect Talbot</u>_ – John Horner, President & CEO, Easton Utilities; Ted Book, Vice President, Easton Velocity – Mr. Horner utilized a PowerPoint presentation to update the Council on the _Connect Talbot_ project, an initiative to ensure that unserved residents of Talbot County have access to broadband.  He stated that as of now, the project is complete, and as such, Talbot County is the first fully funded county in the state of Maryland and is the first county to provide access to all citizens.  Mr. Horner stated that Easton Velocity, a service of Easton Utilities, completed 360 miles of mainline fiber throughout the county, and to date, 2,300 customers have signed up for service.  Mr. Horner provided a brief timeline of the project which began in 2016 when the State of Maryland formed the Rural Broadband Task Force.  He stated that in 2017 Talbot County issued a Request for Interest, and in 2019 Easton Utilities was chosen to partner with Talbot County on the project for extension of broadband access.  He stated that following approval by the Town of Easton to move forward, Easton Utilities entered into a formal agreement with Talbot County, whereby the County provided

<!-- page 4 -->

$3 million in funding as matching funds to initiate the project.  He stated that in 2020 Easton Utilities received $14 million in United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) Reconnect Grant funding and construction began; additional grant funding was received from the Federal Communications Commission (FCC), USDA, the State of Maryland and the American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) as a result of the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic; total grant funding received was $35 million, with the total cost of the project $45 million; the total County investment was over $5 million, including grant funding.  Mr. Book provided a timelapse video of the installation of fiber outlining preexisting service in 2021 to present coverage.  He stated that customers who are interested in being connected are encouraged to do so while grant funding remains available; individuals can determine their eligibility for service at: https://eastonvelocity.com/connecttalbot/ . ## VIII. <u>Eligible for Vote:</u> 

Bill No. 1624, A BILL TO AMEND CHAPTER 190 (ZONING, SUBDIVISION, AND LAND DEVELOPMENT) OF THE TALBOT COUNTY CODE FOR THE PURPOSES OF PROVIDING THAT CANNABIS GROWING AND THE PROCESSING OF CANNABIS GROWN ON-SITE SHALL BE PERMITTED BY SPECIAL EXCEPTION IN THE AGRICULTURAL CONSERVATION, COUNTRYSIDE PRESERVATION, AND WESTERN RURAL CONSERVATION ZONING DISTRICTS, PROVIDING THAT THE PROCESSING OF CANNABIS GROWN OFF-SITE SHALL BE PERMITTED BY SPECIAL EXCEPTION IN THE VILLAGE MIXED, GENERAL COMMERCIAL, AND LIMITED INDUSTRIAL ZONING DISTRICTS, AND PROVIDING THAT CANNABIS DISPENSARIES SHALL BE PERMITTED BY SPECIAL EXCEPTION IN THE LIMITED COMMERCIAL, GENERAL COMMERCIAL, AND LIMITED INDUSTRIAL ZONING DISTRICTS, EACH SUBJECT TO CERTAIN CONDITIONS, AND DEFINING TERMS ASSOCIATED THEREWITH, was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for vote.  Prior to the vote, County Attorney, Patrick Thomas, stated that, if approved, the legislation would allow for the growing and processing of cannabis in the county, as well as allow for cannabis dispensaries in the county, all by Special Exception only with approval from the Board of Appeals.   He stated that the provisions for growing and processing of cannabis are much stricter than in previous legislation considered by the Council, which provides for 1,000-foot setbacks from residential and institutional uses, including schools and increases the minimum lot size requirement to 75 acres, both of which are a significant increase over the previous legislation requiring 500-foot setbacks and a minimum lot size of 20 acres.  He stated that the provisions outlined in the legislation for dispensaries are as strict as are permitted under State law;  Council discussion ensued.  Mr. Callahan stated that, in his opinion, although Council had done a good job with the legislation, he still could not vote for it.  Mr. Stepp stated that he would be voting in favor of the legislation because if the Council doesn’t enact its own legislation, the County’s regulations will default to the State regulations which are much more liberal; Ms. Mielke and Ms. Haythe concurred with Mr. Stepp’s comments. The Council approved Bill No. 1624 by voting 4 – 1 as follows: 

Mr. Callahan – Nay 

Mr. Stepp – Aye
