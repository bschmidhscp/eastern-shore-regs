- IV. <u>Update from University of Maryland Shore Regional Health Project – Ken Kozel, MBA, FACHE,</u> President, University of Maryland Shore Regional Health – Mr. Kozel updated the Council on the following:  (1) Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) star ratings for Shore Regional Health – He stated that the CMS star ratings are national, publicly reported ratings which measure five key elements of performance:  mortality, safety, readmissions, patient experience and timely care.  Mr. Kozel stated that for the timeframe of July 2024 to July 2025, the Chester River Hospital (the University of Maryland Shore Medical Center in Chestertown) received five stars out of five; Easton Hospital (the University of Maryland Shore Medical Center at Easton) received four stars out of five; noting that only 12% of hospitals in the nation receive five stars and only 29% receive four stars; (2) Sale of Easton Hospital property at 219 S. Washington Street – Mr. Kozel stated that Shore Regional Health has entered into a Purchase and Sale Agreement with Bluepoint Living for the hospital property located on 219 S. Washington Street in Easton but will retain ownership of the property and building until the closing period which he stated is 60 days from the date Easton Hospital relocates to the new regional medical center facility, anticipated to be on or before December 31, 2028.  Mr. Kozel stated that the proceeds from the sale of the S. Washington Street property will be used to help offset the cost of the new facility; (3) Construction Progress – New Regional Medical Center – Mr. Kozel stated that the 1,728 wells of the geothermal well system on the new hospital property site have been vertically dug and connection to the utility plant located at the back of the hospital facility has begun.  He stated that the state-of-the-art geothermal system will ensure that the appropriate temperatures and humidity levels are maintained throughout the regional medical center complex while also providing reduced operational costs.  Mr. Kozel stated that the new regional medical center, which has a six-story East tower and a five-story West tower, will have had concrete flooring laid by the end of June, with exterior panels beginning to enclose the structure; once enclosed, interior work will begin.  He stated that construction of the intersection

- V. <u>Presentation of Certificate of Recognition to Cassandra Vanhooser – Prior to presentation of the</u> certificate of recognition, Ms. Vanhooser, who currently serves as the County’s Director of the Department of Economic Development and Tourism, stated that she had accepted a job with the State of Maryland as Managing Director of the Maryland Department of Commerce, Office of Tourism.  She expressed her appreciation to the Council for their support over the past 13 ½ years, stating that she always tried to give her all for Talbot County every day.  She cited several projects during her tenure with the County, including the ongoing construction of the new regional medical center, the Easton Airport Modernization Project, and Talbot County having the distinction of being the first county in Maryland to make broadband available to every citizen.  Council members expressed their appreciation to Ms. Vanhooser for her service to Talbot County and wished her the best in her new role with the State.  The Clerk then read a certificate of recognition into the record which spoke of the professionalism, energy, enthusiasm, and knowledge Ms. Vanhooser brought to her position with Talbot County, first as the Director of the Office of Tourism, and for the last 10 years as the Director of the Department of Economic Development and Tourism, partnering with businesses, community organizations and governmental agencies to attract, retain, and expand the business sector of Talbot County, and marketing the county as a premier destination for visitors, while helping to preserve the exceptional quality of life that makes Talbot County unique.  The certificate of recognition also spoke of Ms. Vanhooser’s efforts to assist the County with navigating the challenges to businesses and individuals during the COVID-19 pandemic, the development of an economic indicator dashboard to provide insight into the health and resilience of local economies and communities, and the preparation and adoption of the 2026-2030 Economic Development and Tourism Strategic Plan to act as a guide for the county’s economic development and tourism into the future. 

The Council presented the Certificate of Recognition to Ms. Vanhooser.

XII. <u>Work Session with Senior Citizens Task Force – The Council held a work session with members of</u> the Senior Citizens Task Force on Tuesday, June 9, 2026, at 2:30 p.m. in the Bradley Meeting Room, South Wing, Talbot County Courthouse, 11 North Washington Street, Easton, Maryland. Nancy Andrew, Director, Talbot County Office of Community Partnerships, and Jenna Marchi, Chair, Talbot County Commission on the Aging, and Julie Lowe, Executive Director, briefed the Council on the housing shortage in Talbot County, specifically affordable housing for those seniors on a fixed income, the 6 to 7 year wait times at affordable senior communities in Talbot County, and the impact the lack of a low-barrier shelter in the county has on the growing number of homeless senior citizens in the county.  Ms. Marchi utilized a PowerPoint presentation to outline factors which can cause homelessness or insecurity within the senior citizen population, including unsafe housing, utility insecurity, cost of housing, lack of adequate transportation, health issues, and accessibility to available resources.  Ms. Marchi stated that the Task Force extensively reviewed what other communities on the Eastern Shore, in the state of Maryland and throughout the nation are doing to keep senior citizens housed, provide short-term intervention and provide longterm solutions for permanent housing. She stated that the Task Force found that there is no single solution; instead communities respond to their local needs.  Ms. Andrew stated that at the present time, there are approximately 40 to 60 senior citizens who are in immediate need of housing in Talbot County.  Ms. Marchi then outlined the three categories on the continuum to address homelessness:  (1) Prevention – assisting individuals with connecting to available resources to assist them with remaining in their homes for as long as possible, providing home repairs and modifications, support services and financial assistance (rent, fuel, Meals on Wheels); (2) Crisis Response – the Task Force identified a senior specific shelter as the most urgent need, a lowbarrier, year-round shelter which is open 24/7 which would prioritize immediate access to safety for those who are facing a sudden housing crisis, fleeing abuse, or are in unsafe living conditions; provide rapid rehousing to help transition individuals who are homeless back into permanent housing through rental assistance on a short-term basis, and provide support services; and transitional housing which would provide temporary housing, along with support services, prior to the individual’s move into permanent housing; and (3) Permanent Housing Solutions – New construction which would provide accessible, affordable subsidized housing; Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU’s) – a secondary, self-contained dwelling unit located on the same property as a primary dwelling; Home sharing – a program connecting individuals willing to share their housing; Set Asides – reservation of a certain number of percentage of housing units for a specific population. Ms. Marchi stated that the different categories require different expertise and different partners _, i.e.,_ Human Services, Emergency Response Systems, Developers/Planners, respectively.  She concluded the presentation by identifying potential areas for consideration by the Council:  Prevention – preserving current local senior services, improving service navigation, strengthening coordination, and gap analysis; Crisis Response – conduct a Feasibility Study for a year-round 24/7 low-barrier shelter and explore models that can safely serve seniors; Permanent Housing – explore affordable and subsidized housing opportunities and evaluate County owned properties for public/private partnerships.  Council discussion ensued with Ms. Marchi, Ms. Andrew, Ms. Lowe and other representatives of the Senior Citizens Task Force in attendance.
