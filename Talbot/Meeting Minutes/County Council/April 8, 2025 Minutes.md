VI. <u>Presentation of Certificates for Talbot Works Business Academy Graduates – Cassandra</u> Vanhooser, Director, Department of Department of Economic Development and Tourism – Ms. Vanhooser expressed her appreciation to the Council for their encouragement and financial commitment to the Talbot County business community.  She stated that the Council’s allocation of grant funding from the American Rescue Plan Act made the Talbot Works Academy program possible.  She expressed her appreciation to Scott Warner and the Mid-Shore Regional Council, which, through the Rural Maryland Investment Fund, provided the financial resources for this cohort of the program, an eight-week entrepreneurship training program.   The program offers entrepreneurs who are looking to start or expand their business an opportunity to learn about cash flow projections, sales and marketing, taxes and insurance, human resources, and how to obtain access to capital.  Several participants of the class briefed the Council on what the class had meant to them.  Ms. Vanhooser stated that since the inception of the Talbot Works Business Academy, those who participated in the program have already hired 11 new employees; three businesses have expanded by opening another location, and at least two participants have opened a second business. 

Ms. Haythe presented the Certificates of Completion to the 15 Talbot Works Business Academy graduates. 

VII. <u>Update on Talbot County Police Accountability Board – Mitchell “Mitch” Ettinger, Chairman,</u> Talbot County Police Accountability Board and Administrative Charging Committee – Mr. Ettinger utilized a PowerPoint presentation to outline the duties of the Talbot County Police Accountability Board, established by Maryland law in 2022 (Maryland Public Safety Code, Ann. § 3-102) and established by Talbot County Bill No. 1497, the purpose of which is to review outcomes of disciplinary matters considered by the Administrative Charging Committee, also established by Maryland law in 2022, and to submit an annual report to the Council which identifies trends in the disciplinary procedures of police officers in the county and to make recommendations on changes in policy which would improve police accountability.  He stated that the Police Accountability Board meets on a quarterly basis with the Law Enforcement Agencies (LEAs) in the county and he provided information on the number of complaints received and the disposition of same.  Mr. Ettinger requested Council consideration as to whether to broaden the mandate of the Police Accountability Board as has been done in other jurisdictions throughout the state.  The Police Accountability Board will continue to update the Council on a regular or as needed basis.

VIII. <u>Update by Maryland Environmental Service – Tim Ford, Managing Director, Environmental</u> Operations, Maryland Environmental Service; Gary Lasako, P.G., Senior Project Manager, Maryland Environmental Service – Mr. Ford stated that Mid-Shore I (Talbot County) closed in 2010 but currently serves as a transfer station and homeowner drop-off location for Talbot County residents.  Mr. Lasako provided statistical information on tonnage of trash, brush and scalehouse transactions at Mid-Shore I for the past four quarters, stating that the tonnages are on track as budgeted for FY25.  He also provided statistical information on the tonnages received for FY18 through present, as well as statistics for the homeowner drop-off, noting customer visits and number of punch cards sold.  Mr. Lasako then updated the Council on activities at Mid-Shore II (Caroline County), providing statistics on billable tonnages received at the landfill for FY21 to present. He stated that Mid-Shore II recently piloted, then purchased, a new trash compactor for the facility which is 15 to 20% more efficient than the compactor which was previously used, significantly increasing compaction, resulting in less fuel and soil usage, thereby producing cost savings. Mr. Lasako reiterated that the goal of a landfill operation is to maximize the air space as much as possible in order to prolong the life of the landfill.  Mr. Ford then briefed the Council on proposed Mid-Shore II tipping fees for FY26 to $71.50 per ton (an increase of $1.50 per ton); $46.50 per ton for brush; and a new proposed new sewage sludge surcharge of $15.00 per ton.  He stated that MES is still considering other options with regard to the sludge surcharge and is taking suggestions from the participating counties.  He stated that Talbot County has suggested utilizing a test-out option, whereby MES allows the County to test out if their PFAS (Per-and Polyfluoroalkyl substances) levels are low.  Mr. Ford stated that sludge contains a higher concentration of PFAS than other materials accepted at the landfill. Council discussion ensued with Mr. Ford and Mr. Lasako as various facets of the presentation were brought forward. Maryland Environmental Service will continue to update the Council on a regular or as-needed basis. 

IX. <u>Presentation of 2024 Diversity Training Report – Donna Pardieu, Director, Administrative Services</u> – Ms. Pardieu briefed the Council on the diversity, equity, and inclusion report for 2024 in accordance with guidelines of the administrative resolution adopted by the County Council on June 23, 2020.  Ms. Pardieu stated that the Council continues to focus on recruiting the best qualified candidates for County employment opportunities and outlined the locations where the employment opportunities are listed/posted, providing the County with exposure to 60 job markets in 28 languages, as well as  thousands of job boards and career sites.  She stated that County employees had completed three online courses through the Kantola Learning System focused on diversity, equity, and inclusion, harassment prevention and employee conduct.  She stated that the Council approved a bilingual pay policy in 2024, and that language related to Equal Employment Opportunity (EEO); Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), anti-discrimination, anti-harassment, and information on reporting possible discrimination was included in the County Employee Handbook which became effective on May 1, 2024.  A Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion statement is posted on the County website. Council discussion ensued with Ms. Pardieu.

B. <u>Request from Neighborhood Service Center – Requested Council consideration of a</u> request from the Neighborhood Service Center for assistance with a critical funding challenge.  Marilyn Neal, Executive Director, Neighborhood Service Center, expressed her appreciation to the Council for their past support and stated that the Neighborhood Service Center (NSC) is currently facing a funding shortage due to a reduction in federal funding which flows to the organization through the State of Maryland in the form of grants and is funding already spent which would normally be reimbursed by the grants.  She stated that the NSC serves as Talbot County’s community action agency to serve the county’s most vulnerable citizens; its mission is to address poverty and inequity by focusing on the root causes of poverty through various programs, including, among others, job training, housing support, food insecurity, and health programs.  She stated that over the past five (5) years, the NSC has served over 9,000 households in Talbot County and she outlined in detail how funding for the organization which is received from both grants and private donors is utilized to serve the community. Ms. Neal then requested Council consideration to appropriate $150,000 in Contingency Funds to the NSC to help fill the funding gap for the remaining portion of FY25 to help offset impacts on the community served by the NSC should the grant funding not be replenished.  She stated that $33,000 has been requested from the municipalities of Easton, Oxford, and St. Michaels.  Council discussion ensued with Ms. Neal.  Mr. Callahan made a motion which was seconded by Mr. Lesher, to loan the Neighborhood Service Center $150,000 as requested and Council discussion ensued with Ms. Haythe and Ms. Mielke indicating they were not prepared to vote on the matter at present.  Council discussion again ensued.  Upon motion by Mr. Stepp, seconded by Mr. Lesher, the Council approved providing the NSC with $50,000 in Contingency Funds, not as a loan, which the County Manager certified are available, to bridge the funding gap and to consider the request for additional funding at a later date by voting 5 – 0 as follows:

XIX. <u>Joint Work Session with Town of Easton and Easton Utilities on EDA Grant for Regional Medical Center Project – The County Council, members of the Easton Town Council and representatives of</u> Easton Utilities held a joint work session on Monday, March 31, 2025, at 4:30 p.m. in the Easton Town Council Chambers, located at 14 S. Harrison Street in Easton.  County Council member Dave Stepp was absent.  Cassandra Vanhooser, Director, Talbot County Department of Economic Development and Tourism, and John Horner, President & CEO, Easton Utilities Commission, briefed the Council on the criteria for submitting applications for U.S. Department of Commerce Economic Adjustment Assistance (EDA) Grants, including, among others, funding of projects located in or serving distressed areas and which serve multiple jurisdictions.  Ms. Vanhooser stated that the Town of Easton, Easton Utilities, and Talbot County, along with Shore Health System, Inc. (now UMMS Shore Regional Health) are partnering on the regional medical center project through a Development Rights and Responsibilities Agreement (DRRA).  Mr. Horner outlined the scope of the $20 million project, of which Talbot County has committed $9 million to bring water and sewer to the regional medical center property line and Easton Utilities has committed $11 million to bring other utilities, including gas and fiber optics, to the property line.  He stated that the maximum amount of funding which can be requested is $3.5 million; a 1:1 match in federal and local funds is also one criterion for seeking funding.  He stated that 700 new jobs (over a 10-year timeframe) are anticipated to be created by the project which will serve the new University of Maryland Medical

<!-- page 14 -->

System Regional Medical Center Campus and Easton Airport.  Mr. Horner then outlined specific infrastructure costs for the project, stating that water and sewer (water main, pump station and approximately 6,000 ft. of force main) are anticipated to cost approximately $4.7 million, or 2/3 of the proposed EDA portion of the project of $7.0 million; the substation (transformer and circuit breakers) will cost approximately $2.3 million, or 1/3 of the proposed EDA portion of the project. Mr. Horner stated that the application will be submitted in the 2<sup>nd</sup> quarter of 2025, and a decision on receipt of funding should be known within 3 to 6 months of grant submittal; the work cannot start before receipt of any EDA grant funding.  He stated that temporary electricity is on the regional medical center project site; electrical infrastructure has been relocated in anticipation of the relocation of MD Rt. 662; the contract for design of the electrical substation has been awarded to Delmarva Power; a temporary force main from the Talbot County Community Center is open; and property owners who will be affected by the relocation of the force main have been identified.  Ms. Vanhooser stated that the new Regional Medical Center will serve the 170,000 residents in the five Mid-Shore counties.  She then briefed those in attendance on the positive economic impact the Regional Medical Center project will have on Talbot County including, supporting ancillary business growth on the Regional Medical Center campus, attracting high quality healthcare and aeronautical jobs, and acting as a catalyst for both aeronautical and non-aeronautical development on 100 acres at Easton Airport.  She stated that the Regional Medical Center is scheduled to open in February 2028.  Discussion ensued with Ms. Vanhooser, Mr. Horner, and Scott Warner, Executive Director, Mid-Shore Regional Council regarding various facets of the presentation and the project. Ms. Vanhooser concluded the presentation by stating that her office will be requesting letters in support of the grant application in the near future from both the Easton Town Council and the County Council.

## **<u>Update on TCPS Blueprint for Maryland’s Future</u>** 

Ms. Jones stated that SB 429, _Excellence in Maryland Public Schools Act_ , has moved from the House to the Senate and noted there may still be some amendments to the legislation.  At this time, funding for special education has been held harmless and there is still discussion on how collaborative time for teachers may be altered.  Dr. Pepukayi stated that Local Education Agencies (LEA) believe there should be more latitude at the local level in implementing the Blueprint for Education.  Ms. Jones noted that projections from the Maryland Department of Legislative Services (DLS) indicate Talbot County’s local share would increase $34.8 million (approximately 63%) between FY26 and FY34.  In FY34 it is projected Talbot County would be required to fund TCPS approximately $90 million.  Discussion ensued about the projected increase and Talbot County’s tax structure.  Council requested additional information on the data behind the projected figures from DLS.  There was also discussion about the mandated increase in teacher starting salaries to

## **<u>Review of TCPS Data from 2019-2025</u>**

## **<u>Update on Cell Phone Policy at Easton Middle School</u>** 

Dr. Pepukayi provided an update on the pilot project at Easton Middle School for use of Yondr pouches for cell phones.  Since December 2024, students have been placing their cell phones in locked Yondr pouches during the school day.  Dr. Pepukayi reported that the pilot project has been successful with an increase in student engagement.  Dr. Pepukayi explained that there is a progressive discipline procedure in place for violations of the cell phone policy.  Teachers can contact the office for any infractions or assistance while they continue to focus on teaching.  The Board of Education will be discussing next steps on the cell phone policy later this month which may include possible expanding the pilot program to other schools. 

## **<u>Update on Chapel District Elementary School Project</u>** 

Mr. Shafer provided an update on the renovation/expansion project at Chapel District Elementary School located in Cordova, Maryland.  The project is on schedule with no change orders to date. The first phase of the project (approximately 30%) is expected to be completed by June.  Mr. Shafer thanked the Council for providing Bill Stagg as the County’s project manager for the project.  Mr. Shafer invited the Council to tour the school in late May.
