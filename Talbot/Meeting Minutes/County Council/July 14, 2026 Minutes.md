IV. <u>Update and Request by Mid-Shore Behavioral Health, Inc. – Kathryn “Katie” Dilley, LCSW-C, Chief Executive Officer, Mid-Shore Behavioral Health, Inc.; Shannon Joyce, MPH, CHES, Behavioral Health Coordinator, Aging Population, Mid-Shore Behavioral Health, Inc. – Ms. Dilley</u> and Ms. Joyce utilized a PowerPoint presentation to outline the mission, vision and organizational values of Mid-Shore Behavioral Health, Inc. (MSBH) which serves as the regional core service agency for the five Mid-Shore counties:  Caroline, Dorchester, Kent, Queen Anne’s and Talbot. Ms. Dilley stated that MSBH is not a direct service provider, it serves as a local behavioral health systems manager that serves at the pleasure of the counties and the Maryland Department of Health Behavioral Health Administration at a systems level role to support the community and behavioral health providers in the region.  She stated that MSBH partners with the five health departments in the region for planning and needs assessment purposes. Ms. Dilley stated that there had been a $16 million increase in funding received over the past six years for behavioral health services for the Mid-Shore Region; MSBH now manages approximately $25 million in federal and State funding which provides programming and behavioral health services to residents of the Mid-Shore.  She updated the Council on the programming and behavioral health services offered through collaborative efforts with various partners and agencies in the following areas:  Crisis, Wellness &

Ms. Dilley stated that MSBH has requested a Mobile Response and Stabilization Service Team to serve Talbot County specifically through funding made available to Maryland’s rural jurisdictions.  She also noted that funding through the Maryland Blueprint for Education has been set aside for expansion of behavioral health services in the public schools and stated that MSBH will work with the school systems in the five Mid-Shore counties to identify priority behavioral health needs and to bring additional behavioral health providers into the school system; 3,963 students were served in the first six months of the year.  Ms. Dilley updated the Council on a recent Needs Assessment conducted in partnership with the organization, Due East, to ascertain what community members and constituents considered primary concerns; counties identified their primary concerns as the amount of alcohol consumption, school mental health and the stigma which often accompanies it, and housing and transportation challenges.  The Talbot County community expressed concerns about alcohol consumption, depression, lack of access to healthcare, and the higher incidence of suicide and suicidal behaviors in young adults.  Ms. Joyce stated that the Mid-Shore Suicide Coalition is working with Channel Marker to expand education and awareness, stating that on September 26, 2026, Mid-Shore Behavioral Health will be in Moton Park for the **Going Purple** event, a regional event for all five counties which rotates to a different county each year.  She stated that the first cohort of the Rural Advancement of Maryland Peers will begin on July 15<sup>th</sup> – the goal is to train 15 individuals over the next two years to become part of a certified peer support network for the behavioral health workforce while also gaining employment. Ms. Dilley concluded her presentation by outlining various presentations, events and community outreach efforts throughout the five-county region. Council members expressed their appreciation to Ms. Dilley and Ms. Joyce for the job they do to provide mental health services to the Mid-Shore counties.  Ms. Dilley then requested the Council’s consideration for a Letter of Agreement to have Mid-Shore Behavioral Health, Inc. continue to serve as the County’s core service agency for management of the public health system for FY27. ## V. <u>Introduction of Legislation:</u> 

A BILL TO AMEND CHAPTER 131 (ROAD NAMING AND PROPERTY NUMBERING) OF THE TALBOT COUNTY CODE FOR THE PURPOSES OF MODERNIZING TALBOT COUNTY’S ADDRESSING STANDARDS TO BE NEXT-GENERATION 911 COMPLIANT AND TO CLARIFY STAFF RESPONSIBILITIES IN ADMINISTERING THE PROVISIONS OF CHAPTER 131 was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for introduction. Prior to introduction, Bryce Yelton, Assistant Planning Officer, stated that the responsibility for road naming and numbering has historically been the responsibility of the Department of Planning and Zoning which would assign an address to a property and forward the information to the property owner via U.S. Mail; however, the State and the County have changed addressing standards.  Katie Stafford, CAD/GIS Specialist, Department of Emergency Management, stated that the information must now be reviewed through the County’s Geographic Information System (GIS) and entered into the National Emergency Number Association (NENA) database which feeds into the 911 software in consultation with the Talbot County Department of Emergency Services. The legislation was introduced by Mr. Callahan, Mr. Lesher, Ms. Mielke, and Mr. Stepp.  A public hearing was scheduled for Tuesday, August 11, 2026, at 5:30 p.m. in the Bradley Meeting Room, South Wing, Talbot County Courthouse, 11 N. Washington St., Easton, Maryland 21601.

She stated that § 64-13 (C) of the Talbot County Code outlines the annual review process and provides for an automatic annual adjustment of the impact fees, if necessary, on July 1<sup>st</sup> each year following review of data from the Engineering News Record Construction Cost Index. Bryce Yelton, Assistant Planning Officer, stated that the Office of Planning and Zoning accepts the impact fees on behalf of the County and outlined the process by which residents and developers pay the fees.
