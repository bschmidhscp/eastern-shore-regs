## VI. <u>Introduction of Numbered Resolutions:</u> 

A RESOLUTION TO AMEND THE TALBOT COUNTY COMPREHENSIVE WATER AND SEWER PLAN FOR THE PURPOSE OF ADDING OR MODIFYING THE TALBOT COUNTY CAPITAL PROJECT FOR FISCAL YEARS 2024 AND 2025 TO ADD A NEW CAPITAL PROJECT IN THE AMOUNT OF $12.0 MILLION FOR THE PURPOSE OF REPLACING AND/OR REHABILITATING THE TOWN OF EASTON SEWER COLLECTION SYSTEM TO MITIGATE INFLOW AND INFILTRATION was read into the record and brought forward for introduction.  Prior to introduction, Ray Clarke, County Engineer, stated that Easton Utilities (Town of Easton) had submitted an application to amend the County’s Comprehensive Water and Sewer Plan for the purpose of adding a capital project for improvements to their sewer collection system. He stated that the capital project is required to be included in the County’s Comprehensive Water and Sewer Plan in order to seek funding from the Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE).  The resolution was introduced by Mr. Callahan, Ms. Haythe, Mr. Lesher, Ms. Mielke, and Mr. Stepp as Resolution No. 345.  A public hearing was scheduled for Tuesday, October 10, 2023 at 6:30 p.m. in the Bradley Meeting Room, South Wing, Talbot County Courthouse, 11 N. Washington St., Easton, Maryland 21601. 

A RESOLUTION TO AMEND THE TALBOT COUNTY COMPREHENSIVE WATER AND SEWER PLAN FOR THE PURPOSE OF ADDING OR MODIFYING THE TALBOT COUNTY CAPITAL PROJECT FOR FISCAL YEARS 2024 AND 2025 TO ADD A NEW CAPITAL PROJECT IN THE AMOUNT OF $8.0 MILLION FOR THE PURPOSE OF REPLACING AND/OR REHABILITATING THE REGION V (TILGHMAN) SEWER COLLECTION SYSTEM TO MITIGATE INFLOW AND INFILTRATION was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for introduction.  Prior to introduction, County Engineer, Ray Clarke, stated that the project is similar to the Region II project in St. Michaels, whereby improvements were made to the roads following improvements to the sewer collection system. He stated that inclusion of the project in the County’s Comprehensive Water and Sewer Plan is necessary in order to seek funding from the Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE).  The resolution was introduced by Mr. Callahan, Ms. Haythe, Mr. Lesher, Ms. Mielke, and Mr. Stepp as Resolution No. 346.  A public hearing was scheduled for Tuesday, October 10, 2023 at 6:30 p.m. in the Bradley Meeting Room, South Wing, Talbot County Courthouse, 11 N. Washington St., Easton, Maryland 21601.

A RESOLUTION TO AMEND THE TALBOT COUNTY COMPREHENSIVE WATER AND SEWER PLAN TO PROVIDE THE EQUIVALENT DWELLING UNITS FOR ALL PHASES OF THE LAKESIDE DEVELOPMENT was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for introduction.  Prior to introduction, County Attorney, Patrick Thomas, stated that the resolution was drafted at Council’s direction following the Council meeting on Tuesday, August 22, 2023 in response to letters received from the Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) dated April 24, 2023, May 9, 2023, and July 21, 2023.  He stated that the resolution outlines the phases of the Lakeside development in Trappe, the equivalent dwelling units (EDUs) for each phase, as well as the existing water and sewer classifications for each of the phases and the number of EDUs in each classification.  Council discussion ensued.  The resolution was introduced by Ms. Haythe and Mr. Stepp as Resolution No. 347.  A public hearing was scheduled for Tuesday, October 10, 2023 at 6:30 p.m. in the Bradley Meeting Room, South Wing, Talbot County Courthouse, 11 N. Washington St., Easton, Maryland 21601. 

A RESOLUTION REGARDING THE TALBOT COUNTY COMPREHENSIVE WATER AND SEWER PLAN TO CLARIFY AND CONFIRM THE WATER AND SEWER CLASSIFICATIONS OF CERTAIN PARCELS INCORRECTLY SHOWN IN EXHIBITS A AND B TO RESOLUTION NO. 281, AS AMENDED was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for introduction.  Prior to introduction, County Attorney, Patrick Thomas, stated that the maps included in the resolution are the same maps which were discussed at the Council work session on Tuesday, June 27, 2023.  He stated that there were several parcels in the town of Trappe that were not part of Resolution No. 281.  He stated that the water and sewer classifications of the parcels were not affected by the resolution, they were incorrectly shown on the exhibits to the resolution; the resolution before the Council was requested by MDE and is a corrective measure to clarify and confirm the water and sewer classifications in the Town. The resolution was introduced by Ms. Haythe and Mr. Stepp as Resolution No. 348.  A public hearing was scheduled for Tuesday, October 10, 2023 at 6:30 p.m. in the Bradley Meeting Room, South Wing, Talbot County Courthouse, 11 N. Washington St., Easton, Maryland 21601. 

A RESOLUTION TO AMEND THE OFFICIAL ROAD NAME LIST FOR TALBOT COUNTY TO CHANGE THE NAMES OF “LINCOLN ROAD” AND “SHELLMILL AVENUE” TO “BELLEVUE SCHOOL ROAD” AND “TURNER AVENUE”, RESPECTIVELY, IN THE VILLAGE OF BELLEVUE, TALBOT COUNTY, MARYLAND, PURSUANT TO § 131-5(C) OF THE TALBOT COUNTY CODE was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for introduction.  Prior to introduction, County Engineer, Ray Clarke, stated that the owners of the property have requested a change in the road names for which Council approval is required; the roads are currently “paper streets” which have never been developed.  The resolution was introduced by Mr. Callahan, Ms. Haythe, Mr. Lesher, Ms. Mielke, and Mr. Stepp as Resolution No. 349.  A public hearing was scheduled for Tuesday, October 10, 2023 at 6:30 p.m. in the Bradley Meeting Room, South Wing, Talbot County Courthouse, 11 N. Washington St., Easton, Maryland 21601.

## VIII. <u>Public Hearing:</u> 

Bill No. 1547, A BILL TO AMEND CHAPTER 11 OF THE TALBOT COUNTY CODE (ALCOHOLIC BEVERAGES) REGARDING OPERATIONS OF CLASS I ALCOHOL DISPENSARY BISTROS, was read into the record and brought forward for public hearing.  Prior to the public hearing, County Attorney, Patrick Thomas, stated that the legislation had been requested by Attorney Zach Smith on behalf of his client, The Wardroom.  He stated that the intent of the legislation is to expand the bistro seating into the wine shop area and to expand the hours of operation in the bistro area, opening at 9:00 a.m. versus the current 11:00 a.m. He stated that the configuration of the seating would require the approval of the Board of Liquor License Commissioners. If approved, the merits of the legislation would apply to all Class I liquor license holders.  Members of the public were then afforded an opportunity to comment on the legislation. Upon motion by Ms. Haythe, seconded by Mr. Stepp, the Council approved bringing Bill No. 1547 to third reader by voting 5 – 0 as follows: 

Mr. Callahan – Aye Mr. Stepp – Aye Mr. Lesher – Aye Ms. Mielke – Aye Ms. Haythe - Aye 

Bill No. 1547 was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for vote.  The Council approved Bill No. 1548 by voting 5 – 0 as follows: 

Mr. Callahan – Aye Mr. Stepp – Aye Mr. Lesher – Aye Ms. Mielke – Aye Ms. Haythe – Aye 

Bill No. 1547 becomes effective as provided for in the legislation.

- F. <u>Request from Talbot County Health Department – Mr. Stamp stated that the Talbot</u> County Health Department is requesting certification of FY24 local matching funds which the State has increased by $123,658 since the Council adopted the FY24 Budget.  Mr. Stamp requested Council approval to certify the core public health services funding agreement for FY24 and to forward a letter to Dr. Herrera Scott, Secretary, Maryland Department of Health, expressing Talbot County’s frustration with having received the request after adoption of the FY24 Budget.  Dr. Maria Maguire, Talbot County Health Officer, provided further details.  She outlined the reasons for the increase in local appropriations, how the funding (match percentage) is calculated by the State, and the timing for doing so; Council discussion ensued with Dr. Maguire and Mr. Stamp.  Mr. Stamp stated that it was the recommendation of staff that the Council President be authorized to complete the required form which would obligate the County to pay the full local match amount, an additional $123,658, which would then be realized in savings through the Health Department’s budget, or the County could utilize contingency funding in the 4<sup>th</sup> quarter for the amount of the increase.  Mr. Lesher made a motion to accept the recommendation of staff and to forward a letter to the State expressing the Council’s consternation about the process; as there was no second to the motion, therefore the motion died.
