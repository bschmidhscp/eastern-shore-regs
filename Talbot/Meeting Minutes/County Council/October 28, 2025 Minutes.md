- III. <u>Proclamation:  Talbot County Free Library – 100</u><sup>th</sup> <u>Anniversary – The Clerk read a proclamation</u> into the record which recognized the 100<sup>th</sup> anniversary of the founding of the Talbot County Free Library.  The proclamation spoke about the growth of the Library over the last century into the pillar of the Talbot County community it is today, providing the first Bookmobile service on the Eastern Shore, establishing the Maryland Room, and growing and adapting to the changing needs of the community and Library patrons through the years by providing books, multimedia resources, broadband internet, public access computers, technology training, programs supporting workforce development, and classes to expand knowledge and spark creativity, all in an atmosphere promoting equity, opportunity and civic engagement.  Upon motion by Mr. Lesher, seconded by Ms. Mielke, the Council approved the proclamation by voting 5 – 0 as follows: 

Mr. Callahan – Aye Mr. Stepp – Aye Mr. Lesher – Aye Ms. Mielke – Aye Ms. Haythe - Aye 

Prior to presentation of the proclamation, Dana Newman, Director, Talbot County Free Library, stated that the Library is here to serve the community, and the staff, volunteers and Board of the Library have a passion to serve people of all ages.  Council members congratulated Ms. Newman and those in attendance on behalf of the Library. 

Mr. Lesher presented the Proclamation to Ms. Newman and representatives of the Talbot County Free Library. 

- IV. <u>Proclamation:  Nace’s Day 2025 – The Clerk read a proclamation into the record which spoke of</u> the establishment of Nace’s Day in 1867 by Nathaniel “Uncle Nace” Hopkins to recognize and honor the emancipation of enslaved individuals which was enshrined in the Maryland State constitution on November 1, 1864, an historical event he kept alive for the remainder of his

<!-- page 2 -->
Minutes – October 28, 2025 P a g e | **2** 

lifetime, and which has now been continuously celebrated for 158 years. The proclamation also spoke of the efforts of Mr. Hopkins, an African American native of Trappe who had served as a member of the Union Army in the United States Colored Troops during the Civil War, as a highly respected leader in the Trappe community and who, upon returning to Talbot County, worked to better the lives of the African American community by helping to establish the first school for African American children in Trappe, created free Black communities throughout Talbot County, advocated with elected officials for the construction of roads to those communities, and was actively involved in the establishment of Scott’s United Methodist Church in Trappe.  Upon motion by Mr. Stepp, seconded by Ms. Haythe, the Council approved the proclamation by voting 5 – 0 as follows: 

Mr. Callahan – Aye Mr. Stepp – Aye Mr. Lesher - Aye Ms. Mielke – Aye Ms. Haythe – Aye 

Prior to presentation of the proclamation, Dale Kevin Brown introduced himself and several other individuals who are direct descendants of Nathaniel Hopkins and spoke of the impact of Mr. Hopkins on the African American community of Talbot County.  He also stated that Mr. Hopkins also impacted the Talbot County community at large, due to the fact that because volunteers from the African American community in Talbot County who served in the Union Army during the Civil War were so numerous, Talbot County only had to participate in the first of the four drafts in the state of Maryland.  Council members expressed their appreciation to the descendants of Nathaniel “Uncle Nace” Hopkins for continuing to preserve a portion of Talbot County’s history. 

Ms. Haythe presented the Proclamation to the descendants of Nathaniel “Uncle Nace” Hopkins in attendance. 

Following the public hearing on Amendment No. 2 to the 2020 Maryland Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) Program, Jeannie Haddaway-Riccio, Member, Board of Directors, Talbot Historical Society, noted several events planned for Nace’s Day on Saturday, November 1, 2025, including a live reenactment of Mr. Hopkins’ legacy by professional actor, Darius Wallace.

VII. <u>Introduction of Legislation:</u> 

A BILL TO AMEND CHAPTER 190 (ZONING, SUBDIVISION, AND LAND DEVELOPMENT), SECTIONS 190-25.2 (TABLE OF LAND USES) AND 190-29 (COMMERCIAL USES), REGARDING FUNERAL HOMES AND CREMATORIA, was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for introduction.  Prior to introduction, Patrick Thomas, County Attorney, stated that the proposed zoning text amendment had been requested by Brendan Mullaney on behalf of his client, Woodlawn Memorial Park, and in July 2025, the Council had approved forwarding the proposed text amendment to the Planning Commission for their review and recommendation.  Mr. Thomas stated that the legislation before Council includes the recommendations of the Planning Commission, developed in consultation with the applicant, which proposed to permit funeral homes and crematoria as special exception uses in the AC (Agricultural Conservation) district, subject to specific criteria as outlined in the legislation. The legislation was introduced by Mr. Callahan, Ms. Haythe, Ms. Mielke and Mr. Stepp as Bill No. 1621.  A public hearing was scheduled for Tuesday, November 25, 2025, at 5:30 p.m. in the Bradley Meeting Room, South Wing, Talbot County Courthouse, 11 North Washington Street, Easton, Maryland 21601. 

A BILL TO AMEND CHAPTER 190 (ZONING, SUBDIVISION, AND LAND DEVELOPMENT) OF THE TALBOT COUNTY CODE FOR THE PURPOSES OF PROVIDING FOR CLASSES OF SHORT-TERM RENTAL LICENSES AND AMENDING CERTAIN PROVISIONS PERTAINING TO SHORT-TERM RENTAL LICENSES, INCLUDING: 1) THE COMPOSITION AND TERM OF THE SHORT-TERM RENTAL REVIEW BOARD; 2) CONTENTS OF NEW APPLICATIONS; 3) HEARINGS, DECISIONS, AND APPEALS; 4) RENEWAL; 5) GROUNDS FOR DENIAL; AND 6) ADDITIONAL RESTRICTIONS was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for introduction.  Prior to introduction, Patrick Thomas, County Attorney, stated that the proposed zoning text amendment, requested by Mr. Lesher and Ms. Mielke, is the same proposal as initially brought forward earlier this year which the Council had voted to forward to the Planning Commission for review and recommendation, with the exception of the following three (3) changes as recommended by the Planning Commission:  the proposed increase in the minimum night stay for certain zoning districts from three days to seven days has been removed; the proposed requirement for a landline telephone has been removed; and the proposed requirement to keep a log of rentals has been removed. Mr. Thomas stated that the Zoning Code only requires that the Planning Commission review and make recommendations on proposed zoning text amendments; it does not require that the legislation be bound by those recommendations; any subsequent substantive amendments would need to go back to the Planning Commission for review and recommendation.  The legislation was introduced by Mr. Lesher and Ms. Mielke as Bill No. 1622.  A public hearing was scheduled for Tuesday, November 25, 2025, at 5:30 p.m. in the Bradley Meeting Room, South Wing, Talbot County Courthouse, 11 North Washington Street, Easton, Maryland 21601.

XII. <u>Consolidated Transportation Plan (CTP) Meeting with Maryland Department of Transportation Representatives – The Council met with representatives of the Maryland Department of</u> Transportation at 3:00 p.m. on Tuesday, September 16, 2025, in the Wye Oak Room at the Talbot County Community Center.  The following individuals were in attendance on behalf of the Maryland Department of Transportation:  Samantha Biddle, Acting Secretary, Maryland Department of Transportation; Will Pines, P.E., Administrator, State Highway Administration; Melissa Williams, Director, Planning & Program Development, Maryland Transportation Authority; Travis Johnston, Director, Locally Operated Transit Systems, Maryland Transit Administration; Christine Nizer, Administration, Motor Vehicle Administration; Ashish Solanki, Director, Office of Regional Aviation Assistance, Maryland Aviation Administration; Bob Munroe, Chief Counsel, Maryland Port Administration; and Ken Fender, District 2 Engineer, State Highway Administration.  Council members in attendance were Mr. Callahan, Mr. Lesher, and Ms. Mielke; Senator Johnny Mautz and Delegate Tom Hutchinson were also in attendance.  Acting Secretary Biddle briefed the Council on recent projects completed in Talbot County, including placement of crosswalks at U.S. Rt. 50 and Dover St. and planned improvements on Matthewstown Road and U.S. Rt. 50 to Black Dog Alley in 2026; she emphasized that the State is utilizing available funding to enhance safety, maintain the transportation system, and support the state’s economy.  The draft transportation budget for FY26 – FY31 is $21.5 billion which includes $400 million in additional revenues approved by the General Assembly for use as a match to acquire additional federal funding, resulting in an increase this year of nearly $300 million. The Department of Transportation’s “Serious About Safety” program continues its commitment to working toward its goal of zero deaths and a reduction of serious injuries on Maryland roadways for all users – motorists, pedestrians, bicyclists, public transit and highway workers.  Mr. Pines stated that signals at U.S. Rt. 50/Airport Road had been reconstructed, and  a new signal had been installed at MD Rt. 331 and Black Dog Alley.  He stated that the Easton Community Bicycle Parking Initiative will receive a $4,500 Kim Lamphier Bikeways grant which will be used for the installation of bicycle racks throughout the town of Easton.  Ms. Williams stated that the Maryland Transportation Authority is currently undertaking Phase 2 of replacement of the concrete slabs on the eastbound span of the Bay Bridge and that the Tier 2 NEPA (National Environmental Policy Act) Study project for a new Bay bridge crossing is currently on schedule.
