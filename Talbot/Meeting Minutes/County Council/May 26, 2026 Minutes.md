## III. <u>Introduction of Legislation:</u> 

A BILL TO AMEND CHAPTER 190 (ZONING, SUBDIVISION, AND LAND DEVELOPMENT) OF THE TALBOT COUNTY CODE FOR THE PURPOSE OF PROVIDING FOR THE PARTIAL OR COMPLETE RELEASE OF A RESERVED LAND AGREEMENT OR A RESERVATION OF DEVELOPMENT RIGHTS AGREEMENT IF THE PARCEL IS SUBJECT TO AN OPTION CONTRACT WITH THE STATE OF MARYLAND AGRICULTURAL LAND PRESERVATION FOUNDATION (MALPF), SUBJECT TO CERTAIN CONDITIONS was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for introduction.  Prior to introduction, County Attorney, Patrick Thomas, stated that Planning & Zoning staff had briefed the Council on the proposed text amendment, as requested by Althea Dulin through her attorney, Zach Smith, at its meeting on Tuesday, April 14, 2026.  He stated that the Council had also reviewed the Planning Commission’s recommendations regarding the proposed text amendment.  Mr. Thomas stated that if the legislation is approved, it would permit the Council to release a Reserved Land Agreement or a Reservation of Development Rights Agreement if the property is subject to an option contract with the Maryland Agricultural Land Preservation Foundation (MALPF) which would establish a perpetual easement on the property and is binding upon the owner; at present, the agreements can only be released if the zoning classification changes.  Zach Smith, speaking on behalf of his clients, Robert and Althea Dulin, briefed the Council on the reason for the request and stated that the Agricultural Land Preservation Advisory Board and Planning Commission are in unanimous support of the legislation, and the Eastern Shore Land Conservancy spoke in favor of it before the Agricultural Land Preservation Advisory Board and the Planning Commission.  The legislation was introduced by Mr. Callahan, Ms. Mielke, and Mr. Stepp as Bill No. 1627.  A public hearing was scheduled for Tuesday, June 30, 2026, at 5:30 p.m. in the Bradley Meeting Room, South Wing, Talbot County Courthouse, 11 North Washington Street, Easton, Maryland 21601.

- G. <u>Request from Local Drug and Alcohol Coordinating Committee (LDACC) – Requested</u> Council concurrence for the submission to the State of a five-year Local Abatement Plan for Use of Opioid Restitution Funds.  Sheriff Joe Gamble stated that in 2023, the Council charged LDACC with drafting a plan for use of national opioid settlement funding which come  through the State of Maryland to Talbot County.  He stated that the two-year plan which was previously submitted is now expiring and a new five-year plan which will be in effect from July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2031, is required to be submitted to the State. He outlined various components of the Plan and the approximate amount of funding to be designated to various segments of the Plan, including the continuation of the Department of Corrections Opioid Use Disorder Program; funding for microgrants to nonprofits in the community, including Grace Street Recovery which serves as the County’s Safe Station;  the promotion of a “dual app” by the Sheriff’s Department and the Department of Emergency Services so that individuals seeking resources to recover from opioid use will be able to access those resources.  Council members expressed their appreciation to Sheriff Gamble and the members of LDACC for helping to ensure that the funding assists those members of the Talbot County community who have been hurt by opioid use and abuse.  Upon motion by Mr. Stepp, seconded by Mr. Lesher, the Council approved the five-year Local Abatement Plan for Use of Opioid Restitution Funds as presented, by voting 4 – 0 as follows:
