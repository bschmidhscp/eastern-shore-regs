County Council **MINUTES** April 22, 2025

- IV. <u>Proclamation:  Sexual Assault Awareness Month – April 2025 – The Clerk read a proclamation into</u> the record in recognition of April as Sexual Assault Awareness Month.  The proclamation spoke of the prevalence of, and violence often associated with, sexual assault, the sometimes long-term serious physical and/or psychological health issues which can result following sexual assaults, and the need for communities to work to provide support services for victims and to prevent sexual assault.  Katie Theeke, Vice President of Marketing and Commications, For All Seasons, Inc., (For All Seasons) stated that For All Seasons provides therapy, psychiatry, rape crisis services and education in the community, and is the only rape crisis center serving the more than 200,000 residents in the mid-shore region and briefed the Council on the prevalence of sexual assault in the mid-shore region.  She stated that this month’s campaign is helping to prevent drug-facilitated sexual assault through the creation of a Sip Safe beverage cover which goes over a drink to prevent an individual’s beverage from being drugged at bars, etc.; the covers have been distributed to Chesapeake College and Washington College.  Myisha Johnson, Victim Advocate, For All Seasons, Inc. then briefed the Council on the duties of her job, including being on-call 24/7 as well as serving as the support person for victims, including in court and at the hospital.  For All Seasons website is www.forallseasonsinc.org and the phone number is 410-822-1018; the crisis hotline is 410-820-5600 (410-829-6143 for Spanish speakers).    For All Seasons will be hosting a 

community art project event at The Ivy in Easton on Wednesday, April 23, 2025, between 11:00 a.m. and 2:00 p.m.; individuals may stop by and decorate denim patches which will be placed in For All Seasons’ Rape Crisis Center to show the community’s support.  Upon motion by Mr. Lesher, seconded by Ms. Haythe, the Council approved the proclamation by voting 5 – 0 as follows:

V. <u>Introduction of Administrative Resolution:</u> 

A RESOLUTION OF TALBOT COUNTY, MARYLAND (THE “COUNTY”), AUTHORIZING THE ISSUANCE AND SALE OF GENERAL OBLIGATION BONDS IN A PRINCIPAL AMOUNT NOT EXCEEDING TWENTY SIX MILLION NINE HUNDRED THOUSAND DOLLARS ($26,900,000) PURSUANT TO THE AUTHORITY OF BILL NO. 1583 ENACTED BY THE COUNTY COUNCIL OF TALBOT COUNTY ON FEBRUARY 25, 2025 (THE “PUBLIC LOCAL LAW”), SECTION 10-203 OF THE LOCAL GOVERNMENT ARTICLE OF THE ANNOTATED CODE OF MARYLAND (2013 REPLACEMENT VOLUME, AS AMENDED) (THE “ENABLING ACT”) AND SECTION 623 OF THE CHARTER OF TALBOT COUNTY, MARYLAND (THE “CHARTER”), THE BONDS, TO BE DESIGNATED “TALBOT COUNTY PUBLIC IMPROVEMENT BONDS, SERIES 2025”, THE NET PROCEEDS OF THE SALE THEREOF TO BE USED AND APPLIED FOR THE PUBLIC PURPOSE OF FINANCING OR REFINANCING THE PLANNING, DESIGN, ACQUISITION, RENOVATION, CONSTRUCTION AND/OR FURNISHING OF IMPROVEMENTS TO AND EXPANSION OF THE CHAPEL DISTRICT ELEMENTARY SCHOOL; PRESCRIBING THE FORM AND TENOR OF THE BONDS AND THE TERMS AND CONDITIONS FOR THE ISSUANCE AND SALE THEREOF AT PRIVATE OR PUBLIC SALE, INCLUDING THE FORM OF THE NOTICE OF SALE; PRESCRIBING THE TERMS AND CONDITIONS OF THE BONDS AND ALL OTHER DETAILS INCIDENT TO THE ISSUANCE, SALE AND DELIVERY OF THE BONDS; PROVIDING FOR THE DISBURSEMENT OF THE PROCEEDS OF THE BONDS; AUTHORIZING THE PREPARATION AND DISTRIBUTION OF A PRELIMINARY AND FINAL OFFICIAL STATEMENT IN CONNECTION WITH THE SALE OF THE BONDS; PROVIDING FOR THE LEVY AND COLLECTION OF ANY TAXES NECESSARY FOR THE PROMPT PAYMENT OF THE MATURING PRINCIPAL OF AND INTEREST ON THE BONDS, AND PROVIDING THAT THE FULL FAITH AND CREDIT AND TAXING POWER OF TALBOT COUNTY, MARYLAND SHALL BE PLEDGED TO THE PAYMENT OF SUCH PRINCIPAL AND INTEREST, SUBJECT TO THE LIMITATION ON THE TAX LEVY SET OUT IN SECTION 614 OF THE CHARTER OF TALBOT COUNTY; AND GENERALLY RELATING TO THE ISSUANCE, SALE, DELIVERY AND PAYMENT OF THE BONDS was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for introduction. ## VI. <u>Eligible for Vote:</u> 

Resolution No. 377, A RESOLUTION TO AMEND THE TALBOT COUNTY COMPREHENSIVE WATER AND SEWER PLAN BY RECLASSIFYING AND REMAPPING CERTAIN REAL PROPERTY LOCATED AT 3965 OCEAN GATEWAY, TRAPPE, MARYLAND, AND FURTHER DESCRIBED AS TAX MAP 55, PARCEL 51, FROM “UNPROGRAMMED” TO “S-1,” IMMEDIATE PRIORITY STATUS PURSUANT TO AN APPLICATION FILED BY THE TOWN OF TRAPPE, was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for vote.  Prior to the vote, Ray Clarke, County Engineer, stated that the purpose of the resolution is to clarify a previous mapping error which incorrectly classified the subject property as “S-1” when it should have been classified as “unprogrammed”.  He stated that the subject property, the Paris Foods Corporation property, was connected to water and sewer service in late 2021 or early 2022.  Mr. Lesher stated that he wished to introduce an amendment to Resolution No. 377 as had been recommended by the Public Works Advisory Board (at their Thursday, March 20, 2025, meeting) which includes a statement acknowledging that the property is already connected to the Town of Trappe’s sewer system but was done so in error.  Council discussion ensued with County Attorney, Patrick Thomas, who stated that the Council had had a public hearing on Resolution No. 377 on Tuesday, April 8, 2025.  Prior to that date, the Public Works Advisory Board had recommended Council approval, with the additional language included in Mr. Lesher’s amendment; the Planning Commission had also recommended Council approval, stating that the resolution was consistent with the 2016 County Comprehensive Plan.  The amendment was then introduced by Mr. Callahan, Ms. Haythe, Mr. Lesher, and Ms. Mielke.  The Council approved the amendment to Resolution No. 377 by voting 5 – 0 as follows: 

Mr. Callahan – Aye Mr. Stepp – Aye (via absentee ballot) 

Mr. Lesher – Aye Ms. Mielke – Aye 

Ms. Haythe – Aye 

Brennan Tarleton, Planning Officer, stated that the additional language offered by the Public Works Advisory Board was not a condition of the Planning Commission’s approval of Resolution No. 377.   Resolution No. 377, as amended, was brought forward for vote and Council discussion ensued.  Mr. Lesher stated that, in his opinion, there is still an outstanding question with regard to Resolution No. 377, whereby, the attorney for the Town of Trappe, during the discussion of Resolution No. 377 at the Council meeting on Tuesday, February 25, 2025, had declined to certify that the industrial discharge from the Paris Foods property was unrelated to the violations at the Trappe Wastewater Treatment Plant. Mr. Lesher made a motion to table Resolution No. 377 until the County had received a response from the Town of Trappe; the motion was seconded by Ms. Mielke.  The motion failed by a vote of 2 – 2 as follows: 

Mr. Callahan – Nay Mr. Lesher – Aye Ms. Mielke – Aye 

Ms. Haythe – Nay

- D. <u>Request from Department of Planning and Zoning – Requested Council approval to</u> have the County participate in the FY2025 Maryland Agricultural Land Preservation Foundation (MALPF) Easement Acquisition Matching Funds Program through the commitment of $990,586.51 in agricultural transfer tax monies.  Brennan Tarleton, Planning Officer, and Elisa Deflaux, Planner III, Department of Planning and Zoning,

<!-- page 6 -->
Minutes – April 22, 2025 P a g e | **6** 

stated that funding for the County’s participation in the program is derived from a percentage of monies collected from agricultural properties in Talbot County which are converted from agriculture to another use.  He stated that the request to designate more funding to the MALPF Matching Funds Program is being made this year in order to avoid having to remit the funding back to the State; agricultural transfer tax monies collected but not used within five (5) years, are required to be returned to the State and are then included for use in its General Fund; the State of Maryland made a 73% reduction to its preservation funding for FY26.   He stated that to date, Talbot County has permanently preserved over 14,000 acres of prime farmland on 91 farms through its partnership in the MALPF program and hopes to leverage this year’s funding to assist with easements on multiple farms.  Council discussion ensued with Mr. Tarleton.
