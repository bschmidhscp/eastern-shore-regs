- IV. <u>Council Recognition of Cadet John M. Schwaninger, IV, Easton Composite Squadron, MD-079, Civil Air Patrol – Maryland Wing – The Clerk read a certificate of recognition into the record</u> noting the accomplishment of Cadet John M. Schwaninger, IV, recent recipient of the Civil Air Patrol Amelia Earhart Award, for sustained excellence in all four areas of cadet life:  leadership, aerospace, fitness, and character.  The certificate also spoke of Cadet Schwaninger’s passing of comprehensive examinations on the topics of leadership and aerospace and his promotion to Cadet Captain, a feat accomplished by only three percent of Civil Air Patrol cadets.  Prior to presentation of the certificate of recognition, Marvin Nielsen, Deputy Commander, Easton Composite Squadron, Civil Air Patrol, U.S. Air Force Auxiliary, stated that Cadet Schwaninger had joined the Civil Air Patrol Cadet Program only three years ago and is currently the cadet commander of the 55-member cadet squadron.  He stated that Cadet Schwaninger plans to attend West Virginia University and major in engineering and is also a distinguished graduate of the Intermediate Aircrew School for the National Emergency Services Academy; he recently received his private pilot’s license.  Council members congratulated Cadet Schwaninger on his outstanding accomplishment and for his inspiration to other youth. 

   - Mr. Stepp presented Cadet Captain Schwaninger with the Certificate of Recognition.

- F. <u>Request from Chesapeake College – Requested Council approval of a request from</u> Chesapeake College for a Letter of Intent to provide local funding for design, construction and equipment for the Queen Anne’s Technical Center Project at Chesapeake College.  Mr. Stamp stated that the Council appropriated the funding in FY24; however, the State funding did not materialize so the County “unallocated” its funding.  Dr. Clifford Coppersmith, President, Chesapeake College, stated that his office has been working with the Maryland Higher Education Commission (MHEC) and other State agencies and has submitted a revised plan for the facility to the Maryland Department of General Services in hopes of being added to the capital project list for 2025; the revised plan includes removal of the marine tech program from the facility and having it remain at the Manufacturing Trade Center, thereby lowering the square footage for the proposed new Queen Anne’s Technical Center and

its cost by approximately $20 million.  Dr. David Harper, Vice President for Workforce and Academic Programs at Chesapeake College, outlined the curriculum to be offered at the Technical Center and provided photographs of similar technical centers at various locations throughout the nation.  Dr. Harper stated that the College will be working closely with businesses in the region to ensure that the equipment in the Technical Center is the correct equipment; the curriculum leads to local jobs, and that faculty members are providing opportunities for short-term certifications and long-term employment so that youth can remain in the area.   He stated that the College is also working with Career and Technical Education (CTE) programs in local high schools to ensure that students can receive the next level of certifications at the College.  Dr. Harper stated that a portion of the spacing in the Technical Center will be fixed spacing, with other spacing considered flexible; the agriculture, welding and electrical programs have been approved by the State for fixed spacing; HVAC, advance manufacturing, the construction trades are included in the square footage for flexible spacing. Karen Smith, Vice President for Administrative Services, Chesapeake College, outlined the amount of square footage in Phase 1 of the project – 63,000 square feet at a total cost of $56,438,240, which includes building design, site work, construction, utilities, equipment and demolition of the current Queen Anne’s building; 75% of the cost will be paid for by the State, with the remaining 25% by the participating counties.  She stated that Talbot County’s portion of design of the project is $207,658 in FY26; construction cost in FY27 is $2,595,726; and equipment cost in FY28 is $272,500 for a total of $3,075,884.  Ms. Smith stated that Chesapeake College’s previous request was $4.31 million.  Dr. Harper provided additional information on the spacing in the proposed facility and Council discussion ensued with the representatives. X. <u>Project Update on Extension of Sewer to Bozman and Neavitt as Outlined in Resolution No. 250</u> – Ray Clarke, County Engineer - Mr. Clarke stated that the Council, in 2018, approved Resolution No. 250 which amended the County’s Comprehensive Water and Sewer Plan to extend sewer service to the villages of Bozman and Neavitt for 610 dwelling units.  He stated that the resolution was subsequently approved by the Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE), design for the sewer extension was completed, and the project was bid out for construction earlier this year; two (2) contractors bid on the three (3) phases for the project: Phase 1A (extension of sewer from Scotts Lane to Bozman and Neavitt) was awarded to the lowest responsive and responsible bidder, Schummer, Incorporated, in the sum of $5,813,450; Phases 1B (boring under Broad Creek) and 1C (St. Michaels to Mount Pleasant before going under Broad Creek) were awarded to the lowest responsive and responsible bidder, Spring and Associates, in the sums of $683,810.00 and $1,640,750.00, respectively.  Mr. Clarke outlined the various phases of the work planned for the project which is anticipated to begin in October 2024. Council discussion ensued with Mr. Clarke who will continue to update the Council as the project moves forward.

XIII. <u>Work Session on Regional Medical Center Project and Transfer of MD Rt. 662 – The Council</u> met with representatives of Shore Health System, Inc., the Town of Easton, the Maryland State Highway Administration, local law enforcement, and County staff at 2:30 p.m. in the Bradley Meeting Room to discuss the proposed closure of MD Rt. 662 and matters related to same as a result of the Regional Medical Center project.  Patrick Thomas, County Attorney, stated that the existing Rt. 662, a State road, needs to be realigned to accommodate the location of the new hospital and that in 2013, the Council approved a road transfer agreement with the Maryland State Highway Administration (SHA) which outlined the terms of the transfer of MD Rt. 662 to the County.  He stated that under the terms of the agreement, SHA would transfer the road to the County after several conditions had been met – one of which was that a new Rt. 662 would be constructed by Shore Health System, Inc. which would then be transferred to SHA and the current route would be transferred to the County and the County would transfer to Shore Health System, Inc.  Mr. Thomas stated that the State’s process for transfer of a road to another public entity is much less cumbersome than to a private entity.  Mr. Callahan clarified that “transfer”

Ryan Showalter, attorney for Shore Health System, Inc., outlined the reasons for the request to close MD Rt. 662 before the new Rt. 662 is built, citing the grading necessary for the site, time for compaction of the soil once graded, and the pouring of the concrete for the facility all at one time instead of in stages.
