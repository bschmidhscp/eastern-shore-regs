- VII. <u>Update on Neighborhood Service Center’s Designation as Talbot County’s Community Action Agency – Mr. Stamp provided a briefing on the Neighborhood Service Center’s (NSC) designation</u> as the County’s Community Action Agency in follow-up to an inquiry made at the Council’s April 15, 2025 meeting.  He noted that in 1964 the federal government launched the "War on Poverty" and created Community Action Agencies (CAAs) to address poverty through community-driven solutions, funded directly by the federal government. In 1981 the Community Services Block Grant (CSBG) Act replaced direct federal funding with block grants to states, consolidating various programs while maintaining the core mission of CAAs: reducing poverty and promoting selfsufficiency. Under the State of Maryland’s Housing and Community Development Article §8-104 of Maryland law, counties may designate a CAA; however, such designation does not obligate a county to provide funding. Designated CAAs must meet state oversight requirements, including annual reporting to the Maryland Department of Housing and Community Development to ensure fiscal and programmatic compliance. Mr. Stamp noted in January 1985, the Talbot County Council

<!-- page 4 -->

designated the NSC as the official CAA. Although not legally required, the County has provided funding support which averaged $83,000 annually from 2008 until 2020 then increased each year to $374,472 in FY 2025 (for both operating and capital funding).  Most of NSC’s funding is derived from federal, state, and private sources and the County’s involvement is limited to its authority to designate the CAA, representation on the NSC board as outlined in its bylaws.  In closing Mr. Stamp noted that NSC is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization governed by bylaws, with representation from local government, community organizations, and individuals who support its mission. NSC operates independently of the Talbot County government. As the designated CAA, the NSC reports to the Maryland Department of Housing and Community Development and the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services.  Council thanked Mr. Stamp for the information provided.

Ms. Dill provided an overview of the Extension Office including their work with agriculture and food systems, home horticulture, watershed development, family consumer science program and the 4-H youth development program.  Ms. Dill referenced the University of Maryland

Ms. Dill stated that the Extension Office, among other duties, provides assistance with nutrient management plans as well as pesticide recertification and training.  She noted that the Women in Agriculture Program will be celebrating 25 years and the Corn Club will be celebrating 74 years.  Mr. Swaine noted that the Talbot County Farm Bureau currently has 203 members with 146 voting members (either farmers, landowners or agribusiness).  He reviewed new policies and amendments to policies adopted by the Talbot County Farm Bureau in October 2024 which include an Anti-Discrimination Statement to align with the Maryland Farm Bureau policy; and with regards to Talbot County policies, to state that the Farm Bureau is opposed to the establishment of a new bike or hiking path between the towns of Easton and Queen Anne on the abandoned railway; and in the event that Talbot County plans to regulate Food Processing Residuals (FPR) storage, they prefer an ordinance similar to that of Wicomico County.
