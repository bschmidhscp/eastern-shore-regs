- III. <u>Proclamation:  Community Action Month – May 2026 - The Clerk read a proclamation into the</u> record in recognition of the role of Community Action Agencies in communities throughout the nation in strengthening the communities they serve by providing food, emergency financial assistance, energy assistance, eviction prevention and other vital services to help low-income families build long-term stability.  The proclamation also spoke of the Neighborhood Service Center in Easton, which was founded in 1969 and for the past 37 years has served as Talbot County’s Community Action Agency, ensuring that programs and services reflect local priorities and needs.  In 2025, the Neighborhood Service Center provided services to one in 13 residents in Talbot County.  Upon motion by Mr. Stepp, seconded by Mr. Lesher, the Council approved the proclamation by voting 4 – 0 as follows: 

Mr. Callahan – Aye 

- Mr. Stepp – Aye Mr. Lesher – Aye 

Ms. Mielke – Aye 

Andy Hollis, Executive Director, Neighborhood Service Center (NSC), stated that the mission of the Neighborhood Service Center is to serve members of the Talbot County community who are impoverished or are on the brink of poverty.  He stated that the organization is continuing to see an increase in requests from the food pantry and homelessness prevention services.  He expressed his appreciation to the Council for the County’s continuing support of the NSC, particularly in light of reductions in funding at the federal and State levels and noted that Mr. Callahan currently serves as president of the Board of Directors for the Neighborhood Service Center.  Mr. Hollis stated that the NSC has opened a temporary pop-up furniture mart on Dover Street in Easton, across from Shore United Bank in space generously donated for use by Mr. Richard Marks; the furniture mart sells good, used furniture donated by members of the community.  He stated that volunteers pick up, deliver, and sell the furniture so that 100% of the proceeds go into the community.  He stated that the pop-up has been open two weekends, and to date has brought in approximately $8,000.  Mr. Hollis expressed his appreciation to the community for their support. He concluded his comments by stating that the Neighborhood Service Center partners with donors, the St. Michaels Community Center, Talbot Interfaith Shelter and St. Vincent DePaul to provide food pantry and other services to those in need in the Talbot County community.  Council members expressed their appreciation to Mr. Hollis and Yvette Robinson, Assistant Director, Neighborhood Service Center, for the work the Neighborhood Service Center does for Talbot County citizens in need.

IV. <u>Presentation and Request by Upper Shore Workforce Investment Board – Ellen LaFrankie,</u> Executive Director, Upper Shore Workforce Investment Board – Ms. LaFrankie expressed her appreciation to the Council for their continued support and the appointment of Talbot County representatives on the Upper Shore Workforce Investment Board (WIB).  She stated that the mission of the WIB is to provide high-quality career and training services to the residents of Talbot County with the goal of connecting individuals to meaningful employment in the region, thereby contributing to the economic health and economic stability of the communities served.  She outlined the purpose of the Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) as the document which establishes the guidelines for workforce partner collaboration on the delivery of high-quality services to individuals and businesses.  She stated that the MOU is required by the Maryland Governor’s Workforce Development Board Policy and that the MOU outlines the requirements for coordination, infrastructure funding and service delivery between the workforce system partners in order to effectively operate the local workforce system with the county; partners include the Maryland Department of Labor, Maryland Division of Rehabilitation Services, the Maryland Department of Social Services and Talbot County Department of Social Services and Chesapeake College.  She stated that the residents of Talbot County benefit from access to workforce training funds, industry recognized credentials and certifications, and comprehensive employment services which support pathways to sustainable employment; businesses benefit from a skilled and prepared workforce.  Ms. LaFrankie stated that the Resource Sharing Agreement outlines the financial and in-kind contributions made by each participating agency to ensure a clear framework for coordinating services and maximizing funds and efficiency;  no County funds are required.   Upon motion by Mr. Lesher, seconded by Ms. Mielke, the Council concurred with the MOU by voting 4 – 0 as follows: 

Mr. Callahan – Aye Mr. Stepp – Aye Mr. Lesher – Aye Ms. Mielke - Aye 

- V. <u>Update by Providence Strategies on 2026 Maryland General Assembly Session – Michael “Mike”</u> Mason, Principal, Providence Strategies – Mr. Mason provided an update on his job as lobbyist on behalf of Talbot County during the 2026 Legislative Session to keep the Council apprised of legislation before the Maryland General Assembly which pertained to Talbot County specifically, or county governments throughout the state.  He stated that he tracked a total of 234 proposed bills during the Legislative Session and he met with the Council each Thursday via Zoom to provide an opportunity for the Council to weigh in on various bills of interest to Talbot County.  Mr. Mason stated that one achievement for Talbot County was the receipt of $500,000 from the State for extension of water and sewer to the new University of Maryland Medical System Regional Medical Center.   He stated that items discussed each week included, among others, proposed legislation on land use policies, education, housing, law enforcement and public safety.  He stated that the Governor is still in the process of signing bills into law and that his office will continue to be engaged with the regulatory process for those bills which become law.  Mr. Mason concluded his comments by expressing his appreciation to the Council for the opportunity to work with county government.  A Legislative Report was provided to the Council.

## VII. <u>Introduction of Numbered Resolution:</u> 

A RESOLUTION TO AMEND RESOLUTION NO. 175, ENTITLED “A RESOLUTION TO ESTABLISH SEWER SERVICE CONNECTION POLICIES FROM THE UNIONVILLE, TUNIS MILLS AND COPPERVILLE SEWER SERVICE AREA LOW-PRESSURE FORCE MAIN COLLECTION SYSTEM TO THE ROYAL OAK PUMP STATION,” FOR THE PURPOSE OF PROVIDING THAT CONNECTION TO THE FORCE MAIN FROM PROPERTY OUTSIDE THE SEWER SERVICE AREA SHALL BE PERMITTED FROM IMPROVED PROPERITES WHERE PUBLIC SEWER IS AVAILABLE, SUBJECT TO CERTAIN CONDITIONS was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for introduction.  Prior to introduction, Ray Clarke, County Engineer, stated that the Council had recently held a work session with members of the Public Works Advisory Board and that the Board supports the proposed amendments to Resolution No. 175. The resolution was introduced by Mr. Callahan, Mr. Lesher, Ms. Mielke, and Mr. Stepp as Resolution No. 394.  A public hearing was scheduled for Tuesday, June 9, 2026, at 5:30 p.m. in the Bradley Meeting Room, South Wing, Talbot County Courthouse, 11 North Washington Street, Easton, Maryland 21601.  If Resolution No. 394 is approved, it would update Resolution No. 175, which outlines the criteria for new sewer connections to the infrastructure being constructed for the properties listed in Resolution No. 250 to address the growing interest by citizens in connecting their properties to public sewer, and to resolve the conflict between the current verbiage of Resolution No. 175 and Resolution Nos. 387 through 391 which are outside the sewer service area set by Resolution No. 175.

## IX. <u>Public Hearings on Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) Projects:</u> 

- For All Seasons, Inc. (MD25-CD-27) – 300 Talbot Street, Easton, MD  21601 – For Renovations to the Behavioral Health and Rape Crisis Center $750,000 CDBG award 

As required by the Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) process, a second public hearing was held on renovations to the For All Seasons, Inc. facility located at 300 Talbot Street in Easton. Mary Kay Verdery, Talbot County Grants Administrator, utilized a PowerPoint presentation to provide an overview of the renovation project at For All Seasons, Inc. which provides essential behavioral health and rape crisis services in the Mid-Shore Region.  She stated the funding for the renovation project was provided by the Maryland Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD) Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) in the sum of $750,000; additional funding of $100,000 was provided by the American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA); $300,000 from the Maryland Department of General Services ; and a $100,000 grant from the Harry and Jeannette Weinberg Foundation.  Ms. Verdery provided before and after photographs of the areas which were renovated or repurposed to support safe, private and emotionally comfortable environments for those affected by trauma. Ms. Verdery stated that the grant requires the tracking of 750 beneficiaries of services provided by For All Seasons, Inc., 675 of whom must be low- and moderate-income individuals.  She stated that the CDBG grant began in September 2024 and funding is required to be spent by July 31, 2026; renovations are anticipated to be completed Summer 2026, with a ribbon-cutting planned for August 11, 2026.  Total renovation cost was $1.2 million.   Ms. Verdery expressed her appreciation to the Council and the Department of Housing and Community Development for their support and stated that it had been a pleasure to work with the staff of For All Seasons, Inc., contractor, Harper & Sons, and Rauch Engineering on the project.  Members of the public were afforded an opportunity to comment on the CDBG project.
