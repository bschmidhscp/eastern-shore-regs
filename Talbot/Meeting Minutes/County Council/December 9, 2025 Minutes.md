- IV. <u>Certificate of Recognition to Cassandra Vanhooser, Maryland Tourism Person of the Year -  The</u> Clerk read a certificate of recognition into the record which spoke of Ms. Vanhooser’s selection as the 2025 Maryland Tourism Person of the Year at the recent Maryland Travel and Tourism Summit.  Ms. Vanhooser, who serves as the Director of the Talbot County Department of Economic Development & Tourism, was recognized for the enthusiasm and commitment she brings toward promoting Maryland as a world class tourism destination, for helping to bring greater visibility of the role of tourism as an important economic driver in Maryland, and for utilizing her many years of professional journalistic experience as a travel writer to strengthen partnerships which support small businesses, celebrate local heritage, and showcase the wonderful quality of life in Talbot County.  Upon motion by Mr. Lesher, seconded by Ms. Haythe, the Council approved the certificate of recognition by voting 5 – 0 as follows: 

Mr. Callahan - Aye Mr. Lesher – Aye 

Mr. Stepp – Aye Ms. Mielke – Aye Ms. Haythe – Aye 

Ms. Vanhooser expressed her appreciation to her peers and colleagues for the honor of being recognized.  She also expressed her appreciation to the Council, County Manager, Clay Stamp, Assistant County Manager, Jessica Morris, and the Department of Economic Development & Tourism team for their support of the Department. 

Ms. Haythe presented the Certificate of Recognition  to Ms. Vanhooser.  Clay Stamp, County Manager, was also in attendance.

## VII. <u>Public Hearing:</u> 

Bill No. 1623, A BILL TO AMEND CHAPTER 11 OF THE TALBOT COUNTY CODE (ALCOHOLIC BEVERAGES) FOR THE PURPOSES OF PERMITTING ON-PREMISES CONSUMPTION OF BEER, WINE, AND LIQUOR AT ALCOHOL DISPENSARY BISTROS, EXPANDING THE SEATING CAPACITY FOR ALCOHOL DISPENSARY BISTROS, EXEMPTING CLASS I LICENSES ISSUED FOR AN URBAN LOCATION FROM THE MARKET TEST SET FORTH IN § 11-8(D)(2) IF THE MUNICIPALITY IN WHICH THE URBAN LOCATION IS LOCATED DOES NOT HAVE AN EXISTING ALCOHOL DISPENSARY BISTRO HOLDING A VALID CLASS I LICENSE, AND EXEMPTING OXFORD CONSERVATION PARK FROM THE 500-FOOT DISTANCE RESTRICTION FOR NEW OFF-SALE LICENSES, was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for public hearing.  Prior to the public hearing, Patrick Thomas, County Attorney, stated that the legislation was requested by attorney, Adam Lynn, on behalf of a client who is interested in opening a restaurant with a small market in Oxford.  Mr. Thomas outlined the concept for the market portion of the proposed business, the Class I Alcoholic Beverages license, and stated that the proposed change to Chapter 11 (Alcoholic Beverages) of the Talbot County Code is similar to what Council previously approved for Thompson Park in Easton.  Members of the public were then afforded an opportunity to comment on the legislation.  Bill No. 1623 is eligible for vote on Tuesday, December 16, 2025, a Special Legislative Day.

Bill No. 1622, A BILL TO AMEND CHAPTER 190 (ZONING, SUBDIVISION, AND LAND DEVELOPMENT) OF THE TALBOT COUNTY CODE FOR THE PURPOSES OF PROVIDING FOR CLASSES OF SHORT-TERM RENTAL LICENSES AND AMENDING CERTAIN PROVISIONS PERTAINING TO SHORT-TERM RENTAL LICENSES, INCLUDING: 1) THE COMPOSITION AND TERM OF THE SHORT-TERM RENTAL REVIEW BOARD; 2) CONTENTS OF NEW APPLICATIONS; 3) HEARINGS, DECISIONS, AND APPEALS; 4) RENEWAL; 5) GROUNDS FOR DENIAL; AND 6) ADDITIONAL RESTRICTIONS, was read into the record by the Clerk and brought forward for vote.   Prior to the vote, Mr. Lesher stated he had been one of the sponsors of the legislation, but upon reflection, realizes that Bill No. 1622 does not address the concerns of some individuals, and raises concerns of others, and in his opinion, the Council needs to take another look at the issue and was therefore withdrawing his sponsorship.  Ms. Mielke, the other sponsor of the legislation, stated that from comments received, approximately twice as many citizens were in favor of the legislation.  She stated that those opposing the legislation were primarily STR (Short-Term Rental) owners who are not Talbot County residents and that 75% of the Short-Term Rentals are owned by LLCs or out-of-state residents, and 22% are owned by other entities.  She stated that the majority of the citizens who are in support of the legislation live in the TR (Town Residential) and TC (Town Conservation) zones which were not addressed in the legislation.  She stated that, in her opinion, the Council needs to amend the legislation to include provisions for the TR and TC zones, then have the legislation reviewed by the Planning Commission.  Ms. Mielke stated that there is a statewide trend to review and amend STR regulations and cited several jurisdictions which have, or plan to do so. Ms. Mielke also withdrew her sponsorship of the legislation.  Patrick Thomas, County Attorney, stated that as there are now no sponsors of the legislation, it dies for lack of a sponsor; without Council action, the legislation will expire on January 1, 2026.
