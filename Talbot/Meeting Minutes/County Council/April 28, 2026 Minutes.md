VI. <u>Presentation by Waterfowl Festival, Inc. on Economic Impact Study – Deena Kilmon, Executive</u> Director, Waterfowl Festival, Inc., stated that the mission of Waterfowl Festival, Inc. is to support Chesapeake Waterfowl and wildlife conservation.  She utilized a PowerPoint presentation to outline the priorities of the organization for the next three to five years which include:  (1) to establish a clear year-round organizational identity; (2) to transform Easton’s Historic Armory into a vibrant center for learning, wildlife conservation, cultural heritage and community engagement; and (3) to celebrate the Waterfowl Festival as a premier wildlife and sporting heritage event in support of conservation.  Ms. Kilmon outlined the organizational structure of Waterfowl Festival, Inc. which includes three employees, a Board of Directors of 15 members, and over 1,000 volunteers, including 300 student volunteers for the Waterfowl Festival event.  Ms. Kilmon then provided statistical information on the impact of the Waterfowl Festival on the economy of Talbot County and the state of Maryland, stating that according to the Institute for Service Research in Virginia, the 2025 Waterfowl Festival had a $5 to $6 million positive financial impact on Talbot County and a $9 million statewide impact;  and the sale of 13,500 tickets (32,400 attendees) purchased represented individuals from 45 states and Mexico; funding from the ticket sales helps to pay for infrastructure supporting organization’s mission.  Statistical information provided by Ms. Kilmon also included the percentages of individuals who attended from out of Talbot County, out of state, the number of overnight stays, and monies spent for lodging and shopping.  She outlined funding sources for Waterfowl Festival, Inc. and stated that since its origination in 1971, over $6 million has been donated to various local, regional and national organizations; $337,035 in scholarship funding has been provided to Talbot County students and many local nonprofits have benefited.  Ms. Kilmon provided information on free and non-ticketed events provided for over 10,000 individuals, several thousand of whom were children, at the 2025 Waterfowl Festival, including, among others, the Chesapeake Mermaid Story Hour, drawing with Tim Young, a raptor exhibition, and the Ward Museum children’s art activities.  Ms. Kilmon concluded her presentation by noting the location of various events planned for the 2026 Waterfowl Festival, the names of the various community organizations and partners which use the Historic Easton Armory for numerous events throughout the year, and the encouraged community support for maintaining the historic structure for the next 100 years.  The Waterfowl Festival will celebrate its 55<sup>th</sup> year in 2026.  Council expressed their appreciation to Ms. Kilmon for the information provided and for work with the Waterfowl Festival, known as a premier event in Talbot County.

She outlined various components of the proposed FY27 Budget which include the required local funding share for Talbot County Public Schools in the sum of $57 million, plus an additional $1.8 million for capital projects (Easton Middle School roof and exterior repairs to the Talbot County Education Center), $619,801 for teachers’ pensions and $307,663 for Universal Pre-K as mandated by the State; the appropriation to, and debt service for, Talbot County Public Schools totals 43% of the FY27 operating budget.  She stated that Fund Balance reserves will be used as follows: $5.5 million for Capital Outlay (equipment) purchases; $4.6 million for Capital Projects; and $2.2 million for Recreation Fund Capital Projects. 

Ms. Sparks outlined how revenue estimates were derived, stating that Property Taxes are the County’s largest revenue source (46.5%) and that the education supplement is incorporated into the Property Taxes but resets to zero (0) each year.  She stated that the other primary source of revenue for the County is Income Taxes (25.1%). Other local taxes such as Recordation and Transfer taxes are 9.4% of General Fund revenues.  She stated that General Fund expenditures have increased 5%, mainly as a fixed cost related to the County’s workforce; Capital Outlay increased by $2.4 million to $5.5 million, $2.3 million of which is the County’s contribution to the construction cost of the Queen Anne’s Technical Center at Chesapeake College.  She stated that the total proposed Property Tax rate has been calculated at $0.8180, which includes an education supplement of $0.0147, provided an example of the amount of Property Tax increase on a property assessed at $300,000, and the amount of Property Tax increase since 2019.  She provided information on the Tax Differential provided to residents in the incorporated towns, stating that the total benefit to the property owners was $4.1 million in FY25. She stated that although Talbot County has had the lowest Property Tax rate in the state for many years, it will no longer retain that distinction due to the increased educational costs mandated by the State under the Blueprint for Education and the fact that the County’s Charter-mandated revenue cap limits its flexibility to increase Property Tax revenue.  Ms. Sparks stated that if the County chooses to not take the two percent (2%) increase each year as provided for in the Charter, or the additional one cent ($.01) to the Property Tax rate for Public Safety in a year as approved, the County cannot make up the lost revenue in future years, even if education or other mandated cost rise sharply.  She stated that Talbot County has the second lowest Income Tax rate in Maryland (2.4%). 

She stated that the proposed budget continues the County’s conservative budgeting practices which have placed the County in a healthy financial position.  She stated that the FY26 General Fund Revenues are anticipated to slightly exceed budgeted projections and expenditures are on track to meet budgeted levels.
