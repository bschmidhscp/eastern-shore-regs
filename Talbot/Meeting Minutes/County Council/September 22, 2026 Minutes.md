VIII. <u>Presentation on Community Repair and Reinvestment Fund (CRRF) and Council Consideration of Adoption of CRRF Distribution Plan – Nancy Andrew, MSW, Director, Talbot County Office of</u> Community Partnerships – Ms. Andrew provided an update on the annual report from the Talbot County Community Reinvestment and Repair Fund (CRRF) as submitted by Talbot Family Network.  Ms. Andrew stated that the Council established Talbot County’s CRRF through Bill No. 1620 in late 2025 which designated Talbot Family Network Board of Directors to serve in an administrative role for distribution of funding received from the CRRF.  The CRRF was created by the Maryland General Assembly in 2023 for the purpose of reinvesting cannabis revenue in communities disproportionately impacted by past cannabis enforcement and related disparities.  She stated that the first annual report reflects nine (9) months of work to build the local structure, review of applications for funding and establishment of performance measures for the programs which receive funding. She stated that through June 2026, Talbot County had received $854,000 in CRRF funding from the State of Maryland; 19 applications had been received requesting $867,000 in funding, of which 10 projects were awarded $200,000 in the initial round; administrative costs were $3,315.  Ms. Andrew then outlined the various categories to which the first round of CRRF funding was provided, ranging from home repair and accessibility for seniors, to early childhood and after-school programming, to workforce and career development, literacy, and efforts to improve outcomes for children and families involved in the child welfare system.  She stated that lessons learned from that process, stakeholder and public input, and data specific to Talbot County were used to formulate the draft Distribution Plan before Council for consideration of approval.

XIV. <u>Meeting with Maryland Association of Counties (MACo) President, Board of Directors, MC Keegan-Ayer and Executive Director, Michael Sanderson – The Council met with Ms. Keegan-</u> Ayer and Mr. Sanderson at 3:00 p.m. on Tuesday, September 22, 2026, in the Bradley Meeting Room. Ms. Haythe, Ms. Mielke, and Mr. Stepp were in attendance and Mr. Lesher joined the meeting by telephone; Mr. Callahan was absent.  Michael Mason, Providence Strategies, and lobbyist for Talbot County, was also in attendance.  Ms. Keegan-Ayer stated that the purpose of MACo, a non-profit, non-partisan organization representing Maryland’s 23 counties and Baltimore City, is to assist local jurisdictions with finding more efficient ways to provide their citizens with the services they need and want, and to give local governments a voice in Annapolis.  Mr. Sanderson provided an update on the following topics: **Solar Facilities** – Mr. Sanderson stated that is a challenge for State leaders to know where solar facilities should be sited, causing tension between State and local leaders; **Affordability** – Mr. Sanderson stated that citizens are concerned about the continuing upward rise of energy prices, the lack of affordable housing inventory to allow individuals to live in the community in which they work, and the cost of childcare.  He stated that Maryland has had a higher cost of living compared to a lot of other states even though Maryland does offer some services that other states do not.  He stated across the nation, as in Maryland, there is “outward migration” to the southern and western parts of the country.  He stated that local elected officials and State Senators and Delegates, whether re-elected or newly elected, can do very little about the competition surrounding electricity rates, housing policies, or interest rates; **State Budget** – Mr. Sanderson spoke about the budgetary challenges facing the State which currently has a huge deficit resulting from a reduced growth in revenues from approximately 3% to 4% per year several years ago to approximately 1% - 1.5%  in recent years.  Mr. Sanderson stated that the State’s revenue deficit of approximately $2 billion is due in part to the loss of federal jobs, the accompanying jobs which provided goods and services to those federal employees and the “outward migration” from the state.   He stated that the enactment of the 10-year Blueprint for Education initiative, funded by the Education Trust Fund in its first years, but which is now out of monies, the rising costs for implementation without the State having a clear funding plan for continuing to fund the Blueprint, also factor into the deficit, despite more of the costs being passed onto the local jurisdictions, thereby requiring utilizing monies from the General Fund.  As such, the State has said “no” to several hundred million dollars per year in construction projects for renovation or replacement of schools constructed in the 60’s, 70’s, and 80’s. **Transportation** - Mr. Sanderson stated that revenues provided by the gas tax have decreased due to a decline in the number of vehicles using conventional motor fuels, despite increases in vehicle registration fees; the transportation budget infrastructure is predicated on revenues received from the gas tax or road tolls.  He emphasized that the State never gets through the budget resolution without participation from local jurisdictions, citing the examples of counties being required to primarily fund their local Tax Assessment Offices and an increased contribution to the teacher pensions in their jurisdiction – both unfunded mandates.  Council discussion ensued with Mr. Sanderson regarding various scenarios for resolving the current State Budget deficit.  He stated that the Governor has requested that all State agencies submit budgets reflecting a 3% decrease (97%) and a 10% decrease (90%) from last year and outlined what, in his opinion, would be the process and the most likely outcome for the State Budget in the next few years, including budget cuts, doing with less, raising fees and taxes and increasing the unfunded mandates for local jurisdictions.  Mike Mason, lobbyist for Talbot County, expressed his appreciation to MACo for their work on behalf of the 23 counties and Baltimore City and concurred with Mr. Sanderson’s comments that the State has huge fiscal hurdles to overcome.  Mr. Mason stated that Governor Moore will be signing an Executive Order relating to land use and development of data centers.
