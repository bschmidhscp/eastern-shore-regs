# Talbot County: By-Right Playbook

## 1. Bottom line

The by-right path is the **Minor Subdivision**: at most **3 new lots**, cumulative from the original parcel, with **no new or modified road** [§ 190-78 "Subdivision, Minor"]. The **Planning Director** decides; preliminary and final plat can be combined and go straight to a staff Compliance Review Meeting (CRM) [§ 190-66.2.A; § 190-68.3.B; § 190-69.4; § 190-70.4]. **No hearing, no posted sign, no newspaper ad, no adjoining-owner letters** apply to a minor; the only public exposure is the Technical Advisory Committee (TAC) agenda on the county website, open TAC meetings with written comments accepted for 7 days after [§ 190-67.3.D; § 190-68.2.B limits posting and mailing to major and small-scale]. The one catch is that the Director **may** send a minor to the Planning Commission "for a public forum" [§ 190-66.2.A.3], which would add agenda advertising and signs [§ 190-67.6.B]. Typical time to a recorded plat is about **5 months (estimate; range 3 to 9)**, because Director decisions are not minuted in the corpus.

## 2. Eligibility test

A parcel stays on the path only if every line passes.

- [ ] **Lot count.** 3 or fewer new lots now, and 3 or fewer cumulative from the original parcel [§ 190-78]. "Original parcel" = parcel as of **1991-06-22** (non-Critical Area) or **1989-08-13** (Critical Area) [§ 190-78 "Parcel, Original"; § 190-8.7.A]. Check every prior split since that date; the application asks whether the parcel has been subdivided since June 1989 [Subdivision Application, p.2].
- [ ] **No road.** Any public or private road created or modified makes it a major (or small-scale) subdivision and the major fee [§ 190-78; Planning Fees p.1 "if New Road"]. Bringing an existing right-of-way up to standard is excepted [§ 190-78 "Subdivision, Major" C].
- [ ] **Septic tier.** Minor works in every tier. Tier I (and sewered Tier II) is sewer-only per State law [Maryland Summary § 2; Env. § 9-206(g)]. In Tier III and IV, a minor on septic is allowed; any 4th lot is small-scale (Tier II unsewered, Tier IV) or major (sewered Tier II, Tier I, III) and goes to the Planning Commission [§ 190-78]. Confirm the parcel's tier on the Talbot map.
- [ ] **Density (zoning).** Must have enough development rights counted from the original parcel [Talbot Summary § 2]:
  - AC, CP, WRC: under 6 ac, 1 per 2 ac; 6 ac and up, 3 plus 1 per 20 ac. Table II-7 "maximum lots" **includes the remainder**: 6 to <20 ac = 3 lots total (2 new plus remainder); 20 to <40 ac = 4 total (3 new plus remainder); 40 to <60 ac = 5 total [§ 190-8.8.D]. A 3-new-lot minor therefore needs about 20+ acres in AC/CP/WRC.
  - AC/CP/WRC subdivisions must be **clustered** (6 to <20 ac: 2 clustered lots on at most 7 ac) with **reserved land** and a recorded reserved-land agreement [§ 190-8.8.A-D; § 190-73].
  - RC (Critical Area): 1 per 20 ac of the 1989 parcel [Table II-6]. RR: 1 per 5 ac, 2 ac lots [Table II-8]. TR/Village: 1 per acre unsewered [Tables II-9, II-10].
- [ ] **Lot minimums.** AC/CP/WRC/TC 1 ac, 100 ft width; RC/RR 2 ac, 200 ft [Talbot Summary § 2].
- [ ] **Frontage and access.** Each lot needs 50 ft frontage on a public or private road (25 ft if joint access) and direct access [§ 190-35.1, 35.2.A]. **In AC, CP, WRC, TC, RC, RR, TR, no more than two lots divided off an original parcel may take access from an existing County or state road; additional lots must use an internal road** unless a waiver finding applies (physical conditions, 10+ ac lots with no remaining rights, or no alternative) [§ 190-35.2.B.5]. This is the sharpest trap: lot 3 off the same road frontage can need a waiver. Ask staff whether the remainder counts.
- [ ] **Flag/pipestem lots.** Allowed in residential districts: stem at least 25 ft wide, stem area excluded from minimum lot area, no more than two pipestem lots with adjacent frontage, and adjacent pipestems share one driveway entrance with recorded access and maintenance easements [§ 190-36.3.B]. No stem through the Critical Area to reach water [§ 190-36.3.B.6].
- [ ] **Critical Area.** 1,000 ft of tidal water or wetlands [§ 190-15.2]. RCA density 1 per 20 ac; lot coverage 15%; buffer at least 100 ft; slopes 15%+ prohibited; BAT septic required [§ 190-15.6, 15.11; Maryland Summary § 1]. Growth allocation is a Council map amendment plus State approval and is not by-right. Critical Area Commission must be notified and the County cannot finalize until it confirms receipt [§ 190-54 (CA notice paragraph)].
- [ ] **Soils.** Each lot needs an approved 10,000 sf sewage area (initial plus two replacements) and passing perc [COMAR 26.04.02.04F; Maryland Summary § 2]. No lot may be created that cannot get a sanitary permit.
- [ ] **Ownership clean.** Title search, easements disclosed, all interest holders notified in writing [§ 190-70.2.C.1, C.2, C.9].
- [ ] **Not already a revision** of a prior minor that used up the cap. A minor and its remainder in Tiers II-IV may not be further subdivided and the plat must say so [Env. § 9-206(l)].

## 3. Step-by-step path

Fee column uses FY26 (effective 2025-07-01) [Planning Fees 2025-2026, p.1-2]. Durations are **estimates** unless cited.

1. **Feasibility, no fee.** We and our attorney: tax map, zoning, tier, 1991/1989 original parcel and prior-split history, Critical Area overlay, development rights. Optional pre-application meeting with Planning (mandatory only for major/small-scale; optional for minor) [§ 190-54.1.B]. Free to $50 "Request to the Planning Officer". 2 to 4 weeks.
2. **Boundary survey and soils/perc (start immediately).** Licensed surveyor; Health Department site evaluation. Perc and site tests must be done when the water table is highest, **February to April** on most Shore soils [COMAR 26.04.02.04B; Maryland Summary § 1]. Missing the window pushes the project about a year. Order the surveyor's topo and wetland flag at the same time. Cost: consultants only.
3. **Forest conservation.** In AC/CP/WRC/TC, retention threshold is 50% (25% or 20% in TR/villages) with reforestation 1/4 ac per acre above and 2 ac per acre below [§ 73-11]. Exempt if wholly inside the Critical Area (Critical Area rules apply instead) or a plat "for agricultural purposes only" [§ 73-3.A(2), (11)]. File a Forest Stand Delineation ($150 simplified / $300 intermediate / $450 full) and Forest Conservation Plan ($450), or a Declaration of Intent ($50) if exempt. Fee in lieu $0.30/sf (non-Critical Area) [Planning Fees p.2]. State rule since 2026-07-01 requires 1:1 replacement [Maryland Summary § 1]; verify the county ordinance has caught up.
4. **Sketch plan application to Planning.** Submit sketch, Subdivision Application (Minor), concept plan covering the **whole original parcel** including reserved land, and fee [§ 190-68; § 190-8.6.H; § 190-54.4]. Fee **$400 + $300 per lot** ($1,300 for 3 lots; Critical Area $400 + $350/lot = $1,450). Planning checks completeness, then schedules the next TAC. 1 to 2 weeks plus TAC calendar.
5. **TAC review.** Agencies (Health, County Engineer, Roads, Soil Conservation, Critical Area, Planning) comment; TAC is open but without public participation [§ 190-67.3]. Within **10 days** the Director issues a notice to proceed or noncompliance [§ 190-68.2.C]. Noncompliance: 9 months to resubmit [§ 190-68.2.D]. About 4 to 8 weeks from filing (estimate).
6. **Combined preliminary/final plat.** If the sketch is "substantially complete and correct" the Director's notice to proceed can authorize a combined preliminary/final plat that goes straight to CRM [§ 190-68.3.B; § 190-69.4.B]. Ask for this in the sketch cover letter. Otherwise add a preliminary TAC stage (about 6 more weeks and a repeat fee, below). Approval lapses 12 months after a notice to proceed, one 12-month extension [§ 190-67.8].
7. **Construction/stormwater/sediment plans.** Final plat requires evidence of approved construction plans, stormwater management and sediment and erosion control [§ 190-70.2.C.3]. Stormwater chapter exempts developments disturbing 5,000 sf or less [§ 164-3.2.C]; a 3-lot minor with driveways usually exceeds that, so plan on an ESD concept and a sediment plan through the Soil Conservation District/County Engineer. Fees are outside the Planning schedule (SCD fee schedule restructured 2026-04-14 per Council digest; amounts not found). Estimate 4 to 10 weeks, overlapping steps 5 to 6.
8. **Health Department sanitary approval.** Perc/soils results and the plat go to the County Health Officer, whose signature block is required on the plat [§ 190-70.2.B.3; COMAR 26.04.03.02K]. Fee not in corpus. 2 to 6 weeks after tests, estimate.
9. **Final plat package and CRM.** Required: surveyor-sealed plat, right-to-farm covenant on deeds and plat, owner's notarized consent, title search, easements, finalized reserved-land and developer agreements, covenants, other agency approvals, written assurance all title interest holders were notified, checklist, 10 paper copies [§ 190-70.2; Subdivision Application p.3]. Minor goes to the next CRM after the notice to proceed [§ 190-70.4]. Resubmittal fee 1/3 of original per extra prelim/final/CRM cycle ($433 for 3 lots) [Planning Fees p.1]. 3 to 6 weeks.
10. **Signatures and recording.** Planning Director, County Engineer, Health Officer sign; then record at the Talbot Circuit Court Land Records. Recording slip to Planning within 5 days [Subdivision Application p.2]. Court recording fees not in corpus (estimate low hundreds of dollars). 1 to 3 weeks.

**Total elapsed (estimate): low 3, typical 5, high 9 months**, driven by the perc window and agency turnaround. Sketch-to-approval for majors ran 3 to 6 months over 2 to 3 PC meetings [PC digest 2024-11-06, 2025-03-05]; a minor skips the PC cycles. Talbot minors are not minuted, so the minor-specific range is not documented.

## 4. Public notice

- **Minor (3 lots, no road, Director decision): no posted sign, no newspaper ad, no adjoining-owner mailing.** Table VII-2 posting, ad and mailing is triggered only where the chapter requires it, and § 190-68.2.B requires it for sketch plans only for "major and small-scale" subdivisions [§ 190-54.5.B; § 190-68.2.B].
- **What does exist:** the TAC agenda is displayed on the county website; TAC meetings are open to the public without participation; written comments are accepted up to 7 days after [§ 190-67.3.D.2-3]. The Planning Commission is not involved and there is no agenda item.
- **Variant that adds notice:** if the Director refers the minor to the Planning Commission "to provide a public forum," the PC meeting is advertised (website plus newspaper 15 days prior) and a sign is posted on each road frontage [§ 190-66.2.A.3; § 190-67.6.B]. The Director, not the applicant, controls this. A community meeting (newspaper plus adjacent owner mailing) can be required only for major and small-scale [§ 190-67.4.A].
- **Private notice:** we must give written assurance to every title-record interest holder [§ 190-70.2.C.9]. This is not public notice.
- **Anything above 3 lots or with a road loses the no-notice status** (small-scale and major require pre-application, PC hearing, posting, ad and adjoining mailing) [§ 190-54.1.B; § 190-67.6; § 190-68.2.B].

**Consultant studies and third-party costs (amounts not in corpus):** boundary and topographic survey and plat; Health Department soils/perc and site evaluation; Forest Stand Delineation and Forest Conservation Plan (or Declaration of Intent); wetland/stream delineation (needed near tidal or nontidal wetlands); stormwater (ESD) concept and sediment and erosion control plan; Critical Area report, buffer plan and lot coverage worksheet if within 1,000 ft; title search; reserved-land agreement and covenants (attorney); surety for any reforestation; Circuit Court recording fees. BAT septic is a builder cost (estimated $19,055 to $31,512 per system) but must be shown as a feasible design on the lot [Talbot Summary § 1; 2016 CompPlan p.121].

## 6. Off-ramps

| Trigger | Result | Design around it |
|---|---|---|
| 4th lot, or cumulative 4+ from the 1991/1989 original parcel | Small-scale or major: PC hearing, posting, mailing, pre-application [§ 190-78; § 190-67.6] | Run the cumulative count first; use a clean parcel or buy a parcel with no prior splits. |
| Any new or modified road or shared access that functions as a road | Major fees, PC [§ 190-78; Planning Fees p.1] | Use existing frontage; keep flag lots with shared driveways under § 190-36.3.B. |
| Third lot cannot access existing road (2-lot limit) | Needs waiver or internal road [§ 190-35.2.B.5] | Plan frontage for lots 1 and 2 on the road and lot 3 as paired pipestem sharing the driveway; get staff reading in writing. |
| Insufficient development rights/remainder lot | Lot count falls [§ 190-8.8.D, Table II-7] | Buy 20+ ac in AC; verify the remainder is counted. |
| Variance (lot width, frontage, setback, Critical Area) | Board of Appeals hearing, posting, mailing, $500 + $300 ad deposit [Planning Fees p.1]. BOA granted 147 of 151 decisions 2016-2026, 58 Critical Area variances [BOA digest] | Draw lots to code; avoid RCA density variances (not allowed under State law). |
| Special exception, rezoning, map amendment | Public hearing; $750 and up [Planning Fees p.1] | Avoid; buy land already zoned correctly. |
| Growth allocation (Critical Area) | Council plus State; $2,500 to $10,000 [Planning Fees p.1; § 190-55.5] | Stay with RCA 1 per 20 ac or buy outside the Critical Area. |
| Water and sewer plan amendment (S-category change) | Council resolution after PC review [Talbot Summary § 5] | Use septic with perc; do not depend on sewer extension. |
| Perc failure or high water table | Lot lost or BAT/mound system needed [COMAR 26.04.02.04] | Test in Feb-Apr before closing; make purchase contingent on perc. |
| Tier IV or unsewered Tier II with 4+ lots | Small-scale only, max 7 lots, PC [§ 190-78]; 8+ appears not possible [Talbot Summary § 4] | Cap at 3 for by-right. |
| Forest clearing over threshold or Critical Area forest limits | FCP, mitigation, fee in lieu [§ 73-11; § 190-15.6.A] | Cluster lots on cleared ground; place driveways off forest. |
| Floodplain, buffer or slope constraints | Lots may not be buildable [§ 70-14; § 190-15] | Screen with GIS before option. |
| Easements (MALPF, conservation) found in title | Added documents; may block lots [§ 190-70.2.C.2] | Title commitment before closing. |
| Director refers to PC, or 12-month approval lapses | Public forum; restart at sketch plan [§ 190-66.2.A.3; § 190-67.8.C] | File final promptly; calendar extension. |
| Comprehensive Plan rewrite (due fall 2026, adoption not found) | Possible density or tier changes | Lock approvals early; monitor Council digest. |

## 7. Open questions to ask staff

1. Does the **remainder parcel** count toward the "two lots with access to an existing road" limit in § 190-35.2.B.5, and what waiver standard does staff apply for a third frontage lot?
2. In AC/CP/WRC, does a 3-new-lot minor still require the full clustered/reserved-land layout of Table II-7 (and a reserved-land agreement), and how are the 2/7-acre cluster caps applied to a minor?
3. How often does the Director refer a minor to the Planning Commission under § 190-66.2.A.3, and what triggers it? Is any posting or mailing ever required for a minor?
4. Real-world timing: sketch to notice to proceed, and notice to CRM, for the last several minors; and does staff routinely authorize the combined preliminary/final?
5. Current forest conservation requirement (1:1 under the 2026 State law?) and whether a simplified FSD satisfies minors; any FY27 planning fee schedule change.
6. Health Department: exact perc season, fees, and whether wet-season tests are required county-wide; Soil Conservation District fees for a 3-lot minor.
7. Tier map status of the target parcel and whether the Comprehensive Plan rewrite is expected to change tiers or density.
