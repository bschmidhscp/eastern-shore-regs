SUBJECT: — Cecil County Sanitary District Policy Memorandum# 1: Allocation of Sanitary Sewer Capacity and Payment of Major Facility Fees 

DATE: December 1, 2017 

1, Purpose. This memorandum establishes policy and procedures for the allocation of sanitary sewer capacity and the payment of Major Facility Fees (“MFF”) for sanitary sewer systems owned by Cecil County, Maryland (“County”) and operated by the Cecil County Department of Public Works (“DPW”). The policy and procedures are intended to be equitable and to support economic development and growth management goals established by the County. 

2. Definitions. 

a. Allocation: A reservation for a particular building or project to discharge a prescribed amount and type of flow to the County’s sanitary sewer system. Allocation is expressed in Equivalent Living Units (“ELU’s”).

b. Benefit Assessment: Some capital projects are undertaken to satisfy a specific community’s needs and/or to provide growth capacity in specific areas, whereby the beneficiaries are specific, rather than general, in nature. The costs of these projects are not included in the calculation of the Major Facility Fee, which is paid by all new and expanded users. In these instances, the County may establish a Benefit Assessment (“BA”) for the project so that the actual beneficiaries of the project will fund all or part of the project cost. Where applicable, Benefit Assessments are in addition to the Major Facility Fee. 

c. Major Facility Fee: A fee paid to the County, prior to the issuance of the Occupancy Permit (including a Temporary Occupancy Permit), to help offset the capital costs ofnew treatment facilities, sanitary sewer interceptors, etc. The Major Facility Fee shall be based on the approved fee in effect at the time of issuance of the Occupancy Permit. The Major Facility Fee is charged on an Equivalent Living Unit basis projected for the project. The Major Facility Fee is separate from any costs the applicant may incur with private contractors for the actual physical connection to the County sewer line. All new connections to the County’s sewer systems must pay a Major Facility Fee, as do any expanded uses. Major Facility Fees can be paid in lump sum or financed as outlined in Exhibit C to this policy memorandum. Major Facility Fees are non-refundable. The Major Facility Fee currently in effect is as shown on the “Wastewater Division Fee Schedule” posted on the Cecil County Government website at www.ccgov.org. 

d. Equivalent Living Unit (“ELU”): A unit of measure used to equate flow demand to an equivalent ofone single family home. An ELU is assumed to be equal to 80,300 gallons per year (average of 220 gallons per day) of sanitary sewage production. 

e. Existing Structure: A residential dwelling or other structure that exists, is legally occupied, and is served by a septic system at the time the County’s sanitary sewer system is extended so as to be in a location to serve that property. 

f. Gallons Per Day (“GPD”): A unit of measure of the demand for sewer. Also, Million Gallons per Day (“MGD”). 

3. Authority to Allocate Sewer Capacity. The Director and Deputy Director of the Cecil County Department of Public Works are authorized to allocate capacity for County sanitary sewer systems. Allocations may also be made by the Chief, Development Plans Review Division of the Cecil County Department of Land Use and Development Services (“DLUDS”), provided that any such allocation is also approved by the Director of Public Works. Allocations are valid only if made in writing, and are constrained by the terms therein and subject to all relevant local, state, and federal regulations. 

4. Approval to Physically Connect to County Sewer. Sewer allocations are essentially a reservation of wastewater treatment plant capacity for a specific project, but do not constitute approval to physically connect to the County’s sanitary sewer system. Approval to physically connect to the County’s sewer system is granted by means of a Wastewater Permit Application, which is normally submitted at the same time as a Grading Permit Application.

5. Eligibility Requirements. In order to be able to request sewer allocation, an applicant must meet the following eligibility criteria: 

a. For a proposed commercial, industrial, or institutional facility where a Minor or Major Site Plan is required by the Zoning Ordinance, an administratively complete site plan must have been submitted to and accepted by DLUDS. In cases where a site plan is not required, an administratively complete Grading Permit application must have been submitted to and accepted by DLUDS. 

b. For a proposed residential Major Subdivision, as defined by the Subdivision Regulations, Preliminary Plat approval must have been received from the Planning Commission. 

c. For a proposed residential Minor Subdivision, as defined by the Subdivision Regulations, an administratively complete application for Minor Subdivision must have been submitted to and accepted by DLUDS. 

d. For an existing lot of record, an administratively complete Grading Permit application must have been submitted to and accepted by DLUDS. 

e. For an Existing Structure currently served by a septic or other on-site disposal system, a sewer line must have been extended so as to serve the parcel. Alternatively, an applicant may execute a Public Works Agreement with the Department of Public Works to extend a public sewer line or otherwise connect to a public sewer line in accordance with the terms of the Public Works Agreement.

7. Pre-Allocation Request Inquiries. The County recognizes that as part of their planning and/or due diligence processes, property owners and/or potential purchasers understandably wish to determine the likelihood of obtaining a sanitary sewer allocation for potential residential subdivision or other proposed development projects. Such parties are welcome to request such information via written request to the DLUDS Development Plans Review Division. The request should contain as much of the information required for allocation requests as is available at the time. DLUDS will provide an assessment regarding the likely availability of sewer allocation and other relevant considerations, with the understanding that such an assessment does not imply or constitute a guarantee of the availability of sewer allocation and that the parties proceed at their own risk with respect to sewer allocation until such time as a formal request for sewer allocation is made and granted. 

### 8. Flow Projection Guidance. 

a. An applicant’s allocation request must include a properly estimated design flow for the project, including the detailed basis for the estimates. In most instances, this design flow is best developed by the project engineer. The County reserves the right to require that the design flow estimate for a project be prepared by a Professional Engineer or Architect licensed in the State of Maryland. 

b. The two standard methods of estimating design flow for domestic (i.e. non-industrial) applications are a) Fixture Count Values (Exhibit A) and b) Design Flow Estimation Tables

(Exhibit B), Either method is acceptable. More complex or unusual projects require an engineering analysis based upon well-documented data and accepted engineering literature. 

c. DLUDS will base its allocation decision on conservative estimates of the flow. DLUDS may use any and all available data sources in its evaluation of the projected flow. 

d. The County differentiates between residential housing types. For example, flow projections vary based on housing type such as a single family residence, a mobile home, a boarding house, and apartments with different numbers of bedrooms. Refer to the Design Flow Estimation Tables (Exhibit B) for guidance regarding projected flow for each respective dwelling type. 

c. Bulk flows (i.e. those having flows in excess of one ELU) must be estimated carefully to ensure that the allocation will not be exceeded except in rare instances; hence, the flow estimate must be conservative. However, the estimate should not be grossly conservative, as this will unnecessarily consume wastewater treatment plant capacity and elevate Major Facility Fees and Benefit Assessments (if applicable) for the applicant. 

f. Commercial or industrial projects that are expected to experience a high degree of variability in daily flow demands will be examined closely before allocation is granted. The County will likely require substantially greater operational information so that the best possible projection can be made. DPW reserves the right to require such facilities to enter into a Special Discharge/Consumption Permit or an Industrial Discharge Permit with the County to regulate the flow quantity and, if the sewage flow is not consistent with domestic sewage, its discharge characteristics. 

g. Commercial, industrial, and other users that expect to generate sewer demand other than seven days per week may not average their flows over seven days or otherwise smooth their usage to suggest a lesser daily demand (e.g., in an attempt to reduce Major Facility Fees). The allocation of flow must assume that the flow froma project will be seen by the wastewater treatment plant every day (not just five days per week). For example, a manufacturing plant that anticipates operating five days per week and using 5,000 GPD (23 ELU) would use an estimated 1.3 million gallons per year; it is not permissible to divide this by 365 days to arrive at 3,562 GPD (17 ELU). 

h. When a projected flow for a bulk user is estimated, it is converted to ELU’s (for purpose of calculating Major Facility Fees, Benefit Assessments, etc.) by dividing the projected flow by 220 GPD/ELU. If the resulting number of ELUs is not a whole number, it is rounded up; the County may make an exception if the projection is within 0.1 of a whole number (e.g., 3.08 ELU could be rounded down to 3 ELU, at the County’s sole discretion). 

i. Sewer allocation, expressed in ELU’s, is normally determined based on hydraulic flows, i.e. the volume of sewage that will be generated per day. However, in cases where the sewage to be generated is uncharacteristic of domestic sewage (i.e. has elevated Biological Oxygen Demand (“BOD”), Total Suspended Solids (“TSS”), nitrogen, phosphorous, alkalinity, etc.), the sewer allocation (i.e. the number of ELU’s) may be increased for the purpose of calculating Major

Facility Fees and/or Benefit Assessments to reflect the fact that the sewage will consume more of the wastewater treatment plant’s capacity in terms of nutrients or other permit limits than would be suggested by its volume alone. For example, an industrial discharge of 2200 GPD (10 ELU’s) that is expected to contain 1320 mg/l BOD (~ six times that of typical domestic sewage) would be considered to be 60 ELU’s for purposes of Major Facility Fees, as it would consume six times the wastewater treatment plant capacity of an equivalent volume of domestic sewage. In this example, any Benefit Assessments for sewer collection system capacity upgrades would probably be based only upon the volume of flow (i.e. 10 ELU’s) because the capacity of the sewer collection system would be unaffected by the elevated BOD; on the other hand, any Benefit Assessments associated with wastewater treatment plant capacity would likely be based on 60 ELU’s. A commercial or industrial user generating such sewage can, of course, pre-treat its wastewater prior to its discharge into the County sewer system to make it consistent with domestic sewage, thus avoiding the need to increase its sewer allocation; in that case, the Major Facility Fees and/or Benefit Assessments would be based solely on the volume of flow. 

9. Increased Flow Demand. 

a. A facility may not exceed its sanitary sewer allocation without written approval from DLUDS. The County will audit usage from time to time to ensure that flows are consistent with the allocation that was issued. A user whose flows exceed their allocation will be notified in writing of that fact and given an opportunity to reduce flows or request an increase in sewer allocation. Users requiring an increase in sewer allocation will be required to pay the Major Facility Fee and any applicable Benefit Assessments for the increase in flow in effect at the time of the request. Anomalous periods of flow not indicative ofabuse will generally not be acted upon. Users who plan to expand their operation or change it in a manner that will increase their flow should request an increase in sewer allocation in the same manner as a new allocation request. 

b. In auditing usage or in evaluating a proposed change in use for an existing building, it is necessary to determine how much sewer allocation a user or property was previously granted. In most cases, the County will have formal records that will show that. In cases where there are no formal records of previous sewer allocations DLUDS will establish the historical baseline sewer allocation by examining historical usage data, typically looking at the previous two years, although DLUDS reserves the right to look at a longer period if, in its sole discretion, it determines that it is necessary to do so in order to get a true and accurate record. The historic flow shall be determined by examination of actual consumption reflected on utility bills from the facility and other information available to the County. 

10. Reservation of Allocations. Allocations cannot be reserved or purchased, although the County reserves the right to enter into Public-Private Partnership Agreements or similar arrangements whereby it grants sewer allocation in return for the financing, design, and/or construction of sewer infrastructure or other valuable consideration. Allocations are issued only for projects that have initial approval status, as detailed above, and are likely to proceed forward in the near future. Allocations are issued on a first come, first served basis. Allocations will typically be issued in accordance with construction phasing plans. The County reserves the right to, from time to time,

#### 11. Expiry. 

a. Generally, allocations will expire 12-24 months from the date of issue, unless renewed in writing by DLUDS. Any allocations without an explicit expiration shall be considered expired two years after the issuance date. If an applicant loses eligibility (e.g., the Concept Plat expires), any allocations issued for the project will expire and a new application must be submitted. Fees paid for expired allocations are non-refundable and non-transferable. The County is not likely to extend expiry for projects that are not being diligently pursued. 

b. An expired allocation can be re-issued at the County’s discretion, upon request in writing, but the allocation shall be considered a new application as it pertains to any required documentation. 

c. When an allocation expires, any unused balance not in substantial use shall expire and can only be re-established with a new allocation request. Unused balances may not be retained or transferred to another project. By way of examples: 

- 1) Residential subdivision lots established by recorded Final Plats shall be considered in substantial use, even if they remain unimproved at the time of expiry. 

- 2) A commercial or industrial facility with an allocation of 5,000 GPD that has been constructed and is operational at, for example, 4,200 GPD as an average shall continue to be entitled to operate up to an average of 5,000 GPD as long as the facility continuously operates. 

- 3) A commercial or industrial facility with an allocation of 5,000 GPD that has been constructed and has been operational for some time at, for example, 2,200 GPD as an average (perhaps the business enterprise didn’t develop as was expected or as quickly as expected) will not be able to claim an historic use of 5,000 GPD in a change of use allocation. If such a facility substantially underutilizes its allocation over an extended period of time (a year or more), they may not sharply increase their flow without review by the County. 

12. Ownership of Allocations. Applicants who are granted allocations do not own the allocations and may not transfer, trade, or sell them. The County will not unreasonably withhold approval to transfer allocations to a proposed buyer ofa site if the use of the property is consistent with the allocation. A request for such transfer must be made in writing, with substantial details of the transaction as they pertain to the allocation.

a. “The Major Facility Fee I pay the County prior to the Occupancy Permit issuance covers the actual work to install the line and make the physical connection from my house (or other structure) to the County’s sewer main.” That is incorrect. The Major Facility Fee helps pay the cost of capital projects which the County undertakes to expand the capacity of the County’s sanitary sewer system. The County does not physically connect your building nor does it pay the costs of your plumber out of the Major Facility Fee; those costs are the applicant’s responsibility. 

b. “If my property is within a mapped sewer service area, I am guaranteed service for sewer.” That, too, is incorrect. The County’s sewer service area maps are but one criteria used by DLUDS to determine eligibility for an allocation. Wastewater treatment plant capacity and sewer collection system capacity are also key factors. 

c. “I have an allocation that was issued some time ago (such as for a residential lot), so I am guaranteed service, even if I wait years to build.” That is also incorrect. Allocations have expiration dates. While they can be extended, circumstances (such as collection system and/or wastewater treatment plant capacities) can change over time which could mean that an extension would not be granted. The County will make every reasonable effort, however, to ensure that sewer allocation remains available for lots of record. 

14. Typical Conditions of Allocations. Conditions are typically attached to sewer allocations. While conditions may vary based on individual circumstances, typical conditions include, but may not be limited to, the following: 

a. Users are prohibited from knowingly exceeding their allocated amount without permission from the County. 

b. If actual flows are seen to be consistently greater than the allocated amount after occupancy begins, an additional allocation must be then requested and additional Major Facility Fees and Benefit Assessment would be due at that time, if allocation is available. 

c. Unless noted otherwise, all allocations are intended for normal strength domestic sewage. Industrial strength wastewater is not permitted under any allocation, except as noted in the specific case. No medical waste may be discharged. d. Non-domestic sewage is permitted only within approved parameters (these flows are included in the total allocation). [Example: Snack bar wastewater is permitted, conditioned upon the proper operation and maintenance of a grease interceptor. Wash down of a tire lube center floor is permitted, conditioned upon the proper operation and maintenance of an oil/water separator.] 

e. The allocation must be substantially in use by [a specific date] or it will expire, unless otherwise extended or revised by the County prior to that time.

15. Variances. 

a. The DLUDS Chief, Development Plans Review or someone acting in that capacity is the approval authority for variances from the policy and procedures outlined in this policy memorandum. An applicant seeking a variance shall submit such a request in writing and shall bear the burden of demonstrating that exceptional circumstances exist that were not the result of actions taken or not taken by the applicant which warrant such a variance. In granting a variance, the DLUDS Chief, Development Plans Review Division or someone acting in that capacity may prescribe such conditions as are deemed necessary and appropriate. 

b. Any party aggrieved by the decision of the DLUDS Chief, Development Plans Review Division or someone acting in that capacity regarding a request for a variance may appeal said decision in writing to the Director/Deputy Director of Public Works/Director of Sanitary Facilities. 

16. Proponent, The Director of the Cecil County Department of Public Works is the proponent for this policy memorandum. Suggestions, questions or concerns regarding this policy memorandum should be addressed in writing to the Director of Public Works, 200 Chesapeake Boulevard, Suite 2400, Elkton, MD 21921 or by phone at (410) 996-5259. 

17. Effective Date. The policy memorandum is effective immediately and supersedes the previous version of Cecil County Sanitary District Policy Memorandum # 1: Allocation of Sanitary Sewer Capacity & Payment of Major Facility Fees dated September 1, 2017.

## Exhibit C: Financing Payment of Major Facility Fees 

#### General. 

1. Major Facility Fees (“MFF”) (sometimes called connection fees) shall be due when billed unless financed using one of the financing options outlined in this policy memorandum. 

2. Unpaid MFF shall be a lien upon the property served until paid in full. 

3. The Department of Finance will process the Major Facility Fee Financing Option Application Form and Agreement within three (3) working days of receipt of a complete application. 

Option 1: New Residential, Commercial, and Industrial Developments (Except MultiFamily Apartments). 

- 1, This financing option is available to owners, builders, and/or developers of new residential, commercial and industrial projects and developments, with the exception of multi-family apartments. Option 3 below applies to multi-family apartment projects. 

2. The property owner can elect to pay the MFF in full or apply to use this financing option. Upon application to, and approval by, the Department of Finance, connection fees may be paid in installments as follows: one-third (1/3) of the total connection fee to be paid when initially billed, with the balance to be paid in annual installments over a period of ten (10) years with interest. 

3. Installment payments of one-tenth (1/10) of the balance due and owing will be paid annually with the property taxes assessed against the property, together with interest. 

- 4, The interest rate on the amount financed is the prime rate as printed in the Wall Street Journal as of the March 31* immediately prior to the date of the Major Facility Fee Financing Option Application Form and Agreement, plus two percent (2%). The interest rate shall remain fixed for the first sixty months. 

5. After the first sixty months, the interest rate will be adjusted to the greater of (a) the prime rate as printed in the Wall Street Journal as of the most recent March 31“ plus 3%, or (b) the interest rate that was assessed on the first sixty (60) month term. A new Amortization Schedule will be prepared as needed, and annual payments will then continue to be billed as before. 

6. Inall instances, any unpaid connection fees and interest thereon will be payable in full on sale or transfer of the property, with the exception of the sale or transfer of the property to a spouse, son, or daughter or the transfer from an incorporated entity to a subsidiary or parent incorporated entity.
