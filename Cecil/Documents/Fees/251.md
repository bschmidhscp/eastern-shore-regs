# <mark>Driving Independence: Cecil Transit Volunteers</mark> Making a Difference 



Before retiring, Michalena Klerlien spent 25 years working in various law firms in Wilmington, DE. Unfortunately, soon after retirement, she was diagnosed with cancer and had to undergo nuclear treatments, which left her unable to drive for at least a year. Wanting to stay independent and not rely on her family, Michalena investigated her options, which led her to Cecil Transit. She was happy to learn that Cecil Transit’s Demand Response service could help her maintain her quality of life and independence. She uses Demand Response for her weekly 

shopping and even uses it to connect with the neighboring DART network in Delaware. 

Ms. Klerlien must travel frequently to Christiana Hospital in Newark, DE, for crucial medical appointments. Cecil Transit’s Demand Response services are reserved for intra-county trips, meaning trips are not provided to locations outside of the County.  For these trips, she has three options:  rely on her family or friends (and the last thing she wants to do is burden them), pay for a taxicab at the cost of $90.00 per oneway trip (almost $200 round trip!), or rely on Cecil Transit’s volunteer program to get her to her appointments.  Cecil Transit provides out-of-county transportation to medical appointments through volunteer drivers for $40 per round trip.  Passengers over the age of 60 or who have a disability may qualify for half fare and pay only $20 per round trip.  Volunteer drivers can make a huge difference in someone’s life who may otherwise have to choose between keeping a critical medical appointment or paying another monthly expense. 

Managing a fixed income can be challenging.  The national average monthly income for a retiree is $1800. After paying essential expenses, including rent, groceries, medical expenses, and utilities, which average about $1440 per month, only about $360 per month remains for all other needs, such as clothing, transportation, insurance, personal care items, and unexpected costs; therefore, a $200 cab fare may not be a viable option.  As a result, an individual may choose not to attend their appointment, which may significantly jeopardize their health and well-being.  Often, individuals must choose between buying groceries and taking an expensive trip to maintain their healthcare appointments. 

Cecil Transit’s volunteers serve as lifelines! Cecil Transit has a limited number of volunteer drivers and currently cannot meet the demand of the number of requests received for out-of-county transportation. On average, Cecil Transit declines approximately 70% of all out-of-county transportation requests due to not having an available volunteer, leaving seniors like Michalena at risk of missing vital care. 

Volunteer drivers make a profound impact on their communities. Pam Burkhardt, a volunteer driver, shares, “Being a volunteer driver means I stay relevant while helping others. I have fun talking with customers and learning their stories.” For riders, a volunteer driver brings security, a sense of connection, and a lifeline to critical services. As Michalena puts it, “Cecil Transit keeps me independent. With out-ofcounty options, I don’t have to rely on my family or scramble for cab fare. “Volunteering as a driver helps seniors like Michalena live independently, stay connected to their communities, and access essential care— all without having to choose between their health and their finances.
