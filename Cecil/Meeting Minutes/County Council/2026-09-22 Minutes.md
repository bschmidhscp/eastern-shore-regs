### PRESENTATIONS 

Update - Cecil County Public Library — Rachel Wright, Executive Director of Cecil County Public Libraries 

Cecil County Public Libraries (CCPL) Executive Director Rachel Wright provided a Power Point Presentation overview of CCPL activities during the summer of 2026. A copy of Executive Director Wright’s presentation was placed in the record of the minutes. 

Executive Director Wright stated that library use has returned to pre-COVID numbers by all ages. Executive Director Wright noted that research indicates reading by young people over the summer break results in better preparation for school, adding that adults who utilize the library set positive examples for youth. 

Executive Director Wright stated that CCPL partnered with Cecil County Public Schools (CCPS) by serving 879 lunches at the Elkton and North East branches. Executive Director Wright announced the addition of a 6 Story Trail, adding that the program encourages families to visit the trails located throughout Cecil County. 

Executive Director Wright stated that the community came together for a memorial exhibit and programs regarding the impacts of 911. 

Executive Director Wright announced that CCPL was the only Maryland institution that was awardeda Big Read Grant, which was used to create the Exploring Appalachia Program. Executive Director Wright provided an overview of the CCPL Elkton Branch renovation project, stating that a pop-up branch is operating on Bridge Street to provide continuation of services. She added that CCPL operates an after-school lounge at the Elkton Community Center. Vice President Meffley queried if book mobile use was included in visitor data. Executive Director Wright answered in the affirmative. 

Councilwoman Branch commended Executive Director Wright for her work in Elkton. President Miller commended Executive Director Wright for her work at CCPL. Director of Administration Schneckenburger commended Executive Director Wright, stating she initiated many projects in the 1.5 years since she assumed her position.

Digital Infrastructure in Cecil County 

Land Use and Development Services (LUDS) Director Stephen O’Connor and Office of Economic Development (EOC) Director Sandra Edwards provided an overview of Digital Infrastructure in Cecil County via Power Point Presentation, a copy of which was reviewed and placed in the record of the minutes. 

Director of Administration Schneckenburger stated that digital infrastructure had been discussed over the past 3-4 months within Administration. He stated the purpose ofthe discussions was to determine current infrastructure and appropriate zoning for potential data centers and added the Administration’s desire for Council input. 

Director of Administration Schneckenburger stated that the County had received no applications for a data center at present, adding that this is the time to proactively identify appropriate locations for these facilities. 

Director Edwards reported that data centers were a popular topic during the Maryland Association of Counties (MACo) Summer Conference. She noted that data centers are a critical infrastructure component of the global digital industry, supporting Artificial Intelligence (Al), e- commerce, banking, healthcare, government operations, etc. 

Director Edwards noted that reliable digital infrastructure supports technology companies and enhances County agencies, emergency services, education systems and health care. Director Edwards noted that data centers can generate substantial real property and personal property tax revenue with little demand on County services, vs. the level of services required to support residential development. 

Director Edwards stated that data centers can provide an economic impact to local vendors and service providers as well. 

Director Edwards stated that a data center would position Cecil County as a strategic Mid-Atlantic hub for technology-driven growth. 

Director O’Connor reported that no data center applications have been received by the County. He stated his role is to determine where data centers should be located and the appropriate zoning, adding his request for Council’s feedback prior to initiation of a formal zoning text amendment. 

Director O’Connor reiterated that there are no pending applications, nor has LUDS been approached by any developer regarding data centers. He stated the County is in a good position to proactively develop a policy to address zoning and location of these facilities. Director O’Connor stated that the scale of digital infrastructure intensity is changing, adding that it is an emerging land use that the County needs to understand. He stated his desire for Council input regarding development of regulations before the County receives an application. Director O’Connor advised Council that the Cecil County Zoning Ordinance does not include data centers. He stated his recommendation that the facilities be permitted exclusively in M-2 Heavy Industrial Districts, adding that a proposed data center in the M-2 Zone would not automatically obtain approval. 

Director O’Connor reviewed (4) proposed Tiers of scale, based upon facility size. He requested Council’s input regarding the proposed tiers, specifically the threshold for Tier 4 designation. Director O’Connor provided examples of facilities that would be permitted within the proposed tiers, adding that scale restrictions would be based upon physical size vs. technology. Director O’Connor provided a map illustrating potential sites for data center facilities in the County, adding that the goal is to be restrictive but not prohibitive. He stated that potential sites included Elk Mills Quarry, Triumph Industrial Park and Principio Business Park. He advised Council that these sites are not being marketed, noting that the referenced sites illustrate where a facility would pass the zoning applicability test. 

Councilwoman Hamilton queried why the Bainbridge site was not listed. Director O’Connor stated that Bainbridge falls within the Town of Port Deposit’s jurisdiction. Director O’Connor stated that regulations such as water usage would not be specified, as data center cooling technology continues to evolve. He stated it would be counterproductive to require a specific process which may become outdated. 

Councilwoman Branch expressed concerns regarding disposal of coolant wastewater. Director O'Connor stated that hearing Council’s concerns was the goal of his presentation. Councilwoman Branch expressed appreciation for the proactive discussion.

Vice President Meffley noted that a golf course can use more water than a data center. Director O'Connor stated that the use of water depends upon the technology used. Vice President Meffley queried the process of coolant wastewater disposal. Director of Administration Schneckenburger stated that “closed loop” technology has evolved significantly, adding that use of coolant and disposal of wastewater are key questions for an applicant. Director O’Connor noted that the County regulates zoning, adding that the power grid is under the regulatory jurisdiction of regional power grid operator PJM and Federal regulators. Director O’Connor stated that County regulations will require that a facility must be designed to bring its own power, adding that a generator will not be included as a power source. Director of Administration Schneckenburger stated that facilities will not be permitted to hook up to the existing power grid. He noted that large data centers in Frederick County have been permitted to pull power from the local grid, which will not happen in Cecil County. He advised Council that if the State Legislature does not permit onsite power generation during its upcoming session, then there will be no data centers in Cecil County. 

Councilwoman Hamilton queried if onsite power generation would be a Cecil County bill vs. a statewide bill. Director of Administration Schneckenburger stated it would likely start as a Cecil County bill, adding his expectation that other counties may be interested in partnering with Cecil. Councilwoman Hamilton queried if a facility would be permitted a Small Modular Reactor (SMR). Director O’Connor advised that SMR’s fall within the Public Service Commission (PSC) regulatory jurisdiction regarding power generation. 

Councilwoman Hamilton queried if excess power from onsite power generation at a data center could be directed to the power grid. 

Director of Administration Schneckenburger stated that the goal would be to require onsite power generation specifically for the facility. 

Vice President Meffley expressed concerns that the State would usurp the County's regulatory authority. Director of Administration Schneckenburger stated that the County’s specific designation of M-2 zoning for data centers advises the State as well as the public that Cecil County is proactively putting clear guidelines and strong restrictions in place. 

Director O’Connor expressed his desire to have Cecil County recognized as a leader regarding regulation of data center facilities. County Executive Adam Streight expressed Administration’s goal to proactively address placement of data center facilities before the State dictates what the County can do. County Executive Streight noted that people generally think of data centers as a major facility which uses massive amounts of water and energy. He expressed Administration’s desire to educate the public in order to alleviate fear of a major facility moving into Cecil. He noted that Cecil County will not become home to a major data center facility. 

County Executive Streight reiterated previous comments that Administration’s goal is to have restrictions in place prior to an application. He noted that the presentation to Council is a starting point, adding that public input is requested. 

County Executive Streight noted his goal that a facility would generate tax revenue to the benefit of County’s citizens. 

Director of Administration Schneckenburger stated Administration’s goal of drafting regulations proactively vs. reactively issuing a moratorium, as has been the case in other counties. Vice President Meffley commended the Administration for its proactive efforts. 

Councilwoman Branch expressed concerns that onsite power generation could lead to additional noise, adding her concerns regarding wastewater and equipment disposal. Director of Administration Schneckenburger noted that Councilwoman Branch’s concerns regarding noise are one of the reasons for Administration’s restriction of the facilities to the M- 2 Zone. 

President Miller commended Administration for not permitting data centers in Agricultural Zones. 

Discussion ensued regarding requirements for fire suppression equipment. Councilwoman Culberson queried if the Department of Emergency Services (EMS) was included in the discussion regarding data centers. Director of Administration Schneckenburger responded in the affirmative. 



Councilwoman Branch noted that data centers, once constructed, do not employ large work forces. Director Edwards concurred, noting that data center jobs offer higher wages.

Councilwoman Hamilton expressed desire that potential data centers utilize local contractors during the construction process. 

Councilwoman Hamilton concurred that data centers do not present a large service burden for EMS and other County services, and suggested that facility-related tax revenue should be placed in a special fund, such as Capital Projects, vs. the General Fund. County Executive Streight concurred with Councilwoman Hamilton, adding that Administration will not pursue revenue for the sake of revenue. 

Director of Administration Schneckenburger advised Council that the State has created a task force to educate counties regarding raising revenue to offset reductions in state funding. He expressed his anticipation that Cecil County will face a substantial financial impact from cuts at the State level. 

Councilwoman Culberson concurred with Councilwoman Hamilton’s comments regarding dependance upon revenue froma single stream. County Executive Streight stated that potential revenue from a data center would be included as part of the County’s diversified financial portfolio. 

Councilwoman Hamilton queried if the proposed zoning text amendment would be introduced to Council once new members are inaugurated. Director O’Connor stated that the current Council is being included in the preliminary discussion, and the new Council members will be included as the resultant legislation progresses. 

Director of Administration Schneckenburger noted that the first meeting with new Council members will take place on December 15*, adding that the Public Hearing will be scheduled at a subsequent Legislative Session, followed by consideration in February.
