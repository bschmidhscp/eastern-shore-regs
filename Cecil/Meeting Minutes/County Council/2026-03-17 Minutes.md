COUNTY COUNCIL OF CECIL COUNTY LEGISLATIVE SESSION MINUTES LSD 2026-06 

March 17, 2026 

The County Council of Cecil County met in Legislative Session at the County Administration Building, Elk Room, 200 Chesapeake Blvd., Elkton, MD. The following members of the Council were present: Al Miller, Council President Robert Meffley, Council Vice President Rebecca Hamilton, Council Member Donna Culberson, Council Member Dawn Branch, Council Member 

NOTE: The recording with transcription of this meeting is available on the County website at www.cecilcountymd.gov 

## CALL TO ORDER 

The March 17, 2026 Legislative Session of the Cecil County Council was called to order by Council President Miller at 7:00 p.m. 

### PLEDGE OF ALLIEGIANCE 

President Miller led all those in attendance in the Pledge of Allegiance. 

# OPENING PRAYER/MOMENT OF SILENCE 

President Miller requested a moment of silence regarding the passing of Cecil County Government employee Dave Palmer on March 16", adding that Mr. Palmer is Council Executive Assistant Christine Palmer’s father-in-law. 

Pastor Richard Berry, Faith Southern Baptist Church, Elkton, Maryland offered the Opening Prayer 

ROLL CALL 

Council Manager Henson called the roll and noted that five members were present. 

## APPROVAL OF AGENDA 

On a motion made by Councilwoman Culberson, seconded by Councilwoman Hamilton, the Council moved to approve the agenda of March 17, 2026. The motion was approved unanimously. 

## APPROVAL OF MINUTES 

On a motion made by Vice President Meffley, seconded by Councilwoman Culberson, the Council moved to approve the minutes of the Legislative Session of March 3, 2026. 

### PUBLIC COMMENT 

Sean Brimm, North East, spoke of the need to proactively fund construction of schools. 

### PRESIDENT AND COUNCIL COMMENTS 

Councilwoman Culberson provided Emergency Services Intel for the previous week, reporting 227 calls for service, 16 of which were mental health related; 44 domestic calls, 26 of which were physical; and 4 overdose calls, 1 of which resulted in a fatality. 

Councilwoman Hamilton stated she attended the County Economic Development Commission meeting on March 4*, during which Mary Kane, President & CEO, Maryland Chamber of Commerce, provided an overview of the business climate in Maryland. 

Councilwoman Hamilton advised Council that Ms. Kane reported Oregon’s legislature recently passed a Constitutional amendment that prohibits the State from imposing new, mandatory programs or increased service levels on local governments without providing funding. Councilwoman Hamilton stated that Ms. Kane commented on new Legislators who are reaching across the aisle, seeking ways to support businesses in Maryland.

Councilwoman Branch wished all in attendance a Happy St. Patrick’s Day. Councilwoman Branch stated she attended the Decoy Book Store ribbon cutting event on March 14" at West Creek Village in North East and encouraged support of small businesses. Councilwoman Branch reported that she and Councilwoman Hamilton have been attending Legislative Committee meetings through the Maryland Association of Counties (MACo). 

Vice President Meffley reported that he serves on the Maryland Rural Development Corporation (MRDC) and commended the group for working through recent issues. Vice President Meffley stated he participated in the Chesapeake City St. Patrick’s Day parade on March 14", adding that he dressed as a leprechaun for the event. He expressed appreciation for the opportunity to interact with citizens during community events. 

Vice President Meffley stated he participated in the Read Across America event at Holly Hall Elementary School on March 3", during which he read to 2"4 and 4"" grade students. Vice President Meffley stated he attended the District 36 Night event in Annapolis on March 9' during which he spoke with delegations from several counties. He stated several bills that can affect Cecil County will be monitored over the next two weeks. 

President Miller stated he attended the Noxious Weed Board meeting on March 10", stating that the Roads Division will continue to spray weeds in a timely fashion. He added that, during the meeting, he heard numerous comments commending the County’s Roads Division for snow and ice removal operations during recent winter storm events. 

President Miller thanked the audience for attending the meeting. 

President Miller stated he attended the Chesapeake City St. Patrick’s Day parade on March 14 and expressed appreciation to the Town of Chesapeake City for hosting the well-attended event. President Miller stated the Farm Bureau 94" Annual Banquet will be held on March 21°.

A Resolution to amend the Annual Budget and Appropriations Ordinance for Cecil County, Maryland for the Fiscal Year ending June 30, 2026 to move expenditure authority from the Wastewater Enterprise Fund to the Department of Public Works — Wastewater Services, in the amount of $200,000 for expenses related to the emergency repair of the Washington Street Force Main.

### Resolution No. 11-2026 

Council Manager Henson stated for the record: 

Resolution No. 11-2026 Supplemental Appropriation — General Fund Balance — Department of Public Works — Roads Division — Snow Removal 

A Resolution to amend the Annual Budget and Appropriations Ordinance for Cecil County, Maryland for the Fiscal Year ending June 30, 2026 to move expenditure authority from the General Fund Balance to the Department of Public Works — Roads Division, in the amount of $750,000 for expenses related to the removal of snow and ice from County roadways. Introduced by Council President at the request of the County Executive 

Introduced and ordered posted on March 17, 2026; and scheduled for consideration on April 7, 2026 

Deputy Director Ondish stated that Resolution No. 11-2026 provides funding for unanticipated expenses resulting from the recent winter storm events. 

Resolution No. 12-2026 

Council Manager Henson stated for the record: Resolution No. 12-2026 Approval —- Amendment — Purchasing Manual 

A Resolution to approve amendments to the Purchasing Manual, herein renamed as the Procurement Manual, effective July 1, 2026 

Introduced by Council President at the request of the County Executive 

Introduced and ordered posted on March 17, 2026; and scheduled for consideration on April 7, 2026 

Director Hamm stated that revisions and updates to the Procurement Manual, which had not been updated since 2018, were presented during the earlier Work Session. He added that the Procurement Manual is utilized throughout Cecil County Government. 

Councilwoman Hamilton queried the source used for determination of meal allowances. Director Hamm stated Federal guidelines for Baltimore, Washington D.C. and Philadelphia were used to establish a meal allowance of $95 per day. He added that the allowance is not provided if an employee is attending a conference or other event at which meals are provided. Councilwoman Branch commended Procurement’s multi-step approval process. 

Director Hamm stated that the County Budget is not a guideline, but the authority which governs County spending. 

Councilwoman Hamilton queried the process for emergency purchases, such as an employee who must purchase a generator, the cost of which exceeds their purchasing card limit. Deputy Director Ondish stated that staff will be educated regarding confirmation of purchase card limits prior to purchasing items. 

Deputy Director Ondish stated that a purchasing card limit may be temporarily increased under specific circumstances, but will be returned to its original limit soon thereafter.
