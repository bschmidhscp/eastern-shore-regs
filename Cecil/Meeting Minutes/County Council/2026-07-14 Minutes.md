On a motion made by Councilwoman Culberson, seconded by Councilwoman Branch, the Council moved to approve the minutes of the Council Work Session of July 7, 2026. The motion passed unanimously. 

#### Presentations 

Discussion — Affordable Housing 

Maryland Department of Community Development — Mira Morgan, Senior Policy Analyst and Jordan Gilmore, Director, Office of Policy Development 

Senior Policy Analyst Morgan and Director Gilmore, representing the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD) presented an overview of affordable housing in Cecil County via Power Point Presentation, a copy of which was reviewed and placed in the record of the minutes. 

Director Gilmore stated that there is a shortage of affordable housing in Maryland as well as across the country. He stated the reason for the shortage is that annual residential building continues to remain at about 40% of levels prior to the 2008 financial crisis. He added that the lack of available housing has resulted in higher costs, noting that approximately 50% of renters pay more than 30%oftheir monthly income for housing and utilities. 



Policy Analyst Morgan stated that statistics indicate younger renters are struggling to afford rent and home ownership resulting in driving residents away from Maryland. She stated this trend will negatively impact Maryland’s economy. 

Director Gilmore reviewed data which illustrated the need for additional housing units to meet demand. 

Policy Analyst Morgan reviewed obstacles to residential building, including costs of materials, interest rates, and restrictive regulations. She stated that DHCD received a grant from the Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) to assist with improving approval timelines. 

Director Gilmore stated that 16% of land in Cecil County is zoned for residential use in Cecil County adding that current zoning regulations deter denser housing. He stated that larger lot sizes lead to higher housing costs by reducing cluster development. 

Director Gilmore noted that Maryland is the 6'" most regulated state in the US, leading to fewer affordable home options for young people as well as senior citizens. He stated that the best way to address denser housing is to direct new building toward population centers.

Policy Analyst Morgan stated that the majority of Cecil County Public Schools (CCPS) are below capacity. She stated the importance of providing affordable housing to teachers, which will result in them remaining in the state. 

Director Gilmore stated DHCD efforts to reduce costs of building through incentives that include property tax abatements, revising fees and using publicly owned land for housing to reduce purchase costs. 

Councilwoman Branch thanked Policy Analyst Morgan and Director Gilmore for their presentation, adding her concerns regarding housing costs. She stated that the Interagency Commission for School Construction (IAC) has outstanding payments to Cecil County regarding the North East Middle / High School facility. Councilwoman Culberson stated that Cecil County cannot be compared with areas such as Baltimore City, stating that Cecil is a rural county with large amounts of farmland. She added that many residents are in mid- low-income brackets and unable to afford housing costs. She expressed concerns that State efforts to streamline building requirements are dictating how local governments regulate building within their own communities. 

Councilwoman Culberson requested input from the Department of Land Use and Development (LUDS) regarding impacts resulting from the State’s goal of relaxing regulations. 

Vice President Meffley stated that improvement in efficiency needs to begin at the State level, adding that State regulations negatively impact construction of infrastructure. He stated that DHCD’s proposals for affordable housing may work in other areas but would not fit in Cecil County. 

Councilwoman Hamilton queried if DHCD’s assessments including the housing ownership market. Director Gilmore answered in the affirmative. 

Councilwoman Hamilton requested clarification regarding Director Gilmore’s comments that the free market does not provide for affordable housing, which results in the need for government subsidies. Director Gilmore stated that housing developers report that houses priced between $400,000 and $500,000 generally sell out. He stated that the issue is identifying affordable housing for retirees and those with disabilities, etc. He stated that affordable housing is not profitable for builders, adding that government can require affordable units via capping of rents. 

Councilwoman Hamilton stated that some of DHCD’s points regarding building costs were based upon State regulations, such as sprinklers. She queried DHCD’s efforts at reducing State restrictions. Director Gilmore stated that DHCD is working at the State level to address restrictions. He added that Gov. Moore issued an Executive Order in September 2025, directing State departments to streamline the process and reduce red tape. He stated the goal of DHCD to put forth policies that result in affordable housing. 

Vice President Meffley queried if the State offered financing programs for developers. Policy Analyst Morgan stated that DHCD provides financing programs for developers as well as lowincome tax credits and gap financing. She stated that DHCD is involved in the financing of most of the affordable housing in Maryland. 

President Miller noted that Maryland ranks low for affordable housing. He expressed concerns regarding unfunded mandates from the State of Maryland. 

Director of Administration Schneckenburger, referring to the Starter and Silver Home Act which was passed during the 2026 Legislative Session, stated that Cecil County would not support upzoning that would result in high density housing, adding that upzoning results in crowding. Director of Administration Schneckenburger stated that several citizens are opposed to large developments, will increase pressure on County government to resist upzoning.

Andrew Ondish, Deputy Director of Finance 

Financial Update — 3 Quarter Fiscal Year 2026 

Deputy Director of Finance Ondish presented the Financial Update for the 3 Quarter of Fiscal Year 2026 (FY26). A copy of the report was reviewed and placed in the record of the minutes. Deputy Director Ondish stated that March is three quarters through the fiscal year; and advised Council that he would focus on items that were above 75%. Deputy Director Ondish stated that no funds were used from the Fund Balance. Deputy Director Ondish noted that property tax funds will continue to flow toward the end of the fiscal year. 

Deputy Director Ondish stated a substantial decrease in overtime costs within the Department of Emergency Services (DES), which he attributed to full staffing levels. 

Deputy Director Ondish reviewed projections for utility costs and income tax payments. Councilwoman Hamilton queried an increase in income tax revenue. Deputy Director Ondish stated that income tax is administered at the State level, adding that the State does not provide for reconciliation of the numbers. 

Director of Administration Schneckenburger stated that some of the increase can be attributed to job growth, adding that Northrop Grumman and Ryder have added many jobs. He noted that commercial and industrial growth attribute to taxes. 

Director of Finance Raymond Hamm noted that the stock market played a role in County revenue.
