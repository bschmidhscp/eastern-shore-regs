COUNTY COUNCIL OF CECIL COUNTY WORK SESSION MINUTES 

February 10, 2026 OPEN SESSION — Elk Room 

4:30 P.M. 

NOTE: The recording and transcription of this meeting are available on the County website at www.cecilcountymd.gov 

PRESENT: Council Members: Robert Meffley, Rebecca Hamilton, Al Miller, Donna Culberson and Dawn Branch; Council Manager L. Michelle Henson; Council Assistant Christine Palmer; members of the Administration and members of the public. 

### Call to Order 

President Miller called the meeting to order at 4:30 p.m. and announced that there were five Council Members present which constitute a quorum. 

## Approval of Minutes 

On a motion made by Vice President Meffley, seconded by Councilwoman Branch, the Council moved to approve the minutes of the Council Work Session of February 3, 2026. The motion passed unanimously. 

## State Legislative Update 

Discussion — HB 0815 — County Council and Board of Education Elections — Single Member Districts — Delegate Kevin Hornberger 

Delegate Kevin Hornberger, District 35B, joined the Work Session via zoom. He stated that he was approached by Delegate Jeffrie Long, District 27B, sponsor of HB 0565, which would require all counties with at-large voting to adopt in-district voting. Del. Hornberger stated that Cecil County has been exempted from previous discussions regarding in-district voting. He stated that Cecil County, as a Charter Government, has the authority to enact this type of legislation via referendum, adding that this was the reason he sponsored HB 0815. He added that the National Association of Colored People (NAACP) requested that he initiate HB 0815. 

Del. Hornberger stated that in-district voting, if approved via referendum, would take effect in 2032, after the 2030 Census. 

Councilwoman Branch thanked Del. Hornberger for joining the meeting. Councilwoman Branch queried why Del. Hornberger did not previously communicate with Council regarding HB 0815. Del. Hornberger stated that in-district voting bills are initiated yearly, adding his purpose for joining the Work Session was to provide details of HB 0815. 

President Miller queried if Cecil County’s District 36 Delegation was consulted regarding HB 0815. Del. Hornberger answered in the negative, adding that he was not informed previously of Del. Long’s sponsorship of HB 0565. 

Councilwoman Culberson stated HB 0815 indicated that in-district voting would be scheduled for the November 2026 General Election. She queried Del. Hornberger’s statement that in-district voting would become effective in Cecil County in 2032. Del. Hornberger responded that if indistrict voting passed at the State level in 2026 then it would be put to referendum in Cecil County in a subsequent election. 

Councilwoman Hamilton noted that HB 0815 amended the State Constitution without amending the County’s Charter. Del. Hornberger stated that the power of elections is vested at the State level. 

Del. Hornberger stated that HB 0815 provides for initiation of in-district voting to be decided by County voters.

Del. Hornberger noted that the Maryland Association of Counties (MACo) has historically opposed bills related to local elections. 

President Miller queried Del. Hornberger if he was aware that the County Council and Administration opposed in-district voting. Del. Hornberger stated that the State Legislature is empowered to pass a bill, adding that HB 0815 provides the most effective avenue to keep the decision of in-district voting within Cecil County. 

Del. Hornberger opined that Gov. Moore and the House leadership will not succeed at redistricting at the federal level, adding that the next logical conclusion is they will look to establish in-district voting on a state-wide level. 

President Miller reiterated that establishing in-district voting on a state-wide level overrides Cecil County’s Charter. President Miller thanked Del. Hornberger for joining the meeting, adding his request that the Delegate inform Council prior to dropping a bill. 

Councilwoman Hamilton queried if HB 0815 would appear on the Cecil County ballot or as a Statewide initiative. Del. Hornberger stated the question would appear on Cecil County’s ballot as an amendment to the State Constitution. 

Councilwoman Branch queried if Del. Long spoke with Del. Hornberger prior to dropping HB 0565. Del. Hornberger answered in the negative. Councilwoman Branch queried if Del. President Miller stated Council received 3 letters of opposition from Cecil County Public Schools (CCPS) Board of Education members. 

County Executive Adam Streight thanked Del. Hornberger for attending the meeting. 

County Executive Streight stated the Del. Hornberger previously stated that he would serve as the eyes and ears of Cecil County and further asked the County to maintain communication. 

County Executive Streight expressed concerns that Del. Hornberger had not mentioned HB 0815 when they met in Del. Hornberger’s office on February 5". He added that during the meeting on February 5", Del. Hornberger did not mention his sponsorship of HB 0887, which impacts Cecil County’s authority to disperse funds from the Video Lottery Terminal (VLT) fund. 

County Executive Streight noted that Cecil County residents know best how to run Cecil County, adding that the County must maintain its autonomy. 

County Executive Streight asked Del. Hornberger to withdraw HB 0815. Del. Hornberger stated he was not willing to pull HB 0815. 

County Executive Streight reiterated his concerns regarding lack of communication regarding the bills. 

Executive Streight, referring to HB 0887, stated that in 2025, the County distributed $700,000 in VLT funds to local non-profits including $100,000 for betting purses at horse races held at Fair Hill. Noting that HB 0887 proposed utilizing $200,000 of County VLT funds to increase betting purses at horse races held at Fair Hill, Executive Streight reiterated his request for proactive communication between Del. Hornberger, County Administration and Council. 

President Miller reiterated his request for direct communication. 

Councilwoman Culberson queried the process for citizens to join legislative sessions. Del. Hornberger stated citizens can observe legislative sessions virtually via the Maryland General Assembly’s website.

### Presentations 

Update — Upper Shore Regional Council — Rural Economic Development Fund Grant — Executive Director Susan O'Neill 

Upper Shore Regional Council (USRC) Executive Director Susan O’Neill stated that the USRC, comprised of Cecil, Queen Anne’s and Kent Counties, is one of five Regional Councils in Maryland. 

Executive Director O’Neill stated that the Hogan Administration created the Rural Economic Development Fund in February 2023, from which $10 million was distributed to each of the five Regional Councils via the Maryland Department of Commerce (Commerce). She stated that each Regional Council was awarded $9.2 million and each County within each Regional Council was awarded $2.9 million after the USRC’s 3% retention. 

Councilwoman Hamilton queried if the funds were to be utilized for economic development. Executive Director answered in the affirmative, noting that administration and distribution of the Rural Economic Development Fund was directed by Commerce via grants. 

Executive Director O’Neill stated that Commerce established parameters for application, review and subsequent dispersing of funds, adding that individual Counties were given the decision to handle administration of the grants as they wished. 

Councilwoman Culberson queried how many County representatives were on each Regional Council. Executive Director O’Neill stated that each Regional Council represents 3 Counties, adding that there are 3 voting members from a Commissioner government and a Charter government has 2 voting members from County Council and 1 vote from the County Executive. 

Executive Director O’Neill stated that the Cecil County Farm Museum applied for and was awarded $150,000 and added that their project was finished, and all paperwork had been submitted. 

Executive O’Neill stated that Cecil County applied for and was awarded $2 million for the Belvidere Road Improvement Project, which was underway, adding that the final draw was dispersed last week, and that most of the paperwork had been submitted. She noted that an unrelated project had not been completed, resulting in an additional allotment of approximately $21,000 which will be dispersed to the Belvidere Road Improvement Project. 

Executive O’Neill stated that the Paris Foundation applied for and was awarded $750,000, which had been drawn down to approximately $150,000 for kitchen equipment. 

President Miller noted that representatives from Paris Foundation, the Farm Museum and the Department of Public Works were in attendance. 

Farm Museum President (FM President) Molly Brumbley stated that the Farm Museum entered into agreements with Bay Country (Contractor) and Cecil County Public Schools (CCPS) in 2024. She stated the purpose of the project was to complete a multi-use, regional agriculture center with a kitchen and bathroom. She stated the facility has not yet received a Certificate of Occupancy due to outstanding issues with the Contractor. 

Vice President Meffley stated his notes reflected that the Farm Museum project would provide a work force training facility for students from the School of Technology. He added that this was the qualifying reason for the grant application. 

President Miller stated that the Farm Museum is currently being used for Farm Bureau meetings and other ag-related events. FM President Brumbley noted the goal of providing education regarding Cecil County’s agricultural heritage and the economic importance of agriculture.
