The Queen Anne’s County Board of Appeals (the “Board”) held a meeting on April 15, 2026 at 5:00 p.m. to consider Case No. BOA-25-10-0231 for a variance application filed by Shipping Creek Partners, LLC (hereinafter the “Applicant”). The Board members present were Chairman Craig McGinnes, Vice-Chairman Howard Dean and Board Member Michael A. Lesniowski. 

At the beginning of the -hearing, the Board established that all requirements were met governing the filing of the variance application, and proper notice of the April 15" public hearing. Board Chairman McGinnes administered the oath to all who wished to testify on the application, including the Applicant. 

### Applicant’s Request 

The Applicant requests a variance to the provisions of § 18:1-41 of the Code of Public Local Laws of Queen Anne’s County (the “Code”), to replace in-kind the existing 141 ft. x 6 ft. pier including a 26 ft x. 21 ft. platform, 3 ft x 9 ft and 3 ft x 40 ft finger piers and two (2) boatlifts and (5) mooring piles with a total pier length of approximately 162 ft. from the mean high water line. The Applicant’s property is located at 304 Butlers Landing Dr., Stevensville, MD. (hereinafter the “Property”). The Property is located in the Neighborhood Conservation-2 (NC-2) Zoning District and Critical Area Limited Development Area (LDA). 

The Applicant submitted a Zoning Certificate Application (No. Z25-08-0420) which was denied by Mike Olds, Zoning Inspector, on October 10, 2025 after determining that the Planning Office had no record of the issuance of a prior certificate/permit and the proposed pier and

### Applicable Provisions of the Code 

The standards the Board must apply to the Applicant’s variance request are set forth in § 18:1-121.B. of the Code. To grant the requested variance, the Board must find as follows: 

1. Literal enforcement of this Chapter 18:1 would result in unnecessary hardship or practical difficulty as the result of specified conditions; 

2. Those conditions are peculiar to the property involved; 3. Those conditions are not the result of any action taken by the appellant; 4. The variance will not be contrary to the public interest; and 5. Evaluation of alternatives proves variance is required. 

Pursuant to §18:1-121.C., the Board shall consider the following in deciding whether to grant a variance: 

1. Ability to use the property for any reasonable purpose, whether or not such purpose is desired by the appellant, in the absence of the proposed variance 

2. The degree to which the proposed variance will affect adjacent property owners 

3. Impact of the proposed variance upon the resource protection provisions of Part 4, Article IX, of Chapter 18:1 

4. The degree to which the situation might be more properly addressed by amending this Chapter 18:1 

In addition, pursuant to § 18:1-122.A. of the Code, the Board must find that any variance granted is no greater than an amount minimally necessary to ameliorate the conditions giving rise to any practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship. 

## Property Description and Department of Planning & Zoning Recommendations 

Michael Olds, Zoning Inspector with the Department of Planning & Zoning presented his staff report. Mr. Olds testified that the Applicant is proposing to construct a 141 ft. x 6 ft. pier

including a 26 ft x. 21 ft. platform, 3 ft x 9 ft and 3 ft x 40 ft finger piers, two (2) boatlifts and (5) mooring piles, with a total pier length of approximately 162 ft. from the mean high water line. 

Mr. Olds indicated the Property is in the Fourth Election District of Queen Anne’s County and identified as Tax Map 70, Parcel 35, Lot 1. He stated that the property consists of 2.0 acres and is part of the Butlers Landing subdivision which was approved in May, 1984. He stated the property is accessed from Butlers Landing to the west and has direct frontage on Long Creek east of Romancoke Rd. Mr. Olds testified that the primary structure on the property was constructed in 1977 and the pier first appears in QAC GIS maps between 1963-1978. He stated that the entire property is located in the Limited Development Area (LDA) of the Critical Area and Floodplain is present toward the rear of the lot/waterfront with a designation of Zone AE (EL 5 FT). 

Mr. Olds testified the Planning Office has no record of approval for the original pier or for its current length and improvements, however, there is a current permit on file (#8260) issued August 10, 1976 for 100 ft. He indicated that the current pier is currently in disrepair. He stated the proposed project does not meet the requirements of Code §18:1-41 which limits the pier length to 150 ft. or % the distance of the waterway from centerline of the body of water, whichever is more restrictive. Mr. Olds testified that the width of the waterway is approximately 528 ft.; therefore, the pier length is limited to 132 ft. and a variance of 30 ft. would need to be granted for the Applicant to construct the proposed 162 ft. pier in this location. 

Mr. Olds stated that there are three existing piers located to the north and south of the property which exceed the maximum permitted length, however, only the one to the north at 167 ft. is properly permitted per a permit issued in 2012. He concluded by stating that Zoning staff has not received any evidence that outlines the practical difficulty regarding this property. 

### Applicant’s Presentation 

Mr. Grant Tanenbaum testified on behalf of the Applicant. He indicated that he bought the property four (4) years ago and the pier was on the property in the current configuration that he is asking to replace. He stated the practical difficulty with the property is that on the eastern side of Shipping Creek the slope of the ground under the water is very intense at approximately 150 ft. and there is only 4-5 ft. of water at high tide. He indicated that at 160 ft. the water depth is 6-7 ft.

304 Butlers Landing Dr. Pier Variance p.4 

He testified that he has sail boats which require 4-5 ft. of draft and with the lift underneath there is considerable difficulty to be able to use them without 6-7 ft. of water. Mr. Tanenbaum testified that Mr. Marsh, who installs boat lifts, located photos from an aerial survey from 1963 which showed a pier extending to 205 ft. He stated the intent is purely to replace in-kind what is already there and what he believed was permitted when purchasing the property and the proposal does not exceed any existing violations. He indicated that the pier is in a significant state of disrepair and is not safe for anyone to walk on as it has significant holes, exposed wires, and rotting wood. He testified that they have shut off the power to the pier and the condition of the pier makes repair impossible. 

In response to a question by Chairman McGinnes, Mr. Tanenbaum stated that he intends to keep a 32 ft. sailboat at the property with a 4 ft. keel. 

Mr. Mike Marsh of Boatlifts Unlimited testified in support of the Applicant. He stated that he initially did a survey of the pier to determine if repair was an option; however, the results indicated that a full replacement would be necessary as the pilings are down to six inches below water line in some places and the hardware is gone. He indicated that with the installation of the lift, the Applicant will lose approximately 1 ft. of water with the steel underneath and without 6-7 ft. if won’t work with the size of his boat and keel. He indicated that the pier in the current configuration was shown on aerial photos in 1978 and earlier, and it was presumed that there was not a problem as it pre-dated COMAR regulations. He stressed that what is being replaced is inkind and will not disturb anything additional. Mr. Marsh stated that he measured the water depth along the existing pier and there is a progression of 0-1 ft. at the bulkhead with a gradual fall until approximately 125-130 ft. and then there is a drop off and at the 162 ft. it is 6 ft. mean low and 7ft mean high. 

In response to a question by Chairman McGinnes, Mr. Tanenbaum testified that the neighbors to his south have piers that are 136 ft. and 167 ft. and the neighbor to the north has a pier that is 167 ft. He indicated that since purchasing the property four years earlier, he has done nothing to the pier and he has no objection to adding safety lighting to the end of the pier ifrequired by the Board.

### Testimony from the Public 

There was no testimony from the public. 

# Findings and Conclusions of the Board 

The Board finds the testimony and evidence presented by the Applicant credible and persuasive. The Board concludes that the evidence justifies approval of the requested variance. Based on the evidence presented, and duly considering the factors set forth in §18:1-121 of the Code as stated on the record at the time of the hearing, the Board specifically finds and concludes as follows: 

1. A literal enforcement of the Code would result in unnecessary hardship and practical difficulty and those conditions are peculiar to the property involved and are not from an action taken by the Applicant. 

2. The variance requested is not contrary to the public interest. 

3. There are no other alternatives that are viable such that the variance is required. 

4. The length requested is the minimum amount necessary to ameliorate the conditions present. 

##### Decision 

Based on the foregoing, by a vote of three in favor and none opposed, the Board grants to the Applicant variances from § 18:1-41 (1) 150 ft limit; and (2) % the distance from mean high water to the centerline of the body of water to replace an existing pier with improvements at a total length of approximately 162 ft from the mean high water line, with the conditions that the Applicant install and maintain safety lighting.

## FINAL DECISION OF THE BOARD 

#### Introduction 

The Queen Anne’s County Board of Appeals (the “Board”) held a meeting on April 15, 2026 at 5:15 p.m. to consider Case No. BOA-25-12-0235 for a variance application filed by James and Karen Monzo (hereinafter the “Applicants”). The Board members present were Chairman, Craig W. McGinnes, Vice-Chairman, Howard Dean and Member, Michael Lesniowski. 

At the beginning of the hearing, the Board established that all requirements were met governing the filing of the variance application and proper notice of the April 15" public hearing. Board Chairman McGinnes administered the oath to all who wished to testify on the application, including the Applicants. 

### Applicants’ Request 

The Applicants are seeking a variance to the provision of § 14:1-53.C(2) of the Code of Public Local Laws of Queen Anne’s County (the “Code”), to construct a 14’ x 28” in-ground pool beyond the shoreward extent of the existing structure on a parcel mapped Modified Buffer Area at their Property located at 650 Chester River Beach Road, Grasonville, MD (the “Property”). 

The Zoning Inspector denied the Applicants’ zoning certificate application Z25-10-0489 to construct an in-ground pool because the Applicants are seeking to locate the pool shoreward of the existing structures on the Property. Pursuant to § 14:1-53.C(2) of the Code, new development or redevelopment shall minimize the shoreward extent of intrusion into the Buffer Exempt Area and shall not exceed the shoreward extent of existing structures located on the Property.

3. Those conditions are not the result of any action taken by the applicant;

## Property Description and Department of Planning & Zoning Recommendations 

Mary Ellen Gray, Senior Planner with the Department of Planning & Zoning, presented her staff report. Ms. Gray identified the Property and its location. The Property is located at 650 Chester River Beach Rd., Grasonville, Maryland. The Property is designated as Parcel 568, Lot 44 on Zoning Map 58E, is zoned Neighborhood Conservation-8 (NC-8) and is 0.31 +/- Ac. in size. The Property is entirely within the State’s Chesapeake Bay Critical Area on land designated Limited Development Area (LDA). The Property is mapped Modified Buffer Area (“MBA”). 

The Property is improved with a single-family dwelling with screened patio on the rear of the Property and waterside steps off the patio, a driveway, walkway and pier. The waterside steps off the rear screened patio are the closest structure to the mean high-water line. Ms. Gray said that the proposed pool is 70.2’ on the north side and 61.1” on the south side from the mean highwater line. She testified that no new lot coverage is being proposed by the Applicants, rather, the Applicants are proposing to remove 1,095 sq. ft. of the existing driveway and replace it with native grass, small shrubs and trees post construction. She said the utilities, including well, water and

sewer line, as well as underground and overhead utility lines, are located on the front of the lot. Ms. Gray stated that after construction of the pool the lot coverage will be reduced from the existing 4,938 sq. ft. to Code compliant impervious coverage of 4,253 sq. ft., which is the maximum lot coverage allowed for the Property. She testified that there are no MD Habitat Protected Areas (HPA’s) on the Property aside from the Buffer. She stated that no trees or vegetation will be removed and the Applicants have submitted a Buffer Management Plan consisting of 4 Northern White Cedar tress within the Critical Area Buffer and the rest of the area is proposed to be planted in native grasses. 

Ms. Gray discussed the applicable standards that the Board must find to grant the variance. She stated that the Board must determine that the use is reasonable and significant to meet the unwarranted or unnecessary hardship standard. The Board has approved previous Buffer variances for pools, and many are located at nearby properties. She stated that the placement of the pool in the front yard or side yard is prohibited due to setbacks and the placement of utilities. She indicated that the proposed pool would result in minimal disturbance in the Buffer and the proposed project will not have a negative impact on water run-off, soil erosion, surrounding water quality or fish and plant habitats. 

Ms. Gray concluded by stating that staffdoes not object to the Board granting the requested variance, but the Board should further identify the unwarranted hardship specifically due to reasonable use. She indicated that in the event of approval by the Board, staff recommends that a condition be included that the Applicants cannot receive a building permit for the proposed improvements until a buffer management plan has been approved by the County.
