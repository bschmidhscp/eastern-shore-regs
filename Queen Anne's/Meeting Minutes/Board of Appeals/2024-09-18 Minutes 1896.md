FINAL DECISION OF THE BOARD 

Introduction 

The Queen Anne’s County Board of Appeals (the “Board”) held a meeting on September 18. 2024 at 5:00 p.m. to consider Case No. BOA-24-07-0187 for a variance application filed by John F. Reitzel and April Moore (hereinafter collectively the “Applicant”). The Board members present were Chairman, Howard Dean, Vice-Chairman, Craig W. McGinnes, and Board Member, Michael A. Lesniowski. 

At the beginning of the hearing, the Board established that all requirements were met governing the filing of the variance application, and proper notice of the September 18, 2024 public hearing. Chairman Dean administered the oath to all who wished to testify on the application, including the Applicant. 

###### Applicant’s Request 

The Applicant requests a variance to the provisions of§ 18:1-19.E.(1)(c)[4][d][i] of the Code of Public Local Laws of Queen Anne’s County (the “Code”), to reduce the required 25 foot front yard setback to 11 feet to construct a deck attached to their existing dwelling. The Applicant’s property is located at 103 1*' St., near Chester, MD, (hereinafter the “Property”). The Property is located in the Neighborhood Conservation-20 (NC-20) Zoning District and Limited Development Area (LDA) Critical Area designation. . 

The Applicant submitted a Building Permit (No. #BR24-06-0476) to construct a deck on an existing footprint. On May 16, 2024, the Queen Anne’s County Zoning Inspector denied the Applicant’s Building Permit after determining that the proposed deck did not meet the minimum setbacks required by § 18-1-19.E of<sup>theCode.</sup>

###### Applicable Provisions of the Code 

The standards the Board must apply to the Applicant’s variance request are set forth in § 18:1-121.B. of the Code. To grant the requested variance, the Board must find as follows: 

- ib Literal enforcement of this Chapter 18:1 would result in unnecessary hardship or practical difficulty as the result of specified conditions; 2. Those conditions are peculiar to the property involved; 3. Those conditions are not the result of any action taken by the appellant; 4. The variance will not be contrary to the public interest; and ab Evaluation of alternatives proves variance is required. 

In addition, pursuant to § 18:1-122.A. of the Code, the Board must find that any variance granted is no greater than an amount minimally necessary to ameliorate the conditions giving rise to any practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship. 

### Property Description and Department of Planning & Zoning Recommendations 

Mike Olds, Zoning Inspector with the Department of Planning & Zoning, presented his staff report. Mr. Olds testified that the Applicant is seeking to construct a 33 ft, x 8 ft. and 18 ft. x 10 ft. front wrap around deck. He said that the Applicant has requested a variance to reduce the required front yard setback from 25 feet to 11 feet. 

Mr. Olds identified the Property as being in the Fourth Election District of Queen Anne’s County and located in the western part of the county, on the central part of Kent Island, Dominion, approximately 2 miles south of US 50/301. The lot is identified as Tax Map 64, Parcel 154 and located at the corner of 103 1% St. and Little Creek Rd. in Chester. He stated that the lot size is 11,100 sq. ft. and is a non-conforming lot within the NC-20 zoning district with a single family dwelling constructed in 1986. He stated that the Applicant is seeking to remove a 10 ft. x 8 ft. front porch, 8 ft. x 23 ft. handicap ramp and a 4 ft. x 4 ft. entry landing and construct a new wrap around deck 33 ft. x 8 ft. and 18 ft. x 10 ft... Mr. Olds indicated that the QAC Zoning Department does not have any record of an approved permit for the existing deck and ramp located on the property. He indicated that as a non-conforming lot, Chapter 18:1-127(E) allows the lot to meet the setbacks of the zoning district closest to the non-conforming size, as such NC-8 setbacks are

###### Applicant’s Presentation 

Mrs. Moore, Applicant, testified that the deck proposed was needed as the steps on the side of the house were collapsing and they were trying to build a safe way to get in an out of their house without anyone getting hurt and property is too narrow to permit the 25 ft. 

Mr. Reitzel testified that initially he wasn’t aware a permit was needed for the proposed deck, and he didn’t become aware until a portion was complete. He stated that there was a prior deck on the property; however, he was unaware who built it or when it was installed. He testified that the old deck was actually slightly larger. In answer to Board questions, Mr. Reitzel stated that the proposed deck was needed as the prior deck was in disrepair and constructed poorly without structural support causing a safety hazard. He stated that in order to use the front of the house, the steps needed replaced as well, and the deck does not encroach any more than the prior deck. He further testified that the deck could not have shifted because of the septic location and existing parking area, and the size was the minimum needed to have an outdoor eating area with a table and chairs. 

###### Testimony from the Public 

There was no testimony from the public. 

##### Findings and Conclusions of the Board 

The Board finds the testimony and evidence presented by the Applicant credible and persuasive. The Board concludes that the evidence justifies approval of the requested variance. Based on the evidence presented, and duly considering the factors set forth in §18:1-121 of the Code, the Board specifically finds and concludes as follows:

1. The proposed deck will encroach less than that which existed prior and the previous deck was not safe. 

2. The shape of the lot and the location of where the home was built is peculiar to this property. 

3. The location of parking and the septic does not permit an alternative location. There are no other alternatives that are viable such that the variance is required.

###### Decision 

Based on the foregoing findings and conclusions, by a vote of three in favor and none opposed, the Board grants to the Applicant: 

A variance from the provisions of § § 18:1-19.E.(1)(c)[4][d][i] to permit the Applicant to reduce the required 25 foot front yard setback to 11 feet to construct a deck on the existing dwelling.

##### IV. Property Description and Department of Planning & Zoning Recommendations 

Doug Summers, Associate Planner with the Department of Planning & Zoning, presented his staff report. He testified that the Applicant is seeking conditional use approval to construct a telecommunications facility, and a variance to reduce the required setbacks from the property lines. Specifically, the Applicant is proposing to construct a 1,974 sq. ft. equipment compound to include a 145’ tall monopole structure. He stated the Applicant is also seeking reduced setbacks from the property line pursuant to the Code. He indicated the property is located in the southwest portion ofthe County, approx. 1 mile south of Batt’s Neck Park, and 2 mile north ofthe Kentmorr Airstrip. He more specifically identified the Property as Tax Map 63, Parcel 168, Lot 3, located at 2525 Romancoke Rd., at the site of the Kent Island Elks Club, and in the CS zone. 

Mr. Summers testified that there are non-tidal wetlands on site with a minimal amount of Critical Area (RCA) areas on site; however, no disturbance is proposed with the tower. He stated that this is tower two of a two tower solution to increase coverage to the mid Kent Island area and bridges a gap between Matapeake State Park to the north and the next tower south which is located 8725 Romancoke Road. The first tower was approved in July, 2024 by the Board. With regard to co-location, the Applicant established a five-mile search ring and no other structures were identified to accommodate the Applicant’s goals, and this was the most commercial like property in the area. The proposed tower location is in a densely wooded area, with priority given to the wetlands to the north, the property lines and a recreational trail across from the site. There will be an access and utility easement established on site. 

With respect to the variance request, Mr. Summers testified that the site is constrained by the property line setbacks. He stated the Code permits the BOA to grant a reduction of the facility setback to no less than % the height of the proposed tower plus 10 feet upon a showing by the Applicant that the proposed tower is structurally engineered in such a manner that a reduced fall zone is adequate by providing an engineer letter, which the Applicant has provided.
