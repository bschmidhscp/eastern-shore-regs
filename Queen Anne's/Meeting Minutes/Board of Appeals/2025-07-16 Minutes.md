###### FINAL DECISION OF THE BOARD 

Introduction 

The Queen Anne's County Board of Appeals (the "Board") held a meeting on July 16, 2025 at 5:00 p.m. to consider Case No. BOA-24-06-0185 for a floodplain variance application filed by Kentmorr Marina Properties, LLC (hereinafter the "Applicant”). The Board members present were Chairman Howard Dean, Vice-Chairman Craig W. McGinnes, and Board Member Michael A. Lesniowski. 

At the beginning of the hearing, the Board established that all requirements were met governing the filing of the variance application and proper notice of the July 16, 2025 public hearing. Chairman Dean administered the oath to all who wished to testify on the application, including the Applicant. 

###### Applicants’ Request 

The Applicant is the owner of a 3,815 sq. ft. parcel of land located at 304 Orchard St., Stevensville, MD 21666 (hereinafter the "Property"). Th Property is located in the Kent Island Suburban Commercial (KISC) Zoning District, is predominately within the Critical Area and designated as Limited Development Area (LDA). 

On April 25, 2024, the Applicant filed for a building permit (BC24-04-0037) to construct anew 41’ x 80’ structure on an existing building foundation to replace an old, damaged structure and which will be used as a boat repair facility. On May 31, 2024, John Kling, Certified Floodplain Manager (CFM), with the Queen Anne’s County Department of Public Works, denied the building permit finding that the application did not comply with QAC Floodplain Management Code §§ 14:3-42A(1),(3) “Elevation Requirements,” and 14:3-41.C(2) requirements regarding “enclosures below the lowest floor,” not exceeding 600 square feet in area (exterior measurement).

###### Testimony and Presentation of the Department of Planning & Zoning 

John Kling, Certified Floodplain Manager, (CFM) Queen Anne’s County Department of Public Works, presented the staff report. Mr. Kling identified the Property as Map 70, Parcel 30, and being stated it is located in the Kent Island Suburban Commercial (KISC) Zoning District and has a Critical Area designation of Limited Developed Area (LDA). 

Mr. Kling referenced his report submitted as Dept. of Public Works Ex. #1, wherein he covered, in depth, the factors that the Board must consider in deciding whether to grant the requested variance. 

Mr. Kling testified that the property had a building years ago that was destroyed, and the Applicant is proposing to build a large garage-type building on the vacant foundation. He stated the permit was denied because the renovation as proposed does not have the building elevated to the flood protection elevation of 7 ft., and the enclosure is larger than 600 sq. ft. He indicated the building proposed is a 3,200 sq. ft. boat repair building within the Special Flood Hazard Area (AE) with a National Flood Insurance Rate Maps (FIRM) Base Flood Elevation (BFE) of 5.0 Feet. He indicated the property consists of a half-acre lot and has two existing structures on the site in addition to the foundation which the proposed building will sit on. He testified that the flood zone just cuts into the building footprint by a few inches. He stated that the use being proposed is a functionally dependent use pursuant to the definition in the Ordinance. 

Mr. Kling concluded by stating that staff does not take exception to the granting of this variance. 

In response to a question by Board Member Lesniowski, Mr. Kling testified that the variance being requested is to both the Federal Regulation and County Regulation.

###### Applicant’s Presentation 

Joeseph Stevens, Esq., counsel for the Applicant, presented the Applicant’s case. Mr. Stevens noted that the exhibits presented by the Applicant outline the Applicant’s in depth response to the factors required to be considered by the Board. 

He stated that the building being requested is a functionally dependent use and is recognized in the County and FEMA regulations as appropriate for a variance and does not threaten the County’s flood insurance program if granted. He indicated that the building that was there was damaged in 2015, and the new building will be a smaller and more condensed building overall than the previous building. 

Jo Anne Baker-Gebelein, with Torchio Architects, testified that the foundation has existed since 1963 and the location was chosen for easy access from the boat lift. She stated that 4.42 ft. is the lowest elevation on the slab foundation. She stated that the CMU walls, which are filled with concrete and reinforced with flood resistant material, will all be above the flood protection elevation set by the County of 7 ft. She stated that they have included vents to provide for the water to escape in the event of a flood and all electric will be placed above 7 ft. and meets the necessary criteria. She testified it is important to be close to the travel lift to utilize the building for its intended purpose of boat repair services. She stated that this is the lowest part of the property, and most of the neighboring properties are above 10 ft. in elevation to where the grade is. 

Ms. Baker-Gebelein testified that in the event of high water, boats will be moved, and all tools will be above the 7 ft. mark or removed daily. She indicated that Kent Island is the biggest island in the Chesapeake Bay and the marina serves commercial and recreational boaters among several other users. She reiterated that the site had been previously used as a boat repair facility prior to being damaged in 2015 and the Applicant is just maintaining that use. Ms. Baker-Gebelein testified that in regards to compatibility with the Comprehensive Plan, economic viability of the commercial industry is important. She indicated that the Applicant will not create any rise or increase in water velocity. She stated that regarding the MDE comments, this is not an accessory use but rather a principal use in this area.

###### Findings and Conclusions of the Board 

Based on the testimony and evidence presented, and duly considering the factors set forth in the Queen Anne’s County Code, including but not limited to, §§ 14:3-65 and 14:3-66, the Board specifically finds and concludes as follows with respect to the requested variance: 

1. The Board adopted the comments as contained in the Architect’s report submitted as Applicant Exhibit #2, specifically as provided for on the last two pages of same, and such is incorporated into their findings as if fully set forth. 

2. The granting of the variance will not be contrary to the public interest. 3. A literal enforcement of these regulations will result in an unnecessary hardship. 4. There is a showing of good and sufficient cause to grant the variance. 

5. The failure to grant the variance will result in a unnecessary hardship due to the physical characteristics of the property.
