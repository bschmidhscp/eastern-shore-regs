### FINAL DECISION OF THE BOARD 

##### Introduction 

The Queen Anne’s County Board of Appeals (the “Board”) held a meeting on January 21, 2026 at 5:00 p.m. to consider Case No. BOA-25-09-0228 for a variance application filed by Jerry Philips & Kimberly Currey (hereinafter collectively the “Applicant”). The Board members present were Board Chairman, Howard Dean, Vice-Chairman, Criag McGinnes, and Board Member Michael A. Lesniowski. 

At the beginning of the hearing, the Board established that all requirements were met governing the filing of the variance application, and proper notice of the January 21, 2026 public hearing. Chairman Dean administered the oath to all who wished to testify on the application, including the Applicant. 

#### Applicant’s Request 

The Applicant requests a variance to the provisions of § 18:1-19.E.(1)(c)[4]{e][i] of the Code of Public Local Laws of Queen Anne’s County (the “Code”), to reduce the required 35 foot front yard setback to 9 feet to construct a replacement 3-story single-family dwelling on pilings. The Applicant’s property is located at 138 Winchester Ave., Grasonville, MD, (hereinafter the “Property”). The Property is located in the Neighborhood Conservation-2 (NC-2) Zoning District. 

The Applicant submitted a Building Permit (No. #BR-25-05-0409) to remove an existing single-family dwelling and to construct a new 3-story house on stilts. On August 19, 2025, the

#### Applicable Provisions ofthe Code 

The standards the Board must apply to the Applicant’s variance request are set forth in § 18:1-121.B. of the Code. To grant the requested variance, the Board must find as follows: 

- A. Literal enforcement of this Chapter 18:1 would result in unnecessary hardship or practical difficulty as the result of specified conditions; 2. Those conditions are peculiar to the property involved; 3. Those conditions are not the result of any action taken by the appellant; 4. The variance will not be contrary to the public interest; and 5. Evaluation of alternatives proves variance is required. 

Pursuant to §18:1-121.C., the Board shall consider the following in deciding whether to grant a variance: 

1. Ability to use the property for any reasonable purpose, whether or not such purpose is desired by the appellant, in the absence of the proposed variance 

2. The degree to which the proposed variance will affect adjacent property owners 

3. Impact of the proposed variance upon the resource protection provisions of Part 4, Article IX, of Chapter 18:1 

4. The degree to which the situation might be more properly addressed by amending this Chapter 18:1 

In addition, pursuant to § 18:1-122.A. of the Code, the Board must find that any variance granted is no greater than an amount minimally necessary to ameliorate the conditions giving rise to any practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship.

# Property Description and Department of Planning & Zoning Recommendations 

Robert Owen, Zoning Inspector with the Department of Planning & Zoning, presented his staff report. Mr. Owen testified that the Applicant is seeking to reduce the required front yard setback requirement along Winchester Creek Road from 35ft to 9ft to construct a three -story single family residence. 

Mr. Owen identified the Property as being in the Fifth Election District of Queen Anne’s County and identified as Tax Map 58E__ Parcel 67. He stated the property is .39 Ac. and is located at the end of Winchester Ave. in Grasonville, on the shores of the Winchester Creek. 

Mr. Owen indicated that there is currently a dwelling on the parcel which has been there since the 1950’s. He stated that the property is zoned NC-2 which normally requires 2 Ac. lots, however, this lot is only 17,000 square feet and is subject to NC-15 building code. The parcel is in the Intensely Developed Area (IDA) and is completely in the flood zone with an elevation of 6 feet. His report indicated that there are no hydric soils on the parcel and no evidence of non-tidal wetlands present. 

Mr. Owen testified that historic zoning permitted the home at the time; however, in 1980 the zoning changed to NC-2 requiring a 150” lot width, making this lot legally non-conforming. He indicated that the Applicant is proposing 4,338 square feet of lot coverage and the proposed dwelling is within the 15% lot coverage limit. He stated the property is mapped modified buffer area and the proposed single-family dwelling is located within the 100’ Critical Area Buffer. He stated the proposed dwelling does not exceed the shoreward extent of existing structures. Mr. Owen testified the proposal is consistent with other lots in the neighborhood with similar lot sizes and the road frontage presents a significant challenge for constructing a dwelling on the lot. 

Mr. Owen concluded by stating that Zoning staff has no objections to the proposal, but the Applicant must present clear evidence of practical difficulty. 

Applicant’s Presentation 

Dorsey Schulz and Garrett Currey testified on behalfof the Applicant. Mr. Schulz testified the house on the property was originally built in 1957 and Mr. Currey’s parents purchased the property in September of 2006. The family used the property as a vacation home. He stated that

Mr. Currey moved in full-time in 2019 and lived there until August 2024 when the condition of the house made it impossible to continue to reside there. Mr. Schulz said that this process was then started with the hope of rebuilding what is existing within current standards. 

Mr. Schulz stated that the Applicant has made every effort to make the site work within the required zoning requirements but due to site constraints and the property line with the road coming in the front corner presents a unique and difficult aspect of the site especially when trying to limit any increase waterward of the impervious structures. He testified that the proposed impervious coverage will be less than what exists presently. 

In response to a question from Board-Member Lesniowski, Mr. Schulz stated that the footprint of the proposed house is 2,974 square feet. He indicated that the first floor is garage area, second floor is the start of living area and then third floor is also living space. 

Mr. Currey testified that he intends to use the existing foundation to rebuild the home and does not want to disturb any other land. He further indicated that the building restriction line is a very odd shape and makes placement of the home very difficult. 

Mr. Schulz testified that the uniqueness of Winchester Ave. protruding into the site is what causes the hardship in this case. He further stated that the houses in the neighborhood are similar in characteristic to this house. 

In response to a question by Vice Chairman McGinnes, Mr. Currey indicated that he talked with two neighbors and neither had an issue. 

#### Testimony from the Public 

Mr. Channa Perera, 137 Winchester Dr., Grasonville, MD, the Applicant’s neighbor, testified in favor of the Applicant’s variance request and he stated he does not believe that it will impact anyone in the neighborhood. 

#### Findings and Conclusions of the Board 

The Board finds the testimony and evidence presented by the Applicant credible and persuasive. The Board concludes that the evidence justifies approval of the requested variance.

Based on the evidence presented, and duly considering the factors set forth in §18:1-121 of the Code, the Board specifically finds and concludes as follows: 

1. The property cannot be used for its current purpose in the absence of the variance as this is the only place the Applicant could locate a reasonable footprint on this property and the property would be unusable without the variance. 

2. The testimony from the neighbor and the lack of Opposition supports that the variance will not impact adjacent property owners. 

3. The variance will have no impact on the resource protection provisions of Part 4, Article IX of Chapter 18:1, and will not be contrary to the public interest. 

4. Itis not practical to amend Chapter 18 to address this specific variance. 

5. A literal enforcement of the setback would result in unnecessary hardship and practical difficulty and those conditions are peculiar to the property involved and are not from any action taken by the Applicant. 

6. There are no other feasible alternatives without the variance. 

7. The variance request is the amount minimally necessary to ameliorate the conditions giving rise to the practical difficulty and unnecessary hardship. 

##### Decision 

Based on the foregoing findings and conclusions, by a vote of three in favor and none opposed, the Board grants to the Applicant a variance from §18:1-19.E.(1)(c)[4]{e][ii] to reduce the required 35 fi front yard setback from Winchester Avenue to 9 ft to construct a 3-story singlefamily replacement dwelling with porch.
