### VII. Findings and Conclusions of the Board 

The Board finds the testimony and evidence presented by Applicants credible and persuasive. The Board concludes that the evidence justifies approval of the requested variance. Based on the evidence presented, and duly considering the factors set forth in §18:1-121 of the Code, the Board specifically finds and concludes as follows: 

1. For the reasons discussed below, the literal enforcement of this Chapter 18:1 would result in unnecessary hardship or practical difficulty as the result of the uniqueness of the Subject Property. 

2. That the practical difficulty arises from the peculiarity of the Subject Property, specifically that it is a 340-foot-long frontage on Cedar Road, creating a narrow waterfront plot with two front-yard setbacks, which shrinks the buildable space into only a 27-foot-wide building envelope. The issue is unique to this property. 

3. That the conditions are not result of any action of the Applicants, because the Subject Property was created prior to zoning regulations. (See Applicants’ Exhibit 8, showing the lot was created before 1947.) Opponents offered testimony that Applicants knew of the setbacks prior to purchasing the Subject Property; however, this is irrelevant to the requirements of a property meeting the criteria for a variance. Additionally, the lot was created prior to zoning regulations and is a legally non-conforming lot.

4. That the proposed variance will not be contrary to public interest. Applicants’ architect, Mr. Dolce, testified that, in his professional opinion, the proposed construction is a modest home design, typical of other homes in the neighborhood, and the proposed designed will have much less of an impact visually than the design Applicants would be forced to build without the granting of a variance. ie A literal enforcement of this Chapter 14:1 would result in unnecessary hardship as the result of specified conditions, which hardship is not shared by owners of other property in the same development area. The Subject Property is irregularly shaped and has several environmental features. 2.
