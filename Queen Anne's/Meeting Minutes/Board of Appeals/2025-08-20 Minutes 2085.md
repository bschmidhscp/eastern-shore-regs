### FINAL DECISION OF THE BOARD 

Introduction 

The Queen Anne’s County Board of Appeals (the “Board”) held a meeting on August 20, 2025 at 5:15 p.m. to consider Case No. BOA-25-03-0211 for a variance application filed by Mikail Kane & Adrienne Kane (f/k/a Adrienne Mancuso) (hereinafter the “Applicants”). The Board members present were Chairman, Howard Dean, and Vice-Chairman, Craig W. McGinnes. 

At the beginning of the hearing, the Board established that all requirements were met governing the filing ofthe variance application and proper notice of the August 20" public hearing. Board Chairman Dean administered the oath to all who wished to testify on the application, including the Applicants. 

### Applicants’ Request 

The Applicants are seeking a variance to the provision of § 14:1-51.A and § 14:1-52.A of the Code of Public Local Laws of Queen Anne’s County (the “Code”), to construct 310’ of fence, including a gate and double gate, within the 300 foot expanded Critical Area Buffer, at their property located at 224 Atkinson Dr., Millington, Maryland (the “Property”). 

The Zoning Inspector denied the Applicants’ zoning certificate application Z25-02-0059 to construct 310’ of fence on the Applicants’ Property. Pursuant to § 14:1-51A of the Code, new development including clearing of exiting natural vegetation, erection of structures, construction of new roads, parking areas or other impervious surfaces, and the placement of sewage disposal systems, are not permitted in the Buffer except as provided in the Buffer exemption section of the Code.

#### Applicable Provisions of the Code 

The standards the Board must apply to the Applicants’ variance request from the Chesapeake Bay Critical Area Act are set forth in § 14:1-66 of the Code. To grant the requested variance, the Board must find as follows: 

1 Literal enforcement of this Chapter 14:1 would result in unnecessary hardship as the result of specified conditions, which hardship is not shared by owners of other property in the same development area; 

2. Those conditions are peculiar to the property involved; 

3. Those conditions are not the result of any action taken by the applicant; 

4. The variance will not be contrary to the public interest or the policies, goals and objectives of this Chapter 14:1 and the Queen Anne's County Critical Area Program; 

5. The variance will not confer upon an applicant any special privilege denied to other owners of like property and/or structures within the critical area; 

6. The variance will not adversely affect water quality or adversely impact fish, wildlife, or plant habitats within the critical area; 

7. The variance is the minimum deviation from the provisions of this Chapter 14:1 that will make possible the reasonable use of land or structures; and 

8. The granting of the variance will be in harmony with the general purpose and intent of this Chapter 14:1 and the Queen Anne's County Critical Area Program and the variance shall not result in a use not permitted in the applicable development area or an increase in the applicable density limitations. 

In addition, the provisions of COMAR 27.01.12.04 require the Board to make certain written findings not addressed in § 14:1-66 of the Code. These additional written findings, set forth in COMAR 27.01.12.04, are as follows: 

ie In accordance with Natural Resources Article, §8-1808(d)(3)(ii), Annotated Code of Maryland, an applicant has overcome the presumption that the specific development activity for which the variance is required does not conform with the general intent of the local jurisdiction’s program; 

2. Due to special features of the site or special conditions or circumstances peculiar to the applicants land or structure, a literal enforcement of the local Critical Area Program would result in an unwarranted hardship to the applicant;

Property Description and Department of Planning & Zoning Recommendations 

Mary Ellen Gray, Senior Planner with the Department of Planning & Zoning, presented her staff report. Ms. Gray identified the Property and its location. The Property is located west of Millington, east of Crumpton and south of Rte. 291 and West of Rte. 301. The property is in the seventh election district of Queen Anne’s County and is located at the end of a cul-de-sac in the Chester River Heights Subdivision. The property has an address of 224 Atkinson Dr., Millington, Maryland and is designated as Parcel 109, Lot 20 on Zoning Map 01. The Property is zoned Neighborhood Conservation-1 (NC-1) and is .64 Ac. The Property is designated Limited Development Area (LDA) within the state’s Critical Area, and the entire property is within an expanded Critical Area Buffer. 

Ms. Gray testified that the Property is improved with a single-family dwelling, patio, driveway and partial privacy fence which was completed in the Spring of 2023. She stated that the entire property contains hydric soils and there are steep slopes on the eastern and western edge of the property which are sensitive areas triggering the expanded Critical Area Buffer to 300’ which encompasses the entire property. She stated that the property was granted a previous variance to construct the single-family home on the property; however the conditions for the

expanded Buffer were overlooked at that time. She indicated that the total area of disturbance is 155 sq. ft. and no trees or vegetation will be removed. Ms. Gray stated that the granting of the variance will require a Buffer management plan at a rate of 3:1 for all new disturbance and for all mitigation to be planted within the Critical Area Buffer. She indicated that the total area of mitigation is 465 ft. and the Applicants have submitted a plan that is consistent with the requirements. 

Ms. Gray discussed the applicable standards that the Board must find to grant the variance. She stated that the entire property is located within the expanded Buffer, and there is an unwarranted unnecessary hardship based upon the conditions of the land and the particular property and there is no other place outside of the Buffer to locate the fence. Given that the singlefamily dwelling is an allowed principal use on the property and a fence is a normal improvement to the property and is modest in nature, a literal interpretation of the statute will deprive the Applicants from a use or structure permitted to others; therefore the granting of the variance is appropriate. 

Ms. Gray concluded by stating that staff finds consistency with the applicable laws in this case and advises the Board grant the variance request in relation to consistency with COMAR 27.01.12.04 and § 14:1-66 of the Code based on an unwarranted hardship being found, subject to the conditions as contained in her report as part of the granting of the variance. 

Applicant’s Presentation 

Mr. & Mrs. Kane testified before the Board. Mr. Kane testified that the fence being requested is for dogs and future children and to have reasonable use of their property. In response to a question by Chairman Dean, the Applicant explained further the characteristics of their property and the proximity to Rte. 301.

### Findings and Conclusions of the Board 

The Board finds the testimony and application provided by the Applicants credible and persuasive. The Board concludes that the evidence justifies approval of the requested variance. Based on the evidence presented, and duly considering the factors set forth in § 14:1-66 of the Code and COMAR 27.01.12.04, and adopting the comments of staff as provided for in Ms. Mary Ellen Gray, Senior Planner’s staff report, the Board specifically finds and concludes as follows: 

1. In this case, the Board finds that a fence is a reasonable and significant use of a property within the community; 

2. A literal enforcement of the Code would result in unnecessary hardship as a result of specific conditions which are not shared by other owners in the neighborhood; 

3. The conditions of the property are peculiar to the property involved and not the result of any action taken by the Applicants. 4, The variance will not be contrary to the public interest or the policies, goals and objectives of the Code or Critical Area Program; eS The variance will not confer upon the Applicants any special privilege denied to other owners of like property; 

6. The variance will not adversely affect water quality or impact fish, wildlife, or plant habitat; 

7. The variance requested is the minimum deviation from the provisions of Chapter 14:1 to allow for the reasonable use of the Property; 

8. The granting of the variance will be in harmony with the general purposes and intent of Chapter 14:1 and the Queen Anne’s County Critical Area Program and the variance shall not result in a use not permitted in the applicable development area or an increase in the applicable density limitations. 

9. Special features exist on the site that are peculiar to the Applicants’ Property and a literal enforcement of the Code would result in unwarranted hardship; 

10. The Applicants have overcome the presumption that the fence in the Buffer does not conform with the general intent of the Queen Anne’s County Critical Area Program; 

11. Due to special features on the site, a literal enforcement of the local Critical Area Program would result in an unwarranted hardship to the Applicants;

##### Decision 

Based on the foregoing findings and conclusions, by a vote of two in favor and none opposed, the Board grants to the Applicants: 

A variance from § 14:1-51.A Buffer standards and § 14:1-52, expanding the Buffer to install a 310 ft. fence within the expanded Buffer, subject to the following condition: 

1. The applicant cannot receive a building permit for the proposed improvements until the minor Buffer Management Plan, in accordance with COMAR 27.01.09.01-3, has been approved by Queen Anne’s County.
