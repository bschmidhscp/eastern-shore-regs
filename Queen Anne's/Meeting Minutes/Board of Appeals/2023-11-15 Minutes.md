#### FINAL DECISION OF THE BOARD 

##### Introduction 

The Queen Anne’s County Board of Appeals (the “Board”) held a meeting on November 15, 2023 at 5:00 p.m. to consider Case No. BOA-23-09-0163 for a variance application filed by Joseph and Linda Barczak (hereinafter the “Applicants”). The Board members present were Chairman, Howard Dean, and Board Members Craig W. McGinnes and Michael Lesniowski. 

At the beginning of the hearing, the Board established that all requirements were met governing the filing of the variance application, and proper notice of the November 15" public hearing. Board Chairman, Howard Dean, administered the oath to all who wished to testify on the application, including the Applicants. 

#### Applicants’ Request 

The Applicants request a variance to the provisions of§ 18:1-19.E.(1)(c)[4][f][ii] of the Code of Public Local Laws of Queen Anne’s County (the “Code”), to reduce the required side yard setback from 15 feet to 10 feet to construct an addition that is 10 feet by 24 feet to an existing attached garage (hereinafter the “garage addition”). The Applicants’ property is located at 105 Oregon Road in the Kent Island Estates Subdivision near Stevensville in the 4" Election District (hereinafter the “Property”). The Property is located in the Neighborhood Conservation-20 (NC20) Zoning District. 

The Applicants submitted a Building Permit (BR23-08-0567) to construct the garage addition over an existing patio that is 10 feet by 24 feet and to reframe the existing garage roof to create an attic and storage space. On September 1, 2023, the Queen Anne’s County Zoning

Inspector denied the Applicants’ Building Permit after determining that the garage addition did not meet the minimum side yard setback of 15 feet as required in § 18-1-19.E. of the Code. 

##### Applicable Provisions of the Code 

The standards the Board must apply to the Applicants’ variance request are set forth in § 18:1-121.B. of the Code. To grant the requested variance, the Board must find as follows: 

1. Literal enforcement of this Chapter 18:1 would result in unnecessary hardship or practical difficulty as the result of specified conditions; 2. Those conditions are peculiar to the property involved; 3. Those conditions are not the result of any action taken by the appellant; 4. The variance will not be contrary to the public interest; and 5. Evaluation ofalternatives proves variance is required. 

In addition, pursuant to § 18:1-122.A. of the Code, the Board must find that any variance granted is no greater than an amount minimally necessary to ameliorate the conditions giving rise to any practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship. 

#### Property Description and Department of Planning & Zoning Recommendations 

Ken Southard, Zoning Inspector with the Department of Planning & Zoning, presented his staff report. Mr. Southard said that the Applicants are seeking to add an addition that is 10 feet by 24 feet to the rear of an existing attached garage that is 24 feet by 24 feet. To add the garage addition as proposed, the Applicants are seeking to reduce the required side yard setback from 15 feet to 10 feet. He said that the Property is located in the Neighborhood Conservation-20 (NC-20) district. 

The Property consists of 21,600 square feet. He said that the Property is made up of hydric soils but that there are no mapped wetlands, floodplain, Critical Area, or sensitive species located within the garage addition project site. The Property was created in 1939 and is served by public sewer and a private well. The Property is part of the Kent Island Estates Subdivision and is conforming for the NC-20 zoning district. 

Mr. Southard said that the existing house was built in 1975. The existing dwelling includes an attached garage, which was constructed between 1978 and 1989. With the proposed garage

##### Applicants’ Presentation 

The Applicants presented their application. Mr. Barczak said that there is an existing concrete patio behind the existing garage that is 10 feet by 24 feet. #### Findings and Conclusions of the Board 

The Board finds the testimony and application provided by the Applicants credible and persuasive. The Board concludes that the evidence justifies approval of the requested variance. Based on the evidence presented, and duly considering the factors set forth in §18:1-121.C. of the Code, the Board specifically finds and concludes as follows: 

1. A literal enforcement of the side yard setback would result in practical difficulty. 

2. The lot is peculiar in that the existing garage encroaches into the side yard setback and the proposed addition is not increasing the encroachment. 

3. The proposed garage addition is modest and reasonable to accommodate the Applicants’ need to store tools and provide a work space. 

4. The Property is narrow. 

5. The existing dwelling on the Property was constructed in 1975 and encroaches in the side yard setback. 

6. The lot was created in 1939, prior to adoption of the Code and establishment of setbacks. 

7. The variance is the amount minimally necessary to ameliorate conditions giving rise to any practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship. 

###### Decision 

Based on the foregoing findings and conclusions, by a vote of three in favor and none opposed, the Board grants to the Applicants: 

A variance from the provisions of §18:1-19.E.(1)(c)[4][fl[ii] to permit the Applicants to reduce the required side yard setback of 15 feet to 10 feet to permit the Applicants to construct a 10” x 24’ garage addition to the existing dwelling.

##### Introduction 

The Queen Anne’s County Board of Appeals (the “Board”) held a meeting on November 15, 2023 at 5:15 p.m. to consider Case No. BOA-23-09-0162 for a variance application filed by Martin and Debra Skinner (hereinafter the “Applicants”). Board Chairman, Howard Dean, administered the oath to all who wished to testify on the application, including the Applicants. 

#### Applicants’ Request 

The Applicants request a variance to the provisions of § 18:1-19.E.(1)(c)[4][f][i] of the Code of Public Local Laws of Queen Anne’s County (the “Code”), to reduce the required 35 foot front yard setback from Graces Lane to 29 feet to construct an additional to an existing single family dwelling. The Applicants’ property is located at 110 Gibbs Road near Grasonville in the 5" Election District (hereinafter the “Property”). The Property is located in the Neighborhood Conservation-20T (NC-20T) Zoning District. 

The Applicants submitted a Building Permit (No. BR23-08-0558) to construct a 10°4” x 18’ bathroom addition with a total floor area of 180 square feet to their existing single family dwelling. On September 1, 2023, the Queen Anne’s County Zoning Inspector denied the Applicants’ Building Permit after determining that the proposed addition did not meet the required front yard setback of 35 feet as required by § 18-1-19.E. of the Code.

#### Property Description and Department of Planning & Zoning Recommendations 

Michael Olds, Zoning Inspector with the Department of Planning & Zoning, presented his staff report. Mr. Olds said that the Applicants are seeking to construct a bathroom addition to their existing single family dwelling. The existing dwelling was constructed in 1974, He said that the Applicants need a 6-foot variance to reduce the front yard setback from 35-feet to 29-feet to construct the bathroom addition. 

Mr. Olds identified the Property and its location. He identified the Property in the Fifth Election District of Queen Anne’s County located at 110 Gibbs Road, Grasonville, Maryland. The Property is 21,126 sq ft. and is zoned Neighborhood Conservation-20T. He said that there are hydric soils on the Property but no mapped wetlands, floodplain, Critical Area, or sensitive species. Mr. Olds said that the Property is a corner lot, fronting Gibbs Road and Graces Lane. Pursuant to § 18App-1, on corner lots, the front setback shall apply to each property line abutting a road. Therefore, the Applicants are required to meet the 35-foot front yard setback off of Gibbs Road and Graces Lane. The existing dwelling fronts on Gibbs Road. 

Mr. Olds said that the Property is a conforming lot for the NC-20T zoning district. He said that the existing dwelling on the Property is setback 30-feet from Graces Lane and does not meet
