Introduction 

The Queen Anne's County Board of Appeals (the "Board") held a meeting on March 18, 2026 at 5:45 p.m. to consider Case No. BOA-25-06-0219 for a floodplain variance application filed by Harris Family Partnership, LLC (hereinafter the "Applicant”). The Board members present were Chairman Craig W. McGinnes, Vice-Chairman, Howard A. Dean, and Board Member, Michael A. Lesniowski. 

At the beginning of the hearing, the Board established that all requirements were met governing the filing of the variance application and proper notice of the March 18, 2026 public hearing. Chairman McGinnes administered the oath to all who wished to testify on the application, including the Applicant. 

# Applicants’ Request 

The Applicant is the owner of a 3,815 Sq. Ft. parcel of land located at 433 Kent Narrows Way, Grasonville, MD (hereinafter the Property"). The Applicant operates a commercial restaurant in said location known as Harris Crab House. In March 1995, the Applicant was issued a variance in Queen Anne’s County Case No. V- 316 to permit the construction of an accessory structure to the Restaurant within the 100-year floodplain to serve as a “waiting area and bar.” (hereinafter interchangeably referred to as “Pavilion,” “Structure” and/or “Dessert First”). The 1995 variance requires the structure to be open on three sides and to be constructed in exact conformance with the plans and specifications submitted with the variance application.

In May 2025, the Applicant filed for a building permit (BC25-05-0057) to renovate an existing 875 sq. ft Commercial Building and raise the structure 5’6 *%” to meet FEMA BFE of 5S’ and enclose, and construct additions of new landings, ramps and stairs. 

On May 15, 2025, John Kling, Certified Floodplain Manager (CFM), with the Queen Anne’s County Department of Public Works, denied the building permit finding that the application did not comply with QAC Floodplain Management Code §§ 14:3-41.C(1) and (2) requirements regarding “enclosures below the lowest floor,” being used solely for parking of vehicles, building access, crawl/under floor spaces, or limited storage; and not exceeding 600 square feet in area (exterior measurement). 

The Applicant is now seeking a variance to the provision of §14:3-41C(1) & (2) of the Floodplain Management Ordinance (the Ordinance"), to allow for the elevation of the lowest floor within the enclosed structure to be elevated to a reduced flood protection elevation of five feet, six and three-quarter inches (5’ 6 34”) for continued use as a coffee shop, and to allow for the floor area to remain larger than 60 square feet as allowed by the 1995 variance. 

#### Applicable Provisions of the Code 

Pursuant to §14:3-63 of the Code, the Board of Appeals has the power to consider and authorize or deny variances from the strict application of the requirements of the Flood Plain regulations. A variance shall be approved only if it is determined to not be contrary to the public interest and where, owing to special conditions of the lot or parcel, a literal enforcement of the provisions of these regulations would result in an unnecessary hardship. 

The standards the Board must apply to the Applicant’s variance request are set forth in §§ 14:3-65 and 14:3-66 of the Code, and are as follows:

   2. In considering variance applications, the Board ofAppeals shall consider and make findings of fact on all evaluations, all relevant factors, requirements specified in other sections of these regulations, and the following factors:

#### Testimony and Presentation of the Department of Planning & Zoning 

John Kling, Certified Floodplain Manager, (CFM) Queen Anne’s County Department of Public Works, presented the staff report. Mr. Kling identified the Property as Map 57, Parcel 0314, and located in the Waterfront Village Center (WVC) Zoning District and has a Critical Area designation of Intensely Developed Area (IDA) and is in flood zone AE. 

Mr. Kling referenced his report submitted as Dept. of Public Works Exhibit #1, wherein he covered, in depth, the factors that the Board must consider in deciding whether to grant the requested variance. 

Mr. Kling testified to the history of the property, noting that a variance was approved in March 1995, to allow the construction of a non-elevated accessory structure in the 100-year floodplain, with said approval being based on the structure having only one wall and three sides being open. He also discussed a prior variance request by the Applicant in April 2025 (BOA-2403-0174) wherein the Board of Appeals denied the Applicant’s request for an additional 275 sq. ft. of permissible enclosed space below the Flood Protection Elevation.

He indicated that the Applicant’s permit submitted in May 2025 was denied as the accessory structure lowest floor elevation proposed was below the flood protection elevation and was being used other than for parking of vehicles, building access or limited storage, and was an area larger than 600 sq. ft. 

Mr. Kling presented an aerial of the proposed project for the Board and referenced his staff report wherein he identified the twelve areas that the Board must consider in granting a variance to the floodplain requirements. Ms. Macfarlane, pointed out that one of the requirements that the Applicant is required to show for the Board to grant the variance, is that there would be an exceptional hardship to the

##### Findings and Conclusions of the Board 

Based on the testimony and evidence presented, and duly considering the factors set forth in the Queen Anne’s County Code, including, but not limited to, §§ 14:3-65 and 14:3-66, and considering the comments as provided by the MDE, the Board specifically finds and concludes as follows with respect to the requested variance: 

1. There would be very little danger, if any, that materials will be swept onto other lands to the injury of others, or to life and property due to flooding or erosion damage if the variance was granted. 

2. The services to the community provided by the property are important. 

3. There are no alternatives than what the Applicant has requested. 4. The property is a functionally dependent use and is closely tied to the waterfront. 5. The proposed use of the property is compatible with the existing and anticipated development of the area. 

6. There will be no effect to the safety of access to the property as a result of the granting of the variance. 

7. The Board adopted the comments as contained in John Kling’s staff report submitted as Dept. of Public Works Exhibit #1, specifically as provided for on the last two pages ofsame, and such is incorporated into their findings as if fully set forth. 

8. There is a showing of good and sufficient cause to grant the variance. 

9. The failure to grant thevariance will result in anexceptional hardship due to the physical characteristics of the property. 

10. Granting the variance for development will not result in increased flood heights beyond that which is allowed. 

11. The granting of a variance will not result in additional threats to public safety; extraordinary public expense, nuisances, fraud or victimization of the public, or conflict with existing local laws.

##### DECISION 

Based on the foregoing findings and conclusions, by a vote of three in favor and none opposed, the Board grants the variance from § 14:3-42A, §14:3-42B, and §14:3-41C of the Floodplain Management Ordinance to permit an existing structure to be raised 5.56 ft which is 1.44 ft below the flood protection elevation of 7 ft. Structure will remain above the base flood elevation of 5 ft and only the enclosures below the 5.56 ft would be compliant with § 14:3-41C.
