## FINAL DECISION OF THE BOARD 

I Introduction 

Pursuant to due notice, the Queen Anne’s County Board of Appeals (the “Board”) held a public meeting on May 21, 2025 at 5:00 p.m. to consider Case No. BOA-24-09-0195 for a variance application filed by Tony Musco and Eileen Mariotti (collectively, the “Applicants”). 

The Board members present were Howard Dean, Chairman; Craig W. McGinnes, ViceChairman; and Michael A. Lesniowski. Also present were William C. Chapman, Esq., Substitute Attorney for the Board; Cathy Maxwell, Clerk to the Board; and Stephanie Jones, Long Range Principal Planner for Queen Anne’s County Planning & Zoning. Mr. Musco and Ms. Mariotti testified in support of their application. 

At the beginning of the hearing, the Board established that all requirements were met governing the filing of the variance application and proper notice of the public hearing. Chairman Dean administered the oath to all who wished to testify. 

IL. Applicants’ Request 

Applicants are owners of improved real property located at 632 Bayside Drive, Stevensville, MD and more specifically identified as Parcel 405, Lot 35 on Map 56 of the Tax Maps for Queen Anne’s County (the “Subject Property”). Applicants request a variance from the provisions of § 14:1-53 C.(2) of the Code of Public Local Laws of Queen Anne’s County (the “Code”), to construct an 8’x 23’ and 7’x 25’ gapped composite deck on either side of a pool and an additional 40 square feet of stairs to widen existing steps for a total of 399 square feet of disturbance to Critical Area buffer.

### V. Applicant’s Presentation 

Mr. Musco said Applicants’ request was created by their discovery that the pool company with whom they contracted mislocated the pool by over two feet. Applicants intended for the pool to be centered with the existing deck attached to their home, he said,. Applicants presented a series of photos, collectively admitted as Applicants’ Exhibit 9, illustrating the mislocation of the pool from its intended position. Mr. Musco said the deck would now be off-center if constructed. “We would have a little bit of pool sticking out on one side, and none sticking out on the other side, so it would look like kind of a ‘patch job’,” he said. Applicants invested significant sums on the pool and permitting, he said, and wanted their pool and deck to be centered. Applicants’ proposal of additional decking around the north and south sides of the pool is a mitigation plan, he said, to try to hide the pool’s mislocation and create symmetry. 

Ms. Mariotti said she was the one who first noticed the pool’s mislocation, but not until the pool shell was already in-ground and she could see from the coping pieces that the pool was off-center. “At that point, we had to make a decision,” Mr. Musco said. “Do we completely

## VII. Findings and Conclusions of the Board 

The Board finds the testimony, application and exhibits provided by Applicants, as well as the written Staff Report and testimony provided by Ms. Jones, credible and compelling. The Board concludes that the evidence justifies approval of the variance request with conditions identified in the Staff Report. Based on the evidence presented, and duly considering the applicable factors from the Code, the Board specifically finds and concludes as follows: 

1 A literal enforcement of this Chapter 14:1 would result in unnecessary hardship as the result of specified conditions, which hardship is not shared by owners of other property in the same development area. In Case No. BOA-23-03-0147 the Board granted Applicants approval to construct a pool and associated deck, finding that literal enforcement of the Code would result in an unnecessary hardship asa result of the earlier specific conditions, specifically due to the expansion of the buffer. As Applicants testified, the pool was not constructed as planned and they cannot obtain the benefit of the earlier approval under the modified conditions. 

2. Those conditions are peculiar to the property involved. The pool, as constructed, is fixture of the Subject Property and has created slightly modified conditions from the previous approval. The expansion of the buffer is specific to the Subject Property and the parcels to the north and south, Of the three parcels in this vicinity that are mapped hydric, the parcel in question is the only one that is entirely mapped hydric soils.

Carol Coates, who lives on Slippery Hill Lane in Queenstown, testified about traffic impacts.
