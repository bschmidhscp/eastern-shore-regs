## SUMMARY OF ARGUMENT 

At the time of the hearing, Oral Argument was heard. Jospeh A. Stevens. Esq., counsel for CBP, argued that the BOA lacks authority to hear an appeal from the Queen Anne’s County Commissioners’ (“Commissioners”) adoption of a Comprehensive Plan (or a component thereof) as such is a legislative action. CBP contends that the Commissioners’ legislative decision to modify the Comprehensive Plan to include the CBP property in the Plan’s designated growth area was made pursuant to Maryland Code, Land Use Article, §3-204(c), which reserves planning power to the “legislative body,” to adopt. modify, remand, or disapprove (i) the whole plan or part of the plan; (ii) a plan for one or more geographic sections or divisions of the local jurisdiction; or (iii) an amendment to the plan. CBP argues that this legislative action is not covered by the Board’s authority to hear matters statutorily granted to it by Maryland Code, Land Use Article, §4-305, and without such statutory power, the Board may not hear the appeal. 

Patrick E. Thompson, Esq. argued on behalf of the Commissioners and adopted CBP’s 

argument. 

2

<!-- page 3 -->
Jesse B. Hammock, Esq., counsel for QACA, argued that the decision by the Commissioners was not purely legislative in nature, but rather it was “quasi 

Judicial/administrative” and as such, an appeal to the BOA is proper. QACA, contends that the request made by CBP was a “rezoning” request, and the Commissioners were acting as an “administrative official” in granting same, thus permitting an appeal to this Board pursuant to the jurisdiction granted by Md. Code, Land Use Article §4-305 and Queen Anne’s County Code §18:1-115. To support this argument, QACA cited to several court cases where itwas determined that certain actions taken by a legislative body can be found to be quasi-judicial or administrative in nature and that “ the ‘County Commissioners’ particular exercise of its distinct roles in a given situation determines the appeal rights of those affected.”” Queen Annes Conservation, Inc. v. County Comm'rs, 382 Md. 306, 323 (2004). 

### FINDINGS AND CONCLUSIONS 

In determining whether the Commissioners’ actions were legislative or adjudicatory in nature, the Board will not consider the matter in a vacuum as the Petitioners would like. Rather, the Board must consider the entirety of the decision-making process involved relative to the adoption of the 2022 Comprehensive Plan. As CBP correctly points out, this is not a “re-zoning” request. CBP’s request was to “re-consider” the inclusion of CBP within the growth area. The decision of which properties to include within the Chester Growth Area was not isolated to the CBP property. 

The Board finds that the actions taken by the County Commissioners to include the CBP property within the Chester Growth Area as part of the adoption of the 2022 Comprehensive Plan, was a comprehensive planning matter and was primarily legislative in nature pursuant to the authority granted it under Md. Code, LandUse Article §3-204.

#### IV. Property Description and Department of Planning & Zoning Recommendations 

Rob Gunter, Development Review Principal Planner with the Department of Planning & Zoning, presented his staff report. Mr. Gunter stated the Applicant is proposing to utilize an existing 3,470 sq. ft. building to operate a Cannabis Licensed Dispensary (the Project"). 

Mr. Gunter testified the project is located east of Kent Narrows in Grasonville, at 202 Pullman Crossing Rd. He stated that the lot is part of the Grasonville Station Subdivision, which originally created nine (9) commercial lots and was approved on October 18, 2000. He further stated that the lot is located in the Urban Commercial (UC) Zone, and a Cannabis Licensed Dispensary is a conditional use in said zone. 

Mr. Gunter indicated that the lot is within the Grasonville growth area. He testified that there are non-tidal wetlands on site but there is no new disturbance proposed, and there are no other natural resources on site. He also stated that a forest conservation plan and storm water management plan was approved with the original subdivision in 2000. He indicated that a small portion of the site (apx. 500 sq. feet) is within the Chesapeake Bay Critical Area (IDA); however, no new disturbance is proposed with the project. He stated that the original site plan approved in 2000 had landscaping requirements and the site is presently not in compliance. He indicated that the Applicant is proposing additional plantings to bring the property into compliance. He testified
