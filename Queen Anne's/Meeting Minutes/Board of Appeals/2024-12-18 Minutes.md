### FINAL DECISION OF THE BOARD 

Introduction 

The Queen Anne’s County Board of Appeals (the “Board”) held a meeting on December 18, 2024 at 5:00 p.m. to consider Case No. BOA-24-09-0196 for a variance application filed by Kevin N. Rhodes and Karen A. Utkewicz (hereinafter collectively the “Applicant”). The Board members present were Chairman, Howard Dean, Vice-Chairman, Craig McGinnes, and Board Member Michael A. Lesniowski. 

At the beginning of the hearing, the Board established that all requirements were met governing the filing of<sup>the varianceapplication,and proper noticeofthe December18, 2024public</sup> hearing. Chairman Dean administered the oath to all who wished to testify on the application, including the Applicant. 

### Applicant’s Request 

The Applicant requests a variance to the provisions of § 18:1-19(E)(1)(c)[4][e][i] of the Code of Public Local Laws of Queen Anne’s County (the “Code”), to reduce the required 35 foot front yard setback to 30 feet to construct a single family dwelling, including covered front porch and rear deck, to replace the previous dwelling demolished in March, 2024. The Applicant’s property is located at 124 Jackson Creek Lane (hereinafter the “Property”). The Property is located in the Neighborhood Conservation-15 (NC-15) Zoning District. 

The Applicant submitted a Building Permit (No. #BR24-05-0360) to construct a one story single family dwelling 48’x44’ to include a 6’x24’ covered front porch and 10°x25’ rear deck with steps to grade. On September 9, 2024, Joshua McCauley, Queen Anne’s County Zoning Inspector, 

1

<!-- page 2 -->
denied the Applicant’s Building Permit after determining that the proposed dwelling did not meet the minimum setbacks required by § 18:1-19(E)(1)(c)[e][i] of the Code. 

### Applicable Provisions of the Code 

The standards the Board must apply to the Applicant’s variance request are set forth in § 18:1-121.B. of the Code. To grant the requested variance, the Board must find as follows: 

1. Literal enforcement of this Chapter 18:1 would result in unnecessary hardship or practical difficulty as the result of specified conditions; 

2. Those conditions are peculiar to the property involved; 3. Those conditions are not the result of any action taken by the appellant; 4. The variance wil! not be contrary to the public interest; and 5. Evaluation of alternatives proves variance is required. 

In addition, pursuant to § 18:1-122.A. of the Code, the Board must find that any variance granted is no greater than an amount minimally necessary to ameliorate the conditions giving rise to any practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship. 

# Property Description and Department of Planning & Zoning Recommendations 

Joshua McCauley, Zoning Inspector with the Department of Planning & Zoning, presented his staff report. He testified that the Applicant proposes to build a one story single family dwelling to include a covered front porch and rear deck to replace the dwelling demolished in March 2024. He said that the Applicant has requested a variance to reduce the required front yard setback from 35 feet to 30 feet. 

Mr. McCauley identified the Property as being in the 5" Election District of Queen Anne’s County and located in the western part of the County, on the northern side of Grasonville and abuts Long-Point Park. The lot is identified as Tax Map 58D, Parcel 512 and is 14,470 sq. ft. and is within the NC-15 zoning district and Critical Area Limited Development Area (LDA). He indicated that there are hydric soils located on the property. He stated there are no mapped wetlands, floodplains, critical area, or sensitive species located within the project site. He described the property as being a “corner lot” which is defined by County Code as abutting two streets or roads at their intersection, He stated that due to being a comer lot, each property line abutting a road must comply with the front yard setback requirements. He stated the former

dwelling was constructed in 1968 and was 28 ft from the property line. He indicated that there is an accessory structure located on the southside of the lot. The dwelling as proposed will protrude into the side street setback by five feet. The proposed dwelling is 8 ft. from the proposed accessory structure, which is compliant with the County Code which requires a minimum of6 ft. 

Mr. McCauley concluded by stating that Zoning staff has not received any evidence that outlines the practical difficulty regarding this property. 

#### Applicant’s Presentation 

Mr. Kevin Rhodes, Applicant, testified that the proposed residence was intended to be for their daughter. He stated that one road abutting the property is more of a “right-of-way” and not a traveled road. Moving the house an additional 5 ft. would cause issues with the location of the accessory structure and create setback issues on the other side of the property. 

Mrs. Karen Utkewicz testified the right of way is not maintained in any way by the County and the neighbor who uses the road is fine with the proposal. 

In response to a question by Chairman Dean, Mr. Rhodes testified that right of way used to function differently until the Royal Farms was built and now it has little function. 

In response to a question by Vice-Chairman McGinnis, Mr. Rhodes stated the concrete garage structure causes the difficulty creating the need for a variance. 

Ms. Vivian Swinson, Zoning Administrator, testified in response to a question by Member Lesniowski, stating that the property did not qualify for “grandfathering” because the Applicant had removed the prior structure. She stated that if it was a renovation, then the setback could remain non-conforming. 

Mr. Rhodes testified that the home is modest in size at 28’ x 48” and it could not be trimmed down and still meet their needs. He indicated the property is on County sewer and has a private well, that is located in the back of the garage area limiting the ability to shift the house a different direction. He stated that any different attempt at placement would also violate a setback requirement. 

Anastasia Rhodes, daughter of Applicant, testified that the house footprint could not be turned by 90 degrees based on placement of the garage and the size of the lot.

In response to a question by Ms. Richard, Mrs. Rhodes testified that the house is proposed as 8 ft. from the garage instead of the 6 ft. minimum required due to a doorway and walkway located between the house and the garage. 

### Testimony from the Public 

Edmond Utkewicz, 32072 River Rd., Millington, MD testified in favor of the project. He stated that the new residence will improve the look of the neighborhood and is a lot better than what existed prior. 

### Findings and Conclusions of the Board 

The Board finds the testimony and evidence presented by the Applicant credible and persuasive. The Board concludes that the evidence justifies approval of the requested variance. Based on the evidence presented, and duly considering the factors set forth in §18:1-121 of the Code, the Board specifically finds and concludes as follows: 

- 1, There are no other alternatives that are viable such that the variance is required. 

2. The variance will not affect neighboring properties and will have no impact on resource protection provisions. 

3. The variance request is the amount minimally necessary to ameliorate conditions giving rise to any practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship. 

4. A literal enforcement of the setback would result in unnecessary hardship and practical difficulty and those conditions are peculiar to the property involved and are not from an action taken by the Applicant. 

##### Decision 

Based on the foregoing findings and conclusions, by a vote of three in favor and none opposed, the Board grants to the Applicant: 

A variance from the provisions of §18:1-19(E)(1)(c)[4][e][i] to permit the Applicant to reduce the required 35 foot front yard setback to 30 feet.

### FINAL DECISION OF THE BOARD 

#### Introduction 

The Queen Anne’s County Board of Appeals (the “Board”) held a meeting on December 18, 2024 at 5:15 p.m. to consider Case No. BOA-24-10-0199 for conditional use approval to permit a selective cut commercial forestry operation within deed-restricted open space. The project being located at 501 Red Lion Branch Rd., in the 7th Election District (the “Property”) filed by Jay and Linda Davidson (hereinafter the “Applicant”). The Board members present were Chairman, Howard Dean, Vice-Chairman, Craig McGinnes, and Board Member, Michael A. Lesniowski. 

At the beginning of the hearing, the Board established that all requirements were met governing the filing of the conditional use application, and proper notice of the December 18, 2024 public hearing. Board Chairman, Howard Dean, administered the oath to all who wished to testify on the application, including the Applicant. 

# Applicant’s Request 

The Applicant is seeking conditional-use approval under the provisions of § 18:1-12 of the Code of Public Local Laws of Queen Anne’s County (the “Code”), to conduct a select cut commercial forestry operation in deed-restricted open space on approximately 16.7 + acres of land. Commercial forestry is permitted as a conditional use in the AG zoning district within deedrestricted open space that resulted from single-family cluster development pursuant to § 18:1-12 of the Code.

### Applicable Provisions of the Code 

The standards the Board must apply to the Applicant’s request for conditional use are set forth in § 18:1-94 of the Code. To approve the conditional use, the Board must find as follows: 

1. The proposed use at the proposed location shall be consistent with the general purpose, goals, objectives, and standards of the Comprehensive Plan, this Chapter 18:1, or any other plan, program, map, or ordinance adopted, or under consideration pursuant to official notice, by the County. 

2. The proposed use at the proposed location will not result in a substantial or undue adverse impacts on adjacent property, the character of the neighborhood, traffic conditions, parking, public improvements, public sites or rights-of-way, or other matters affecting the public health, safety, and general welfare. 

3. The proposed use at the proposed location will be adequately served by, and will not impose an undue burden on, any of the required improvements referred to in this Chapter 18:1, Part 7. Where any such improvements, facilities, utilities, or services are not available or adequate to service the proposed use at the proposed location, the applicant shall, as part of the application and as a condition of approval of the conditional use, be responsible for establishing ability, willingness, and binding commitment to provide such improvements, facilities, utilities, and services in sufficient time and in a manner consistent with the Comprehensive Plan, this Chapter 18:1, and other plans, programs, maps, and ordinances adopted by the County. 

In addition, § 18:1-123. B. requires the Board to make the following findings to approve a conditional use: 

(5 The conditions concerning that conditional use as detailed in this Chapter 18:1 exist; 2. The conditional use conforms to the Comprehensive Plan; and 3. The conditional use is compatible with the existing neighborhood. 

Last, pursuant to Maryland Annotated Code, Land Use Article §1-303, the Board must include in its evaluation of the Comprehensive Plan, pursuant to the above-cited section, certain consistency findings. The Board’s approval of a conditional use must further, and not be contrary to, the following items in the Queen Anne’s County Comprehensive Plan: 

1. Policies;

### I. Property Description and Department of Planning & Zoning Recommendations 

Doug Summers, Associate Planner with the Department of Planning & Zoning, presented his staff report. He identified the Property as Queen Anne’s County Tax Map6 Parcel 43 Lot 9, in the northern part of the County, approximately 2 way between Millington and Crumpton, on the northside of Red Lion Branch Rd., approximately one mile east of its intersection with Dudley’s Comer Rd. He stated that the property is within the Agricultural (AG) zoning district. He noted that there are non-tidal wetlands on site, and a Non-Tidal BMP agreement has been submitted. He also indicated that there is a USGS identified intermittent stream that runs along the western edge of the property and a streamside management plan has been approved and establishes an 85” buffer off of the stream center line. He also indicated that Applicant proposes to mark the timber to be harvested in the buffer area. Mr. Summers testified that the MD Department of Natural Resources (DNR) has confirmed the site lies within a drainage area of Red Lion Branch which supports freshwater mussel communities, including a state endangered species. He indicated that the site also has habitat for forest interior-dwelling birds (FID’s), and DNR has issued a letter with twelve (12) recommendations with respect to same. 

Mr. Summers stated that the Applicant has submitted a timber harvest plan which consists of a 16.7 acre select-cut operation. The current basal area is 110 sq. ft./ac. and the post-harvest basal area has been listed at 70 sq. ft./ac. He indicated that natural regeneration is expected within two years following harvest. He also testified that site access spans two properties; however, the Applicant has a legal interest in both properties. 

He concluded by stating that no agencies who have reviewed this conditional use request have offered any objection to the project as proposed, and the project is consistent with the 2022

Mr. Summers indicated that staff supports the Board of Appeals in granting conditional use to the Applicant to carry out a selective cut commercial forestry operation on their AG zoned Property within deed restricted open space, with the 6 conditions as noted in his staff report. 

In response to a question by Chairman Dean, Mr. Summers stated that they are able to cut to the property line but as this is a select-cut operation trees will remain along the property line.
