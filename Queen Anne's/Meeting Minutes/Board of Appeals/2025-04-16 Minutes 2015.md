L Introduction 

Pursuant to due notice, the Queen Anne’s County Board of Appeals (the “Board”) held a public meeting on April 16, 2025 at 5:00 p.m. to consider Case No. BOA-25-02-0209 for a variance application filed by John J. Lonergan, Jr. (hereinafter the “Applicant”). 

The Board members present were Chairman, Howard Dean; Vice-Chairman, Craig W. McGinnes; and member Michael A. Lesniowski. Also present were William C. Chapman, Esq., Substitute Attorney for the Board, Cathy Maxwell, Clerk to the Board; and Mike Olds, Zoning Inspector for Queen Anne’s County Planning & Zoning. Genevieve H.R. Macfarlane, Esq. appeared on behalf of the Applicant. No members of the public testified during Public Comment. 

At the beginning of the hearing, the Board established that all requirements were met governing the filing of the variance application and proper notice of the public hearing. Chairman Dean administered the oath to all who wished to testify on the application, including the Applicant. 

Il. Applicant’s Request 

The Applicant, John J. Lonergan, Jr., is Trustee of John J. Lonergan, Jr. Living Trust dated December 19, 2018, (“Applicant”), which is the owner of improved real property located at 401 Sullivan Farm Lane, Stevensville, MD (the “Subject Property”). Applicant requests a variance from the provisions of§ 18:1-41 of the Code of Public Local Laws of Queen Anne’s County (the “Code”), to construct a replacement 188-ft x 6-ft. pier with a 35-ft. x 6-ft. walkway, 23-ft. x 17-ft. platform, one (1) 29-ft. x 3-ft. finger pier, one (1) 7-ft. x 3-ft. finger pier, two (2) boat lifts with associated piles, two (2) personal watercraft lifts with associated piles, one (1) Davit hoist and six (6) mooring piles (collectively, the “Replacement Pier”) with a total pier length of approximately 205 feet from the mean high water line (‘“MHW”), which exceeds the required 150-foot limit.

1. § 18:1-41 Piers states: 

A pier may not extend into any body of water a distance greater than 150 feet, as measured from the mean high-water line, unless the Board of Appeals allows a greater length as a variance. In no case may the length ofa pier exceed % of the distance from the mean high-water line to the center line of the body of water. The distance is hereby established as the tributary harbor line. All proposed piers must obtain approval of the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers and the State of Maryland as necessary. 

2. § 18:1-42 provides, in relevant part, that one pier is permitted as an accessory use or structure on a residential lot that has a minimum of 150 feet of water frontage. ... or existed as a legal lot of record prior to April 9, 1987.

- (1) Literal enforcement of this Chapter 18:1 would result in unnecessary hardship or practical difficulty as the result of specified conditions; 

- (2) Those conditions are peculiar to the property involved; 

- (3) Those conditions are not the result of any action taken by the appellant; (4) The variance will not be contrary to the public interest; and (5) Evaluation of alternatives proves variance is required. 

4. In addition, pursuant to § 18:1-122.A. of the Code, the Board must find that any variance granted is no greater than an amount minimally necessary to ameliorate the conditions giving rise to any practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship.

##### IV. Property Description and Department of Planning & Zoning Recommendations 

Mr. Olds presented his Staff Report (Planning & Zoning Exhibit 1). Mr. Olds testified that the Subject Property is in the Fourth Election District and located in the western part of Queen Anne’s County. The Subject Property has been under its current ownership since 2020. It is identified as Parcel 185 on Map 63 of the Tax Maps for Queen Anne’s County. The Subject Property is approximately 15.87 acres, located in the Countryside (“CS”) zoning district and Resource Conservation Area (“RCA”). Sullivan Farm Lane is accessible via Eareckson Lane. The Subject Property fronts Cox Creek. Mr. Olds said Applicant is seeking to remove the existing pier and construct the Replacement Pier, described in Section II hereinabove. The Replacement Pier, he said, would not extend % of the distance of the waterway in front of the Subject Property, which is 2,425 feet across, making the % distance limit 606.25 feet. He said he was unable to locate a permit for the existing 205-foot pier located on the Subject Property, as shown in aerial photographs in Applicant’s Exhibit 3. 

Mr. Olds said that in July 2024, the Board approved a 187.5-foot pier for 205 Fantasy Lane, two parcels to the south of the Subject Property, in Case No. BOA-24-04-0177. ##### VI. Testimony from the Public

###### VII. Findings and Conclusions of the Board 

The Board finds the testimony and evidence presented by the Applicant credible and persuasive. The Board concludes that the evidence justifies approval of the requested variance. Based on the evidence presented, and duly considering the factors set forth in §18:1-121 of the Code, the Board specifically finds and concludes as follows: 

1. Literal enforcement of this Chapter 18:1 would result in unnecessary hardship or practical difficulty as the result of specified conditions. Applicant’s testimony established that, at 150 feet from MHW, he frequently cannot get his boats on and off lifts or access deep water, and that several neighboring properties under similar conditions have piers greater than 150 feet. 

2. Those conditions are peculiar to the property involved. Applicant’s testimony established that these conditions exist throughout all of the water frontage of the Subject Property. 

3. Those conditions are not the result of any action taken by the Applicant. The conditions are water depth and the inability to access deep water. 

4. The variance will not be contrary to the public interest. This is a replacement of a structure in the same dimensions as the current pier has existed for 22 years, and no neighbors testified in opposition. Replacing a dangerous, failing pier in kind is in the public interest. 

5. Evaluation of alternatives proves variance is required. Applicant has credibly testified that he cannot exercise his riparian rights with a pier less than the requested length, and that no alternative location on the Subject Property would allow for a different result.

For the second factor of §18:1-94, Mr. Cotter said the two most relevant categories of substantial or undue adverse impacts are on adjacent property and the character of the neighborhood. He said Ms. Combs’ property is demonstrably close to the planned Recreational Facility, and that he did not believe there is another area within the AG zone that would have this kind of impact on a neighboring property. The parcels were once contiguous, he said, and whether or not it was ever intended that the 2010 subdivision of the Subject Property be for commercial or institutional use, Ms. Combs’ home is unique in its proximity to the proposed use. Mr. Cotter said there is no evidence to suggest the Applicant looked at any other properties in the county to determine limits on impact. 

Regarding the character of the neighborhood, Mr. Cotter said multiple people testified that the area of the Subject Property was a very agricultural community. The proposed use is inconsistent with that character, she said, especially when combined with the Subject Property’s location in a Priority Preservation Area. 

Mr. Cotter said there are inconsistences in the application, that it states twice that there is only one affected property, and its owner does not oppose the application, but Ms. Combs testified that she never spoke with anyone about it. He said whether that was negligent or intentional, such a statement is clearly inaccurate and showsa lack of due diligence. 

Mr. Cotter said Applicant in its narrative relies on Cnty. Council ofPrince George s Cnty. v. Brandywine Enterprises, Inc., 109 Md. Mr. Cotter said the key distinction in Schultz v. Pritts is whether this particular use in this particular location has adverse effects beyond other locations within the same zoning district. He said the distinction is subtle, but important, because this particular use, at this particular time, in this particular location would have adverse effects on Ms. Combs that would not be present at other locations in the AG zone. There are miles and miles of farmland without homes right next to the proposed use, he said.

He said he disagrees with the characterization of the neighborhood. Anyone who travels this stretch of Rt. 213, he said, knows what is in the area: a lot of traffic and a lot of businesses. He said the Board’s focus should be on what makes the Subject Property unique, and that it is uniquely situated for the proposed use. Findings and Conclusions of the Board 

The Board finds the testimony, application and exhibits provided by the Applicant unpersuasive to grant the conditional use, and the testimony and evidence by those opposed to the application credible and persuasive. Based on the evidence presented, and duly considering the applicable factors from the Queen Anne's County Code, the Board specifically finds and concludes as follows: 

1. The proposed use at the proposed location is consistent with the general purpose, goals, objectives, and standards of the Comprehensive Plan. 

2. The proposed use will result in a substantial or undue adverse impact on the adjacent property, the character of the neighborhood, traffic conditions, parking, public improvements, public sites or rights-of-way, or other matters affecting the public health, safety, and general welfare. 

3. The proposed use at the proposed location will be adequately served by, and will not impose an undue burden on, any of the required improvements referred to in Chapter 18:1, Part 7. 

###### VII. Decision 

Based on the foregoing findings and conclusions, by a vote of two in favor and one opposed, the Board denies the Applicant’s request for conditional use approval to construct a forprofit institutional recreational facility containing indoor and outdoor courts for paddle sports, a fitness center, and a clubhouse.
