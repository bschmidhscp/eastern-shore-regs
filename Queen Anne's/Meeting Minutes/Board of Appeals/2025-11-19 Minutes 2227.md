### FINAL DECISION OF THE BOARD 

#### Introduction 

The Queen Anne’s County Board of Appeals (the “Board”) held a meeting on November 19, 2025 at 5:00 p.m. to consider Case No. BOA-25-09-0229 for a variance application filed by Patrick J. Baltzer and Nicole A. Egerton (hereinafter collectively the “Applicant”). The Board members present were Board Chairman, Howard Dean, Vice-Chairman, Criag McGinnes, and Board Member Michael A. Lesniowski. 

At the beginning of the hearing, the Board established that all requirements were met governing the filing ofthe variance application, and proper notice ofthe November 19, 2025 public hearing. Chairman Dean administered the oath to all who wished to testify on the application, including the Applicant. 

#### Applicant’s Request 

The Applicant requests a variance to the provisions of § 18:1-19.E.(1)(c)[4]fe][iii] of the Code of Public Local Laws of Queen Anne’s County (the “Code”), to reduce the required 50 foot rear yard setback to 35 feet to construct a 16’ x 21’ screened porch and 19” x 16’ deck. The Applicant’s property is located at 1010 Love Point Rd., Stevensville, MD, (hereinafter the “Property”). The Property is located in the Neighborhood Conservation-15 (NC-15) Zoning District.

<!-- page 2 -->
1010 Love Point Road Setback Variance p.2 

The Applicant submitted (1) a Building Permit (No. #BR-25-07-0586) to remove an existing deck and to construct a new 16’ x 21’ screened in porch and 19’ x 16’ deck with steps to grade. On September 11, 2025, the Queen Anne’s County Zoning Inspector denied the Applicant’s Building Permit after determining that the proposed deck did not meet the minimum rear yard setbacks required by § 18-1-19.E of the Code. 

### Applicable Provisions of the Code 

The standards the Board must apply to the Applicant’s variance request are set forth in § 18:1-121.B. of the Code. To grant the requested variance, the Board must find as follows: 

[6 Literal enforcement of this Chapter 18:1 would result in unnecessary hardship or practical difficulty as the result of specified conditions; 

2. Those conditions are peculiar to the property involved; 3. Those conditions are not the result of any action taken by the appellant; 4. The variance will not be contrary to the public interest; and 5. Evaluation of alternatives proves variance is required. 

Pursuant to §18:1-121.C., the Board shall consider the following in deciding whether to grant a variance: 

1. Ability to use the property for any reasonable purpose, whether or not such purpose is desired by the appellant, in the absence of the proposed variance 

2. The degree to which the proposed variance will affect adjacent property owners 

3. Impact of the proposed variance upon the resource protection provisions of Part 4, Article IX, of Chapter 18:1 

4. The degree to which the situation might be more properly addressed by amending this Chapter 18:1 

In addition, pursuant to § 18:1-122.A. of the Code, the Board must find that any variance granted is no greater than an amount minimally necessary to ameliorate the conditions giving rise to any practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship.

### Property Description and Department of Planning & Zoning Recommendations 

Mike Olds, Zoning Inspector with the Department of Planning & Zoning, presented his staff report. Mr. Olds testified that the Applicant is seeking to remove the current deck located on the rear of their home which is located 35’ from the rear lot line and presently non-conforming, and to construct an addition to the existing single-family dwelling of a 16° x 21’ screened in porch and a 19’ x 16’ deck with steps to grade. He indicated the Applicant has requested a variance to reduce the required rear yard setback from 50 feet to 35 feet. 

Mr. Olds identified the Property as being in the Fourth Election District of Queen Anne’s County and located. The lot is identified as Tax Map 48 — Block GG, Parcel 149, Lot 26 and is approximately .35 Ac. located at 1010 Love Point Rd., Stevensville, MD, in the Cloverfields subdivision which was created prior to any subdivision regulations. He stated that the lot is a conforming lot located within the NC-15 zoning district. He testified that the residential structure was constructed in 1998, and is approximately 53’ from the rear lot line. He stated that the entire parcel contains hydric soils and there are non-title wetlands on the property, however, they are not located in the project area. He indicated that the Department does not have a record of a permit being issued for the existing deck on the property, and GIS data would indicate that it was constructed between 2004-2008. 

Mr. Olds concluded by stating that Zoning staff has not received any evidence that outlines the practical difficulty regarding this property. 

At the conclusion of Mr. Olds’ testimony, the Board entered Planning and Zoning Exhibit 1, in addition to Applicant Exhibits 1-6 into evidence. 

#### Applicant’s Presentation 

Patrick Baltzer testified on behalf of the Applicant. Mr. Baltzer stated that he purchased the property in 2022 and was unaware that the deck was not permitted. He indicated that he is looking to replace the existing deck in kind with the addition of a screened in area. 

In response to a question by Vice-Chairman McGinnes, Mr. Baltzer testified that the deck will be the exact same footprint of the deck being removed. He indicated that they have concrete footers surrounded by gravel and the deck itself is in great condition. He stated that he would like

to save as much of the existing deck as possible to use for the screened in porch and deck area. He testified that due to the mosquitos in the area, the screened in porch is necessary in order to use his property and the kids can be outside during the summer and it will also be a way to ensure that the kids do not have access to the water for safety reasons. 

In response to a question by Vice-Chairman McGinnes, he testified that his back neighbor has a deck and as well as a number of other homes in the neighborhood. 

The Board clarified that should the Board grant the variance, the Applicant would not be required to remove the old deck and rebuild same. Rather, he could use as much of the current deck as necessary provided he meets the setback requirements permitted by the variance. 

### Testimony from the Public 

There was no testimony from the public. 

### Findings and Conclusions of the Board 

The Board finds the testimony and evidence presented by the Applicant credible and persuasive. The Board concludes that the evidence justifies approval of the requested variance. Based on the evidence presented, and duly considering the factors set forth in §18:1-121 of the Code, the Board specifically finds and concludes as follows: 

1. The property cannot be used for its current purpose in the absence of the variance. 

2. The variance will have no impact on adjacent properties or the resource protection provisions of Part 4, Article IX of Chapter 18:1, and will not be contrary to the public interest. 

3. It is not practical to amend Chapter 18 to address this specific variance. 

4. A literal enforcement of the setback would result in unnecessary hardship and practical difficulty and those conditions are peculiar to the property involved and are not from any action taken by the Applicant. 

5. There are no other feasible alternatives without the variance. 

6. The variance request is the amount minimally necessary to ameliorate the conditions giving rise to the practical difficulty and unnecessary hardship.

##### Decision 

Based on the foregoing findings and conclusions, by a vote of three in favor and none opposed, the Board grants to the Applicant a variance from §18:1-19.E.(1)(c)[4]e][iii] the required 50 ft. rear yard setback to 35 ft. in order to construct 16 ft. x 21 ft. screened porch and a 19 ft. x 16 ft, deck.

For the reasons set forth in the foregoing Opinion, it is this _20th___ day ofJanuary, 2026 ordered that the variance requested for Patrick J. Baltzer and Nicole A. Egerton, in Case No. BOA25-09-0229, be granted.

Introduction 

The Queen Anne’s County Board of Appeals (the “Board”) held a meeting on November 19, 2025 at 5:15 p.m. to consider Case No. BOA-25-08-0226 for a variance application filed by Timothy John Schmidt, II and Julia Elizabeth Schmidt, Trustees of the Truslow Farm Trust (hereinafter collectively the “Applicant”). The Board members present were Chairman Howard Dean, Vice-Chairman, Craig McGinnes, and Board Member Michael A. Lesniowski.

#### Applicant’s Request 

The Applicant requests a variance to the provisions of § 18:1-41 of the Code of Public Local Laws of Queen Anne’s County (the “Code”), to replace in-kind an existing pier which extends 202.2’ channelward of mean high tide. The total project includes an 8’ x 10’ walkway landward of mean high water, an 8’ x 150’ portion ofexisting pier, an 8’ x 10” portion of<sup>existing</sup> pier to be redecked, an 8’x 15’ existing “L” shaped platform to be redecked, and a replacement piling cluster for a total length of 202.2 feet in the Neighborhood Conservation -2 Zoning District. The Applicant’s property is located at 204 Truslow Farm Lane, Chestertown, MD (hereinafter the “Property”). 

The Applicant submitted a Zoning Certificate Application (No. Z25-01-0045), which was denied by the Queen Anne’s County Zoning Inspector after determining that the Planning Office

### Property Description and Department of Planning & Zoning Recommendations 

Bill Baum, Zoning Inspector with the Department of Planning & Zoning, presented his staff report. Mr. Baum testified that the Applicant is proposing to replace a pier in-kind with improvements which extends 202.2 feet channelward of mean high tide. He said that the Applicant

<!-- page 10 -->
Truslow Rd. - Schmidt Pier Variance p.3 

has requested a variance to extend the distance the pier may extend into the water from 150° to 203.’ 

Mr. Baum identified the Property as being located southeast of Chestertown at the end of Truslow Farm Lane and totaling].6 Acres. He stated the property directly fronts the Chester River, is identified as Tax Map 9, Parcel 117, and is located within the NC-2 zoning district. He indicated the lot was created by a subdivision which occurred in 1979; however, the dwelling on the property was constructed in 1917 and the pier first appears in QAC aerials between 1963-1978. He stated that the property is located in the Critical Area — Limited Development Area (LDA) and contains a wide variety of natural resources; however, none will be disturbed by the project. 

Mr. Baum testified there is floodplain and possible tidal wetlands along the shoreline, however, no hydraulic soils are present. He stated that the Planning Office has no record of approval for the original pier or for its current length and improvements. He indicated that the proposed pier is consistent with the approval the Applicant received from the Maryland Department of the Environment. 

Mr. Baum concluded by stating that zoning staff has no objection to the proposed variance provided the Applicant presents evidence to the Board that outlines the practical difficulty regarding this property. 

In response to a question from Board Member Lesniowski, Mr. Baum indicated that the pier was constructed sometime between 1963 — 1978. He testified that there would not have been a permit requirement in 1963, and although unclear when the pier was actually constructed, it does show on the 1979 subdivision plat. 

At the conclusion of Mr. Baum’s testimony, the Board entered Planning and Zoning Exhibit 1, in addition to Applicant Exhibits 1-9 into evidence, 

#### Applicant’s Presentation 

Joseph Stevens, Esq. attorney and agent on behalf of the Applicant, presented the Application. Mr. Stevens stressed that this is a complete in-kind replacement. He stated the pier is 178” long, and the 202’ is to the outer pilings which currently exist and the proposed replacement will be no wider or longer than what currently exists.

Mrs. Julia Schmidt testified as the owner of the property. She stated that she and her husband purchased the property in June 2024 and since that time they have been renovating the house and hope to be able to move in soon. She indicated the problem with their property is that due to how the shoreline “jogs in” at their property it creates a much wider shoal with much shallower water then the neighboring parcels. She stated that the unique topography of their particular property is what creates the need for the variance. Mrs. Schmidt testified that the reconstruction of the pier will not disturb anything beyond the existing footprint and will not have any additional environmental impact. She stated that the water depth at the end of the pier will be just shy of 3 feet and just enough to dock a recreational boat at a safe water depth. Mrs. Schmidt testified that the river at the location of the pier is almost 2,000’ wide and there is no reasonable alternative that would provide access to 3’ of water other than the pier being proposed. She stated that she spoke with her neighbor and the owner of the boat which docks at her neighbor’s property and neither have an objection. 

In response to a question by Vice-Chairman McGinnes, Mrs. Schmidt testified that the current pier is in severe disrepair and has been let go for years. She indicated that she is hopeful to use the existing pilings, but she is unclear if it will be possible. 

In response to a question from Board Member Lesniowski, Ms. Schmidt stated that she does not have an objection to a condition which would require safety lighting on the end of the pier. 

Mr. Stevens concluded by directing the Board to the water depth measurements admitted as Applicant Exhibit #4 and asked the Board to approve the variance being requested. 

#### Testimony from the Public 

There was no testimony from the public. 

### Findings and Conclusions of the Board 

The Board finds the testimony and evidence presented by the Applicant credible and persuasive. The Board concludes that the evidence justifies approval of the requested variance.

Based on the evidence presented, and duly considering the factors set forth in §18:1-121 of the Code, the Board specifically finds and concludes as follows: 

1. Without the variance, the Applicant would not have the ability to use the property for any reasonable purpose. 

2. Amending Chapter 18 is not a viable option for the Applicant. 

3. The variance will not affect neighboring properties and will have no impact on resource protection provisions. 

4. A literal enforcement of the pier length provisions of the Code would result in unnecessary hardship and practical difficulty and those conditions are peculiar to the property involved and are not from an action taken by the Applicant. 

5. The variance requested is not contrary to the public interest. 

6. There are no other alternatives that are viable such that the variance is required. 

7. The length requested is the minimum amount necessary to ameliorate the conditions present as that is what is needed to get to navigable water. 

##### Decision 

Based on the foregoing findings and conclusions, by a vote of three in favor and none opposed, the Board grants to the Applicant a variance to replace in kind an existing pier with improvements at a total length of approximately 203 ft. from the mean high water line which exceeds the required 150 ft. limit. This would be subject to the condition of installing and maintaining overnight safety lighting.
