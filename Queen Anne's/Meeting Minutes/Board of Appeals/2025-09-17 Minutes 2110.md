FINAL DECISION OF THE BOARD 

Introduction 

The Queen Anne’s County Board of Appeals (the “Board”) held a meeting on September 17, 2025 at 5:00 p.m. to consider Case No. BOA-25-06-0222 for a variance application filed by Timothy J. Nagy & Jacqueline Nagy (hereinafter collectively the “Applicant”). The Board members present were Chairman, Howard Dean, Vice-Chairman Craig McGinnes, and Board Member Michael A. Lesniowski. 

At the beginning of the hearing, the Board established that all requirements were met governing the filing of the variance application, and proper notice of the September 17, 2025 public hearing. Chairman Dean administered the oath to all who wished to testify on the application, including the Applicant. 

#### Applicant’s Request 

The Applicant requests a variance to the provisions of § 18:1-19.E.(1)(c)[4][e][iii] of the Code of Public Local Laws of Queen Anne’s County (the “Code”), to reduce the required 50-foot rear yard setback to 20 feet to construct a deck attached to the rear of a existing dwelling. The Applicant’s property is located at 100 Skyler Ave., Chester, MD, (hereinafter the “Property”). The Property is located in the Neighborhood Conservation (NC-15) Zoning District. 

The Applicant submitted a Building Permit (No. #BR25-05-0406) to construct a deck on the rear of their existing residence. On June 16, 2025, the Queen Anne’s County Zoning Inspector denied the Applicant’s Building Permit after determining that the proposed deck did not meet the minimum setbacks required by § 18-1-19.E of the Code.

#### Applicable Provisions of the Code 

The standards the Board must apply to the Applicant’s variance request are set forth in § 18:1-121.B. of the Code. To grant the requested variance, the Board must find as follows: 

1. Literal enforcement of this Chapter 18:1 would result in unnecessary hardship or practical difficulty as the result of specified conditions; 

2. Those conditions are peculiar to the property involved; 3. Those conditions are not the result of any action taken by the appellant; 4. The variance will not be contrary to the public interest; and Eb Evaluation of alternatives proves variance is required. 

In addition, pursuant to § 18:1-122.A. of the Code, the Board must find that any variance granted is no greater than an amount minimally necessary to ameliorate the conditions giving rise to any practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship. 

### Property Description and Department of Planning & Zoning Recommendations 

William Baum, Zoning Inspector with the Department of Planning & Zoning, presented his staff report. Mr. Baum testified that the Applicant is seeking to construct a 22” x 36’ “L” shaped deck with steps to grade. He indicated that the Applicant has requested a variance to reduce the required rear yard setback from 50 feet to 20 feet. 

Mr. Baum identified the Property as being in the Fourth Election District of Queen Anne’s County and identified as Tax Map 57, Parcel 111, Lot 3, totaling .75 Ac. and located on Skylar Ave. in Chester. He stated the Parcel is zoned NC-15, is entirely located in the Critical Area with a Limited Development Area (LDA) designation and there are non-tidal wetlands on the site. He indicated the parcel was created in 1990 and historic zoning permitted a minimum lot width of 60 ft. In 2004 the zoning for the property changed to NC-15 which requires a 70” lot width; as such, the lot is legally non-conforming. 

Mr. Baum testified that the zoning change has created a challenge to creating a rear deck on the lot and the proposal is comparable to other lots in the neighborhood with similar sized lots. He concluded by stating that Zoning staff has no objection to the approval of the variance.

#### Applicant’s Presentation 

Mr. Nagy, the Applicant, testified that there is no road or right of way behind the property, just woods. He indicated the request for the variance is being made to permit him to use and enjoy his property. Mr. Nagy stated that when he purchased the property he was unaware of the setback requirements. He testified that the only access to the back of his home is a simple flight of stairs with no landing. The secondary access from the back of the home is blocked off for insurance requirements because there presently is no deck and can’t be used. Mr. Nagy indicated that the addition of the deck would make the property safer to use especially as he and his wife get older. He stated that, if granted, they will use a local deck building company with a very good reputation to ensure it’s built pursuant to County Code. 

Mrs. Nagy testified that the current stairs are not very large to accommodate pets and everyday use and she has fallen down the stairs in the past. 

In response to a question by Vice-Chairman McGinnes, both Mr. and Mrs. Nagy indicated that the blocked doorway is an important part ofegress in the event ofan emergency as it is directly from the living area. They further stated that everyone around them has a similar sized deck, and it is a modestly sized deck with a 10’ x 12’ foot walk area and a dining area for eating outdoors. Mrs. Nagy testified that the property is unique given its pie shape anda 3’ tall railing will be installed around the entirety of the deck. 

In response to a question by Board Member Lesniowski, Mr. and Mrs. Nagy both confirmed that they are seeking a variance to reduce the setback to 20 feet from the property line. 

#### Testimony from the Public 

There was no testimony from the public. 

### Findings and Conclusions of the Board 

The Board finds the testimony and evidence presented by the Applicant credible and persuasive. The Board concludes that the evidence justifies approval of the requested variance.

Based on the evidence presented, and duly considering the factors set forth in §18:1-121 of the Code, the Board specifically finds and concludes as follows: 

1. A deck is necessary for the Applicant to be able to enjoy the outdoor area of their property. 

2. The property is not safe without the addition of the deck area given the access issues. 

3. Without the variance the Applicant would be impeded from reasonable use of their property. 

4. Granting the variance will not affect neighboring properties, will have no impact on resource protection provisions of the Code, and will not be contrary to public interest. 

5. The deck proposed is modest in size and the variance request is the amount minimally necessary to ameliorate conditions giving rise to any practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship. 

6. There are no other viable alternatives than proposed for the Applicant to be able to enjoy his property and secure proper access. 

7. A literal enforcement of the setback would result in unnecessary hardship and practical difficulty and those conditions are peculiar to the property involved and are not from an action taken by the Applicant. 

##### Decision 

Based on the foregoing findings and conclusions, by a vote of three in favor and none opposed, the Board grants to the Applicant: 

A variance from the provisions of §18:1-19.E.(1)(c)[4][e][iii] to permit the Applicant to reduce the required 50 ft. rear yard setback to 20 ft. in order to construct a 22 ft. x 36 ft. deck to the existing dwelling.

# IV. Property Description and Department of Planning & Zoning Recommendations 

Steve Johnson, Senior Planner with the Department of Planning & Zoning, presented his Staff Report on the application. Mr. Johnson stated the Applicant is proposing a bird banding station compromised of a 2,082 sq. ft. main building, a 432 sq. ft. trellis, a 576 sq. ft. pavilion, and a 80 sq. ft. shed in the CS zoning district (the "Project”) which is a conditional use under §18:1-15.C.(13). The Applicant is seeking approval for a duration of 18 months. 

Mr. Johnson provided the Board with an overview of the history of the project, pointing out that the Applicant previously received conditional use approval on March 5, 2024; however, the approval expired on March 5, 2025, thus requiring the Applicant to return to the Board as the Code does not have a provision for extensions. He indicated that the current request is substantially the same as previously approved with the exception of an added shed. 

Mr. Johnson stated the Property is identified as Tax Map 4 Parcel 109 and is located northeast of Kingstown and southwest of Crumpton and North of McGinnes Rd. He indicated the site totaling 415.321 +/- Acres is located off Round Top Road in Chestertown. He testified that the property is currently being utilized for agricultural purposes and a moveable tunnel station for avian research exists on the site. He stated there have been no changes to the area or parcel since the original approval. 

Mr. Johnson indicated that the property is zoned AG and CS and the proposed project is located entirely within the CS zoning district. He stated that the property is completely encompassed by a Rural Legacy easement and DNR and the Developer are currently working on an amended easement for the siting of the project, hence the need for the increase in duration of approval. 

Mr. Johnson testified the site is partially located within the sensitive species project review area and DNR has indicated that the Delmarva Fox Squirrel and FIDS habitat exist in the forested area of the property but there will be no disturbance within the sensitive species project review area or the forested areas and DNR has offered no objection to the project. He indicated that 73.794 acres of the property are located within the Chesapeake Bay Critical Area Resource Conservation Area (RCA) designation; however, no disturbance within the Critical Area is proposed. The property consists of several natural resources; however, there is no disturbance proposed to same. He stated that to address Forest Conservation requirements, the Applicant has placed .73 acres of existing woodlands within the lease area into forest conservation long term preservation. 

Mr. Johnson stated that the bird banding station is proposed to be located on a 300 ft x 500 ft. lease area and access to the site will be via Kennel Lane which is located on an adjacent parcel and owned by the Grubers and recordation of a lease agreement and access agreement is a condition prior to site plan approval.

### Vv. Applicant’s Presentation 

Genevieve Macfarlane, attorney and agent on behalf of the Applicant, presented the Application on behalf of the Applicant. She stated that the Applicant is currently working with DNR and the landowners that are encumbered by the Rural Legacy Easement to do an amendment to encompass the footprint of the research area. She stated that DNR indicated that it was not required; however, the Applicant felt it was best practices to have the amendment updated. She stated that as there are around 40 landowners that process has been a lengthy one and unfortunately it could not be completed within the initial approval timeframe. She indicated the Applicant is asking for a little more time (i.e. 18 months instead of 12) with this approval than is common in a conditional use approval and believes that time frame will allow enough time to get the easement issue resolved. 

Valerie Imbruce, Director for the Center for Environment and Society, testified on behalf of the Applicant, Washington College. She indicated that she oversees the program that is presently running out of the property in a former pheasant shack, she stated the current setup is very difficult as there are approximately 7 staff and various interns and visitors and there is no water or climate control despite the project running through all seasons. She stated, to date, the program has banded more than 350,000 migratory birds and being in the Atlantic flyway provides a high volume of bird species and number. The current banding station has been running out of the shack for 30 years and predated the Washington College coordination of the program due to the landowner’s interest in this research. The College would like to grow the program and showcase the work being done to further the unique educational experience. 

Ms. Imbruce testified that the location of the research station as proposed is important because ef the nets that catch the birds have been in that location for the entirety of the program and for continuity of data, the station needs to stay as located. After submittal DNR took a different position, indicating that although DNR supports the project they have to look at the location and the person is no longer with DNR and they cannot honor an email. She stated that position shifted the timeline and caused conversation to ensue with DNR, counsel, and the Applicant and the owners of the property.
