# **SUSTAINABLE COMMUNITY – STEVENSVILLE ‘SSCA”:** 

Ms. Stephanie Jones, Principal Planner, Planning and Zoning, submitted to the Board for their approval the Sustainable Community – Stevensville ‘SSCA”. 

The Sustainable Communities Program was established by the Sustainable Communities Act of 2010 to target revitalization into the areas designated as ‘Sustainable Community Areas’. Sustainable Communities are defined as a place where public and private investment and partnerships achieve development of a healthy local government, protections and appreciation of historical and cultural resources, mix of land uses, affordable and sustainable housing, and employment. The detailed application is to open opportunities for what is the proposed Stevensville Sustainable Community Area. The designation of a Sustainable Community provides access to a multiagency toolbox of financing programs and tax incentives which then provides incentives and furthers economic development, businesses growth, and revitalization projects within the Sustainable Community. The ‘SSCA’ is proposed to include approximately 213 acres of residential, commercial, and mixed-use properties located to the east of MD Route 8 and to the north of U.S Route 50/301 on Kent Island. The ‘SSCA’ is located within the PFA and growth area, but specifically includes the Arts and Entertainment District and Stevensville Historic District.  In order to move forward through the approval process a resolution must be signed by the County Commissioners as an indication of the approved application, Action Plan, and map. Following County Commissioner approval, by resolution, the entire package will be provided to the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD). A State inter-agency team will review applications and make recommendations to DHCD Secretary. Approval of the application will be made by the Governor’s Sustainable Growth Subcabinet on the recommendation of the Secretary of Housing and Community Development.

# **BUILDING MORATORIUM DECISIONS - RESOLUTION #25-12 ADEQUATE PUBLIC FACILITY ORDINANCE (APFO) REVISION 2026 COMPREHENSIVE WATER AND SEWERAGE PLAN (CWSP) ADOPTION WATER & SEWER ALLOCATION POLICY:** 

Mr. Alan Quimby, Director, Department of Public Works, submitted to the Board for their approval the Building Moratorium Decisions - Resolution #25-12 Adequate Public Facility Ordinance (APFO) Revision 2026 Comprehensive Water and Sewerage Plan (CWSP) Adoption Water & Sewer Allocation Policy. 

Two actions remain to conclude the Temporary Building Moratorium which expires on August 12, 2026 (unless extended by the Board):  1) revisions to the APFO as it regards high school capacity, and 2) the adoption of the CWSP which, as a prerequisite, requires agreement on the draft Water & Sewer Allocation Policy. 

# **1. APFO Issue** 

As was previously presented, staff recommended raising the capacity of high schools from the current value 100% to 110% has merit given the mobility nature of high school students. Given the opportunities for work-study, dual enrollment, off campus CTE studies, etc., a high school’s actual capacity is less dictated by the actual number of students enrolled.  Addressing this issue would require a revision to County Code 28-7 ‘Adequacy Standards by Public Facility’, Article C.1.b. ‘School System – Level of Service Standard’. We are prepared to have the County Attorney draft a revision to County Code 28-7.C.1.b to consider raising high schools capacity’s Level of Service standard to 110% for introduction at the September 8, 2026 meeting and with a public hearing to be held on September 21, 2026. 

# **2.  CWSP - Allocation Policy Issue** 

The final issue that needs to be decided before we can initiate the adoption of the 2026 CWSP is the Water & Sewer Allocation Policy. The latest draft dictates two allotments of the remaining wastewater treatment plant capacity; one allotment from the remaining capacity of the 2007 expansion to 3,000,000 gallons per day (gpd) as well as the recent rerating allotment of 300,000 gpd secured in June 2026. 

Regarding the 3,000,000 gpd allotment, the estimated remaining capacity of 150,000 gpd is to be allocated amongst the following three categories:
