# **REQUEST FOR ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT INCENTIVE FUNDING (EDIF) COMMUNICATIONS ELECTRONICS SYSTEMS INTEGRATED SOLUTIONS:** 

Ms. Heather Tinelli, Director, Economic Tourism and Development submitted to the Board for their review and approval the Request for Economic Development Incentive Funding Communications Electronics Systems Integrated Solutions. 

Communications Electronics Systems (CES) is a locally owned security installation and service based in Stevensville. The company specializes in the installation, maintenance, monitoring, design, and sales of electronic access control, video surveillance, and intrusion detection systems for mid- to large-sized commercial businesses, as well as municipal and state government clients. CES successfully met the terms of its first EDIF agreement in 2024; this is a second request and demonstrates the benefit of EDIF funding for business growth and retention. CES Integrated Solutions is expanding its operations to meet increasing service demands by acquiring additional office and warehouse space at 194-196 Log Canoe Circle. As part of the expansion, the company will increase its office and warehouse footprint, purchase new office equipment and furniture, and add new service vehicles. The total project cost is estimated at $250,190, and the company is requesting $50,000 in EDIF funding to support the expansion. The remainder will be self-funded. Currently, CES employs 24 full-time staff, and as a direct result of this expansion, the company will create 10 additional full-time positions over the next three years, including technical, office, and warehouse staff. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Moran, seconded by Commissioner Wilson, the Board unanimously agreed to approve an Economic Development Incentive Fund (EDIF) conditional loan up to $50,000 to Communications Electronics Systems Integrated Solutions for their Office Expansion Project. The disbursement of funds is contingent upon a signed agreement between the EDIF Commission and Communications Electronics Systems Integrated Solutions, outlining the terms and conditions of the funding the project’s performance as submitted in the EDIF application. 

As part of the motion, Commissioner Dumenil asked questions and/or made comments on this topic.

# **FY2025 COMMUNITY REINVESTMENT AND REPAIR FUNDS ALLOCATIONS:** 

Ms. Lacey Amos, Local Management Board Administrator, submitted to the Board for their approval the FY2025 Community Reinvestment and Repair Funds Allocations. 

The Community Reinvestment and Repair Fund (CRRF), established under the Maryland Cannabis Reform Act of 2023, is a critical initiative designed to reinvest cannabis tax revenue into communities disproportionately impacted by cannabis prohibition. The fund focuses on promoting social equity, economic development, and addressing key community needs. The Queen Anne’s County Local Management Board serves as the Community Reinvestment and Repair Commission (CRRC) to administer the fund. Its duties include overseeing the public grant application process, recommending fund allocations, and ensuring transparency and community input. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner Dumenil, the Board unanimously agreed to approve the allocations for the FY2025 Community Reinvestment and Repair Funds in the amount of $595,000 effective March 1, 2025.

# **CHARACTER COUNTS! FEBRUARY COMMISSIONERS SPOTLIGHT:** 

Commissioner Dumenil read the Character Counts! February Commissioners Spotlight honoring the Chesterwye Center. 

The Character Counts! pillar celebrated in February is RESPECT. This pillar is both given and received and at its core is simply treating others the way you would want to be treated. Our honoree this month ensures that all people, especially those with developmental disabilities, are treated with RESPECT and dignity. 

<mark>The Chesterwye Center was founded i</mark> n 1968 by Bobby Ann Nash in response to the scarcity of services for individuals with developmental disabilities in Queen Anne's County. Inspired by her son's needs, she rallied support and established the center as a community-driven initiative. Programs and services provided by the center include support in transitioning students from the high school system, employment coaching and opportunities, and transportation services. All these years later, Chesterwye continues to serve the community by providing comprehensive Day and Residential Services, supporting 45 individuals daily. <mark>Most recently, at the annual Queen Anne’s County Chamber of Commerce “Celebrating Business” dinner on November 7th, of 2024, Chesterwye Center was granted the FIRST EVER Non-Profit of the Year Award!</mark> 

Chesterwye Center is committed to expanding its services to reach even more citizens with disabilities within Queen Anne's County. Their goal is to continue to grow in the community for
