A public hearing was held at 6:00 p.m.in the County Commissioners meeting room to discuss the following amendment request: 

This proposal is a map amendment request for an improved property just south of Matapeake Estates.  Matapeake Estates is located in SKI Phase 4.  The request is to change the sewer service designation from S-6 (No Planned Service) to S-2 (service on demand). Environmental Health has deemed the existing septic system to be failed with no suitable soils on site. 

The amendment, should it be approved, will have two conditions: 

1.  Applicant must secure an easement from the Matapeake Estates subdivision in order to allow the physical connection. 

2.  No connection can be made until the SKI Phase 4 community mains are constructed and placed in service (‘in service’ being defined as all the vacant lots in Phase 4 have been assessed).

The property is currently served via a documented failing on-site sewage disposal system and the remaining soils on the site are deemed to be unsuitable for a replacement system. The proposal is to amend the property from S-6 (No Planned Service) to S-2 (Service On Demand) with an ‘L’ designation (Limited Development) which would require any

# **AMERESCO SOLAR PRESENTATION:** 

Mr. Alan Quimby and Mr. Paul Spencer met with the Board and did a presentation on Ameresco Solar.  It was noted that the County has been approached by Ameresco to enroll the County, via a Credit Purchase Agreement under the State of Maryland’s Aggregate Net Metering program (ANEM), into a solar array they are planning to construct in the near future in Cecil County.  If you may recall, the County entered into a similar arrangement with Nexamp in late 2020.  Unfortunately, that arrangement has yet to yield any of the promised electricity savings as Nexamp has had a difficult time getting the array constructed, also in Cecil County.  Ameresco will utilize the remaining electric accounts not already committed to Nexamp. 

The parameters of the agreement are similar to what Nexamp proffered.  Ameresco will provide energy supply and delivery at a cost of 90% of what we are currently being charged. It is anticipated that his arrangement will save the County $35,000 per year at current rates.  Eastern Shore of Maryland Educational Consortium’s (ESMEC) energy consultant has reviewed the proposal and he has verified the cost savings.  The County currently buys power through ESMEC (currently from Washington Gas Energy Services) at a reduced rate than what we would be paying Delmarva Power (currently $0.0876/kwH with WGES as opposed to DP’s rate of $0.0936). The 10% ‘discount’ would apply to any power provider we use. 

However, the 10% ‘discount’ does not only apply to just the supply cost, but all ‘volumetric’ costs (anything charged per kWh), including delivery charges – which are significant.  Also, the 10% ‘discount’ is fixed for the term of the Agreement (25-years), regardless of where the supply and delivery rates may go. 

Ameresco’s business model supports this arrangement, basically they believe they can build and provide energy at a much cheaper rate than a conventional supplier.  In return, their investors know they have a stable customer, i.e. the County, to offset the cost of the array’s construction. 

It was noted that if the Board believes the proposal has merit, a Credit Purchase Agreement and associated documents be will provided on June 11<sup>th</sup> for execution. 

The Board agreed to move forward with this proposal.

# **Proclamation #24-19 Emancipation Recognition and Giving Day** 

Commissioner Corchiarino read the following proclamation: 

## **24-19** 

**WHEREAS,** June 19, 1865 is considered the date when the last slaves in America were freed, when General Gordon Granger rode into Galveston, Texas and issued General Orders No. 3, on June 19, announcing the emancipation of enslaved Texans, almost two and ahalf years after President Abraham Lincoln signed the Emancipation Proclamation; 

**WHEREAS,** the 19th of June- Juneteenth- became a day of jubilation and liberation for former slaves and is the Nation’s oldest known celebration of the ending of slavery in our nation, and is celebrated in local African American communities throughout the country;and today emphasizes African-American education, culture, art, history, and achievement; 

**WHEREAS,** the Kennard Alumni Association and its community partners will celebrate, Saturday, June 8, 2024 as a day of commemoration by hosting a Juneteenth Festival Celebration and Museum Giving Day, with a focus on the joys and values of Freedom and Community. A community festival will be held at Kennard African American Cultural Heritage Center where local African American artists will perform, crafters will offer their wares, lecturers will educate, and guests will view and experience the exhibits chronicling African American history and culture in Queen Anne’s County; 

**WHEREAS** , the African American community is encouraged to submit, for donation or copying items and memorabilia, to the Kennard African American Cultural Heritage Center and Museum to stand as lasting memorials of the contribution of the black community to Queen Anne’s County; 

**WHEREAS** , this Juneteenth celebration is not only an African American milestone in our nation but an opportunity to educate and dialogue with all citizens and reflect on our shared history in Queen Anne’s County;

# **LETTER TO GOVERNOR WES MOORE - NEW MEASURES FROM THE ATLANTIC STATE MARINE FISHERIES COMMISSION (ASMFC) LIMITING ATLANTIC STRIPED BASS:** 

This is a letter of opposition to the Atlantic States Marine Fisheries Commission’s Addendum II to Amendment 7 of the Interstate Fishery Management Plan for Striped Bass.  The one fish bag limit, increased time slot and restricted season dates will negatively affect the Charter Boat Industry.  These regulations will affect local small business models that operate in the charter boat and the commercial fishing industry. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner Dumenil, the Board unanimously agreed to execute the opposition letter to Governor Moore regarding the New Measures from the Atlantic State Marine Fisheries Commission (ASMFC) Limiting Atlantic Striped Bass with changes.
