# **PROCLAMATION #23-50 “MR. DAN TABLER 99**<sup>**TH**</sup> **BIRTHDAY”** 

Commissioner Dumenil read the following proclamation: 

23-50 

WHEREAS, Mr. Dan Tabler was born in West Virginia and moved to Queen Anne’s County as a youngster when his father came to Centreville in 1933 to reopen Centreville National Bank. Mr. Tabler celebrated his 99<sup>th</sup> birthday on October 7<sup>th</sup> ; and 

WHEREAS, Mr. Tabler, a retired newspaper man, got his start as a paper delivery boy. He is a former editor and longtime contributing writer and columnist for The Record-Observer. Most people will remember his “Writer’s Notebook” column; many have been featured in it over the years; and 

WHEREAS, Mr. Tabler started his first column as a teenager, “Dots and Dashes by Dan.” He served in the Army in public relations at Camp Lee during World War II, where he wrote stories about the camp, some of the men, celebrities that came to give USO shows for the troops, and more. His column was called “The Army Life.”; and 

WHEREAS, Mr. Tabler has served as a reporter, editor, photographer, ad salesman, and even helped out the press on occasion. Dan was inducted into the Maryland, Delaware, D.C. Press Association Hall of Fame in 2009 and was a board member and officer for the forerunners of MDDC — the Delaware Press Association and the Maryland Press Association — and later for MDDC in the 1970s; and 

WHEREAS, Mr. Tabler’s dedication and community spirit didn’t stop with just writing about it. Dan has always been an active volunteer. In 2002, Dan was named Queen Anne’s Most Beautiful Volunteer; and 

WHEREAS, Mr. Tabler is a former member of the Centreville Town Council and has been a member of the Goodwill Fire Company for 76 years, holding many different positions during that time. He was inducted into the Maryland State Firemen’s Association Hall of Fame in 2012; and 

WHEREAS, Mr. Tabler is a member of the Centreville Lions Club, where he was recognized as a Melvin Jones Fellow; a member of the American Legion, a member of the Kent and Queen Anne’s Volunteer Fire, EMS and Rescue Association; and

# **SHORE RIVERS LETTER OF SUPPORT FOR SHORELINE RESTORATION GRANT AVALON FARM** 

This letter of support for Shore Rivers is for a grant application from Maryland Department of Natural Resources Chesapeake and Coastal Grants Gateway for design for shoreline restoration at Avalon Farm.  Avalon Farm is located along Little Creek and is across from Little Creek Public Landing. This grant request is for design services for $100 K.  The construction will be approximately $1.5 M. The Avalon Farm peninsula is approximately 4,226 feet, with an extremely vulnerable 500-foot area that is unprotected and adjacent to an 800-foot jetty at the entrance of Little Creek in Chester. This quickly eroding 500 feet of shoreline serves as a natural barrier that protects marinas, a public boat launch, docks, homes, and other infrastructure located along Little Creek Road. If the eroding area of Avalon Farm’s shoreline remains unrestored it will eventually erode completely, like nearby Little Island, and destroy the navigability of Little Creek. 

On a motion by Commissioner Wilson seconded by Commissioner Dumenil the board unanimously agreed to execute the Letter of Support for ShoreRivers Department of Natural Resources grant application for design of a living shoreline for Avalon Farm.

# **NETWORK EQUIPMENT REPLACEMENT** 

Ms. Megan DelGaudio, IT Manager submitted to the board a  recommendation to use a pre-established Carroll County Public Network government contract to replace aging core network equipment with Skyline Technology Solutions The core equipment has been in production since 2013 when we originally worked with Skyline to setup the original fiber network. The current equipment is located at three core locations throughout the County which serves as the primary hub for all internal and external communications for all departments. This infrastructure is also shared with the BOE.  County staff reviewed current options and only one device, the MX-480, provided for all current functions while also offering flexibility for future expansion.  The cost for three production and one spare device along with optics, project management and implementation is $276,093.96.   Adequate funding is available in the County IT Fiber infrastructure budget. 

On a motion by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner Dumenil the board unanimously agreed to approve the replacement of the current aging IT core network equipment using the CCPN government contract in the amount of $276,093.96.
