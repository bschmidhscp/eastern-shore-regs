# **FOR ALL SEASONS UPDATE – PROCLAMATION #26-04 HUMAN TRAFFICKING AWARENESS:** 

Commissioner Wilson read Proclamation No. 26-04 for Human Trafficking Awareness Month. 

**WHEREAS,** nearly 25 million children, women, and men are trafficked and deprived of their freedom, human rights, and dignity every year and, according to UMD School of Social Work, there have been over 1,000 reports of Child Sex Trafficking screened by Maryland DSS CPS screening units since 2013; and 

**WHEREAS,** human trafficking is a serious problem on the Eastern Shore, whereby traffickers use violence, threats, deception, and other manipulative tactics to force and coerce both young people and adults, boys and girls, to engage in commercial sex acts or to provide labor against their will; and 

**WHEREAS** , human trafficking is among the fastest-growing criminal industry in the world, second only to drug trafficking; and 

**WHEREAS** , the State of Maryland has enacted comprehensive anti-human trafficking laws, with a focus on criminal prosecution and victim support; and 

**WHEREAS** , Queen Anne’s County recognizes the considerable moral and economic harm of human trafficking to our communities, our states, and the nation, and recognizes that bringing a greater awareness to this problem will help victims; and 

**WHEREAS,** by bringing awareness to human trafficking within the Mid-Shore region of Maryland, we bring victims hope, and work to secure the natural born freedoms all human beings should enjoy; and 

**WHEREAS** , Queen Anne’s County recognizes that increased public awareness and education within our community will provide more opportunities to recognize and aid in the fight against human trafficking; and 

**NOW, THEREFORE, WE, THE COUNTY COMMISSIONERS OF QUEEN ANNE’S COUNTY** , do hereby declare the month of January **HUMAN TRAFFICKING AWARENESS MONTH** and urge all citizens to actively work toward the eradication of human trafficking.

# **PUBLIC SAFETY PEER SUPPORT PROGRAM:** 

Mrs. Beverly Churchill, Director, Human Resources, along with other staff from the Department of Emergency Services presented to the Board the Public Safety Peer Support Program Presentation. 

The public safety community has developed a comprehensive Peer Support Program to address the unique and growing mental health challenges faced by Volunteer Fire Departments, the Office of the Sheriff, EMS, 911-Center, Detention Center, and the Centreville Police Department (representing over 525 public safety personnel).  The goals of the program are: (1) development and training of a peer support group, (2) partnering with a third-party for timely and reliable mental health support, and (3) providing 24/7 communication access to resources including peer support across all public safety sectors.  The estimated cost for this program for February – June 2026 is $35,750.   They have applied for a grant through CareFirst and are in the process of applying for a Motorola grant for future annual funding.  At this time, staff are requesting support for the new Public Safety Peer Support Program and FY26 start-up funding ($35,750). Should the County be awarded one or more grants, this start-up funding will be applied to the grant(s). 

On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner Dumenil, the Board unanimously agreed that Queen Anne’s County proceeds with implementation of the Public Safety Peer Support Program to include $35,750 in start-up funding for FY26. 

As part of the motion, Commissioner McLaughlin asked questions and/or made comments on this topic.

# **SUDLERSVILLE SKEET CLUB AGREEMENT:** 

Mr. Shane Moore, Chief Roads Engineer, Dept. of Public Works, submitted to the Board for their approval the Sudlersville Skeet Club Agreement. 

The Sudlersville Skeet Club has a mission to “further youth and adult shooting sports, teach and promote safety and enhance outdoor hunter safety”. The Skeet Club has been located at the County’s closed Price Chapel Landfill on Duhamel Corner Road since 1998. The original Lease Agreement was executed in January 1998 and terminates in 2048. The Skeet Club has been investing and improving the property over the years and is now planning for major future capital improvements. As such, they requested a new lease agreement to remove the 2048 termination date. Recent improvements completed include: a new septic system, three additional trap fields, outdoor lights, a pavilion and relocation of their clubhouse.  They have a current project proposal to construct a new clubhouse and want long-term security to use this property for their 100-plus members. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner Dumenil, the Board unanimously agreed to execute the new Sudlersville Skeet Club Agreement.

# **WHITE MARSH COMFORT STATION:** 

Mr. Steve Chandlee, Director, Parks and Recreation, submitted to the Board for their approval the White Marsh Comfort Station. 

Parks and Recreation received sealed bids for the construction of a fully-plumbed comfort station at White Marsh Park. They received 12 responsive bids ranging from $545,361 up to $1,287,723. Harper & Sons, Inc. was the lowest responsive bidder to provide general contracting services including site work and building construction. This firm is well-established in the region and references from three past clients were all consistently positive. The Department has evaluated all other aspects of their bid as responsive and responsible and is requesting approval to award the contract to Harper & Sons, Inc. Once completed, the facility will consist of an ADA flush restroom with men’s and women’s rooms (5 stalls, 1 urinal) and water fountain/bottle-fill station, serviced by well and public sewer and centrally located near the baseball fields. The estimated completion date for the project is September 1, 2026. Staff recommended the award of the contract to Harper & Sons, Inc. in the amount of $545,361. Adequate funding is available via a POS grant ($191,940) and the capital project budget for Park comfort stations ($353,421). 

On a motion made by Commissioner Corchiarino seconded by Commissioner Wilson, the Board unanimously agreed to authorize the Department of Parks & Recreation to contract with Harper & Sons, Inc to build the comfort station at White Marsh Park for $545,361.00, with funding from DNR-Program Open Space and Department’s Capital Project for Pavilions/Concessions/Comfort Stations.

# **RURAL LEGACY PRIORITY AREA FY27:** 

Mr. Joe Pippin, Soil Conservation Aide, Land Preservation, submitted to the Board for their approval the Rural Legacy Priority Area for FY2027. 

Queen Anne’s County has two existing Rural Legacy (RL) Areas, Lands End and Foreman Branch. When applying for grant funding for preservation in both RL areas, the Maryland Department of Natural Resources requests that priority be given for one of the two areas. In FY26 the County Commissioners designated the Foreman Branch Rural Legacy area as priority over the Lands End Rural Legacy Area. It is usually rotated between the two areas every other year, however, there was no funding in FY26. Therefore, it was recommended to stay with the Foreman Branch Area as the prioritized area for funding in FY27. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner Dumenil, the Board unanimously agreed to execute the Foreman Branch Rural Legacy Area as the area for priority funding for FY27.
