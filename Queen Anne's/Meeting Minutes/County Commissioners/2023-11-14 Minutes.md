# **TOWN OF CENTREVILLE – WASTEWATER TREATMENT PLANT EXPANSION** 

This text amendment reflects a request to increase the wastewater treatment capacity at the Town of Centreville’s wastewater treatment plant.  The wastewater treatment plant currently has a permitted capacity of 542,000 gallons per day and utilizes groundwater discharge via spray irrigation on a seasonal basis on its lands located at 751 Hope Road (April 1 through November 30) and a surface water discharge into Gravel Run stream the remainder of the year (December 1 through March 31).  This amendment seeks to increase the capacity to 1,000,000 gallons per day with the increase in capacity being surface water discharged year-round.  In order to accommodate this surface water discharge increase, the current wastewater treatment plant outfall point is proposed to be extended from its current location within the Gravel Run stream (immediately adjacent to the wastewater treatment plant) to a new point directly into the Corsica River.  The existing spray irrigation facility will remain in operation seasonally at its current capacity. 

The Town Council reviewed the need for the proposed amendment.

# **4-H PARK STORMWATER REPAIRS CONTRACT AWARD:** 

Mr. Lee Edgar, Chief of Engineer submitted to the Board for their review and approval the 4-H Park Stormwater Repairs. Sealed proposals were received for the 4-H Park Stormwater Repairs portion of Queen Anne’s County 4-H Park Infrastructure Upgrade project. The work involves installation of new and replacement storm drain, plus appurtenant site grading and stabilization which includes approximately 26,000 square feet of asphalt pavement. This is the first major phase of a Park Infrastructure Upgrade project first initiated by the County 4-H Park Association, Inc. in 2022. This project is funded by a $1,200,000 grant and the 4-H Park Capital Budget. Seven bids were received and Martin’s Excavation and Hauling, LLC submitted the lowest bid in the amount of $638,238.10. Adequate funding is available with the Rural Investment Grant and in the approved Capital Budget. The Director of Public Works is asking authorization to issue the Notice of Award and execute the Contract to Martin’s Excavation and Hauling, LLC in the amount of $638,238.10. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson , seconded by Commissioner Dumenil, the Board unanimously agreed to award the Queen Anne’s County 4-H Park Stormwater Repairs contract to Martin’s Excavation and Hauling, LLC of Centreville, Maryland in the amount of $638,238.10 and authorize the Director of Public Works to issue the Notice of Award and execute the contract on behalf of the County Commissioners.

# **EARLY VOTING CENTER - KENT ISLAND FIRE DEPARTMENT:** 

The Election Law Article sets the number of early voting centers that each county is required to have, Queen Anne’s County is required to have one early voting center and may also have an optional early voting center. The Kent Island Fire Department has been an optional early voting center in addition to our site in Centreville since 2014. The Kent Island site has been utilized by more voters than the Centreville site. To comply with the State Law, County officials must approve the optional voting center and the Board of Elections is recommending that Kent Island Fire Department continue to be utilized as an optional early voting center for the upcoming election as well as all future elections. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner Dumenil, the Board unanimously agreed to continue the utilization of Kent Island Fire Department as an optional early voting center for the upcoming election as well as all future elections.

# **LETTER OF SUPPORT - HOTEL TAX INCREASE:** 

The Worcester County Commissioners are seeking support for enabling legislation from Maryland legislature that would grant Eastern Shore Code Home Rule Counties the authority to increase the hotel room tax from five percent to a maximum of six percent. This increase allows tourism related expenses to be covered. Hotel room tax revenues provide a dedicated source to fund tourism and tourism-related activities, and the return for each dollar invested in tourism marketing generates $31 in visitor spending, making the authority to increase hotel room tax by up to an additional one percent mutually beneficial to each of our Eastern Shore Code Home Rule Counties, as we promote tourism-related activities that attract residents and visitors alike to live, play, and explore our Eastern Shore Communities. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner Corchiarino, the Board unanimously agreed to execute the Letter of Support to the Worcester County Government for enabling legislation granting the Eastern Shore Code Home Rule Counties the authority to increase the hotel room tax from 5% to a maximum of 6%. As part of the motion, Commissioners Wilson and McLaughlin asked questions and/or made comments on this topic.

# **BUDGET AMENDMENT #CC61 - FIRE IMPACT FEES:** 

The County Code states that the county reimburses the 50% and the 25% discounts for commercial property within and outside the growth area. This amendment provides budget authority for the FY23 transfer out of the General Fund and into the Fire Impact Fee funds for these discounts. The amendment is balanced with an increase in property tax revenue, which the County has received in addition to what was already budgeted for FY23. The first line of this amendment removes the existing budget authority of $90,000 that was included in the existing budget and the remaining lines for the transfer in are based on the actual commercial impact fees received. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner McLaughlin, the Board unanimously agreed to approve Budget Amendment #CC-61 – Fire Impact Fees.
