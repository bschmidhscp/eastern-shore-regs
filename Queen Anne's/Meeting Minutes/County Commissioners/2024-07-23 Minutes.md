<u>QAC Commissioner’s Character Counts! Spotlight for July 2024: Election Judges</u> 

CITIZENSHIP is the pillar Character Counts! celebrates in July. Being a good CITIZEN of your community includes being respectful of the communities needs and stepping up when needed to support your neighbors. One way county citizens can do that is to serve as an election judge. One of the most important roles “We the People” have in guiding our country is the power to vote for our representatives. An election judge plays a very important role in the electoral process by administering voting procedures in an assigned precinct or early voting center and ensuring a fair and accessible election for all eligible voters. 

It can be a long day but can also be a rewarding experience. In addition to financial compensation, these judges sleep well knowing they played a role in the shaping of our country and helped other voters exercise their right to vote.

# **BROADBAND – MIDDLE MILE FIBER REQUEST FOR INFORMATION RESPONSE RECOMMENDATION:** 

Ms. Megan DelGaudio, IT Support Manager submitted to the board for their review and approval the Broadband Middle Mile Fiber Request for Information Response Recommendation. In March, a request was approved by the Commissioners to advertise a Request for Information for a Middle Mile fiber build to fiber to areas that the Long Driveway Grant Program funds cannot be used to build directly to. The recent Difficult to Serve Grant award ($1.1M) from the State is planned to be used to complete the build to the homes in these areas once middle mile fiber is available. The Request for information was open until near the end of May and responses were reviewed at the Broadband Advisory Council meeting in June. 

Two responses were received, one from Choptank Fiber and one from Talkie Communications. Ten areas were requested to be quoted for a build. Choptank Fiber provided a response for two areas at a total cost of $821,520 with a county contribution of $400,000 to provide middle mile to 27 residents at a cost of approximately $30K/household. Talkie Communications provided a response for all ten areas at a total cost of $1,524,153 with a contribution from the County of $762,077 to provide service to 105 households at a cost of approximately $15K per household.  Due to the expansion of all areas at a lower cost Talkie Communications is being recommended as the Internet Service Provider. The Public Fiber project currently has approximately $915K available to cover associated costs. 

On a motion made by Commissioner McLaughlin, seconded by Commissioner Wilson, the Board unanimously agreed to approve the request to move forward with Talkie Communications to build the ten Middle Mile fiber areas for a total cost of $1,524,153 with a contribution of County funds in the amount of $762,077 where the Long Driveway Grant Program will supplement a build to 105 homes. Talkie Communications will fund the remaining 50% of the project.
