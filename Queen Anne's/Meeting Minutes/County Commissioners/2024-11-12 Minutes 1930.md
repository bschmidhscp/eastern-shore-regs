Aspire at Ewing Farm - The proposal is for 290 dwellings (72 townhomes and 218 single
family lots) located on Main Street (Md Rt 18) in Grasonville.  The allocation necessary to support
the development is 72,500 gpd (using 250-gpd per dwelling as noted in the Rate Schedule).  There
are two known off-site sewer and water infrastructure issues that this development will need to
evaluate as part of their Adequate Public Facilities Ordinance (APFO) report.    
Although the development will have to construct it on sewerage pumping station, it will
have to rely on the existing sewerage transmission piping to convey the sewage to Kent Narrows’
Pump Station No. 1.  This pipe has had numerous leaks over the years and any additional flow will
only increase the likelihood and frequency of leaks.  The development will need to evaluate this
issue and provide  mitigation.  
The existing Grasonville water treatment plant does not have adequate capacity to support
this development and adding capacity on-site has proven to be problematic (and would do nothing
to improve redundancy).  The connection from Oyster Cove to Grasonville (more specifically to
the Chester River Beach overpass) has long been planned and remains the final primary
interconnection of the Sanitary District’s water system. Allocation fees to be paid by this
development could be utilized to cover about ¼ of the cost. Additional mitigation for the public
water must also be further evaluated in conjunction with the APFO report.  
Mr. Alan Quimby, Director of Public Works reviewed the proposal.  The Board had 
various questions.

PROCLAMATION #24-46 DIABETES AWARENESS MONTH:
Isabella Corchiarino read Proclamation #24-46 Diabetes Awareness Month.
WHEREAS, diabetes is a chronic health condition affecting millions worldwide, which can lead
to serious complications and diminished quality of life; and
WHEREAS, there are three primary types of diabetes:

Gestational diabetes develops during pregnancy in women who did not previously
have diabetes. 

Type 1 diabetes occurs when the body's immune system mistakenly attacks and
destroys insulin-producing cells in the pancreas. Early signs include increased
thirst, frequent urination, sudden weight loss, blurred vision, fatigue, and slow-
healing sores. 

Type 2 diabetes results from insulin resistance, where the body doesn't use insulin
effectively.
WHEREAS, early detection of type 1 diabetes is crucial. A simple blood sugar test can help
prevent delayed or misdiagnosis, which can lead to potentially life-threatening diabetic
ketoacidosis; and
WHEREAS, diabetes awareness is essential to educate the public about prevention, management,
and treatment; and
WHEREAS, people with diabetes may need to administer insulin injections or perform finger-
prick blood glucose tests in public. These are normal management practices that should be
conducted without public scrutiny; and
WHEREAS, increased awareness can empower individuals to seek early diagnosis and
intervention, reducing the risk of complications and encouraging further research to find better
treatments and a cure; and
WHEREAS, November is designated as Diabetes Awareness Month, a time to promote 
education, awareness, and support for those living with diabetes; and

<!-- page 14 -->
November 12, 2024
14
NOW, THEREFORE WE THE COUNTY COMMISSIONERS OF QUEEN ANNE’S 
COUNTY do hereby proclaim the month of November as Diabetes Awareness Month and 
encourage all citizens to learn more about diabetes, support those affected, and participate in 
local events aimed at raising awareness and promoting healthy lifestyles.

– TRANSIT DEVELOPMENT PLAN:

Thompson, has reviewed and signed the documents.

Capital Grant Agreement for a Transit Development Plan for $100,000 to certify that the Queen
Anne’s County Department of Community Services Area on Aging will operate the Public Transit

EMERGENCY SERVICES – AMBULANCE STRETCHER PURCHASE:
Mr. Zach Yerkie, EMS Chief, Department of Emergency Services, submitted to the Board
for their approval the Emergency Services – Ambulance Stretcher Purchase.
The Department of Emergency Services received capital funding in FY25 to purchase two
new EMS Transport Units. The approved capital project amount of $941,235.00 has been verified
by the Department of Budget and Finance and is available as of this date. Two 2024 Ford F-550
4x4 Horton Model #623 Ambulances were approved for purchase on September 24, 2024 for a
total of $752,854.00, leaving an uncommitted fund balance of $188,381.00. The Department of
Emergency Services is requesting to purchase two PowerPRO2 stretchers and two accompanying
PowerLOAD systems from Stryker Medical for a total of $128,251.52 to outfit these transport
units.  
On a motion made by Commissioner McLaughlin, seconded by Commissioner Wilson,
the Board unanimously agreed to authorize the Director of the Department of Emergency Services
to purchase (2) PowerPRO2 stretchers and (2) PowerLOAD systems from Stryker Medical totaling
$128,251.52.
As part of the motion, Commissioner McLaughlin asked questions and/or made 
comments on this topic.

BUDGET AMENDMENT #CC-5 – FEDERAL RECOVERY (ARPA) FUNDS:
Mr. Jeff Rank, Director of Budget & Finance, submitted to the Board for their approval the
Budget Amendment #CC-5 – Federal Recovery (ARPA) Funds.
This amendment exchanges ARPA funding and prior year fund balance for various
projects.  There is currently $1,030,791 of unspent ARPA funds.  This amendment will authorize
re-allocating those funds to projects that have current spending.  The net change in ARPA funding
is zero.  This amendment also increases the spending authority in the Sanitary Talkie project which
will be funded with ARPA funds.  This is a housekeeping item in order to change the funding
sources for these projects in order to spend down the remaining ARPA funds by the end of 2024.  
On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner Corchiarino, 
the Board unanimously agreed to approve Budget Amendment #CC-5 – Federal Recovery 
(ARPA) funds.

RECONCILIATION:
