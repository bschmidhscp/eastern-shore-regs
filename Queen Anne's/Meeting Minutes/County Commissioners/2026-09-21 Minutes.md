# **RESOLUTION NO. 26-15 TEMPORARY SIGNS ON COUNTY PROPERTY:** 

A RESOLUTION OF THE COUNTY COMMISSIONERS OF QUEEN ANNE’S COUNTY, MARYLAND, ADOPTING A POLICY REGARDING TEMPORARY SIGNS ON COUNTY PROPERTY AND RIGHTS OF WAY. 

WHEREAS, Queen Anne’s County does not currently regulate the placement of signs, including temporary signs, on County property and County rights of way except for County Parks which prohibit attaching or posting notices or signs on park property except by permit. 

AND WHEREAS, Queen Anne’s County wishes to discourage the proliferation of such signs on County owned lands and rights of way. 

NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED this 21st day of September, 2026, by the County Commissioners of Queen Anne’s County that it shall be the policy of Queen Anne’s County that no sign, temporary or otherwise, shall be posted, displayed or attached to County property, including County roads and rights of way unless expressly approved by the County Administrator or his or her designee. This policy shall apply to all County-owned land and property except County Parks which shall continue to be governed by Section 22-19 of the Code of Public Local Laws. 

AS WITNESS the hands and seals of the County Commissioners the day and year above written.

Ms. Rachael Carmody, Local Management Board Administrator, submitted to the board for their review and approval the Queen Anne’s County Community Reinvestment and Repair Fund Distribution Plan. 

This item is seeking County Commissioner approval for the Community Reinvestment and Repair Fund (CRRF) Distribution Plan before submitting it to the Maryland Office of Social Equity in October. The CRRF, established through Maryland’s 2023 Cannabis Reform Act, provides cannabis tax revenue to support social equity, economic development, and community 

September 21, 2026 

15

<!-- page 16 -->
needs. The County’s Community Reinvestment and Repair Committee (CRRC) developed the plan using local data, community feedback, and existing County planning efforts to identify and rank community priorities for funding. New regulations effective June 30, 2026, require the plan to include community needs assessments, public input, and outreach to lowincome communities. # **2026 COMPREHENSIVE WATER AND SEWERAGE PLAN (CWSP):** 

The County is beginning the adoption process for the 2026 Comprehensive Water and Sewerage Plan, with a public hearing planned for October 27, 2026, and adoption targeted for November 10, 2026. The Planning Commission has recommended adoption. The updated Plan primarily provides an inventory of existing and planned water and sewer facilities, incorporates 28 previous amendments and updates from incorporated towns, and addresses areas with public health concerns. A key issue is that the Kent Island wastewater treatment plant is now at its regulatory capacity following a 10% capacity increase, largely due to limits on nitrogen discharged into the Chesapeake Bay. The Plan also includes an Allocation Policy that guides how remaining water and sewer capacity is distributed, with priority given to areas of public health concern and certain commercial and residential uses. 

# **MARLING FARMS & DOMINION (MFD) PUBLIC HEALTH CONCERN AREAS UPDATE NO. 1:** 

Staff continues to advance planning for the Marling Farms and Dominion Public Health Concern Areas, which include 504 improved properties, 77 vacant unbuildable lots, and 5 vacant buildable lots. Key challenges include the relatively small number of unbuildable lots available to help offset costs to existing homeowners and the potential need for significant upgrades to Pump Station No. 2 to handle additional wastewater flow. Staff plan to retain a second engineering firm to review the hydraulic model and develop options for the pump station. State funding will be essential, and the County must first obtain a Public Health Exemption through MDE and the Smart Growth Coordinating Committee. Project information is expected to be submitted to MDE in October, with a funding application due January 31, 2027. Broader public outreach is recommended once funding, project costs, and potential homeowner impacts are better defined.
