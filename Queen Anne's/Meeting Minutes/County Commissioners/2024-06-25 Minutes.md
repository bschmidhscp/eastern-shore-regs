# **CRUMPTON BUILDING RENOVATION – STATE OF MARYLAND CAPITAL PROJECTS GRANT AGREEMENTS:** 

Mr. Alan Quimby, Director, Department of Public Works, Mr. Lee Edgar, Chief of Engineering, and Mr. Shane Moore, Chief Roads Engineer submitted to the Board for their review and approval the Crumpton Building Renovation State of Maryland Capital Projects Grant Agreements. The Maryland General Assembly has authorized grant funding in the amount of three hundred thousand dollars ($300,000) to support renovation of the Crumpton Building, an existing unused former school building located south of Crumpton, for the purpose of serving the Pure Hearts Foundation as a recreational and educational facility for children and adults with special needs. The funding was communicated to the State in January 2023 to be a priority for Queen Anne’s County and was subsequently authorized in two parts: $250,000 via the Maryland House and $50,000 via the Maryland Senate. To receive the grant assistance, the County Commissioners must enter into two (2) Capital Projects Grant Agreements with the State Board of Public Works (BPW).  The grant award is consistent with approved FY24 budget authority and supplements an additional $150,000 in local funding via the approved FY24 and FY25 capital budgets. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner McLaughlin, the Board unanimously agreed to execute the State of Maryland Capital Projects Grant Agreements in the amounts of $250,000 and $50,000 for the Crumpton Building Renovations.

# **FY2025 SHREWSBURY FARM LANE AND UPLAND LANE REHABILITATION PROJECTS:** 

Mr. Alan Quimby, Director, Department of Public Works, Mr. Lee Edgar, Chief of Engineering, and Mr. Shane Moore, Chief Roads Engineer submitted to the Board for their review and approval the FY2025 Shrewsbury Farm Lane and Upland Lane Rehabilitation Projects and requests to award the Shrewsbury Farm Lane and Upland Lane Rehabilitation Projects to David A. Bramble, Inc, of Chestertown Md, in the amount of $767,302, via the existing Wicomico County Rehabilitate Piedmont Employee Parking Lots Contract. 

Both Shrewsbury Farm Lane (located off Rt 213) and Upland Lane (located off Willow Branch Road) were constructed in the late 2000’s and have had significant road base/surface failures. There is adequate funding available in the FY2025 Asphalt Overlay Project Capital Budget.

Bramble, Inc, of Chestertown Md, in the amount of $767,302, via the existing Wicomico County Rehabilitate Piedmont Employee Parking Lots Contract.

# **TALKIE CABLE FRANCHISE AGREEMENT:** 

Mr. Todd Mohn, County Administrator, submitted to the Board for their review and approval the proposed Cable Television Franchise Agreement with Talkie Communications, Inc. As a wired internet provider that offers video services and uses the County’s public rights-of-way, Talkie is a “cable operator” under federal law.  As such, it must obtain a cable franchise from the County. 

The Agreement provides the County with the benefits and legal protections permitted under law while at the same time enabling Talkie to compete in the Queen Anne’s County marketplace. In addition, the Agreement is designed to provide Talkie with competitive equity with the two other cable operators in the County, namely the incumbent cable operator, Breezeline, and the new and recently-franchised competitive cable operator, Comcast. The County and its cable consulting team of CBG Communications, Inc. and Cohen Law Group, recommended approving the agreement.

# **FY2025 IMPACT FEE SCHEDULE:** 

Amy Moredock, Director of Planning and Zoning submitted to the Board for their review and approval the FY2025 Impact Fee Schedule. On July 1 of each year, the amount of the residential development impact fee is automatically adjusted to account for inflationary increases in construction costs. Section 18:3-6.B(1) establishes the utilization of the most recent twenty-city annual national average data from the Engineering News Record Construction Cost Index for this inflationary adjustment. This year the increase is 1.5% which continues to mark a return to the more traditional, pre-pandemic numbers. 

This increase is automatic each year; however, Section 18:3-6.C of the County Code allows the County Commissioners to waive the inflation adjustment for any given fiscal year retaining the existing fee schedule. Last year, the inflationary adjustment was 1.8%, and the Commissioners unanimously voted to keep fees flat at 0% increase (carrying over the same rates that went into effect for FY23). 

During the 2023 Legislative Session, the County collaborated with Delegates Arentz, Ghrist, and Jacobs and Senator Hershey to put forward HB0940/SB1170. The purpose of this legislation was to amend § 20– 700 of the Local Government Finance title as relates to taxes and development impact fees by adding § 20–707. This addition was not successful and proposed to authorize the use of revenue from development impact fees to finance the capital costs for replacement, maintenance, or expansion of public works, improvements, or public facilities in all code counties. The County will pursue a localized piece of legislation with our delegation during the next legislative session. The result of this piece of legislation will guide and finalize the update to County Code § 18:3 Development Impact Fees of the QAC Code of Public Laws. 

As a result of the need to pursue a legislative addition to § 20–700 of the Local Government Finance title and to then finalize the update to § 18:3 Development Impact Fees of the QAC Code, the staff is recommending to keep fees flat at a 0% increase until such time as the result of the actions outlined above may be considered. 

On a motion made by Commissioner McLaughlin, seconded by Commissioner Wilson, the Board unanimously agreed to move to keep the FY25 Impact Fee Rate flat at 0% in accordance with the County Code 18:3-6.C. until such time as the result of the actions outlined above may be considered.

# **MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING – MID SHORE REGIONAL COUNTIES AND MARYLAND ENVIRONMENTAL SERVICE – SOLID WASTE DISPOSAL:** 

The Mid Shore Regional Counties in conjunction with Maryland Environmental Service (MES) have successfully concluded negotiations to extend the in-service life of the Mid Shore II Regional Landfill (MS II) situated in Caroline County through 2042. MES will provide Caroline County a host fee for each ton of residential and commercial waste accepted at MS II.  From July 1, 2024, through December 31, 2030, the host fee will be $3.00 per ton. From January 1, 2031, through the date MS II stops accepting waste, the host fee will be $6.00 per ton. 

Section 7.01 of the Memorandum also requires each County to designate an individual as the contractual representative with full authority to give and receive any notice or other communication required by this Memorandum or take any action in regard to the interest of their respective County under this Memorandum. To ratify this extension arrangement, MES has provided each County the “Amended and Restated Memorandum of Understanding by and between each of the Mid Shore Regional Counties and Maryland Environmental Service” for the continuation of solid waste disposal at Mid Shore II.   County Attorney Patrick Thompson has reviewed the MOU for legal sufficiency and confirmed that it is suitable for execution. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner McLaughlin, the Board unanimously agreed to execute the Amended and Restated Memorandum of Understanding by and between the Mid Shore Regional Counties and the Maryland Environmental Service for Solid Waste Disposal and also designate the County Administrator as the contractual representative to give and receive any notice or other communication required by the Memorandum or take any action in regard to the interest of Queen Anne’s County.
