# **CIRCUIT COURT DRUG COURT CASE MANAGER CONTRACTUAL FULL TIME POSITION:** 

Queen Anne’s County Circuit Court has a problem solving court which typically is designed for those rehabilitating from drug addiction and there was a need to add a contractual staff position to improve the facilitation of the program. This position will be funded using the opioid restitution funds. This is an excellent use of a portion of the opioid restitution funds, i.e. to provide a means of rehabilitation through the CART program for those suffering from opioid addiction. 

In open session, on a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner Dumenil, the Board unanimously agreed to approve a full-time contractual Circuit Court Drug Court Case Manager and fund the position using the Opioid Restitution Funds for two years.

# **CONTRACT AWARD FOR FY2025-FY2027 NUTRITION PROGRAM:** 

Ms. Anne Sparks, Chief of Aging and Transportation of the Area Agency on Aging submitted to the board for their approval the contract award for FY2025-FY2027 Nutrition Program. This is a contract with a food service provider that will provide the meals for our Congregate Meal Program in our four Senior Centers and for our Home Delivered Meals Program. These programs are funded through Federal Older Americans Act grants, a State Nutrition grant, and local county funds.  The Nutrition Programs currently serve 10,000 meals per year in Senior Centers from Congregate Meals and 13,000 meals per year through our Home Delivered Meals. 

The current food services contract will expire on September 30, 2024 and the Area Agency on Aging started the process to collect bids from potential providers per the County’s procurement policy. 

The bid opening was held on August 20, 2024 and the bids received were as follows: 

- Watson Hall Partners, dba Business Food Solutions: $6.79 per Congregate Meal and $7.69 per Home Delivered Meal. 

- Eat Sprout: $8.50 per meal for both Congregate and Home Delivered Meals. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner Dumenil the Board unanimously agreed to award the food services contract for the Area Agency on Aging to Watson Hall Partners dba Business Food Solutions and give permission for the Area on Aging to execute the contract.

# **AMENDMENT TO EASEMENT LEGAL DOCUMENTS – TERMINATION OF OPEN SPACE EASEMENT - JENNIFER MACGLASHAN:** 

Mr. Doug Summers, Associate Planner for the Department of Planning and Zoning, submitted to the board for their approval the Amendment to Easement legal documents – Termination of Open Space Easement for Jennifer MacGlashan. 

This owner is requesting approval to re-classify/amend the subdivision technique utilized to create the current 5.001 ac. lot from cluster to slide technique. The slide lot technique is permitted within subject parcel zoning district (AG), and the subject parcel is permitted (1) slide lot based on acreage. Consequently, the slide lot technique does not require deed-restricted open space, thus the current owner is seeking to terminate all open space that was required with the original cluster lot technique. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Corchiarino, seconded by Commissioner Wilson, the Board unanimously agreed to execute the Termination of Open Space Easement for the Jennifer MacGlashan Amendment to Easement Plan.

# **BOE PERFORMANCE AUDIT:** 

The Queen Anne's County Board of Education is funded by both the County and the State of Maryland. The BOE has faced several challenges over the past few years which has now led to potential staff and service reductions to our local school system. The financial challenges include: 

- Significant turnover and instability in its accounting function over the past several years, particularly in the CFO position and finance office. 

- Requests for operational funding for the 2024-2025 fiscal year that far exceed its maintenance of effort requirements and previously agreed-upon increases. 

- Budgetary requests that have increased materially over time despite flat student enrollment numbers. 

Despite these concerns, the Commissioners provided an additional $6.2 million in funding over last year (FY24) which is $7.0 million above the <u>required</u> MOE. This equates to a total County allocation of $75.1 million plus an additional $42.1 million in State funding for a total operating budget of $118.4 million for FY25. 

However, the BOE is currently operating with a projected budgetary deficit in just six weeks into this fiscal year (FY25) and there are concerns that this trend will continue to get worse in the coming years. 

The County does not suspect any fraudulent activity, but believe it is prudent for transparency to our citizens that we engage a third-party firm to conduct a "performance audit" in order to assess the operational decisions of the school system; evaluate whether the finance office is properly structured and designed to deliver complete, accurate, and reliable financial information and ensure that no fraud is occurring. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Moran, seconded by Commissioner Wilson, the Board unanimously agreed to authorize the County Administrator to solicit and hire an independent auditing firm to conduct a “performance audit” of the Board of Education’s financial records and budgetary development procedures to not exceed in the amount of $100,000.
