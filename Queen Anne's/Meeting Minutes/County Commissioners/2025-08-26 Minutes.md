# **LEGISLATIVE:** 

# **PUBLIC HEARING -  CWSP– BRENNAN/PREVOZNIK PROPERTIES:** 

Brennan/Prevoznik Properties: These two properties are in the same ownership and are currently both improved with single-family dwellings located at 233 and 235 Great Neck Road in Stevensville. Both properties are currently served via a documented failing on-site sewage disposal system and the remaining soils on the site are deemed to be unsuitable for a replacement system The proposal is to amend the property from S-6 (No Planned Service) to S-2 (Service on Demand) with an ‘L’ designation (Limited Development) which would require any intensification on this site, such as a subdivision, to be required to re-amend the Plan. The applicant will, at their expense, construct a denied access sewer main within the right of way of Great Neck Road in order to connect to the existing low pressure sewer system that currently serves the Matapeake Professional Park. 

Mr. Patrick Thompson, County Attorney, conducted the hearing on behalf of the Commissioners and noted all legal requirements had been met. No public comment was given. The record will remain open for two weeks.

# **PUBLIC HEARING - COUNTY ORDINANCE NO. 25-10 RENEWABLE ENERGY EMERGENCY LEGISLATION:** 

An emergency bill entitled AN ACT CONCERNING Community solar generating systems, energy storage devices, and solar energy generating stations to provide consistency with State Law known as the Renewable Energy Certainty Act and required by SB931 and HB1036. For the purpose of adopting supplemental use regulations for community solar generating systems, energy storage devices, and solar energy generating stations in Queen Anne’s County; defining key terms for such uses; establishing purpose statements for such uses; providing site plan approval standards for such uses; establishing a 5% limitation of the siting of solar energy generating stations within the County’s Priority Preservation Area; establishing a payment into a compensatory preservation contribution made to the county when solar energy generating stations and energy storage devices are developed on identified Class I soils; prohibiting solar energy generating stations within Tier 1 and Tier 2 Growth Areas; providing for zoning districts where such uses may be permitted; and generally regulating, addressing and providing for community solar generating systems, energy storage devices, and solar energy generating stations; By adding Section 18:1-57.2, amending Section 18App-1, and repealing Sections 18:1-9.Y, 18:1-38.1, and 18:1-95.S of the Code of Public Land Laws of Queen Anne’s County; and 

An amendment to an emergency bill entitled: AN ACT CONCERNING Community solar generating systems, energy storage devices, and solar energy generating stations to provide consistency with State Law known as the Renewable Energy Certainty Act and required by SB931 and HB1036. For the purpose of amending County Ordinance No. 25-10 to align the contribution amount of the Compensatory Preservation Contribution more closely with the methodology established in § 2-511(e) of the Agriculture Article of the Maryland Annotated Code and to clearly reflect the intention of the applicability of SB931 and HB1036 to the purpose statement. By amending pending County Ordinance No. 24-09. 

Mr. Patrick Thompson, County Attorney, conducted the hearing on behalf of the Commissioners and noted all legal requirements had been met. No Public comment was given. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner Dumenil, the Board unanimously agreed to adopt County Ordinance 25-10 Renewable Energy Emergency Legislation.

# **PUBLIC HEARING- COUNTY ORDINANCE NO. 25-11– INCURRENCE AND ASSUMPTION OF INDEBTEDNESS – TOWN OF SUDLERSVILLE:** 

A public local law to authorize and empower Queen Anne’s County, Maryland (the “County”), from time to time, to incur and assume indebtedness in an aggregate amount not to exceed five million five hundred thousand dollars ($5,500,000) in connection with the county’s acquisition, for the benefit of its Queen Anne’s county sanitary district, of certain water and sewer public facilities from the town of Sudlersville, Maryland (the “town”), including the assumption by the county of certain indebtedness of the town, all as more particularly described herein, and to evidence such assumption of indebtedness by the issuance, upon its full faith and credit, of one or more series of its general obligation bonds (the “bonds”) in like paramount; empowering and directing the county to adopt a resolution in accordance with section 19-504(d) of the local government article of the annotated code of Maryland (2013 replacement volume and 2024 supplement) (the “code”) prior to issuing all or any part of the bonds; empowering and directing the county to levy ad valorem taxes in rate and amount sufficient to provide funds for the payment of the maturing principal of and interest on the bonds and pledging the county’s full faith and credit and unlimited taxing power to the payment thereof; exempting the bonds from the provisions of sections 19-205 to 19-206, inclusive, of the code; authorizing the issuance of refunding bonds; and relating generally to the issuance and sale of such bonds. 

Mr. Patrick Thompson, County Attorney, conducted the hearing on behalf of the Commissioners and noted all legal requirements had been met. No public comment was given. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner Dumenil, the Board unanimously agreed to adopt Ordinance #25-11 Incurrence and Assumption of Indebtedness – Town of Sudlersville.

# **GROUND WATER AND LANDFILL GAS MONITORING BID:** 

Mr. Shane Moore, Chief Roads Engineer, Public Works, submitted to the Board for their review the Ground Water and Landfill Gas Monitoring Bid. 

This Ground water and landfill gas monitoring bid are post closure requirements, from the Maryland Department of the Environment, for the County’s three closed county landfills, Centreville, Price Chapel and Glanding. This is a 3-year contract (FY2026 through FY2028) with two (2) 1-year contract extensions (FY2029 and FY2030).  A total of eight bids were received. Beginning in 2024, MDE started requiring analyzation for PFOA, PFOS, PHHxS, PFNA, PFNS AND HFPO-DA per COMAR 26.04.07.09, 26.04.07.17, 26.04.07.20 and/or 40CFR Part 258, however the County was granted a 1-year waiver to coincide with this 2025 Ground Water and Landfill Gas Monitoring Bid. The Environmental Monitoring Plans for Centreville, Price Chapel and Glanding closed landfills were updated to meet new MDE requirements and included in the Ground Water and Landfill Gas Monitoring Bid.  In addition, the FY2026 Solid Waste Budget was increased to accommodate new sampling and reporting requirements. It was recommended to award the Ground Water and Landfill Gas Monitoring contract to Hill Consulting, of Annapolis, Maryland, in the amount of $107,850. Adequate funding is available in the FY 2026 Solid Waste Operating Budget. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Dumenil, seconded by Commissioner Moran, the Board unanimously agreed to award the Ground Water and Landfill Gas Monitoring Bid to Hill Consulting of Annapolis, Maryland in the amount of $107,850.

# **BROADBAND DIFFICULT TO SERVE PROPERTIES GRANT MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING AMENDMENT:** 

Ms. Megan DelGaudio, IT Support Manager, Department of Information and Communication Technologies, submitted to the Board for their review the Broadband Difficult to Serve Properties Grant Memorandum of Understanding Amendment. 

This amendment is to the Home Stretch/Difficult to Serve Properties grant. The State made funds available to the County to work with Internet Service Providers to get broadband to homes in the County that have long driveways or live in areas not easily accessible.  In March, the State accelerated the grant with a requirement to have a list of addresses to be served by the grant provided to them.  The State worked directly with ISP’s to approve addresses based on estimated cost. We identified 130 addresses to be served by Talkie and Choptank by September of 2026. The State negotiated with the ISP’s for them to pay 60% of the cost of the build with the State paying the remaining 40%.  The County will provide administrative support and act as a conduit for funds paying for builds then being reimbursed. State grant funds now provided are $793,124 with a maximum amount of $8,000 per eligible unserved location. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner Dumenil, the Board unanimously agreed to execute the Amendment to the Broadband Difficult to Serve Properties Grant.

# **CONTRACT FOR A FIBER OPTIC NETWORK CONNECTION OVER BAY BRIDGE:** 

Ms. Megan DelGaudio, IT Support Manager, Department of Information and Communication Technologies, submitted to the Board for their review the Contract for a Fiber Optic Network Connection over Bay Bridge. 

The IT Department requested approval to sign a service contract with the State Department of Information Technology for a redundant fiber optic connection over the Bay Bridge. This connection will provide the County an added layer of resiliency by completing a fiber ring that will allow continued network operation if there is a failure/cut to the current fiber main in the northern part of the County or other failures/cuts to other backbone connections that link everything together. The service contract is for $63,800 for the balance of FY 2026 and will be $75,960 per year going forward.  Adequate funds for this project were allocated in the approved FY 2026 operating budget. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner McLaughlin, the Board unanimously agreed to authorize the Director of Information and Communication Technologies to execute the agreement for a Fiber Optic Network Connection over the Bay Bridge.
