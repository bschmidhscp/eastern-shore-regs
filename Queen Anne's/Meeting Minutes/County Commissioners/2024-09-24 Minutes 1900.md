# **PROCLAMATION #24-38 FOR ALL SEASONS SUICIDE PREVENTION MONTH:** 

Commissioner Wilson read Proclamation #24-38 for “Suicide Prevention Month” and presented it to Ms. Carly Palmer, Outreach Coordinator and Ms. Susan Ahlstrom, Regional Navigator from For All Seasons. 

**WHEREAS** , in the United States, one person dies by suicide every 11 minutes, even though most of these deaths are preventable; and 

**WHEREAS,** 49,476 people in the United States, including 608 people in Maryland, died by suicide in 2022, as reported by the Centers for Disease Control; and 

**WHEREAS,** in Maryland, suicide is the 2nd leading cause of death for 10 – 14 year-olds and the 3rd leading cause of death for ages 15 – 34; and 

**WHEREAS,** <mark>according to the 2021 Youth Risk Behavior Survey, conducted by local school systems with the CDC, in the 12 months prior to the 2021 survey,</mark> 15.4 <mark>% of Queen Anne’s County teens attempted suicide;</mark> and 

**WHEREAS,** more Marylanders die by suicide than by homicide every year; and 

**WHEREAS,** the suicide rate in Maryland’s rural Mid-Shore region is 40% higher compared to the state average; and 

**WHEREAS** , suicide affects us all, with each death by suicide affecting an average of 115 people; and 

**WHEREAS,** the economic toll of suicide on society is immense. Suicides and suicide attempts cost the nation almost $70 billion per year in lifetime medical and work-loss costs alone; and 

September 24, 2024

<!-- page 4 -->
**WHEREAS** , many individuals experiencing suicidal thoughts and behaviors or who die by suicide never receive effective behavioral health services for many reasons, including the difficulty of accessing the services of healthcare providers professionally trained to reduce suicide risk and the stigma of seeking treatment. We urge all Queen Anne’s County citizens to: 

1. Support the development of accessible behavioral health services for all citizens of our county in an effort to reduce the risk of suicide and mental health challenges for people of all ages and backgrounds; and 

2. Recognize suicide as a significant public health risk and declare suicide prevention and mental health support a priority; 

**NOW, THEREFORE, WE, THE QUEEN ANNE’S COUNTY COMMISSIONERS** do hereby declare the month of September as **_No Matter What…You Matter_ Suicide Prevention Month** and urge all citizens to actively work toward the prevention of suicide and to reach out to one another and **ASK** – “Are you okay? **LISTEN** – without judgment, and **SHARE** – vital resources to promote an inclusive and mentally healthy community. 

# **ECONOMIC AND TOURISM DEVLOPMENT DEPARTMENTAL UPDATE:** 

Ms. Heather Tinelli, Director of Economic and Tourism Development presented to the Board the departmental update for Economic and Tourism Development. Some highlights are listed below: 

- Queen Anne’s County’s unemployment rate is 2.8%, which is the lowest in Maryland (with Carroll County and Howard County). 

- Hotel Tax Revenue: One of the ways visitations are tracked is through Hotel Tax Revenue. In FY24, Queen Anne’s County generated $1.15M in hotel tax. In FY23, the county generated $991K. This is an increase of 16% from the previous year. 

- Career & Workplace development is a partnership between Queen Anne’s County ETD and Queen Anne’s County Public Schools. Currently there are eight career development programs in place with included 229 placements in Career Building Internships. 

- QAC ETD and QACPS coordinates apprenticeships and internships in several fields including culinary, engineering, technology, and trades. 299 Internships for QACPS Students and three department of Labor Certified apprenticeships. Forty-nine career development events took place for Queen Anne’s County Public School Students.

# **PARKS & RECREATION “TRAILS PROGRESS UPDATE”** 

Mr. Steve Chandlee, Director of Parks and Recreation and Mr. Steve Cohoon, Public Facilities Planner at Public Works, presented to the Board an update on the Progress of the Trails. Some highlights are listed below: 

Cross County Connector Trail Extension Feasibility Study: 

- QAC Received an $80,000 grant from the Baltimore Regional Transportation Board 

- Grant is a direct result of adopting the 2024 QAC Pedestrian & Bicycle Master Plan 

- Grant is to conduct a feasibility study to extend the Cross County Connector Trail from Long Point Park to Nesbit Road

# **REQUEST FOR ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT INCENTIVE FUNDING – TEN EYCK BREWERY, LLC:** 

Ms. Heather Tinelli, Director of Economic and Tourism Development presented to the Board for their approval a Request for Economic Development Incentive Funding – Ten Eyck Brewery, LLC. 

The Economic Development Incentive Fund Commission recommended the approval of a conditional loan of up to $50,000 to Ten Eyck Brewery, LLC, allowing it to expand its production capacity to reach new markets and improve its customer experience by purchasing additional fermentation tanks and increasing its outdoor seating. The expansion of Ten Eyck Brewery, LLC will allow the business to meet growing demand, increase revenue, and enhance its standing as a key destination in Queenstown. The EDIF Commission has recommended that the award be conditioned upon the creation of 2 new full-time equivalent positions and 1 part-time position. The job creation requirement must be met within three years of disbursement of funding and the business is to remain in full operation in the county for an additional two years, resulting in a five-year commitment. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner Dumenil, the Board unanimously agreed to approve the disbursement of up to $50,000 from the Economic Development Incentive Fund to Ten Eyck Brewery, LLC for the purchase of equipment and outdoor seating. The disbursement of funds is contingent upon a signed agreement between the Economic Development Incentive Fund Commission and Ten Eyck Brewery, LLC and will outline the conditions of the agreement and performance of the project in accordance with the application submitted to the Economic Development Incentive Fund Commission.

# **REQUEST FOR ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT INCENTIVE FUNDING – RURAL ROOTS CULINARY MARKET:** 

Ms. Heather Tinelli, Director of Economic and Tourism Development presented to the Board for their approval a Request for Economic Development Incentive Funding – Rural Roots Culinary Market. 

The Economic Development Incentive Fund Commission recommended the approval of a conditional loan of up to $50,000 to Arnaiz Chocolat & Confections for the start-up of Rural Roots Culinary Market, a shared commercial kitchen and retail space for local culinary professionals in Queen Anne’s County. The facility will be located at 1104 Price Station Road, Centreville, Maryland. The loan request is specifically to fund kitchen equipment, ensuring that both new and experienced culinary entrepreneurs have access to high-quality tools necessary for their businesses. 

The Rural Roots Culinary Market will fill an important industry gap and repurpose a vacant restaurant space to create a comprehensive culinary hub. The EDIF Commission has recommended that the award be conditioned upon the creation of 2 new full-time equivalent positions and 3 part-time positions. The job creation requirement must be met within three years of disbursement of funding and the business is to remain in full operation in the county for an additional two years, resulting in a five-year commitment. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner Dumenil, the Board unanimously agreed to approve the disbursement of up to $50,000 from the Economic Development Incentive Fund Commission to Arnaiz Chocolat & Confections for the purchase of equipment to create and outfit the Rural Roots Culinary Market, The disbursement of funds is contingent upon a signed agreement between the Economic Development Incentive Fund Commission and Arnaiz Chocolat & Confections and will outline the conditions of the agreement and performance of the project in accordance with the application submitted to the Economic Development Incentive Fund Commission. 

As part of the motion, Commissioner Moran, Commissioner Wilson and Commissioner Dumenil asked questions and/or made comments on this topic.
