# **COMPREHENSIVE WATER AND SEWER PLAN HEARING:** 

December 10, 2024 

3

<!-- page 4 -->
The County Commissioners of Queen Anne's County held a public hearing at 5:40 p.m. in the Commissioner's meeting room located in the Liberty Building, 107 North Liberty Street, Centreville, Maryland 21617. The purpose of the hearing is to consider amending the Queen Anne's County 2011 Comprehensive Water and Sewerage Plan. 

The following proposal was considered: 

**BARNSTABLE** – This property is currently agricultural land consisting of two farms located on either side of the three improved properties (not included in the development proposal) the center one of which has a street address of 2032 Main Street in Chester. The proposed development is shown on Tax Map 57 as part of parcel 43 (Lowery Farm) and part of parcel 68 (Dream Farm), is approximately 13 acres in size, of which a portion immediately adjacent to Main Street is zoned Town Center (TC) and the other to the rear of the site is zoned Countryside (CS).   The proposal is to amend the property from S-3/W-3 to S-2/W-2 to accommodate a proposed development consisting of 82 townhomes. Flows from the use are anticipated to be 20,500 gpd. Sewer service will be provided via Vacuum Sewer Collection Station ‘G’ whereas water service would be provided by the Stevensville/Chester water system. 

**ASPIRE at EWING FARM -** This property is currently predominately agricultural land located at 5400 Main Street in Grasonville. It is shown on Tax Map 58-I as Parcel 40, is approximately 112 acres in size, and is zoned Grasonville Planned Residential Neighborhood (GPRN). The proposal is to amend the property from S-3/W-3 to S-2/W-2 to accommodate a proposal consisting of 72 townhomes and 218 single family lots.   Flows from the use are anticipated to be 72,500 gpd.  Sewer service will be provided via a constructed gravity sewer pump station whereas water service would be provided by the Grasonville water system. 

Patrick Thompson, Esquire conducted the hearing on behalf of the county and noted all legal requirements were met.

# **MOSQUITO CONTROL USER FEES:** 

Mr. Dave MacGlashan, Chief Property Manager, Department of Public Works submitted to the board for their approval the Mosquito Controller User Fees. This program provides a low-cost Mosquito Control Spray Program to the citizens of Queen Anne’s County. The program is primarily funded through participating citizens user-fees. The fees have remained unchanged over the past two (2) spray seasons (2023 & 2024). With increasing chemical, equipment and labor costs, expenses are now starting to exceed revenues.  In working with MDA to continue a low-cost, sustainable spray program, user-fee increases are proposed to offset increasing expenses beginning in 2025.

# **FERRY POINT HABITAT ENHANCEMENT PROJECT REDESIGN:** 

Mr. Steven Chandlee, Director of Parks and Recreation, submitted to the board for their approval the Ferry Point Habitat Enhancement Project Redesign. 

This is an extension document for a Coastal Resiliency Program Capital Project Grant Agreement with DNR in the amount of $25,750.  Due to delays related to permitting through the Critical Area Commission and comments received from DNR surrounding the project’s direction, the project will not be completed as scheduled.  DNR has agreed to an additional no-cost time extension through June 30, 2025 and has modified the scope of work to better reflect evolving priorities, technical comments and permitting timelines. The project will result in the regrading of a vulnerable 200-foot stretch of bank to withstand storm-surge erosion and reinforcing existing armoring on the northeast peninsula. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Dumenil, seconded by Commissioner McLaughlin, the Board unanimously agreed to authorize the Department of Parks & Recreation to execute the Coastal Resiliency Program Capital Project Grant Agreement Extension #2 with the Department of Natural Resources for $25,750.

# **CLEAN CHESAPEAKE RESOLUTION #24-08 REVISION:** 

This is a shortened version of Resolution 24-08. The resolution outlines the need to support a comprehensive management plan to address the sediment loadings in the upper Chesapeake Bay behind the Conowingo Dam. This shorter and more direct version has been prepared to concentrate on the most relevant history and call to pursue action.
