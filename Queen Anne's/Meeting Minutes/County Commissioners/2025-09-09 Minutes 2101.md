# **CELL TOWER LEASE AGREEMENT – LOVE POINT PARK:** 

Mr. Dave MacGlashan, Chief Property Manager, Public Works, submitted to the Board for their approval the Cell Tower Lease Agreement at Love Point Park. 

September 9, 2025 

4

<!-- page 5 -->
Love Point Park has been identified by Verizon as a “Service Gap Fill-In” site. This new macro tower pole is needed as existing surrounding cell tower sites are having difficulty keeping up with current coverage demand. The new service will dramatically improve coverage in all directions as related to residential communities, schools, parks, businesses and vehicular coverage. Improved safety and security should also be noted with the improved coverage. 

The new telecom facility will replace an existing Love Point Park field light pole with a new “joint use” light pole that has the capacity to not only serve the lights on the tennis court, but also Verizon’s antenna at the top of the pole. Verizon designed the site to fit in aesthetically with the surroundings.  The baseball field lights are 90’ feet in height. This replacement pole will be 95’ at the top. This lease is for an initial term of five (5) years at fourteen thousand four hundred ($14,400) dollars annually. Each year the rent will increase two percent (2%).The County Attorney has reviewed and approved the lease, and the Parks Director has approved the new tower location.

# **2011 COMPREHENSIVE WATER AND SEWERAGE PLAN AMENDMENT 11-25 – BRENNAN/PREVOZNIK PROPERTIES DECISION:** 

Mr. Alan Quimby, Director, Public Works, submitted to the Board for their approval the 2011 Comprehensive Water and Sewerage Plan Amendment 11-25 – Brennan/Prevoznik Properties Decision. 

A public hearing was held on Tuesday, August 26, 2025, to discuss the following amendment request. Brennan/Prevoznik Properties – These two properties are in the same ownership and are currently both improved with single-family dwellings located at 233 and 235 Great Neck Road in Stevensville. They are shown on Tax Map 56 as Parcels 284 and 40, respectively. Both properties are currently served via a documented failing on-site sewage disposal system and the remaining soils on the site are deemed to be unsuitable for a replacement system (refer to the attached letter from Environmental Health). The proposal is to amend the property from S-6 (No Planned Service) to S-2 (Service on Demand) with an ‘L’ designation (Limited Development) which would require any intensification on this site, such as a subdivision, to be required to re-amend the Plan. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Moran, seconded by Commissioner Dumenil, the Board unanimously agreed to approve the map amendment request to upgrade the sewer service designation for the Brennan/Prevoznik properties from S-6 to S-2 ‘L’.

**THE GUNSTON SCHOOL, INC. – CITIZEN SPONSORED CRITICAL AREA TEXT AMENDMENT - #25-12 GROWTH ALLOCATION NON-ADJACENCY STANDARDS:** 

Ms. Stephanie Jones, Principal Planner, Planning and Zoning, submitted to the Board for their approval the Gunston School, Inc. – Citizen Sponsored Critical Area Text Amendment - #25-12 Growth Allocation Non-Adjacency Standards. 

During the August window, Critical Area Text Amendment/County Ordinance #25-12 was submitted by The Gunston School for consideration. The applicant is seeking to amend §14:1 as follows: 

September 9, 2025

<!-- page 9 -->
#25-12 - § 14:1-76.D Growth allocation process. To amend the growth allocation review criteria to include non-adjacency standards for the expansion of non-profit institutional uses that provide secondary education or youth camps that existed prior to June 29, 1988. 

Since this is an amendment to the County Critical Area provisions, the review process contains extra steps which involve Critical Area Commission action. In accordance with §14:1-74, the amendment is subject to the following process: 

- Conveyance to the Planning Commission for investigation and recommendation; 

- Consideration by the Planning Commission to include §8-1800 et seq. of the Natural Resources Article of the Annotated Code of Maryland, the Queen Anne's County Critical Area Program, the Comprehensive Plan, and this Chapter 14:1; 

- Planning Commission to hold public hearing and convey recommendation to County Commissioners; 

- County Commissioners conceptual approval or disapproval; 

- Critical Area Commission review (2-part process), public hearing, and approval; 

- County Commissioners public hearing; and 

- Final approval by the County Commissioners. 

Planning and Zoning is recommending conveyance to the Planning Commission for investigation and recommendation.

# **SOUTH ISLAND TRAIL PHASE 3 – DECLARATION OF CRITICAL AREA FOREST PROTECTION EASEMENT:** 

Mr. Steve Chandlee, Director, Parks and Recreation, submitted to the Board the South Island Trail Phase 3 Declaration of Critical Area Forest Protection Easement. 

As part of the development of the South Island Trail – Phase 3, staff is presented a Declaration of Critical Area Forest Protection Easement and plat for review and signature. These instruments were prepared and reviewed by the County Attorney to address Critical Area standards as required. The easement established a deed restriction for a portion of the Crumpton Park property to remain forested in perpetuity in compliance with Critical Area standards for development of the project. The easement consists of 0.560 acres of proposed mitigation to address vegetation removal in the location of the proposed trail and 16.307 acres of forest protection in the location of existing Forest Interior Dwelling Bird (FIDB) habitat. The mitigation plan will satisfy Critical Area Commission requirements for disturbance related to trail construction and minimal tree removal. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Moran, seconded by Commissioner McLaughlin, the Board unanimously agreed to approve the Offsite Critical Area Mitigation Plan for the South Island Trail – Phase 3 and execute the Declaration of Easement.
