# **<u>QAC Commissioner’s Character Counts! Spotlight for August 2024</u>** 

In August, Character Counts! celebrates the pillar of **RESPECT** in all its forms; **RESPECT** for self, **RESPECT** for others, and **RESPECT** for community. Our honoree this month, exemplifies **RESPECT** and actively promotes this pillar in Queen Anne’s County. 

In 2013, Kent Island resident, Robert Woolley, opened The Edge Training Academy with the goal of helping student athletes **RESPECT** themselves by creating an environment that would foster their personal and athletic growth, helping them create opportunities for themselves past the high school playing level. He soon realized that it wasn’t just student athletes lacking resources, opportunity, and support to meet their personal growth needs and expanded the location to hold a full gym open to all. 

In 2016, they expanded and opened The Edge Arena, a 25,000 sq. ft. building that hosts a variety of events, including youth & adult leagues, tournaments, team practices, open plays, and fitness classes where teamwork and **RESPECT** for others is encouraged. The facility also hosts birthday parties, proms, church services, community gatherings, and much more. 

But that wasn’t enough, behind the scenes, their non-profit arm, Giving the Edge Foundation shows **RESPECT** for community and ensures their connection to the county they love. With a commitment to empowering the next generation of our community, Giving the Edge Foundation promotes character development by hosting community activities, funding positive behavior incentives in local schools, offering financial support for Edge programming, and much more. 

The Edge, in all aspects, believes that showing **RESPECT** and providing people opportunities and support encourages them to become successful, caring, responsible, and productive citizens who will understand the importance of giving back and helping others.

# **US 301 TRAFFIC COUNTER UPDATE:** 

Mr. Steve Cohoon, Public Facilities Planner of Public Works, provided a summary of the increased daily traffic volume on US 301.  Two recent questions related to traffic on US 301 are How much has traffic increased since the Middletown bypass opened in 2019? Has the Key Bridge accident had an impact on US 301 as it relates to long distance travel or freight moving up and down the Mid-Atlantic region avoiding Baltimore? 

# **<u>TRAFFIC COUNTS ON US 301</u>** 

The Middletown Bypass opened on January 11, 2019, and the County has seen traffic continue to increase on the US 301 corridor. To quantify the increase in traffic since the bypass opened, we compared 2018 weekday average daily traffic (ADT) counts to 2023 weekday ADT at the intersection of US 301 and MD 313 (Galena Road). Since the Middletown bypass has opened the traffic volume on US 301 at MD 313 is an average of 31.5 % higher. Since the Middletown bypass has improved travel times in Delaware many long-distance travelers are using US 301 as an alternative to I 95 and the congestion around Baltimore. 

The Key Bridge was struck and collapsed on March 26, 2024, which removed a major north/south freight corridor in the Baltimore region while increasing congestion on the beltway, Harbor Tunnel and Fort McHenry Tunnel. In general, average daily traffic on US 301 has increased from 2023 to 2024 when comparing January to July of each year. The percent of ADT which is truck traffic has not increased significantly since the Key Bridge collapse when reviewing the data from April, May, June and July 2023 and 2024. It is important to note that since the traffic volumes have increased the number of trucks has also increased even if the % of truck traffic remains

# **<u>US 301 SAFETY IMPROVEMNTS in the 2024 PRIORITY LETTER</u>** 

This year’s attached Priority Letter is consistent with the letters of the past years and requested at grade improvements on US 301 to improve safety and reduce crashes. Specifically, the intersection of US 301 and MD 405 is a high crash location which needs immediate attention to improve safety and reduce accidents. As traffic continues to increase on US 301 more accidents will occur. The County urged MDOT to move forward with funding the design and construction of the necessary safety improvements to reduce crashes and fatalities at US 301 & MD 405 and within the entire US 301 corridor. 

SHA has reported that between January 2019 and December 2023, 76 crashes have been reported at this crossover. Recognizing the need to improve safety at the US 301 & MD 405 intersection, SHA has initiated a closure of the crossover at the intersection starting on July 29, 2024.  SHA will study how closing the crossover impacts the intersections to the North (US 301 & MD 19) and the south (US 301 at Hayden Road).
