# **COMPREHENSIVE WATER AND SEWER PLAN HEARING:** 

The County Commissioners of Queen Anne's County held a public hearing at 5:35 p.m. on Tuesday, February 27, 2024, in the Commissioner's meeting room located in the Liberty Building, 107 North Liberty Street, Centreville, Maryland 21617.  The purpose of the hearing is to consider amending the Queen Anne's County 2011 Comprehensive Water and Sewerage Plan. 

This property, owned by the Foxx Farm LLC, is predominately vacant agricultural lands which reside within the incorporated limits of the Town of Sudlersville. It is shown on Tax Map 13G as Parcel 17, it is approximately 96 acres in size, and it is zoned Single Family Residential (SF).  The proposal is to allow for the construction of 98 townhomes and 125 single family lots. The property currently holds a sewer and water service map designation of S-3/W-3.  The amendment request is to upgrade the sewer and water service map designation to S-2/W-2 (which equates to service on demand).   Flows from the use are anticipated to be approximately 55,750 gpd.   Sewer and water service will be provided via the Town of Sudlersville’s sewer and water system. 

Patrick Thompson, Esquire conducted the hearing on behalf of the county and noted all legal requirements were met. 

Joe Stevens, Esquire reviewed the proposed amendment.

# **COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT BLOCK GRANT (CDBG) – REQUEST FOR SECOND PUBLIC HEARING** 

Mr. Mike Clark conducted the hearing on behalf of the County.  The purpose of this hearing is to update the public on the activities, accomplishments, and progress of three projects funded under the Maryland Community Development Block Grant Program. These federally funded programs are designed to assist governments with activities directed toward neighborhood and housing revitalization, economic development, and improved community facilities and services. 

The projects to be discussed are: 

1. Queen Anne's County Adaptive Reuse Feasibility Study of the Former Sudlersville Middle School 

2. COVID-19 Grant: Emergency Rental Assistance and Home Delivered Meals 

3. New Roof for Foxxtown Senior Center and Apartments 

Overview of Projects 

Although the feasibility study was successfully completed in December 2022, the grant period officially ended in November 2023. It comprehensively assessed the former Sudlersville Middle School building and grounds, offering valuable insights into adaptive reuse options. The recommendations highlight efficient and cost-effective approaches for redevelopment, paving the way for informed decision-making. 

No public comment 

COVID-19 Grant: Emergency Rental Assistance and Home Delivered Meals 

The Home Delivered Meals program, supported by a COVID-19 grant, successfully provided nutritious meals to residents aged 60 and older. Originally awarded through a Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) for Rental Assistance and Quarantine Sheltering, the program demonstrated adaptability by amending the grant to redirect funds to address critical needs. Prior to closing, Home Delivered Meals was offered additional funds of $39,000 by CDBG and an extension of time. We were able to utilize the additional funds to support seniors in QAC, closing and completing the project in December 2023. 

No public comment

# **FY25 BUDGET – 457(B) MATCH:** 

Ms. Beverly Churchill, Director of Human Resources submitted to the Board for their review and approval the 457(B) match.  The 457(b) plan is a supplement to Social Security and the Maryland State Retirement & Pension.  The 457(b) essentially completes the three prongs of recommended retirement savings. The match would be in the form of a flat dollar for ease of administration to be effective January 2025.  A flat dollar is consistent with other county government matches.  We have calculated the cost for a $10.00 match.  It is recommended that the matching program be in the form of a 401(a) plan.  This will allow employees to continue to contribute their own funds at the maximum allowed by IRS guidelines.  This will also shield the County’s contribution from FICA tax so that employees will keep more of their money in the year its contributed and pay these taxes in later years.  In addition, it provides for ease of administration of the funds such as setting recommended vesting following the completion of five years of full-time service.  Employees would continue to invest in the funds that are currently administered through Voya.  Employees receiving a match and leaving employment prior to vesting would forfeit the match which would be retained by the plan to pay for administrative expenses and/or fund costs. The match would be payable each budget year as funding is available.  The amount of the match can also be adjusted annually based on available funding. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Wilson, seconded by Commissioner Corchiarino, Commissioner Moran absent the Board unanimously agreed  that the 457(b) Governance Committee under the direction of the Director of Human Resources, engage the services of Bolton to develop and implement a 401(a) plan to present to the Commissioners for final approval with an anticipated start date of January 2025.

# **HB899 – HIGHER EDUCATION- FIREFIGHTERS AND EMS RECRUITMENT AND RETENTION – SCHOLARSHIP AND LOAN REPAYMENT ASSISTANCE SUPPORT LETTER:** 

This is a letter of support for HB899 – Higher Education- Firefighters And Ems Recruitment And Retention – Scholarship And Loan Repayment Assistance This bill is the work product of the _Commission to Advance & Strengthen Firefighting & EMS Within Maryland_ (the Commission), which comprised nearly all stakeholders within Maryland’s firefighter ecosystem, including: the Maryland Association of Counties (MACo), the Maryland Municipal League (MML), the Maryland State Firemen’s Association (MSFA), the International Association of Firefighters (IAFF), the Maryland Professional Fire Fighters Association (PFFM), the Maryland Fire and Rescue Institute (MFRI), the Maryland Institute for Emergency Medical Services Systems (MIEMSS), the Metropolitan Fire Chiefs Association, and the Maryland Fire Chiefs Association. The Commission’s final report provided data on the current state of Maryland firefighter and EMS personnel recruitment and retention, as well as several recommendations to fortify Maryland’s emergency response capacity. While Maryland’s average 9-1-1 response times are above the national average, there are a growing number of Maryland communities where this is not the norm. If current recruitment and retention trends continue, very soon many Marylanders may not receive the timely response to a 9-1-1 call that they rightfully expect. Aggressive recruitment and retention efforts for Maryland’s firefighter and EMS workforce are needed to ensure these essential functions are fully staffed.
