# **PROCLAMATION NO. 25-40: FIRE PREVENTION WEEK:** 

Commissioner Wilson read Proclamation No. 25-40 for Fire Prevention Week. 

**WHEREAS** , in 1922, the National Fire Protection Association founded National Fire Prevention week to take place the week of October 9th, commemorating the date of the Great Chicago Fire in 1871; and 

**WHEREAS** , Queen Anne’s County Government is committed to ensuring the safety and security of all those living in and visiting our county; and 

**WHEREAS** , fire remains a serious public safety concern both locally and nationally, and the presence of lithium-ion batteries in many household devices introduces unique fire risks; and 

**WHEREAS** , most of the electronics used in homes daily — including smartphones, tablets, laptops, power tools, e-bikes, e-scooters, and toys — are powered by lithium-ion batteries, which if misused, damaged, or improperly charged, can overheat, start a fire, or explode; and 

**WHEREAS** , the National Fire Protection Association<sup>®</sup> (NFPA<sup>®</sup> ) reports an increase in batteryrelated fires, underscoring the need for public education on the safe use of lithium-ion batteries; and 

**WHEREAS** , residents should follow three key calls to action: Buy only listed products, charge batteries safely, and recycle them responsibly to prevent battery-related fires; and 

**WHEREAS** , lithium-ion batteries store a large amount of energy in a small space, and improper use such as overcharging, using off-brand chargers without safety certification, or exposing batteries to damage can result in fire or explosion; and 

**WHEREAS** , the proper disposal and recycling of lithium-ion batteries help prevent environmental hazards and reduce fire risks in the home and community; and 

**WHEREAS** , Queen Anne’s County first responders are dedicated to reducing the occurrence of fires through prevention, safety education, and community outreach; and 

**WHEREAS** , the 2025 Fire Prevention Week™ theme, “Charge into Fire Safety™: Lithium-Ion Batteries in Your Home,” serves to remind us of the importance of using, charging, and recycling lithium-ion batteries safely to reduce the risk of fires in homes and communities.

# **PROSPECT BAY LIVING SHORELINE AGREEMENT:** 

Mr. Shane Moore, Chief Roads Engineer, Department of Public Works, submitted to the Board for their review the Prospect Bay Living Shoreline Agreement. 

The Prospect Plantation West Homeowners Association, d/b/a Prospect Bay Country Club, are currently working to permit the conversion of an existing rip-rap revetment to a living shoreline including shoreline frontage on two County roads, Keel Haul Court and South Harbor Drive. 

For any shoreline improvements located channel ward of the mean high-water line, the adjacent property owner is responsible for the ongoing maintenance of the shoreline stabilization improvements. Currently, we are responsible for the existing rip-rap along the County right-ofway portions of Keel Haul Court and South Harbor Drive and have previously made repairs to the revetment. As the Prospect Plantation West Homeowners Association, d/b/a Prospect Bay Country Club, have initiated the living shoreline project staff asked that they assume all future maintenance responsibilities for the living shoreline. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Moran, seconded by Commissioner Wilson, the Board unanimously agreed to execute the Real Estate Agreement with the Prospect Plantation West Homeowner Association, d/b/a Prospect Bay Country Club, to allow the construction of a living shoreline on a portion of the lands of Keel Haul Court and South Harbor Drive rights-ofway. 

As part of the motion, Commissioner Wilson and Commissioner Corchiarino asked questions and/or made comments on this topic.

# **PROCLAMATION #25-42 WHITE CANE AWARENESS DAY:** 

Commissioner Moran read Proclamation No. 25-42 for White Cane Awareness Day. 

**WHEREAS** , the white cane, which every blind citizen of our county has the right to carry, demonstrates and symbolizes the ability to achieve a full and independent life and the capacity to work productively in competitive employment; and 

**WHEREAS** , the white cane, by allowing every blind person to move freely and safely from place to place, makes it possible for the blind to fully participate in and contribute to our society and to live the lives they want; and 

**WHEREAS** , every citizen should be aware that the law requires that motorists and cyclists exercise appropriate caution when approaching a blind person carrying a white cane; and 

**WHEREAS** , Maryland law also calls upon employers, both public and private, to be aware of and utilize the employment skills of our blind citizens by recognizing their worth as individuals and their productive capacities; and 

**WHEREAS** , the State of Maryland, through its public agencies and with the cooperative assistance of the National Federation of the Blind of Maryland, can and should facilitate the expansion of employment opportunities for and greater acceptance of blind persons in the competitive labor market: 

**NOW THEREFORE, WE, THE QUEEN ANNE’S COUNTY COMMISSIONERS** do hereby proclaim October 15, 2025, as White Cane Awareness Day in Queen Anne’s County and do call upon our schools, colleges, and universities to offer full opportunities for training to blind persons; upon employers and the public to utilize the available skills of competent blind persons and to open new opportunities for the blind in our rapidly changing society; and upon all citizens to recognize the white cane as a tool of independence for blind pedestrians on our streets and highways.

# **PARTNERSHIP WITH THE MARYLAND MESONET PROJECT – LAND USE AGREEMENT:** 

Mr. Steve Chandlee, Director, Parks and Recreation, submitted to the Board for their review and approval the Partnership with the Maryland Mesonet Project – Land Use Agreement. 

The Maryland Mesonet Project is an initiative of the University of Maryland and Maryland Department of Emergency Management whose mission is to monitor rapidly changing weather conditions to improve severe weather warning lead times to the public and local decision-makers. Its aim is to work with local communities to install at least three stations in each of Maryland’s counties. Mesonet proposes to install the third of four such weather stations in Queen Anne’s County at White Marsh Park. The weather station is comprised of rapid-sampling equipment transmitted wirelessly to UMD. This data provides twelve metrics and 170 data points each minute allowing for precision monitoring and forecasting.  No departmental resources will be required to monitor, service or maintain the station. 

On a motion made by Commissioner McLaughlin, seconded by Commissioner Moran, the Board unanimously agreed to execute the Land Use Agreement between Queen Anne’s County Board of Commissioners and University of Maryland as part of its Maryland Mesonet Project at White Marsh Park.

# **PROVIDENCE FARM SEWER ALLOCATIONS – TOWN OF CENTREVILLE:** 

The County has a Public Works Agreement (PWA) with the Town of Centreville for the Providence Farm Subdivision, which has since been under development with the Vincit Building, the BOE Administrative Office Building and the YMCA-Active Aging Center. The original 2010 PWA provided for 53 sewer & water allocations for use on this campus. The PWA included an allocation sunset forfeiture (recapture) clause date of October 12, 2017.  The County secured the 53 allocations with a 20-percent deposit and subsequently paid portions of them in full as development and connections progressed.  As development was continuing, the PWA was amended in September 2017, and we were required to pay an additional 5-percent deposit on the remaining allocations in order to secure them through November 24, 2025. All the required deposits are non-refundable. The County has 15 allocations remaining, for which we have paid a total reservation deposit of $51,652.50.   The County Complex has one additional undeveloped 3.96-acre lot along with a 2.50-acre remnant lot adjacent to the new BOE Admin Building for future development. There were two options to secure the remaining 15 allocations for these parcels. 

<u>Option 1 – Purchase the remaining 15 allocations in full.  The purchase price is $10,330.50 per</u> allocation or a total of $154,957.50. 

<u>Option 2</u> – Request a 2nd Amendment to the PWA and pay an additional non-refundable 5- percent deposit or $10,330.50. 

On a motion made by Commissioner Moran, seconded by Commissioner McLaughlin, the Board agreed to purchase the remaining 15 sewer and water allocations for future development of the County Office Building complex from the Town of Centreville for a total cost of $154,957.50.
