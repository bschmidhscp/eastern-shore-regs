# Queen Anne's County: By-Right Playbook

Cite key: "Checklist" = Documents/Planning Applications and Forms/Checklist---Minor-Subdivision-PDF (rev. 3/9/2017). "Application" = Minor-Subdivision-Application-PDF (12-18 form). "Fee Findings" = 01. Digests/Fee Findings.md. Code is current through 06-13-2026. State rules (tiers, perc, BAT, Critical Area): see 01. Maryland/00. Summary.md.

## 1. Bottom line

The path is the **minor subdivision**: up to 7 lots including easements and rights-of-way [18App-1 "Minor subdivision"]. **Only 5 lots or fewer can be approved by staff**; the Planning Commission (PC) may delegate minors to the Planning Director, but only the PC can approve more than 5 lots or any public road [§ 18:1-162]. So the working by-right ceiling is **5 lots, no public road, Planning Director signs** (delegation resolution itself not found in corpus, see section 7). **There is no public hearing**, but notice is not zero: the applicant must mail written notice of the submission to all adjoining owners (tax-record addresses) and show proof with the application [§ 18:1-163.A]; the Checklist also asks for proof of notice to the Fire/EMS Commission and contact with the volunteer fire department [Checklist p.1; § 18:1-160.D]. No sign posting or published notice for minors was found. Typical time to a recorded plat is **about 6 to 9 months (estimate, no minor-subdivision timing in corpus)**, driven by the perc season and the 2-set agency reviews.

## 2. Eligibility test

Parcel must pass every item to stay on the path.

1. **Zoning and density.** In AG and CS the by-right techniques are permitted uses [§ 18:1-14.B(18),(19),(27)]:
   - Sliding scale: 1 new lot on the first 100 acres, plus 1 per additional 100 acres or part thereof [§ 18:1-14.D(1)(c)]. Lot 20,000 sf minimum, 130 ft width, 35 ft frontage [§ 18:1-14.D(1)(c),(f),(g)].
   - Large lot: site area / 20 acres; 20-acre minimum lot, 500 ft width, 35 ft frontage [§ 18:1-14.D(1)(b),(c),(f),(g)]. Such lots can never go below 20 ac or be re-cut as cluster or TDR sender [§ 18:1-13.K(1),(3)].
   - Cluster: 0.125 lot per acre, 16-acre minimum site, 85% open space; cluster lots cannot be re-subdivided [§ 18:1-14.D; § 18:1-13.K(5)]. Open space and covenants add attorney work.
   - E, SE, SR and NC: large-lot density is site area / 2 ac (E), 1 ac (SE), 30,000 sf (SR) [§ 18:1-16.D, -17, -18; Summary section 2]. Lot width and frontage in these districts mostly point to § 18:1-36 (not read); nonresidential frontage 35 ft.
2. **Cumulative cap.** Sliding-scale lots are counted from the **original parcel size** only; the original parcel owner keeps future rights, and the applicant must file a cover letter giving the parcel history [§ 18:1-13.K(4); Application p.3 "Sliding Scale letter"]. Pull the deed chain and prior plats back to the original tract before buying. State law adds: a Tier II to IV minor subdivision and its remainder cannot be re-subdivided beyond the minor total, and the plat must say so [Env. § 9-206(l); MD Summary section 2].
3. **Total lots (7 max; 5 for staff approval)** counting lots, remainder if platted, easements and rights-of-way [18App-1; § 18:1-162.A(2)(a)].
4. **No new public road; no road extension.** Private road deed and covenants are possible but the Checklist treats them as required items [Checklist p.1]; a public road forces PC approval [§ 18:1-162.A(2)(b)]. Subdivision must have roads "adequate to provide access to all lots" [§ 18:1-172.B(1)]. Keep every lot on existing frontage.
5. **Frontage and flag lots.** 35 ft minimum frontage in AG and in E/SE/SR nonresidential [§ 18:1-14.D(1)(g)]. A flag lot is allowed with a stem at least 20 ft wide [§ 18:1-172.B(1)]; the Checklist asks for at least 35 ft of road frontage plus a 20-ft stem [Checklist p.2]. Flag sewage reserve areas are not allowed [§ 18:1-172.B(4); § 18:1-174.B(7)]. Shared-driveway lot limit: not found.
6. **Septic.** Well and septic need Health Department approval; every lot needs a 10,000 sf area for an initial and two replacement systems [COMAR 26.04.02.04F, in MD Summary]. QAC's SB 236 minor definition is 7 lots, so Tier IV allows a minor on septic [MD Summary section 2]. County Tier map: not found. Public sewer or water needs an allocation and sanitary-subdistrict location; Kent Island sewer is capacity constrained, and Res. 25-12 froze approvals above 1,750 gpd (7 homes) in KN/S/G, so small minors are outside it (extension status not found) [Summary sections 1, 5].
7. **Critical Area (within 1,000 ft of tidal water).** RCA density is 1 per 20 acres [§ 14:1-39.C]; LDA impervious 15% of site [§ 14:1-38.D(8)]; slopes over 15% prohibited [§ 14:1-38]; no new development in the Buffer [§ 14:1-51]; growth allocation needs a Commissioners map amendment [§ 14:1-76]. BAT septic required [MD Summary]. Treat any RCA parcel as low-yield.
8. **Not a conversion trigger:** administrative subdivisions are only lot-line moves or combinations that create no more lots than they start with [§ 18:1-166, -167]. They do not create new lots.
9. **Existing structures** that block conformity may need PC relief [§ 18:1-161.B]; existing nonconformities must be cured and none created [Checklist p.3].
10. **Easements and restrictions:** check MALPF, MET, Rural Legacy, CRP, forest conservation or open-space covenants on the parcel; MALPF coordinator sign-off needed [Checklist p.2; Application p.2].
11. **Historic structures** proposed for demolition trigger review [§ 18:1-91].

## 3. Step-by-step path

Durations are estimates unless cited. The code lays out a formal schedule only for PC-approved applications (STAC, 25-day cutoff, 15-day administrative approval, PC meeting) [§ 18:1-182.B,C]. Whether a delegated minor follows the same calendar is unconfirmed.

1. **Pre-application meeting (planning staff).** Required for anything needing PC approval [§ 18:1-182.A]; request it anyway. Bring a property map showing resource protection lands; staff do a site visit; you then prepare a rough sketch [§ 18:1-182.A(3)]. Fee: none found. Time: 2 to 4 weeks (estimate).
2. **Title and original-tract work (attorney, surveyor).** Establish the original parcel, prior sliding-scale or minor lots, easements. Prepare the sliding-scale history letter [§ 18:1-13.K(4)]. 2 to 4 weeks (estimate).
3. **Boundary survey, topography, soils and natural resources (surveyor/engineer).** Complete boundary survey, 2-ft contours (not required for sliding-scale lots), soils units, wetlands/hydric soil screen, 100-yr floodplain, streams and buffers [§ 18:1-180.H; Checklist pp.3-4]. Wetlands jurisdictional determination when hydric soils, a water source or hydrophytic vegetation are present; Corps permit if nontidal wetlands are impacted [Checklist p.4]. 4 to 8 weeks (estimate).
4. **Perc and soil testing (Health Department, Environmental Health).** Needed for projects on well and septic [Application p.3]. Where seasonal high water table soils apply, tests must be done when the water table is highest, which on the Shore means roughly February to April [COMAR 26.04.02.04B and MD Summary]. Missing the window can add 9 months or more. Fee: not found; confirm with Health Department. Each lot needs 10,000 sf for an initial plus two replacement systems.
5. **Forest conservation (Planning/Forest Conservation).** Applies to minors on tracts of 40,000 sf or more, except Critical Area land (handled under the CA ordinance) and administrative subdivisions [§ 18:2-4.A,B(2)]. Submit worksheets, Forest Stand Delineation and Forest Conservation Plan; a notarized Declaration of Intent can support an intra-family transfer exemption [Checklist p.1; § 18:2-4.B(11)]. Written notice to adjoining owners is required at FCP submittal [§ 18:2-10]. Reforestation 1:1, 1/2:1 in a Priority Funding Area [§ 18:2-14.A]. Ord. 26-12 will repeal and replace Chapter 18:2 [PC digest 2026-04-09]; re-check before filing.
6. **Stormwater (Public Works, Engineering).** Chapter 14:4 requires ESD for residential development; the standard plan covers residential development disturbing over 5,000 sf [§ 14:4-10.A]. Subdivision must show stormwater facilities [§ 18:1-172.B(2)]. Subdivision-level threshold for minors: not extracted; ask staff. Soil Conservation District reviews erosion and sediment control [§ 18:1-172.B(3); fee QASCD-Fee-Schedule-10_6_2025].
7. **Notices before filing (applicant).** Mail written notice of the submission and purpose to all adjoining owners; mail notice to the Fire/EMS Commission; contact the volunteer fire department and offer to meet [§ 18:1-163.A; § 18:1-160.D; Checklist p.1]. Collect utility will-serve letters (power and telephone) [Checklist p.1].
8. **Submit the minor subdivision application (Planning and Zoning, devrev@qac.org).** Minimum 2 complete sets: application, cover letter, sealed plat, one deed copy, adjoining-owner notice form [Application p.3]. Concurrent referral sets as applicable: Public Works Engineering; Health (septic/well); Critical Area worksheet and Environmental Assessment; Forest Conservation; Soil Conservation District; Fire Marshal; local VFD; sanitary district (public water/sewer); SHA if on a state road; Parks within 1/2 mile of parkland; incorporated town within 1 mile; Bay Bridge Airport within 1/2 mile [Application p.3; Checklist p.1]. Incomplete submittals are not reviewed [Application p.4; § 18:1-163]. Fees in section 5. Review time: 2 to 4 months (estimate).
9. **Agency reports, comments and revisions (all agencies above).** Each agency files a signed report approving or conditioning the plans [§ 18:1-183]. Expect 1 to 2 resubmittals (estimate). Remaining paperwork: open space covenants and long-term protective agreements reviewed by the PC attorney, road deed and covenants, MPDU items if applicable [Checklist p.1].
10. **Approval.** If delegated and 5 lots or fewer, the Planning Director signs the final plat, which authorizes recording [§ 18:1-162.B(1)]. If 6 or 7 lots or a public road, the PC must act; the code then requires a regular meeting at least 10 days after receipt of a complete package [§ 18:1-190.A]. Required improvements must be finished or covered by a guarantee before approval [§ 18:1-189].
11. **Final plat package (surveyor).** Three Mylars, 8 to 10 paper copies and a CAD file; plat sealed by a registered land surveyor; right-to-farm and seafood-industry statements; stream buffer note [§ 18:1-186.A,D(3); § 18:1-180.E(7),(8); Checklist p.4]. Certificates required: owner, surveyor, Public Works, Environmental Health, P&Z, Fire Marshal, Finance Officer, notary; PC and PC attorney only if PC approves [Checklist p.4; § 18:1-187]. The plat must also carry the no-further-subdivision note where State law requires it [Env. § 9-206(l)]. Health Department/MDE signature required before recording [COMAR 26.04.03.02K]. 3 to 6 weeks (estimate).
12. **Record at the Circuit Court land records.** A plat cannot be recorded until approved and signed; developer pays recording and certified-copy costs [§ 18:1-192]. Recording fee: not found. 1 to 2 weeks (estimate).

Total estimate: 4 months (fast, perc season hit) to 12+ months (missed perc season, resubmittals), typical 7. This is an estimate; no minor subdivision appears in the PC minutes digest. The only timing data point is a 10-lot major (Placek's Place), preliminary 2023-10-12 to final 2024-02-08 (about 4 months) [PC digest].

## 4. Public notice

- **Public hearing:** none for a minor approved by staff. The PC acts at a regular meeting (agenda, no hearing process found) only for over 5 lots, public roads, or if the PC has not delegated [§ 18:1-162; § 18:1-190].
- **Adjoining-owner letters: required.** The applicant must give written notice of the submission and purpose to all adjacent owners at the tax-record mailing address and file evidence satisfactory to the Planning Director [§ 18:1-163.A]. This applies to administrative, major and minor applications and to plat revisions. The same applies again at Forest Conservation Plan submittal [§ 18:2-10].
- **Fire/EMS Commission and VFD notice:** required by the Checklist; contact with the volunteer fire department per § 18:1-160.D [Checklist p.1].
- **Sign posting, published notice:** none found for minor subdivisions.
- **Can the notice be avoided?** Only the **administrative subdivision** (lot-line moves or combinations, no net new lots) is exempt from § 18:1-178 to -192, but § 18:1-163.A still lists it [§ 18:1-166.B; § 18:1-163.A]. It cannot create lots, so there is no truly notice-free path that adds lots. The practical goal is the letter-only variant: 5 lots or fewer, no road, no variance.

## 6. Off-ramps

| Trigger | Result | Design around |
|---|---|---|
| 6 or 7 lots | PC approval required, adds agenda and 10-day lead [§ 18:1-162.A(2)(a); § 18:1-190] | Cap at 5 lots; sell remainder as a parcel (but see sliding-scale remainder rules) |
| 8+ lots | Major subdivision: sketch, preliminary, final, PC, 30-month preliminary life [18App-1; § 18:1-184] | Stay at or under 7; do not rely on later phases |
| New or extended public road | PC only [§ 18:1-162.A(2)(b)] | Use existing frontage; private road only if the County accepts it |
| Variance (width, frontage, setback, Buffer) | Board of Appeals; fees and timing not found. BoA granted 43 of 45 cases [BoA digest, Summary] | Choose parcels that meet 35 ft frontage and 130 ft width without relief |
| Matters sent to PC: TDRs, noncontiguous development, water-sewer plan amendments, growth allocation, BoA recommendations [§ 18:1-178] | Sketch or special application before PC | Avoid TDR, noncontiguous and growth-allocation designs |
| Existing structure on a new lot that cannot conform | PC may modify [§ 18:1-161.B] | Avoid parcels with structure conflicts |
| Needs public sewer or water | Allocation, sanitary subdistrict, Commissioners; allocation being denied (Barnstable 1-4, 2026-09-08) [Summary section 5] | Use well and septic outside the Kent Narrows/Stevensville/Grasonville area |
| Failed perc or no 10,000 sf reserve area | Lot lost or redesign | Test every proposed lot in season before closing |
| Critical Area RCA density, buffer, 15% impervious | Lots reduced; variance or growth allocation needed [§ 14:1-39, -51, -76] | Skip RCA parcels or confine lots to outside 1,000 ft |
| Sliding-scale rights already used, or the parcel was itself created by large-lot or cluster | No lots available [§ 18:1-13.K] | Verify the original tract history before closing |
| State minor-subdivision cap: prior Tier II to IV minor on the tract | No further lots [Env. § 9-206(l)] | Title and plat search first |
| Easements (MALPF, MET, Rural Legacy) | Holder approval; likely blocks [Application p.2; Checklist p.2] | Check Conservation Lands map pre-contract |
| Pending rules: Ord. 26-12 forest rewrite, Comprehensive Zoning Code Update, Res. 25-12 extension | Thresholds may change [Summary section 7] | Re-read code history before each filing |

## 7. Open questions to ask staff

1. Has the Planning Commission delegated minor subdivision approval to the Planning Director (the § 18:1-162 resolution), and does the Director also sign as the final approving official for 5 or fewer lots with no PC agenda item at all?
2. Is a minor subdivision one combined application and plat, or is it still preliminary and final in two steps? What is the actual calendar for a delegated minor (the STAC and cutoff schedule appears to be written for PC items)?
3. Do the remainder parcel and any easements count toward the 5-lot and 7-lot counts and toward per-lot fees? Is the remainder required to be platted under sliding scale?
4. What does the Health Department charge and require for perc/septic review, what is the county's Tier map status, and are wet-season tests required county-wide?
5. What stormwater/ESD threshold applies to a minor subdivision (versus the 5,000 sf residential standard plan), and will Public Works require a plan at subdivision or only at permit?
6. Are the 2024 planning fees still current for FY27, and does the Application's "Fire/EMS Commission notice" require a specific letter form? Also: any plan to extend Res. 25-12 or adopt the KN/S/G allocation policy that would touch small minors?
