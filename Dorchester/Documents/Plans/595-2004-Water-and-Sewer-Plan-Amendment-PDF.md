### RESOLUTION NO. 595 

A RESOLUTION of the County Council of Dorchester County amending the 2004 Dorchester County Comprehensive Water and Sewer Plan (the “Plan”) by changing the designation of an area of land located at 1548 Taylors Island Road, and shown on Tax Map 50, Grid 12, Parcel 23. For this property the applicant requests amendments to Chapter 4 Sewage, Chapter 4, Table 4-1 and to amend the Sewer Service Area Map Exhibit 4-E from S-2, 2 to 5 years to S1, existing to 2 years. S-1 means that community sewer is available now, or will be made available within two years. 

WHEREAS, State law mandates that all Maryland counties adopt a Comprehensive Water and Sewer Master Plan; and 

WHEREAS, Dorchester County adopted its most current Plan on March 16, 2004; and 

WHEREAS,a request was filed by the Dorchester County Sanitary District (the “applicant’) on behalf of Coastal Seafood, Inc. (the “owner’) to amend the aforementioned table and map to correct a wastewater allocation estimate for this property which is connected to the Woolford-Madison Sanitary District Sewer Service line; and 

WHEREAS, this improved property has been served by the WoolfordMadison Sanitary District Sewer Service line since its initial construction and installation and only recently discovered that the wastewater allocation of 250 gallons per day is inadequate for the existing home and business located on the property; and

Water and Sewer Plan Amendment 

Madison-Woolford-Susquehanna Point Sanitary District No. 7 

This application for amendment to the Dorchester County Water and Sewer plan is being requested by the Dorchester County Sanitary District, Inc. Sanitary District No. 7 serves the Madison, Woolford and Susquehanna Point communities. 

It has recently come to the attention of the Sanitary District that the wastewater allocation of 250 gallons per day is inadequate for 1548 Taylors Island Road, Woolford Maryland. This allocation was originally determined and included in the 2004 Dorchester County Water and Sewer plan. However, during the construction and installation of the Madison, Woolford, Susquehanna sewer system the allocation deficiency was not caught by the Health Department, Engineer or Contractor; therefore a remedy was not made during construction and installation. 

A simplex grinder pump system was installed for the home and business; however, it is not sufficient to handle the wastewater flow. The septic system which use to collect all the wastewater from the home and business on this parcel was abandoned during construction. The property owner contacted the health department and questioned why it was not caught during the project; the health department informed the property owner that they used the tax records which show the property zoned as residential and did not double check for accuracy. 

The 

As recommended by Freemire and Associates distributor for Environmental One low pressure grinder pump systems the Sanitary District is proposing to install a duplex grinder pump system allowing capacity of the current wastewater flow and future increased flows. 

This situation was an oversight and the Sanitary District is accepting the responsibility of correcting this mistake. If this situation is not corrected immediately, wastewater overflows could and will probably occur in the near future.
