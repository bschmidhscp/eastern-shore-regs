RESOLUTION NO. __587 

A RESOLUTION of the County Council of Dorchester County amending the 2004 Dorchester County Comprehensive Water and Sewer Plan (the “Plan’) by changing the designation of an area of land located on the west side of Suicide Bridge Road, and shown on Tax Map 12, Grid 17, Parcel 24. Said land being known as the Suicide Bridge Restaurant. The restaurant requests amendments to Chapter 4, Sewage, Chapter 4, Table 4-2 and Table 4-6, and to add a Sewer Service Area Map (no planned service to S-1 to 2 years). S-1, means that community sewer is available now, or will be made available within two years.

WHEREAS, Dorchester County adopted its most current Plan on March 16, 2004; and

(landowners) to amend the aforementioned text, charts and map to accommodate plans to connect the restaurant to a public wastewater collection and treatment system that can provide high quality treatment of all wastewater generated by the subject property as indicated on the attached exhibits; and

#### Suicide Bridge Restaurant, Inc. Request for Map Amendment to the Dorchester County Water & Sewer Plan Supplemental Narrative 

Suicide Bridge is one of the most popular restaurants on the Delmarva Peninsula. Located on Cabin Creek, a tributary of the Choptank River, the restaurant is located between the towns of Secretary and Hurlock in Dorchester County, Maryland. Suicide Bridge Restaurant seats up to 500 diners, and also serves patrons on two historic paddle wheelers and hosts weddings and other special events on the grounds of the restaurant. The restaurant is located on a three-acre parcel of land located on the north side of Cabin Creek that is addressed as 6304 Suicide Bridge Road, Hurlock and designated as Parcel 24 of Dorchester County Tax Map 12 (“Subject Property”). 

The Restaurant is currently served by a domestic well and an on-site septic system and tile disposal field. The tile disposal field is old and has difficulty handling the 5,000-6,000 gallon average daily wastewater flow. Summer season peak flows are approximately 9,000-10,0000 gallons per day. The owner is pumping and hauling the wastewater to the Town of Hurlock treatment plan during high flow periods to avoid any environmentally damaging overflows from the system. The existing system is not officially failing, but hydraulic failure would probably occur if regular pumping of the system ceases. The Dorchester County Health Department will not approve a renovation or replacement of the existing tile field due to limiting soil conditions and seasonal high groundwater throughout the Subject Property. 

The best permanent solution for this on-going wastewater problem is connection of Suicide Bridge Restaurant to a public wastewater collection and treatment system that can provide highquality treatment of all wastewater generated by the Subject Property. The wastewater treatment plant in the Town of Secretary does not have available capacity to treat the flow from the Restaurant. The Town of Hurlock is willing to permit the extension of municipal sanitary sewer service to the Subject Property. The owner of the Subject Property will be responsible for all costs to extend the service, which will be provided via a tri-plex low pressure grinder pump and an approximately 4.7 mile, 4" PVC forcemain connection to a Hurlock sanitary sewer system sewer manhole. The forcemain will be constructed within easements established for the benefit of the Subject Property and the Town of Hurlock along the alignment generally depicted by the attached service area map. The forcemain will be constructed with cleanouts and blow-offs and will meet design standards as specified in the 10 States Recommended Standards for Wastewater Facilities. The pump station and forcemain will be owned, operated and maintained by the Town of Hurlock at the expense of the property owner. Wastewater from the Subject Property will be collected in a series of septic tanks and grease traps and effluent only will be discharged to the tri-plex pumping station to be pumped to the Town of Hurlock. Solids from the septic tanks will be periodically pumped out and disposed of at the Hurlock wastewater treatment plant. 

As designated by the proposed map amendment, the forcemain is proposed for approval and construction as a denied access line that is sized and constructed to accommodate flows from the Subject Property. Pursuant to the service agreement between the owner of the Subject Property and the Town of Hurlock, access to the forcemain is limited to Subject Property, absent an amendment of the agreement by both parties and express approval by the County Council.
