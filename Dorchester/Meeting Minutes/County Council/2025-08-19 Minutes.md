#### County Council of Dorchester County Open Meeting Minutes August 19, 2025 

The County Council of Dorchester County convened into Open Session on August 19, 2025 at 5:00 p.m., in Room 110 of the County Office Building, 501 Court Lane, Cambridge, Maryland 21613. A roll call of the members was conducted. Present in Room 110 were: George L. Pfeffer, Jr., President; Mike Detmer, Vice President; Rob Kramer, Jr.; William V. Nichols; and, Ricky C. Travers. Also present in Room 110 were: Jerry Jones, County Manager; and, Irene Barnes, Executive Operations Specialist. Charles MacLeod, County Attorney, joined the meeting late. 

Upon motion by Councilman Travers, seconded by Councilman Nichols, the Council unanimously adjourned into Closed Session at 5:01 p.m., pursuant to the following sections of Title 3 of the General Provisions Article of the Maryland Annotated Code, §3-305(b)(1) to discuss the appointment, employment, assignment, promotion, discipline, demotion, compensation, removal, resignation, or performance evaluation of appointees, employees, or officials over whom it has jurisdiction; §3-305(b)(13) to comply with a specific constitutional, statutory, or judicially imposed requirement that prevents public disclosures about a particular proceeding or matter; and, §3-305(b)(7) to consult with counsel to obtain legal advice. The Written Statement for Closing a Meeting under the Open Meetings Act is attached hereto.

#### ANNUAL TRANSPORTATION MEETING DISCUSSION 

The Council added the following items to the list of concerns pertaining to various state roadway matters throughout the County, leading up to the annual meeting with Maryland Department of Transportation officials on September 16, 2025: 

- e Route 16 / Stone Boundary intersection options (signal, roundabout or striping change) e Noxious weeds on County/State Roads & Right-of-Ways e Eldorado Road / Puckum Road intersection flooding concerns

#### ACTION ITEMS 

REQUEST TO PROCEED - CELL 2 LANDFILL CONSTRUCTION DESIGN — SOLID WASTE 

Upon motion by Councilman Travers, seconded by Councilman Kramer, the Council unanimously approved the request of John Cooney, Solid Waste Manager, to proceed with a Service Order for Project No. NCP2025 through Geosyntec Consultants, Inc. for the construction design of the Beulah Landfill Cell No. 2, at the cost of $199,216.00, which will be funded by the Solid Waste FY26 Operating Budget. 

REQUEST TO AMEND CONTRACT — NEW BEULAH CELL CLOSURE — SOLID WASTE 

Upon motion by Councilman Travers, seconded by Councilman Kramer, the Council unanimously approved the request of Mr. Cooney to proceed with a Service Order for Project No. ME2604 through Geosyntec Consultants, Inc., to include design support, construction quality assurance management and field services, laboratory services and a certification report, for the closure construction of<sup>theBeulahlandfillcellentitled“NewBeulah,”whichisan</sup> additional cost of $679,294.00 to be funded by the Landfill closure account. 

In response to Councilman Pfeffer’s inquiry, Mr. Cooney said 1) the cells cannot be filled to capacity all at once, as it must be completed in layers, alternating between cells; 2) the current cell is expected to last through 2027, with a transition to Cell No. 2 in 2028; and, 3) there are eight total cells, with an estimated overall lifespan of 45 years. 

BID AWARD RECOMMENDATION~ PROCEED WITH PUBLIC HEARING — BOBTOWN ROAD FARM LEASE - PUBLIC WORKS 

Upon motion by Councilman Travers, seconded by Councilman Kramer, the Council acknowledged the recommendation of Matt Ozman, Public Works Director, to award the Bobtown Road Farm Lease to the highest bidder, Delmarva Native Plants, LLC., at $500 per acre and unanimously agreed to proceed with a public hearing. 

REQUEST TO PROCEED — TAR & CHIP ROADWAYS-~ PUBLIC WORKS 

Upon motion by Councilman Kramer, seconded by Councilman Nichols, the Council unanimously approved the request of Mr. Ozman to proceed with a current contract with American Paving Fabrics to tar and chip approximately 29.92 miles, over the course of the season, for an estimated cost of $788,634.65, which will be funded by the FY26 Operating Budget.

In response to Councilman Pfeffer’s inquiry, Mr. Ozman said: 1) all roadways within the request are re-tar and chip; 2) 29 miles will be completed in the fall, with additional roadways in the spring; and, 3) maximizing the total miles completed during one mobilization results in cost savings of up to $500,000 over the next several years, as each mobilization costs approximately $30,000. 

REQUEST TO SOLE SOURCE — AMBULANCE PURCHASE — EMERGENCY MEDICAL SERVICES 

Upon motion by Councilman Detmer, seconded by Councilman Kramer, the Council unanimously approved the request of Debbie Wheedleton, Emergency Medical Services Chief, to sole source with Atlantic Emergency Solutions (AES) the purchase of one 2027 Ford F-550 XLT 4X4 Diesel Road Rescue Ultramedic ambulance, at the cost of $409,011.00, which will be funded by the FY26 Capital Budget line item entitled, Purchase New Ambulance. 

The Council acknowledged that the sole source request is due to the following: 

- e The company will produce a customized and manufactured ambulance to the County’s specifications and needs; 

- e The County has purchased several ambulances through AES over the last several years; e There are no other local companies that can provide an ambulance with the specifications within the 36-month timeframe; and, 

- e They maintain the current ambulance fleet.

#### COUNTY MANAGER COMMENTS 

Mr. Jones asked all to be mindful of<sup>theeffectsof HurricaneErin,includingfloodinundation,</sup> and provided a map of potential flooding throughout the County. He said: 1) Public Works and Emergency Management will be on-call; 2) Ms. Wheedleton and Zach Phillips, EMS Deputy Chief, have repositioned EMS units throughout the County; and, 3) all hurricane related operations will be tracked for purposes of potential reimbursement. 

Councilman Pfeffer encouraged all to watch their local news stations, stay informed and heed all warnings given by knowledgeable sources.

### COUNCIL COMMENTS 

Councilmen Travers, Detmer, and Pfeffer emphasized the importance of attending the Maryland Association of Counties (MACo) conferences, noting that they provide valuable opportunities to engage directly with state officials and advocate on behalfof the County. 

#### PUBLIC COMMENTS 

In response to inquiries by Sharon Smith, resident, Councilman Pfeffer said: 1) the meeting with legislators, on September 16, 2025, will provide an opportunity for the County to express their concerns that may be addressed through legislative changes; and, 2) rural counties hold virtual meetings each Monday during legislative session, but this year they will begin in the fall, prior to the session beginning. 

Councilman Detmer added that the MACo conferences provide counties with an opportunity to recognize that, while challenges may differ in scale, they are often shared across all counties. 

#### ADJOURNMENT 

With no further business to discuss, and upon motion by Councilman Travers, seconded by Councilman Detmer, the Council unanimously adjourned the meeting at 7:04 p.m.
