This RFP will be for the single grinding of the Dorchester County Landfill's brush pile. The brush pile comes from Dorchester County
residents and tree service companies doing business in Dorchester County. All ground material stays at the landfill for use in
stabilization projects and as a traction aid during inclement weather. Since the woody material is not screened for diseases, it cannot
be sold to the public or landscape companies. Brush pile grinding is a landfill permit requirement.

The PROTECT Competitive Grant Program provides funding to plan for and make surface transportation more resilient to current and
future weather events, natural disasters, and changing conditions, such as severe storms, flooding, drought, levee and dam failures, sea
level rise, and extreme weather. Planning grants are available with no local cost share and can include studies, as well as engineering
design.
Many roads in southern Dorchester County become inundated during higher-than-normal high tides and storm events. Projected
changing conditions are anticipated to increase flooding occurrences and severity. Public Works could benefit from a complete roads
inventory identifying specific areas of vulnerability on individual roads, determining the source of flooding (i.e. - adjacent to a tidal
waterway, undersized culvert pipe, lack of stormwater drainage), providing solutions for the flooding, building a risk-based
vulnerability assessment, and prioritizing resiliency projects. A Transportation Resilience Improvement Plan (TRIP) as described in
23 USC Section 176 (PROTECT program) will provide this information based on existing and future conditions.
Public Works will have a priority project list to use in implementing roads improvements, and the TRIP will also be an important tool
for future state and federal funding requests. Included in the risk assessment and improvement prioritization will be information on
individual roads and the ability of emergency response during flooding events.
While the scope of work for the TRIP in this grant application will be limited to recommended improvements versus engineered
design for most roads, we are proposing that the grant fund full resilience design of two key sole-access roads - Elliott Island Road
and the Hoopers Island Road Causeway. Both of these roads have severe flooding and structural issues and they are the sole access to
the villages they serve. We are including engineering design costs for solutions to improve the resiliency of these roads, improve
access, and plan for changing conditions.
This project ties into the County's Hazard Mitigation Plan and more specifically, Goal 9 "Protect public infrastructure, especially
evacuation routes", and the related objectives to mitigate problem road sections, upgrade or replace stormwater management features,
and improve evacuation routes to mitigate life-threatening road conditions and road closures. In addition, Goal 6 recommends
ensuring that public funds are used in the most efficient manner by prioritizing new mitigation projects, starting with sites facing the
greatest threat to life, health and property.
The State of Maryland adopted a Transportation Resilience Improvements Plan in 2024, and under the PROTECT program, they
receive formula funding for mitigation initiatives. Each year, MDOT prepares a PROTECT Priority Resilience Project List of
mitigation projects that have been identified within the state as a priority for funding under the federal PROTECT program. This list
is then submitted to the Federal Highway Administration. After meeting last week with MDOT staff, we have received confirmation
that Dorchester County's TRIP project, including full design for Elliott Island Road and the Causeway, will be added to this year's
PROTECT Priority Resilience Project List. This will assist us with future PROTECT funding by reducing the local match on
Resilience Grants from 20% to 17%, 13% or 10% depending upon the available funding and project scope.
Community engagement is an important part of the preparation of a TRIP and the scope of work will include community meetings and
stakeholder participation. The grant application is due October 9th and we are still finalizing the application and budget. Our
preliminary budget is as follows:
Transportation Resiliency Improvement Plan - $750,000
(southern Dorchester County)
Full Engineered Design for Improvements to Elliott Island Road - $100,000
Full Engineered Design for Improvements to the Hoopers Island Road Causeway - $500,000

As the Acting Project Leader for the Chesapeake Bay National Wildlife Refuge Complex, I oversee the 
management of public lands in this region, most notably the Blackwater National Wildlife Refuge. 
Spanning over 33,000 acres, Blackwater is a sanctuary of global ecological importance and a massive 
economic driver for the Delmarva Peninsula. A majority of Blackwater’s acreage lies within a low-
lying, mapped floodplain, some of which is increasingly vulnerable to high tides, storm surges, sea level 
rise, and severe weather events.

now occurs on a routine basis during high tides and minor storm events—public access to our lands is 
compromised. This can create public safety hazards, disrupt federal land management operations, and 
inflicts immediate economic damage on the local tourism and outdoor recreation economies that rely on 
refuge visitors. 
 
The proposed Transportation Resilience Improvement Plan will serve as a vital, proactive mechanism to 
address these shared challenges. By evaluating current and future infrastructure vulnerabilities under 
changing environmental conditions, the TRIP will provide a data-driven plan for prioritizing capital 
investments. Improving these critical roadway corridors will ensure long-term, climate-resilient public 
access to these public lands, protecting both federal investments and the natural heritage of the

I.  PURPOSE AND ELIGIBILITY 
 
A.  It is the purpose of these grant guidelines, provided in accordance with the policies adopted 
by the Maryland Tourism Development Board (“MTDB”) on September 20, 1995, as amended 
and in concurrence with actions taken during a meeting on October 7, 1997, to govern the 
award of grants to Maryland political subdivisions to supplement local funds for tourism 
marketing and development. This program is known as the Destination Marketing Organization 
Grant Program and is administered by the Office of Tourism Development (“OTD”) on behalf of 
MTDB, an agency of the Department of Commerce (“Commerce” or “the Department”). 
By authority granted to MTDB under the Economic Development Article in the Annotated Code 
of Maryland, Section 4-213 (10) and (11), MTDB is to, “cooperate with other public units and 
private organizations to develop and promote the State’s tourism and travel industries” and 
“encourage, assist, and coordinate the tourism activities of local and regional promotional 
organizations.” Section 4-214 further provides that the MTDB “shall set policies for spending 
money on tourism advertising, written and graphic materials, cooperative and matching 
promotional programs, and other tourism and travel developmental and promotional activities 
for the State; spend money of the Fund to plan, advertise, promote, assist, and develop the 
tourism and travel industries in the State; and beginning in Fiscal Year 2012, provide grants of 
not less than $2,500,000 in total each fiscal year to destination marketing organizations for the 
purpose of attracting visitors to the State.” 
 
B.  Each Maryland County, Ocean City and the City of Baltimore is eligible for a Destination 
Marketing Organization (DMO) grant. No more than one DMO grant shall be awarded per 
jurisdiction. MTDB will only award grant funds directly to the DMO that is recognized by the 
chief elected official of the jurisdiction. 
 
C.  Each DMO shall participate in the Tourism Economic Impact Report for the State of 
Maryland and Maryland’s DMO’s. The annual cost for participation is eligible for reimbursement 
through this program as noted in Section VI.F.1. of these guidelines.

White Cane Awareness Day 
 
 
WHEREAS, the white cane, which every blind citizen of our County has the right to 
carry, demonstrates and symbolizes the ability to achieve a full and independent life and the

Introduction of amendments to Accessory Dwellings in Article IX § 155-50 (Supplementary Use Regulations) of the
 Dorchester County Code

DETAILED DESCRIPTION 
 
Revision Date: July 16, 2026
Susan Webb
9/29/2026
The proposed amendments to the Dorchester County Zoning Ordinance update the Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) provisions to
comply with new Maryland state legislation. The amendments establish standards for permitting ADUs on residential properties,
including requirements for location, size, and other applicable development standards. These changes are intended to bring the
County’s regulations into compliance with state law while providing additional housing opportunities and maintaining appropriate
local oversight of residential development.

<!-- page 141 -->
COUNTY COUNCIL
OF
DORCHESTER COUNTY, MARYLAND
BILL NO.
2026 -
AN ACT CONCERNING: Amendment of Chapter 155 (Zoning), Article IX § 155-50
(Supplementary Use Regulations) of the Dorchester County Code to Authorize and Regulate 
Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) in Conformance with Title 4, Subtitle 5 of the Land Use 
Article of the Annotated Code of Maryland.
WHEREAS, Title 4, Subtitle 5 of the Land Use Article of the Annotated Code of Maryland 
mandates that local jurisdictions with planning and zoning authority adopt local legislation 
authorizing the development of Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) on land zoned for single-
family residential use; and
WHEREAS, the County Council of Dorchester County, Maryland, seeks to promote diverse 
housing options while maintaining public health, safety, welfare, and structural consistency 
within existing residential communities; and
WHEREAS, the County Council desires to amend Chapter 155, Article IX (Supplementary Use 
Regulations) of the Dorchester County Code to provide for construction of Accessory Dwelling 
Units that meet public health, safety, and welfare standards.
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ENACTED AND ORDAINED by the County Council of 
Dorchester County, Maryland, as follows:
SECTION ONE. ENACTMENT OF SUPPLEMENTARY USE PROVISIONS BE IT 
ENACTED that a new Section 155-50QQ, entitled "Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs)," be and 
is hereby added to Chapter 155, Article IX of the Dorchester County Code to read as follows:
§ 155-50QQ. Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs).
(1)
Purpose. 
The purpose of this section is to provide standards for Accessory Dwelling Units that expand 
housing opportunities while preserving the rural character of Dorchester County, protecting 
public health and safety, and ensuring compatibility with surrounding development.

<!-- page 142 -->
(2)  
Applicability.
(a) One (1) Accessory Dwelling Unit may be established on any lot containing one lawfully 
established single-family detached dwelling. 
(b) Accessory Dwelling Units shall be considered accessory uses and shall not constitute a 
separate principal use. 
(c) Exclusion of Guest Cottages. Guest cottages established or regulated pursuant to Section 
155-50(OO) of this Chapter are distinct accessory structures and shall not be classified, 
permitted, or considered as Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) under this section.
(3)
Definitions.
Accessory Dwelling Unit means a secondary dwelling unit that is on the same lot, parcel, or 
tract as a primary single-family detached dwelling. 
(4)
Eligibility Requirements.
An Accessory Dwelling Unit may be approved only when: 
(a) The principal dwelling is a lawfully established single-family detached dwelling;
(b) There are no outstanding zoning, building, or code enforcement violations that would prevent 
the issuance of a zoning or building permit; and
(c) All applicable County, State, and federal permits and approvals required for the Accessory 
Dwelling Unit have been obtained. 
(5)
General Standards.
An Accessory Dwelling Unit shall: 
(a) remain subordinate to the principal dwelling; 
(b) remain located on the same lot as the principal dwelling; 
(c) not be subdivided or conveyed separately from the principal dwelling or lot;

<!-- page 143 -->
(d) not be counted toward residential density calculations; and
(e) contain complete independent living facilities. 
(6)
Location.
An Accessory Dwelling Unit may be located: 
(a) within the principal dwelling; 
(b) attached to the principal dwelling; 
(c) above or within an accessory structure; or
(d) in a detached accessory structure. 
(7)
Dimensional Requirements.
An Accessory Dwelling Unit shall: 
(a) comply with all minimum setback requirements applicable to accessory structures within the 
zoning district; 
(b) comply with the maximum building height permitted for accessory structures; 
(c) comply with maximum lot coverage requirements; and
(d) not exceed seventy-five percent (75%) of the gross floor area of the principal dwelling. 
(8)
Water Supply and Sewage Disposal.
(A) Where served by an individual well or on-site sewage disposal system, approval from the 
Dorchester County Health Department shall be required prior to issuance of any zoning permit or 
building permit. 
(B) Nothing contained herein shall authorize an Accessory Dwelling Unit where adequate water 
supply or sewage disposal cannot be demonstrated. 
(C) Approval of an Accessory Dwelling Unit shall not constitute approval of an existing well or 
septic system. Compliance with all applicable requirements of the Dorchester County Health

<!-- page 144 -->
Department and the Maryland Department of the Environment shall be demonstrated prior to 
issuance of any permit. 
(9)
Environmental Regulations.
An Accessory Dwelling shall comply with all applicable: 
(a) Floodplain regulations; 
(b) Chesapeake Bay Critical Area regulations; 
(c) Stormwater Management requirements; 
(d) Forest Conservation requirements; 
(e) Wetlands regulations; and
(f) all other applicable federal, State, and County environmental regulations. 
(10)
Permits Required.
Prior to occupancy, the applicant shall obtain: 
(a) Zoning Permit; 
(b) Building Permit; 
(c) Electrical Permit; 
(d) Plumbing Permit; 
(e) Mechanical Permit; 
(f) Health Department approval, where applicable; and
(g) Certificate of Occupancy. 
(11)
Design Standards.

<!-- page 145 -->
A detached Accessory Dwelling shall utilize exterior building materials, roof form, siding, and 
architectural features generally compatible with the principal dwelling to the extent reasonably 
practicable. 
(12)
Existing Regulations.
Except as specifically modified by this section, all other provisions of Chapter 155 relating to 
accessory structures, building permits, floodplain management, Critical Area regulations, 
subdivision requirements, stormwater management, and other applicable laws shall remain in full 
force and effect.
SECTION TWO: Be it further enacted that if any section, subsection, sentence, clause, phrase, or 
portion of this Ordinance or Section 155-50QQ is for any reason held invalid or unconstitutional 
by any court of competent jurisdiction, such portion shall be deemed a separate, distinct, and 
independent provision, and such holding shall not affect the validity of the remaining portions 
thereof.
SECTION THREE: And be it further enacted and ordained that all bills, ordinances, or parts 
thereof inconsistent with the provisions of this Bill are hereby repealed to the extent of such 
inconsistency.
SECTION FOUR: And be it further enacted and ordained that General Code Publishers is 
directed to codify the above amendments to Chapter 155 of the Dorchester County Code 
accordingly.
SECTION FIVE: And be it further enacted and ordained pursuant to § 308 of the Charter of 
Dorchester County, Maryland that promptly after enactment of this Act, the County Manager 
shall cause a fair summary of this Act to be published at least once in a newspaper of general 
circulation in Dorchester County, Maryland.
SECTION SIX: And be it further enacted and ordained that this Bill shall be known as Bill No. 
2026-___ of Dorchester County, Maryland and shall take effect sixty (60) days after its final 
passage.

Protecting existing airport infrastructure and maintaining safe access and operations throughout

vehicles throughout the project.

Re-striping and restoring all disturbed taxiway / taxilane markings with 6” wide yellow paint (no 
black borders or glass beads required) using waterborne paint meeting Federal Specification TT-B-
1235D, Type III, color code 33538 per Federal Standard No. 595 and at an application rate of 115 
sq.ft. per gallon.

pavement surface free of defects.

The newly expanded CRRF Committee met recently to review the status of the County's Cannabis fund account, discuss last year's
program, recommend a structure for a new grant cycle, and review a draft Request for Proposals. In accordance with MD Code
14.17.23 and the County's recently adopted CRRF Distribution Plan, the Committee also reviewed quantitative and qualitative data to
prioritize community needs. Highlights of these discussions included the following:
CURRENT CANNABIS FUNDS/NEW GRANT CYCLE: The balance of unobligated Cannabis funds totals $546,435.12 as of
September 25, 2026. The Committee recommends up to $360,000 be released for the new grant cycle, representing up to 8 grants at a
maximum of $45,000 each.
PRIORITIZING COMMUNITY NEEDS: The Committee reviewed a packet of reports addressing poverty rates, unemployment
percentages, school chronic absenteeism rates, housing statistics, homeless figures, local and state rates for alcohol and substance
abuse treatment, behavioral health statistics and incarceration numbers. The packet of reports included some portions of Moving
Dorchester Forward's recently completed community survey entitled, "Data Snapshot & Needs Assessment 2026" which provided
valuable community information. Based on this information, the CRRF Committee recommends the following as priority community
needs:
-- Homelessness
-- Programming for youth and young adults, focusing on ages 16 to 24
-- Mental health and trauma informed care
The Committee also recommends that grant applications addressing these specific themes receive an additional 5 points when scoring
the applications.
REQUEST FOR PROPOSALS (RFP): A few minor modifications were made to the last RFP to include language from the new
CRRF regulation, as well as to:
-- Require that this grant funding be accounted for separately from the organization's other transactions, and that a general ledger
report be submitted with the quarterly financial reports; and
-- Include in the narrative a discussion of how the organization plans to sustain the proposed activity after grant funding ends.
If approved, the RFP will be released October 7th and the application deadline will be November 9th at 3:00 p.m. The CRRF
Committee will review and score the applications and make recommendations to the Council at its December 1st meeting. The grant
performance period is recommended to be December 1, 2026 to November 30, 2027.
By approving the Request for Proposal, the Council approves the recommended funding for the new grant cycle, the priority
community needs, additional points for applications addressing those needs, and the timeline.

Background 
On November 8, 2022, Maryland voters passed Question 4 by ballot referendum, making it 
legal for individuals aged 21 and older to possess and consume cannabis in Maryland on or 
after July 1, 2023. Furthermore, on April 8, 2023, the Maryland General Assembly passed the 
Cannabis Reform Act (HB556/SB516) which allowed for cannabis sales to adults 21 and older 
from licensed dispensaries and established a framework for adult-use cannabis sales beginning 
July 1, 2023, including the conversion of existing medical cannabis business licenses to both 
medical and adult-use cannabis, and created the new Maryland Cannabis Administration to 
oversee both medical and adult-use programs. On May 2, 2023, the Governor of Maryland 
signed the Cannabis Reform Act into law. The Cannabis Reform Act establishes the Community 
Reinvestment and Repair Fund to provide financial support to community- based organizations 
that serve communities which were the most impacted through the disproportionate 
enforcement of the cannabis prohibition before July 1, 2022. Dorchester County has received a 
percentage of the Community Reinvestment and Repair Fund and on February 18, 2025 the 
County Council adopted Bill No. 2025-1 establishing a policy to govern how the fund may be 
used.  In July, 2026, the Joint Committee on Administrative, Executive, and Legislative Review 
approved COMAR 14.17.23 entitled, “Community Reinvestment and Repair Fund” which details 
how Cannabis funds may be used and sets forth requirements for distribution.  On September 
15, 2026, the Dorchester County Council approved the CRRF Distribution Plan at a public 
hearing.

Purpose 
The Dorchester County Council (the “Council”), with funding from the Community 
Reinvestment and Repair Fund utilizes the Request for Proposal (RFP), a competitive bid 
process, to select community-based organizations to provide community-based initiatives 
intended to benefit low- income communities, as well as disproportionately impacted areas, as 
defined by MD Code Ann., Alcoholic Beverages and Cannabis §36-101, and the administrative 
costs related to these programs. This RFP will provide guidance for the development and 
submission of proposals for these funds. 
Eligible applicants 
Community-based organizations are eligible to submit a proposal. A community-based 
organization is defined by COMAR 14.17.23 as “a nonprofit organization where community 
residents are involved in program design, implementation, and evaluation, often taking on 
leadership roles”.  For purposes of this document, community organizations are further defined 
as a public or private company, corporation, limited liability company, neighborhood group, 
organization, or partnership that provides educational, vocational education or rehabilitation, 
job training, health, or social services to individuals in designated communities. 
 
Further, bidders must be in compliance with the laws regarding conducting business in the 
State of Maryland and shall provide a “Certificate of Status” from the Maryland Department of 
Assessments and Taxation, evidencing that the business entity is in “good standing” with the 
State of Maryland.1 
 
An organization will not be considered if: 
x 
It has been debarred, suspended, or otherwise excluded from or ineligible to apply for

Information Regarding Swimming Pool Fence Requirements
Dorchester County Council Meeting 10/6/2026
Department of Planning and Zoning
A request has been made from a contractor for the County Council to consider removing the Zoning 
Code requirement in Chapter 155, Article IX, requiring fence enclosures for pools, in the event an 
automatic pool cover is provided. 
The below research was conducted and is being provided to Council for that consideration.
Public Safety 
The CDC identifies drowning as the leading cause of death among U.S. children ages 1–4. It reports 
that four-sided isolation fencing, separating a pool from the home and yard, is associated with an 
83% lower drowning risk than three-sided property line fencing. This statistic compares fencing
configurations.
A properly installed fence limits unsupervised access even when an automatic cover is open, not 
secured or unavailable. Covers provide an additional layer of protection when correctly closed, 
secured and functioning; they cannot cover a pool while it is being used. Fencing, covers and active 
adult supervision serve different protective functions.
Dorchester County’s Current Zoning Language
Chapter 155, Article IX, Supplementary Regulations for Fences and Walls, subsection (4)(d), states:
“A secure fence at least four feet in height shall enclose any in-ground swimming pool located within 
200 feet of any adjoining property line or public way.”
The County provision uses the specific term “fence.” The State adopted swimming pool code uses the
broader term “barrier”; barrier is not synonymous with pool cover.
Maryland Law and Code Language
COMAR 09.12.56 incorporates the 2024 International Swimming Pool and Spa Code (ISPSC), with 
Maryland amendments effective March 30, 2026. Under COMAR 09.12.56.02, the State standards 
generally apply to pools and spas for which a local jurisdiction receives a building permit application 
on or after December 31, 2023. Pre-existing operating pools generally are outside those standards 
unless later work involves a substantial and material structural change.
COMAR 09.12.56.05(B)(2) provides:
“Non-public swimming pools shall be surrounded by a barrier that complies with Sections 305.2.1 
through 305.7.”

<!-- page 315 -->
COMAR 09.12.56.05(A) amends ISPSC §305.1 so that a qualifying powered automatic safety cover 
does not eliminate the obligation to comply with the specified barrier provisions. COMAR 
09.12.56.05(B)(4) separately addresses qualifying non-public spas and hot tubs with lockable ASTM 
F1346 covers; that exception does not apply to ordinary residential swimming pools.
Important to note: Dorchester County must comply with the applicable minimum requirements established by 
Maryland law and COMAR. The County may also exercise its zoning authority to impose local requirements, 
provided those requirements do not conflict with State law, and are not lesser standards than the States. 
Physical Barriers Recognized by the State Adopted Code
ISPSC §305 addresses compliant fences, solid walls, qualifying mesh fencing, building wall 
arrangements and onground/aboveground pool wall arrangements. Approval depends on the 
applicable height, openings, climbing resistance, gate and access requirements. A product described 
as a “pool safety fence” is not automatically code compliant; the Building Official must review the 
proposed installation against the adopted provisions and Maryland amendments.
Published Requirements in other Maryland Counties
County: Published residential pool requirements
Anne Arundel: Fence, wall, building wall or approved barrier at least 48 inches high; self-closing and 
self-latching access gates.
Howard: Pool inspection criteria include a barrier at least 48 inches high, opening limits and self-
closing/self-latching pedestrian gates.
Montgomery: Private-pool enclosure provisions require an approved fence or wall; published 
requirements generally specify a five-foot enclosure for newer pools.
Frederick: County pool-permit guidance specifies a 48-inch barrier for qualifying pools, including 
inflatable pools at least 24 inches deep.
County requirements may appear in building, health or property codes rather than zoning ordinances. 
The comparison above summarizes published guidance; the applicable adopted text should control 
individual permit decisions.
Council Considerations and Recommended Action
x
Retain the existing local fence requirement rather than deleting an established life-safety 
provision.
x
Ask the Building Official and County legal counsel to reconcile the zoning language with 
Maryland’s current barrier provisions, including gates, approved physical enclosures and the 
treatment of existing pools.
x
Removing a local zoning provision would not waive independently applicable State pool-barrier 
requirements. If the County removed the zoning requirement for a fence, State Code would still

<!-- page 316 -->
be applicable. Local jurisdictions, by law, cannot reduce code requirements mandated by the 
State.
x
If the ordinance is revised, distinguish the County’s term “fence” from the State code’s broader 
term “barrier,” and specify which compliant physical enclosures are acceptable.
Summary & Recommendation for Council
The Department of Planning and Zoning supports retaining Dorchester County’s swimming pool 
fence requirement. A physical enclosure is an important, independent measure to restrict 
unsupervised pool access, particularly by young children. Automatic covers and alarms provide 
useful additional protection but should not replace a required fence or other approved physical 
barrier. Maryland’s current pool standards likewise require compliant barriers around non-public 
swimming pools, including those equipped with qualifying powered automatic safety covers. The 
Department recommends retaining the local requirement and reviewing its wording with the Building 
Official and legal counsel for consistency with State standards and applicability to existing pools.
