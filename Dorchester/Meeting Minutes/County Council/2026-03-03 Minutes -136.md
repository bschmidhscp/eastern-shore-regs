Open Meeting Minutes
March 3, 2026

The County Council of Dorchester County convened into Open Session on March 3, 2026 at
5:07 p.m., in Room 110 of the County Office Building, 501 Court Lane, Cambridge, Maryland
21613. A roll call of the members was conducted. Present in Room 110 were: George L. Pfeffer,
Jr., President; Mike Detmer, Vice President; and, Rob Kramer, Jr. Also present in Room 110
were: Jerry Jones, County Manager; Charles MacLeod, County Attorney; Irene Barnes,
Executive Operations Manager; and Grace Wittig, Executive Assistant. William V. Nichols was
absent. Ricky Travers joined the meeting late.

vote, Councilman Nichols and Councilman Travers absent, adjourned into Closed Session at
5:08 p.m., pursuant to the following sections of Title 3 of the General Provisions Article of the
Maryland Annotated Code, §3-305(b)(1) to discuss the appointment, employment, assignment,
promotion, discipline, demotion, compensation, removal, resignation, or performance evaluation
of appointees, employees, or officials over whom it has jurisdiction; §3-305(b)(13) to comply
with a specific constitutional, statutory, or judicially imposed requirement that prevents public
disclosures about a particular proceeding or matter; and, §3-305(b)(7) to consult with counsel to
obtain legal advice. The Written Statement for Closing a Meeting under the Open Meetings Act
is attached hereto.

REQUEST TO PROCEED — COUNTY WEBSITE — INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY (IT)
COUNCIL’S OFFICE

Upon motion by Councilman Travers, seconded by Councilman Kramer, the Council, by a4 to 0
vote, Councilman Nichols absent, approved the recommendation of Don Keyes, IT Director, and
Mr. Jones to proceed with transitioning the County website to a new host, CivicPlus at $42,500,
which will promote transparency through regional consistency, meet the federal Web Content
Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) Version 2.1 Level AA requirements, under Title II of the
Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), before the April 26, 2027 deadline, enhance mobile
accessibility, and increase security and risk mitigation specifically for government websites,
funded by the FY26 Contingency Fund.

The Council acknowledged that the implementation and launch of the new website will take
roughly six to eight months.

LETTER OF SUPPORT REQUEST ~ SENATE BILL 755 NATURAL RESOURCES -
STRIPED BASS RECREATIONAL SEASONS AND FISHERIES REGULATIONS
DELMARVA FISHERIES ASSOCIATION, INC.

Upon motion by Councilman Detmer, seconded by Councilman Kramer, the Council, by a 4 to 0
vote, Councilman Nichols absent, approved the request of Delmarva Fisheries Association to
provide a letter of support for SB 755 entitled, Natural Resources - Striped Bass Recreational
Seasons and Fisheries Regulations, for the purpose of prohibiting DNR from establishing a
recreational catch and release season for striped bass; requiring DNR to establish certain
recreational and charter boat summer and fall striped bass seasons and a trophy season for striped
bass; requiring DNR to complete and submit to certain State entities for review an economic
impact statement for any proposed fisheries regulations identified by DNR as having a major
impact on interested stakeholders; and generally relating to recreational fishing seasons for
striped bass and fisheries regulations.

Councilman Pfeffer stated that he and Councilman Kramer received a neutral response from
watermen, indicating that there may be some concerns that remain.

PUBLIC COMMENTS

Melissa Wright, representing Dorchester Educators, spoke on the importance of funding
education and supporting teacher salaries. She provided an overview of a typical day in the life
of a teacher and the many responsibilities involved in classroom operations. While emphasizing
the need to do what is best for students, she also highlighted the importance of supporting
teachers. She acknowledged state mandates but stressed the need to invest in teachers to ensure
there is an adequate workforce and requested the Council’s full support.

Deanna Green, resident, spoke about the need to support all school employees, noting that
educators include not only teachers but also cafeteria staff, bus drivers, office staff, and other
support personnel. She emphasized that many of these positions receive very low wages and
some still require state support in addition to their salary.

Shay Lewis-Sisco, City of Cambridge Commissioner, Ward Two, spoke about the significant
increase in property values and urged greater investment in the education system, stating it
should remain a top priority. She asked the Council to support Senate Bill 764, House Bill 1205
and House Bill 473. She also requested that Council meeting documents be made available on
the County website for individuals who may have difficulty hearing the proceedings.

Sharon Smith, resident, echoed Ms. Lewis-Sisco, and urged the Council to provide further
background information regarding the Council meeting agendas.
