LEGISLATIVE SESSION
INTRODUCTION - COUNTY INFRACTIONS

Legislation was introduced for the purpose of amending Chapters 65 (Building Code), 82
(Electrical Standards), 102 (Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration), 110
(Minimum Livability Code), 119 Plumbing), and 134 (Stormwater Management) of the
Dorchester County Code by providing that violations of each such chapters shall be a County
infraction and subject to a fine not to exceed $5,000.

‘Susan Webb, Planning & Zoning Director, provided additional information.

Upon motion by Councilman Travers, seconded by Councilman Detmer, the Council, by a 4 to 0
vote, Councilman Nichols absent, agreed to proceed with the publication of a public hearing.

INTRODUCTION - PROPERTY MAINTENANCE

Legislation was introduced for the purpose of repealing and reenacting Chapter 121
(Property Maintenance) of the Dorchester County Code for the purpose of adopting the
current edition of the international property maintenance code, with amendments, as the
property maintenance code for Dorchester County, Maryland.

Ms. Webb provided additional information.

Upon motion by Councilman Detmer, seconded by Councilman Travers, the Council, by a4
to 0 vote, Councilman Nichols absent, agreed to proceed with the publication of a public
hearing.

INTRODUCTION - WHARVES, DOCKS, MARINAS, PIERS, & HARBOR
FACILITIES, PUBLIC

Legislation was introduced for the purpose of amending chapter 150 (Wharves, Docks,
Marinas, Piers and Harbor Facilities, Public) of the Dorchester County Code by providing
that the County may take appropriate action to remove obstructions by manner of an
authorized private towing contractor.

Ms. Webb provided additional information.

RESOLUTION INTRODUCTION — MARYLAND ASSOCIATION OF COUNTIES
(MACO) POOLED OTHER POST-EMPLOYMENT BENEFITS (OPEB) TRUST
ADOPTION AGREEMENT - FINANCE

Upon motion by Councilman Travers, seconded by Councilman Kramer, the Council, by a 4 to 0
vote, Councilman Nichols absent, approved the request of Karen Tolley, Finance Director, to
introduce a Resolution to approve an adoption agreement for the MACo Pooled OPEB Trust
Fund for the purpose of accumulating and investing assets to fund other post-retirement benefits.

Mr. Jones expressed the importance of planning for the future, strengthening employee benefits,
and supporting retirees. He stated that he hopes to build upon the current plan in subsequent
years for further enhancement.

In response to Mr. Jones inquiry, Ms. Tolley explained that the non-pension benefits are owed to
our retirees and that this Resolution will strengthen the County’s financial standing. She said
almost every County on the Eastern Shore participates in similar pooled OPEB agreements.

COUNTY MANAGER COMMENTS

Mr. Jones reiterated the importance of the community completing the strategic planning survey.
He said that the deadline has been extended to February 24", and encouraged everyone to
participate, employees of the County and residents. He assured the survey is completely
anonymous and that the County is looking for concepts and ideas to better the community going
forward.

COUNCIL COMMENTS

Councilman Detmer: 1) encouraged participation in the surveys relating to the County’s strategic
planning process; 2) spoke in favor of the legislative bills introduced to amend Planning &
Zoning codes; 3) expressed that enhancing employee benefits is essential; 4) expressed his
appreciation for those that compiled the earmark funding recommendations; and, 5) stated that
Blue Crab Solar & Storage, LLC, (9782) said the Maryland Public Service Commission will holt
a public hearing in Linkwood, Maryland on February 18, 2026, which will also be livestreamed.

Councilman Pfeffer expressed his appreciation of the collaborative efforts between departments
to recommend projects for earmark funding, and extended his condolences to Addie Eckhardt on
the passing of her husband.

PUBLIC COMMENTS

David Beverley, resident, spoke in opposition of Legislative Session items A, B, and D. He
stated that raising the cap for County infractions could have a counterproductive effect on
residents and requested that the Council consider maintaining the current fees or develop a tiered
fee system.
