### August 4, 2026 

The County Council of Dorchester County convened into Open Session on August 4, 2026 at 4:34 p.m., in Room 110 of<sup>the CountyOfficeBuilding,501CourtLane,Cambridge,Maryland</sup> 21613. LEGISLATIVE SESSION 

INTRODUCTION — COUNTY COUNCIL COMPENSATION — COUNCIL’S OFFICE 

Ms. Barnes said for introduction is legislation to amend Chapter 10-Commissioners, §10-2Compensation, of the Dorchester County Code, to increase the annual salaries of the members of the County Council and the President of the County Council; providing that in accordance with Article III, Section 35 of the Maryland Constitution and Md. Code Ann., Local Gov’t § 10-302, such salary increases shall not apply to sitting members during their current term of office and shall take effect at the beginning of the following termofoffice. 

Upon motion by Councilman Detmer, seconded by Councilman Travers, the Council, by a 4 to 0 vote, Councilman Nichols absent, agreed to proceed with the publication of a public hearing. 

INTRODUCTION — ZONING AMENDMENT - TABLE OF PERMITTED USES BY ZONING DISTRICT — UNCLASSIFIED 

Ms. Barnes said for introduction is legislation to amend Chapter 155-Zoning, Attachment 1, Table of Permitted Uses by Zoning District, of the Dorchester County Code, to repeal the Unclassified category, which provides that non-commercial personal storage uses are permitted in B-1 Neighborhood Business, B-2 General Business, I-1 Light Industrial, and I-2 Heavy Industrial zoning districts. 

Upon motion by Councilman Travers, seconded by Councilman Kramer, the Council, by a 4 to 0 vote, Councilman Nichols absent, agreed to proceed with the publication of a public hearing. 

PUBLIC HEARING ~ BILL NO. 2026-8 - FARM BREWERIES & DISTILLERIES — PLANNING & ZONING 

Ms. Barnes said a public hearing is being conducted on Bill No. 2026-8 to provide an opportunity for public comments, prior to consideration of Bill No. 2026-8, to amend Chapter 155-Zoning and 155.50-Supplementary Use Regulations, of the Dorchester County Code, for the purpose of permitting farm breweries and farm distilleries. 

Councilman Pfeffer asked for public comments. 

William Layton, resident, spoke in favor of the zoning amendment.

PUBLIC HEARING ~ BILL NO. 2026-9 - RURAL RESIDENTIAL (RR) DISTRICT — MINIMUM LOT SIZE STANDARDS — PLANNING & ZONING 

Ms. Barnes said a public hearing is being conducted on Bill No. 2026-9 to provide an opportunity for public comments, prior to consideration of Bill No. 2026-9, to amend Chapter 155-Zoning, 155.33-Rural Residential District, and applicable district density and bulk regulations of the Dorchester County Code, for the purpose of updating the intent of the RR District and revising minimum lot size standards based on the availability of public community water and sewer services. 

Councilman Pfeffer asked for public comments. 

In response to an inquiry by Sharon Smith, resident, Susan Webb, Planning & Zoning Director, said the intent is to allow development on smaller lots where water and sewer are available to remain consistent with the State of Maryland, encourage development and preserve agricultural land. 

Councilman Pfeffer asked for additional public comments, hearing none, the public hearing was closed. 

Upon motion by Councilman Detmer, seconded by Councilman Kramer, the Council adopted Bill No. 2026-9 the roll call was: Pfeffer — aye; Detmer — aye; Kramer — aye; Nichols — absent; and, Travers — aye. 

Upon motion by Councilman Travers, seconded by Councilman Kramer, the Council, by a 4 to 0 vote, Councilman Nichols absent, adjourned into Open Session. 

## RECONVENE INTO OPEN SESSION 

## RESOLUTIONS 

## TEMPORARY DATA CENTER MORATORIUM 

Upon motion by Councilman Travers, seconded by Councilman Kramer, the Council, by a 4 to 0 vote, Councilman Nichols absent, adopted a resolution to establish a temporary moratorium on data centers in Dorchester County for a period of 12 months, effective immediately. 

The Council directed staff to provide a copy of the resolution to municipalities and encourage them to consider adopting a similar temporary moratorium.

## PROPOSED RESOLUTIONS — POLICY AMENDMENTS 

Upon motion by Councilman Travers, seconded by Councilman Kramer, the Council, by a 4 to 0 vote, Councilman Nichols absent, approved the request of Suzanne Hixon, Interim Human Resources Director, to post the following policies within County departments for 10-days to obtain employee comments, prior to consideration at its August 18, 2026 meeting: 

1. Social Media: To establish a personnel directive regarding the personal use of social media 

2. Uniform & Dress Code: To establish a standard operating procedure regarding consistent, safety-conscious standards for employee dress, grooming, and uniforms 

3. Americans with Disabilities Act: to amend and readopt a standard operating procedure to ensure that employees and applicants are assessed on the basis of their merit and performance, not on the basis of any disability 

- 4, Equal Employment Opportunities: To effectuate the principles and mandates of the anti-discrimination law 

5. Family & Medical Leave Act: To provide employees with a general description of their rights 

6. Sick & Safe Leave: To establish rules governing the accrual, use, administration, and limitations of sick leave for Dorchester County employees 

7. Volunteer: To establish standards for the recruitment, screening, supervision, and management of volunteers serving County departments 

## ACTION ITEMS 

REQUEST TO PROCEED - SALT AGREEMENT - STATE HIGHWAY ADMINISTRATION (SHA) - PUBLIC WORKS 

Upon motion by Councilman Kramer, seconded by Councilman Travers, the Council, by a 4 to 0 vote, Councilman Nichols absent, approved the request of Matt Ozman, Public Works Director, to enter into a Three-Year Salt Utilization Agreement to purchase additional salt from the SHA during snow events, as needed, in an amount not to exceed $51,236 for the period of September 1, 2026, through August 31, 2029, funded by the respective operating budgets. 

## COUNTY MANAGER COMMENTS 

Mr. Jones said, regarding bulkhead concerns, that the County, nor the City, has jurisdiction to install mooring balls in the Cambridge Creek, as this falls under the State of Maryland and federal government, however, there are discussions are being held, which the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers will need to be involved. 

In response to an introduction by Mr. Jones, Mr. Ozman said when the County is inundated with rain, the lead time for ditch drainage is 48 hours, and if standing water remains after this timeframe to contact Public Works to evaluate.
