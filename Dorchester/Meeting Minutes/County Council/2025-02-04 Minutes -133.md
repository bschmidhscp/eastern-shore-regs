County Council of Dorchester County
Open Meeting Minutes
February 4, 2025

The County Council of Dorchester County convened into Open Session on February 4, 2025 at
5:01 p.m., in Room 110 of the County Office Building, 501 Court Lane, Cambridge, Maryland
21613. A roll call of the members was conducted. Present in Room 110 were: George L. Pfeffer,
Jt., President; Mike Detmer, Vice President; Rob Kramer, Jr.; William V. Nichols; and, Ricky C.
Travers. Also present in Room 110 were: Jerry Jones, County Manager; Charles MacLeod,
County Attorney; and, Irene Barnes, Executive Operations Specialist.

Upon motion by Councilman Kramer, at 5:02 p.m., seconded by Councilman Nichols, the
Council unanimously adjourned into Closed Session, pursuant to the following sections of Title 3
of the General Provisions Article of the Maryland Annotated Code, §3-305(b)(1) to discuss the
appointment, employment, assignment, promotion, discipline, demotion, compensation, removal,
resignation, or performance evaluation of appointees, employees, or officials over whom it has
jurisdiction; §3-305(b)(13) to comply with a specific constitutional, statutory, or judicially
imposed requirement that prevents public disclosures about a particular proceeding or matter;
and, §3-305(b)(7) to consult with counsel to obtain legal advice. The Written Statement for
Closing a Meeting under the Open Meetings Act is attached hereto.

PROCLAMATION

NATIONAL OUTDOOR SHOW MONTH — FEBRUARY

The Council presented a Proclamation declaring February as National Outdoor Show Month.
PRESENTATIONS

BLACKWATER NATIONAL WILDLIFE REFUGE

Marcia Pradines Long, Complex Leader, Chesapeake Marshlands, provided information on
projects and happenings at Blackwater National Wildlife Refuge.

Ms. Long answered questions posed by the Council during the presentation.
CAMBRIDGE RESILIENCY INITIATIVE UPDATE - CITY OF CAMBRIDGE

C. Bucky Jackson, City Engineer, City of Cambridge, introduced Lawrence A. White, Make
Cambridge Resilient Project Manager, City of Cambridge, who presented an update on the Make
Cambridge Resilient Initiative, including the following:

1) A flood mitigation project along the Choptank River that extends from Cambridge Creek
to connect Pinks Pond;

2) Increasing the elevation up to seven feet;

3) Constructing berms in front of residents’ properties; and,

4) Using one third of Gerry Boyle Park for stormwater management.

Mr. White answered questions posed by the Council after the presentation.

The Council asked Mr. White to work with the property owner of Pinks Pond regarding a
proposed connection.

Upon motion by Councilman Travers, seconded by Councilman Nichols, the Council
unanimously adjourned into Legislative Session.

LEGISLATIVE SESSION

INTRODUCTION ~ 911 FEE INCREASE

Ms. Bames said for introduction is legislation to amend Chapter 85 — Emergency Telephone
Charge, § 85-1 of the Dorchester County Code by increasing the 911 fee for Dorchester County,
Maryland to $2.00 per month.

Upon motion by Councilman Travers, seconded by Councilman Detmer, the Council
unanimously agreed to proceed with the publication of a public hearing.

POLL CONFIRMATIONS

Upon motion by Councilman Travers, seconded by Councilman Detmer, the Council
unanimously confirmed its decision in the interim of meetings, via poll, to:

* Provide a letter of support for House Bill 222 (cross-filed with Senate Bill 175) entitled
“Public Safety - Corrugated Stainless-Steel Tubing for Fuel Gas Piping Systems -
Requirements and Prohibitions” for the purpose of prohibiting the sale, offer for sale,
transfer, or distribution of corrugated stainless steel tubing that does not meet certain
qualifications; requiring certain tubing used in the construction of fuel gas piping systems
in certain buildings to meet certain qualifications; and generally relating to corrugated
stainless steel tubing, by a 5 to 0 vote.

* Provide a letter of opposition for House Bill 321 (cross-filed with Senate Bill 303)
entitled “Pharmacy Benefits Managers ~ Definition of Purchaser and Alteration of
Application of Law” for the purpose of altering the definition of "purchaser" for the
purpose of certain provisions of State insurance law governing pharmacy benefits
managers to exclude certain nonprofit health maintenance organizations; and repealing
certain provisions that restrict applicability of certain provisions of law to pharmacy
benefits managers that provide pharmacy benefits management services on behalf of a
carrier, by a 4 to 0 vote.

BAY RESTORATION FUND WASTEWATER & SUPPLEMENTAL ASSISTANCE GRANT
APPLICATIONS — MDE - WATER & WASTEWATER

Upon motion by Councilman Travers seconded by Councilman Kramer the Council unanimously
approved the request of Marilyn Williams, Grant Writer, on behalf of Peter McElroy, Water &

COUNCIL’S COMMENTS

Councilman Travers expressed his condolences to the family of the late Jeff Hurley and noted
Mr. Hurley’s dedication to the community.

Councilman Detmer: 1) said a Choptank Solar and Storage Open House will be held on February
5, 2025 from 4:00 p.m. ~ 7:00 p.m. at the Packing House; 2) suggested that the Council firm up a
list of solar recommendations to present to the Planning Commission; 3) said the Comptroller's
Office had an internal 1099 printing issue; and, 4) expressed that there are massive budget
challenges underway.

Councilman Pfeffer: 1) reiterated Councilman Detmer, adding that, preliminarily, the State is
asking for an additional $3-4 million, increasing the Board of Education (BOE) funding mandate
to $27 million, which may result in a 10% property tax increase; 2) said the BOE already has a
$10 million shortfall which totals to roughly $14 million in deficits 3) stated that employee
reductions were implemented years ago, however, further cuts are not possible as the County is
already operating with minimal staff; 4) welcomes all budget suggestions, which can be sent to
infx@docogonet.com; and, 5) highlighted that these mandates must be paid, otherwise the State
withhold income taxes from the County, causing additional financial shortfalls.

PUBLIC COMMENTS

Kathleen Abbott, resident, said the National Outdoor Show will be held on February 21-22,
2025.

Sharon Smith, resident, suggested that residents reach out to the local legislators by mail and
phone to express their budget concerns. Council encourages the community to send letters.

Morgan Tolley, resident, suggested putting the legislators’ addresses on social media.

Mr. Jackson suggested revisiting the necessity of all facilities and consolidating schools.
Councilman Pfeffer said there are 4,500 students in the County, and they are brainstorming ideas
with the BOE, but do not want to put the kids at risk, He said the County cannot afford the
Blueprint for Maryland’s Future.
