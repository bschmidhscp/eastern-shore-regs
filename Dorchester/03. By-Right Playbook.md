# Dorchester By-Right Playbook

Cites: "Code" = Dorchester County Code in `Code/`; "Att. 2" = § 155 Attachment 2 (`Documents/Code/Attachments/DO0950-155b Density Lot Size Bulk.md`); "Res. 695" = FY27 fee schedule; "State Summary" = `01. Maryland/00. Summary.md`. Code text is current only through 06-03-2025; Bill 2026-9 (RR lot sizes, adopted 2026-08-04) and a density-table amendment are not codified.

## 1. Bottom line

The path is a **Minor Subdivision: up to 7 lots from a parcel as it existed on April 1, 1995** [§ 140-6]. Preliminary and final plat can be run together, and after every agency signs, **the Planning Commission Chairman can sign the final plat without a Commission vote** (he may instead send it to a regular meeting) [§ 140-19.B; § 140-21.D(1)]. **No public hearing and no adjoining-owner letters, sign posting or published notice were found anywhere in Chapter 140 for a minor.** The one exposure is the **Planning Commission agenda**: the Director "shall present" a preliminary plat to the Commission at its first regular meeting, and sketch plans go to the Commission too [§ 140-17.C; § 140-14.A(2)]. Typical time to a recorded plat is about **4 to 6 months (estimate; no minor-subdivision timing data exists in the Planning Commission digest, which has 0 subdivision items)**.

## 2. Eligibility test

Pass every line to stay on the path.

- [ ] **Lot count.** 7 or fewer lots total, counted from the single lot, tract or parcel existing 4/1/1995, "all at one time or lot by lot" [§ 140-6]. Eight or more is a Major (sketch, preliminary, Commission votes, guaranty) [§ 140-11.B; § 140-21.D(2)]. The remainder counts toward the 7 if it is a lot; residue land of 3 acres or more can be shown and used later but is not an approved lot [§ 140-6 "Residue land"].
- [ ] **Prior subdivision history.** Plat must list every division of the parent since 3/15/1972 [§ 140-16.B(1)(i); § 140-20.B(1)(h)]. Pull the chain of title to 4/1/1995 first; lots already carved out since then consume the 7.
- [ ] **Density (the real cap).** Zoning density, not the 7, usually binds. AC pre-1972 parcels: 3 units plus 1 per 15 additional acres (1 per 10 clustered). 1972-95 AC lots: under 10 ac 2 units; 10-20 ac 3; over 20 ac 3 plus 1 per 15. AC-RCA, RC, RR-C, RR-RCA, SR-RCA: 1 per 20 ac. **RR and SR show no density cap ("n/a"), only lot size** [Att. 2]. RR and SR are therefore the target districts.
- [ ] **Lot size.** RR 40,000 sf (20,000 clustered; cluster in RR needs shared water and sewer [§ 155-34.C(2)(d)]). SR 20,000 sf unserved, 15,000 with sewer, 10,000 with water and sewer. AC 40,000 sf. Village 10,000 to 20,000 [Att. 2]. Bill 2026-9 may have cut RR sizes where community water/sewer exist; **text not in corpus, confirm**.
- [ ] **Width and frontage.** Min lot width at front lot line on a local road 100 ft (AC, RR; 80 ft SR, RR-RCA clustered); minor collector 140 ft; limited access or major collector 200 ft; 100 ft at the building line in AC/RR [Att. 2]. Front setback 40 ft local, 60 ft minor collector, 100 ft major collector (AC/RR).
- [ ] **Septic tier.** Under State law (Env. § 9-206(g)) Tier IV (all AC/RC land) allows a minor subdivision on septic only, so the 7-lot minor is the ceiling; Tier III (RR, Village per the 2021 plan) allows a minor on septic [State Summary § 2; 2021 CompPlan p.40]. Tier map is described as "draft to be adopted"; adoption status **not found in corpus**. Every lot needs a Health Department-approved septic area (10,000 sf for initial plus two replacements) [§ 140-27; COMAR 26.04.02.04F].
- [ ] **Critical Area.** RCA lots yield 1 per 20 ac (no variance); LDA/IDA per local program. Anything needing growth allocation leaves the path (see § 6) [§ 68-8.C; § 68-12; State Summary § 3]. Buffer 100 ft minimum, and 200 ft from tidal water for new RCA subdivisions [State Summary § 3]. Forest conservation does not apply to subdivisions entirely inside the Critical Area [§ 140-59.A], but Critical Area review does.
- [ ] **No new road.** Frontage on an existing public road for every lot. Any private road serving more than one lot must meet public-road design standards and have a maintenance entity, which is a new road in practice [§ 140-26.A(2)(b), (d)]. Public rural-section roads serve up to 10 lots [§ 140-26.A(1)(b)].
- [ ] **Access rules.** Road class governs driveways [§ 140-26.C]: major collector, lots created after 10/1/1996 must reach it by a minor collector or local road; minor collector, only 2 new lots per parent get direct driveways and the third and later must use a local road; local roads unrestricted except in cluster (two direct driveways, third lot needs a new road). **Flag/panhandle lots are not addressed by name.** What exists: a **single interior lot** may use an easement, or a shared driveway if the Commission approves; additional interior lots need a public or private road; two lots may share a driveway if the Commission or (for minors) the Chairman finds it furthers the intent [§ 140-26.A(2)(c); § 140-26.C(1)(f)]. Plat must carry "vehicular egress and ingress is restricted" where needed [§ 140-26.C(1)(g)].
- [ ] **Right-of-way dedication.** Up to 25 ft from centerline of an existing county road is required as a subdivision condition [§ 140-26.B(6)].
- [ ] **No variance or waiver needed.** Setback/width variances go to the Board of Appeals; subdivision waivers go to the Commission with certified-mail notice (see § 6) [§ 140-46].
- [ ] **Not an exempt transfer used as a lot.** Agricultural divisions (5+ ac tract and 5+ ac remainder, no dwelling) and adjoining/exchange plats skip full review but the parcel is "not an approved building lot" [§ 140-4.A(2), (3)]. They do not deliver sellable lots for a builder.

## 3. Step-by-step path

Fees are FY27 [Res. 695 pp.10-12]. Durations are estimates unless cited.

1. **Control and diligence (2 to 6 weeks, estimate).** Option the land. Order title chain back to 4/1/1995 and 3/15/1972 for the subdivision history list. Check zoning, Critical Area map, flood map, comp-plan tier. Who: attorney, surveyor.
2. **Pre-application conference with the Director (1 to 2 weeks).** Optional but "encouraged" for minors; sketch plan is mandatory only for majors [§ 140-11.B]. Ask the open questions in § 7. Fee: none found.
3. **Field work, in parallel (4 to 10 weeks, estimate).**
   - Boundary survey and topography (2-ft contours if required) [§ 140-16.B(2)]. Parcels 25 ac or larger never on a plat may show the boundary from deed if the Director waives a full survey [§ 140-16.B(2)(a)[2]].
   - **Soils and perc:** Health Department perc testing; where soils have high seasonal water tables, tests must be done when the water table is highest. Shore wet-season window is about **Feb through April** (State Summary § 2; COMAR 26.04.02.04B). Missing the window can add 6 to 10 months. County Code § 131-3 lists perc/subdivision soil evaluation at **$400 per individual site or $250 per subdivision site** (shared facility 4 lots or fewer $600).
   - Nontidal wetland and stream delineation, Forest Stand Delineation, habitat check (DNR) [§ 140-16.B(2)(c); § 140-20.B(2)(f)].
4. **Forest conservation (Planning staff; 2 to 4 weeks, run with step 5).** Applies to subdivisions of 40,000 sf or more outside the Critical Area; afforest to 20% (agricultural/resource) or 15% (other); cleared forest below that is replanted at 2:1; clearing thresholds 50% AC/RC/RR-C, 25% RR, 20% SR/V [§ 140-63; Table 140-64]. Exempt: child/grandchild lots with a 5-year declaration, or fully within the Critical Area [§ 140-59.A, E]. FSD $250 (simplified/intermediate) or $500 (full); Forest Conservation Plan $500; declaration of intent $50; replacement fund $0.10/sf [Res. 695 p.12]. State law now requires 1:1 replacement of cleared acres (since 1 Jul 2026) [State Summary § 1]; county code text says 2:1 below threshold, confirm which governs.
5. **Application: combined preliminary and final plat to the Director (submit week 0 of formal review).** Items: signed application, plat (18x24 or 24x36 mylar, scale 1"=50/100/200'), seven or nine preliminary copies, survey, lot layout, septic and well locations, development-rights statement, subdivision history, right-to-farm note in RC/AC/AC-RCA/RR-C [§ 140-16.A, B(1)(k); § 140-19.B; § 140-20]. Fee: **$500 up to 4 lots, plus $300 per additional lot, plus recording** [Res. 695 p.11] (wording ambiguity noted in § 5). Critical Area review $50 [Res. 695 p.10].
6. **Completeness check (immediate; Director).** Incomplete returned in writing [§ 140-17.A; § 140-21.B]. Within 5 days of a complete final submission the Director distributes to County Engineer/Roads Board, Health Department, Soil Conservation District, Sanitary Commission if applicable, others [§ 140-21.C].
7. **Planning Commission look at preliminary (next regular meeting, within 1 month of receipt).** The Commission evaluates and informs the applicant in writing; approval is conditional and good for 1 year [§ 140-17.C-D; § 140-18]. This is the only Commission appearance for a minor. Because preliminary and final may run together, ask staff whether the combined minor is handled at one meeting or on the Chairman's signature only (open question 1).
8. **Agency sign-offs on the final plat (4 to 10 weeks, estimate).** Signature blocks: Planning Commission, Health Department, Roads Board, Soil Conservation District, Sanitary District and Recreation and Parks Board if applicable [§ 140-20.B(1)(f)]. Health Department signs only after perc/site approval; State rule requires MDE (in practice health department) signature before recording [COMAR 26.04.03.02K]. Soil Conservation District sediment/erosion and stormwater: **stormwater management is exempt if land disturbance is 5,000 sf or less** [§ 134-6.C]; lot-by-lot disturbance on a plat-only project is normally below that, since we pull no building permits, but ESD for the roads/driveways would apply if disturbed above 5,000 sf (permit $300 standard / $800 engineered, Res. 695 p.12).
9. **Chairman signs the minor final plat** after all agencies sign, without the full Commission, unless he elects to bring it to a meeting [§ 140-21.D(1)]. Improvement guaranty (120% surety) applies only where improvements (roads, utilities) are required [§ 140-36]; with lots on an existing road it should not be triggered.
10. **Recording.** The Commission causes the plat to be recorded with the Clerk of the Circuit Court for Dorchester County, then sends a letter of approval and the recording receipt to the applicant [§ 140-7.C-D; § 140-8]. Recording fee: **not in corpus**. Deadline: the final plat must be submitted within 1 year of preliminary approval or approval is void; the Director can extend for minors [§ 140-21.A; § 140-46.A].
11. **Sell lots.** Per State rule, lots cannot be sold or built on until MDE has the plat and water/sewer method [Env. § 9-206(j)] and the plat must state that the minor subdivision and its remainder cannot be further subdivided in Tiers II to IV [Env. § 9-206(l)].

## 4. Public notice

- **Minor subdivision (the path): no adjoining-owner letters, no sign posting, no published notice and no public hearing are required by Chapter 140.** Searches of the corpus for "notice", "adjoining", "posting" and "sign" against the Code found no subdivision notice rule other than § 140-46.B (waivers) [corpus search 2026-10-13; § 140-14 to § 140-21].
- **Planning Commission agenda: yes, at least once.** Sketch plan goes to the Commission for "review, recommendations and approval" [§ 140-14.A(2)], and preliminary plats are presented at its first regular meeting [§ 140-17.C-D]. A regular-meeting agenda is posted under the Open Meetings Act; it is not a mailed or hearing notice. The Chairman's signature path on the final avoids a second agenda [§ 140-21.D(1)].
- **Variants that avoid more notice:** skip the optional sketch plan (minors may), run preliminary and final together [§ 140-19.B], and design so no waiver is needed (waivers require certified-mail notice to immediate owners at least 7 days before the meeting [§ 140-46.B(2)(a)]).
- **Notice that does exist elsewhere:** variances and special exceptions carry advertising costs and a Board of Appeals hearing (fees in Res. 695 p.11) and growth allocation and rezoning require hearings; none apply on the path.

## 6. Off-ramps

| Trigger | Result | Design around it |
|---|---|---|
| 8th lot from the 4/1/1995 parent | Major: mandatory sketch, Commission votes, guaranty [§ 140-6; § 140-11.B; § 140-21.D(2)] | Stay at 7 including any remainder lot and prior splits |
| Tier IV land (AC/RC) with major | Septic majors barred by State law [Env. § 9-206(g)] | Buy RR/SR (Tier III) |
| Density table yields fewer lots than 7 | Cannot create lots without density | Run density math first (AC 3 + 1/15 ac; RCA 1/20) [Att. 2] |
| New road or private road serving 2+ lots | Public-road design standards; effectively major scope [§ 140-26.A(2)(b)] | Only existing frontage; at most one interior lot via easement or two lots via shared driveway |
| Direct access onto major/minor collector beyond allowed | Lot must use a local road [§ 140-26.C(3)-(4)] | Choose local-road frontage |
| Subdivision waiver or modification | Commission vote plus certified-mail notice to neighbors [§ 140-46.B] | Meet every standard as written |
| Zoning variance (width, setback, area) | Board of Appeals hearing, advertising [Res. 695 p.11] | Lot sizes with margin over Att. 2 |
| Special exception / rezoning / text amendment | Hearing, Council action [Res. 695 p.12] | Stay in permitted residential use |
| Critical Area growth allocation | 300-ft tidal setback, Commission, Council and State approval, $1,200 [§ 68-12; Res. 695 p.12] | Avoid RCA; buy outside 1,000 ft |
| Water and sewer plan amendment (for sewered density/lot size) | Council action; $200 plus ad [Res. 695 p.12] | Use unserved lot sizes, septic |
| Perc failure / wet-season timing | Lots lost or delayed [COMAR 26.04.02.04] | Test in Feb to Apr; option contract with perc contingency |
| Final plat not submitted within 1 year of preliminary | Approval void, new rules apply [§ 140-21.A] | Calendar the date; request Director extension [§ 140-46.A] |
| Prior subdivisions consumed the cap | Fewer lots than expected | Order subdivision history early |
| Pending law change | Bill 2026-9 (RR lot sizes) and density-table amendment (PC 2026-05-06; Council 2026-04-21) not codified [Council digest] | Read the bills before pricing |

## 7. Open questions to ask staff

1. For a combined minor preliminary/final, does the Commission still vote at a regular meeting, or can the Chairman sign on staff review alone? Is there an agenda posting with owner notice?
2. Current Tier map status and whether Dorchester ever received a Tier IV exemption (AC yields above 1 per 20 ac).
3. RR and SR minimum lot size and frontage after Bill 2026-9, and what changed in the Attachment 2 density table.
4. Fee reading: does "$300 per additional lot" start after lot 4 (7-lot = $1,400) or after lot 1? Also the Circuit Court recording fee and Health Department perc/plat-review fees.
5. Flag/panhandle lots and interior-lot rules: is a lot with a 25 ft access strip acceptable, and is the "100 ft at front lot line" met at the strip?
6. Actual elapsed time for the last few minor subdivisions (no data in corpus), and whether the building excise tax is suspended for FY27.
