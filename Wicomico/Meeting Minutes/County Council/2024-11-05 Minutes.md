Public Hearing: Legislative Bill No. 2024-09 — To Amend Chapter 90 of the Wicomico County Code titled “Tax Exemptions” to add a new Section 90-2 titled “Airplane Landing Strips Tax Exemption” to exempt from County Real Property Taxation the Essential Portions of Improved Airline Landing Strips as certified by the Maryland Aviation Administration.

Public Hearing: Legislative Bill No. 2024-10 — To Amend Chapter 90 of the Wicomico County Code titled “Tax Exemptions” by repealing in its entirety Section 90-7 titled “Hotel or Multifamily Residential Development Property Tax Credit.” 

Mr. Benson said this is better known as the Horizon tax credit that was adopted by the County in 2021. He explained, at the time it was adopted, there was a section or provision in the section that said this

would be reevaluated every three years for renewal or reevaluation. He said the three years is up as of now, so the reason for this being on the agenda is to comply with the current code to allow the Council to reevaluate this. He said, if adopted in its current form, this would repeal the existing language. 

Mr. Cannon opened the Public Hearing. 

Mr. Michael Weisner came to the podium and said I reside in Wicomico County and am here to ask that you repeal the hotel and multifamily residential development property tax credit commonly referred to as Horizon. He said a number of years ago the State of Maryland along with most jurisdictions in the State adopted a policy called Growth Pays for Growth. He said this public policy called for all new developments to not only cover the direct costs of their project, but also to pay impact fees to cover the indirect expenses created by these projects, such as expanded school buildings, new community parks, and utility expansions, such as sewer and water capacity upgrades. He said over time, this policy faded away mostly due to the fact that these large impact fees stymied growth excessively. He said now the pendulum has swung too far in the opposite direction and the Horizon tax credit is a prime example of that. He said in addition to developers not having to pay for offsite infrastructure costs these projects incur, this tax credit relieves them of responsibility from paying even their fair share of their property tax bills, taxes that are needed to support the additional services that will be used by the residents that occupy these new structures. He said waiving real estate taxes doesn’t remove the demand for public services required by new residents in these developments, the County is still obligated to provide all the usual County services, the major cost being educating the school-aged children who live in our community that will occupy these units. He said the Horizon program transfers these costs to all other taxpayers in the County who have to make up for the slack that these tax credits have been given away to the developers. He said for a long time this Council and the ones preceding have been good stewards of the monies entrusted to their care, but this tax credit program is out of keeping with that tradition. He said it is time to sunset Horizon, or, should I say, I would like to see the sun set on the Horizon. 

Ms. Carolyn Wagamuth came to the podium and said I am a resident of the City of Salisbury and I am here tonight to ask you to sunset the Horizon program. She said Wicomico County taxpayers are unduly burdened by the Horizon tax credit program to greedy developers. She then asked, whatever happened to living within your means? She said I do that, other taxpayers do that, so why shouldn’t the developers do the same? She said, if they can’t afford to build something then they just shouldn’t build it. She said all of these items are dependent on current taxes, not taxes years down the road, we should all live so long. She said the Horizon tax credit has no requirement for developers to build anything affordable, no affordable housing. She said luxury housing is how they are terming it, but luxury housing will not solve our affordable housing crisis, it will do the opposite and it will not bode well for homeownership in our County. She said developers are building primarily rental homes, rental townhouses, and rental apartments. She said sadly, even those who espouse to be advocates for affordable income housing are falling for this false narrative and its fuzzy math. She said the Horizon tax program is simply a giveaway to developers, a way to line developers’ pockets with taxpayer dollars. She said the County and its citizens are not benefiting from this.
