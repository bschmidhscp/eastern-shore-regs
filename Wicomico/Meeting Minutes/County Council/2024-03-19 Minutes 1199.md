Robert A. Benson, Esg., Council Attorney
Public Hearing: Legislative Bill No. 2024-01: An Act to Amend Chapter 90 of the Wicomico County Code
titled “Tax Exemptions,” Section 90-9, to allow a property tax credit to be granted to an auxiliary of a
bona fide volunteer fire company, and to provide that only one tax credit is allowed per dwelling.
Mr. Benson said
I know the Council knows this, but for the benefit of the public, in December the
Council adopted a tax credit provision for volunteer fire associations within Wicomico County and
provided a mechanism to allow that to happen that is provided for under Maryland law. He said the
original contained a definitional section for what would be considered a member of a volunteer fire
1

<!-- page 2 -->
company. He said, since its adoption, a question arose as to whether an auxiliary of a volunteer fire
department was included within that definitional section. He said, in looking at it and in speaking to a
number of different volunteer fire companies, we got a mixed answer as to whether auxiliary members
were considered members of the volunteer fire companies. He said some said they were and some said
they weren’t, so the purpose of this is to clean up any discrepancy to include auxiliary members as being
part of the group that would be entitled to a tax exemption. He clarified, they have to meet the same
conditions that a volunteer firefighter has to meet, specifically, they have to earn 50 points and those 50
points are pursuant to the definitional section of the Wicomico County Volunteer Firefighters
Association, which follows the Maryland Volunteer Firefighters Association rules.
Mr. Benson said, when this was adopted, Ms. Oland from the Finance Office suggested that the
application process include proof that the person making the application meets the 50-point
qualification that is required when they get a tax exemption from the State of Maryland for income
taxes, so, as part of that, they have to meet that definitional section.
Mr. Benson said the other purpose of this is to clarify that, if there is more than one member who owns
a property, the County is only granting one tax exemption per property.
Mr. Benson said that is the purpose of this, but there was an email that came in about this. He
explained, the original provision that was adopted established a maximum amount per year of
$750,000. He said that was done because it was not readily apparent how many volunteer fire members
there would be or what the total impact could be on the budget for the County, so when the Council
adopted this they elected to put in a maximum dollar amount so that issue is taken care of. He clarified,
I mention this because
I know the Council got an email and
I just wanted to make sure that we address
the issues that were raised in the email.
