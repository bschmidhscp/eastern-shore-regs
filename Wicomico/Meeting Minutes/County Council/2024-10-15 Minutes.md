Introduction: Legislative Bill No. 2024-09 — To amend Chapter 90 of the Wicomico County Code titled “Tax Exemptions” to add a new Section 90-2 titled “Airplane Landing Strip Tax Exemption” to exempt from County Real Property Taxation the Essential Portions of Improved Airplane Landing Strips as certified by the Maryland Aviation Administration. 

Mr. Benson said this was specifically raised at a prior meeting on behalf of Bennett Airport located on Naylor Mill Road, and this recognizes that private landing strips are a benefit to the County and the State and would enable property that has a private landing strip to a tax exemption. 

Mr. Andrew Illuminati, Esq., came before Council and said, as Mr. Benson said, this is an exemption that is allowed by State Law when an entity meets four requirements — it is located on privately owned land, used by the public, licensed under the Transportation Article, and, most importantly, it is identified in the Maryland Aviation System Plan as essential to the State’s air transportation system. He said the process to determine which portions of the land are exempt is all set by State Law, but the County needs to allow for this exemption if it is going to allow that airport to take advantage of it. 

Mr. Holloway clarified, just a portion of the property, to which Mr. Illuminati responded, the essential portions of the airplane landing strip because that way, if someone has 1,000 acres but only a little area is used, only the area used would receive the benefit. Mr. Holloway clarified, it wouldn’t be the whole property, to which Mr. Illuminati responded, correct.

She said this plan was developed through a process through the National Association of City and County Health Officials as well as the standards and measures from the Public Health Accreditation Board (PHAB). She said we have completed our first full year of the five-year cycle, and our current plan has four major strategic priority areas, and those include health improvement, communication, workforce, and technology. She said each year the Health Department publishes an annual report that provides updates on these strategies and programmatic activities under each of these priority areas, and today’s presentation provides a brief highlight from this report, but we encourage you to fully review this on our website at your earliest convenience. 

Ms. Gray said the first area is health improvement, and this work is addressed through our Community Health Improvement Plan (CHIP). She said every three years, the Health Department, in addition to Somerset County Health Department and Tidal Health, do a comprehensive data dive into all of the health outcomes that may be changing and priorities for the County. She said this includes secondary data, which was a lot of what we went over in the May Board of Health meeting, but we also do primary data collection through focus groups and key informant interviews. She said we have actually just started the next three-year cycle for this data and planning process, so over the next few months we will probably encourage your constituents to fill out our upcoming resident survey as well as participate in some of these focus groups. She said our current plan will end this fiscal year and includes three areas — access and health equity, behavioral health, and chronic disease and wellness. She said each of these areas now has a dedicated subcommittee to perform work with our Wicomico County Local Health Improvement Coalition (LHIC). 

Ms. Gray said, when you look at some of the data for access and health equity, the major strategies are increasing access to healthcare overall, increasing health literacy, as well as addressing the social determinates of health. She said we won’t focus on all of these data points explicitly, but a few key indicators under this umbrella as we walk through health improvement. She said, when we look at the percentage of those uninsured and those individuals in poverty in the County, we haven’t seen much change; however, we have seen a pretty sizable increase in the median household income in Wicomico County as well as an increase in the percentage of high school graduates. 

Ms. Gray said, to complement these key indicators and look at what the Health Department has done in the past fiscal year, we worked to address health access and equity by launching a new subcommittee for the LHIC to do some of the work in this area.
