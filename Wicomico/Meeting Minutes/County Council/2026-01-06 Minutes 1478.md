Veto Message:
Dear President Cannon and Council:
Pursuant to my authority under the Wicomico County Charter, | hereby veto Legislative Bill 2025-14.
This veto is based on substantive legal and governance deficiencies within the bill that undermine
auditor independence, create inconsistent regulatory standards, and establish a precedent that is
contrary to sound internal control principles and public accountability.
Lack of Structural Independence
Legislative Bill 2025-14 purports to establish the independence of the County Internal Auditor;
however, the bill fails to do so in any meaningful or enforceable manner. While Section 13-3(B)
states that the Auditor “shall be considered independent,”
other provisions of the bill directly
conflict with that assertion.
The bill permits the Auditor’s work to be assigned, directed, waived, or influenced by external
governing bodies, including discretionary waivers by the County Council for audits involving the
Council itself. Independence cannot be declared by statute while simultaneously allowing
operational control and selective exemptions that compromise objectivity. Independence must be
structural, not aspirational.
As written, the bill creates the appearance of independence without providing the protections
necessary to ensure it, thereby exposing the County to both reputational and legal risk.
Exemption of “Other Activities” From Professional Standards
The most significant defect in Legislative Bill 2025-14
is its explicit exemption of “Other Activities”
from the Global Internal Audit Standards.
Section E(2) of the bill states that fiscal impact reviews, hotline administration and investigations,
and assistance to individual County Council members “will not be subject to the Global Internal
Audit Standards”. These activities are not ministerial or incidental in nature; they are substantive
oversight and investigative functions that directly impact policy, personnel, and financial decision-
making.
By legislatively exempting these activities from recognized professional standards, the bill creates a
dual audit framework in which some work is governed by global standards and other work is not.

<!-- page 10 -->
This inconsistency is indefensible from a governance, risk, or compliance standpoint and is
fundamentally at odds with the purpose of adopting professional standards in the first place.
Precedent and Governance Risk
Codifying exemptions from professional audit standards sets a dangerous precedent. Once
exceptions are embedded in County Code, future expansions of non-standardized audit activity
become easier tojustify, further eroding accountability and uniform oversight.
This approach undermines internal controls, weakens public confidence, and increases the
likelihood of disputes over audit scope, methodology, and conclusions. It also invites challenges
regarding selective enforcement, political influence, and lack of due process—particularly where
audit-adjacent activities are performed outside accepted standards.
Inconsistent and Selective Adoption of Standards
While the bill repeatedly references the Global Internal Audit Standards, it adopts them selectively
and inconsistently. Professional standards are not modular tools to be applied at convenience;
they are comprehensive frameworks designed to ensure independence, objectivity, consistency,
and credibility.
Partial adoption with express carve-outs does not strengthen the audit function—it weakens it.
Conclusion
For the reasons Stated above, Legislative Bill 2025-14 is legally and structurally flawed. It does not
establish a truly independent internal audit function, it exempts significant oversight activities from
professional standards, and it sets a precedent that undermines sound public administration.
Accordingly, | veto this bill.

and consistently aligns the County’s internal audit function with recognized professional standards
while preserving true independence and accountability.
