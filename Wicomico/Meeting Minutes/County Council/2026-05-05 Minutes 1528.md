Public Hearing: Resolution No. 67-2026
— Approving a zoning waiver for City of Salisbury Lands of
Nicholas Fenzel-North Schumaker Drive annexation in the City of Salisbury.
Mrs. Hurley explained, as background, the proposed municipal zoning designation is R-10A
residential, which is inconsistent with the County’s current zoning of R-20 residential. The
petitioner intends to construct 60 townhome units consistent with the use of property located in
the City’s R-10A residential zoning district, but at a higher density, so this requires a zoning waiver.
Mr. Cannon opened the Public Hearing.
Ms. Carolyn Wohlgemuth came to the podium and said | live in Granby’s Cove in the City and
County. | urge you to vote no on the zoning waiver. This waiver far exceeds the 50 percent density
threshold, and the R-10A use of townhouses and apartments differs substantially from R-20 use of
single family detached homes. Two major concerns are the environmental threat to Schumaker
Pond and the inappropriate zoning designation. Schumaker Pond is designated a nontidal wetland
of special concern in COMAR that carries substantial stormwater implications. Higher density
zoning increases impervious surface, stormwater volume, and pollutant loading, raising the risk of
Ss)

<!-- page 7 -->
algae blooms and downstream flooding that will affect neighbors, Schumaker Park, the new
Wicomico Library, Salisbury Zoo and Park, and the Wicomico River. After annexation, increased
runoff from this site will still affect the County’s MS4 pollutant reduction obligations and
downstream critical area waters. That exposure is a powerful reason to vote no on this waiver. Next
is the issue of zoning appropriateness. This property is zoned R-20 in the County for low density
single family detached homes. R-10A zoning more than doubles and potentially triples the
allowable R-10 density while allowing townhouses and apartments not permitted at all under R-20.
That fundamentally different intensity and fundamentally different use is yet another powerful
reason to vote no on this waiver. Especially important is that the City’s own Planning staff did not
recommend R-10A. In the August 2025 Salisbury Infrastructure and Development staff report,
“Staff found that R-10S zoning would be the appropriate designation for this development. This
zoning maintains the integrity of the nearby single family zoning. Staff recommends the parcel be
zoned R-10S upon annexation.” She said R-10S zoning is lower density housing that is closer to R-
10 zoning and consistent with the adjacent neighborhood and homes bordering Schumaker Pond.
The City Planning Department rejected R-10A zoning. The County should do the same. For these
reasons, | ask the County to vote no on this zoning waiver.
