Mr. Benson said, by way of background, in 2021 the Legislature of the State of Maryland authorized
Wicomico County to grant a property tax credit relating to hotel and residential development that is
known as the Horizon tax credit. He said that change was made in Section 90-7 of the County Code. He
said, in that section, a tax exemption was approved for a hotel or multi-family residential area within a
certain geographical area, which in this case relates to the downtown area or the building that’s across
the street near 132 East Main Street.
Mr. Benson said, for the record I don’t have anything to do with this, but my office was located at 132
East Main for more than 20 years, so just so there’s no confusion,
I have nothing to do with it beyond
having been in that building for 20+ years.
Mr. Benson explained, the amount of the tax credit is set forth in the Code and it starts at 80% of the
increased property tax and then goes down to 70%, 60%, 50%, 40% and 30%; it is a 10-year tax credit.
He said the County Code requires that the credit shall become available for an eligible building the first
year in which the building property taxes would increase due to construction or renovation. He said the
increase must be in the amount of at least $10 million dollars and the property owner must make an
application within 90 days of receiving the assessment notice. He said the application is to be reviewed
by the Finance Department and the application must be submitted for approval by the County Executive
and the County Council, so this requires both the Executive and the Council to approve this jointly.
Mr. Benson said in this situation, the property owner received a notice from the Department of
Assessments and Taxation that his property tax went from approximately $2 million per year to
approximately $30 million. He said that property tax notice was sent out on or about January 1, 2024.
He said the applicant filed an application for the tax credit on or about September 6, 2024. He said the
purpose of being here tonight is for the County to hear from the applicant with regard to the application
for the tax credit and to determine whether the applicant is entitled to the tax credit. He said the
fundamental problem is that the applicant was required to make the application within 90 days. He said
the applicant made the application within 9 months, not 90 days, and the issue is whether that means
the applicant cannot get the tax credit, or can the County, nonetheless, waive the time requirement. He
said the applicant is here, as I understand it, to make a presentation to explain what happened.
Mr. Benson said, again, the County Code was amended in compliance with authorization from the State
of Maryland. He said it is in the Tax Property Article of the State of Maryland, and
I would just note that
in the Tax Property Article, Section 7-104 talks about exemptions and abatement of taxes, which are
State abatements, and when those things have to be done. He said, if an owner of property is subject to
an exemption on June 30 and files an application on or before September 1, the notice would have
come out in January, but by law I think the notices from the State are required to be out by January 1 or
thereabouts. He said there is no specific provision in Maryland about this that I see as to what the rights
of the County are in this regard.
Mr. Benson said
I would note that, as it relates to an assessment notice, in Maryland, if a property
owner receives an assessment notice from the State and disputes that assessment notice, they have a
period of time in which to file an appeal, but in Section 8-419 of the Tax Property Article, there is a
provision in the State Law that says:
Notwithstanding, if they would defy protest of an assessment after the date offinality, the date of
finality is January 1. The appropriate official may issue an order decreasing or abating assessment to
correct an improper assessment or to prevent injustice.
Mr. Benson clarified, the only point is, if you look for guidance in the Tax Property Article in the State of
Maryland, there are provisions to overlook a late filing, at least for an appeal on a tax assessment. He
