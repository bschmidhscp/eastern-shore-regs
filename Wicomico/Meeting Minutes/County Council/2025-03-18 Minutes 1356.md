# Laura Hurley, Council Administrator 

Mrs. Hurley said we have one housekeeping matter before we get started, which pertains to Legislative Bill 2025-01. She said pursuant to Charter Section 411, the amended version of Legislative Bill 2025-01 concerning commercial dog kennels was presented to the County Executive on February 19, 2025. She said, under the Charter, the County Executive had 21 days to either approve or veto the amended Bill. She said, on March 3, 2025, the County Council received a letter from the County Executive stating she would not sign the amended Bill due to a difference in interpretation regarding the procedural reconsideration of the Bill. She said, in accordance with Charter Section 411, if the County Executive fails to return the Bill within the prescribed 21-day period, the Council Administrator must document this failure in the official record, and then the Bill shall become law. She said this serves as official notice to the Council and to the public that the 21-day period has passed and the County Executive has neither signed nor vetoed the amended Bill, and, as required, this will be recorded in the official minutes. 

Public Hearing: Resolution No. 36-2025 — To amend the Fiscal Year 2025 Capital Budget to authorize the County Executive to accept a Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) Pre-Disaster Mitigation Grant Award in the amount of $29,482.50 for the Wicomico County Cove Road Acquisition Project. # Proposed Farmer’s Market Permit: 

Mr. Matthew McConoughey, Local Health Officer, came before the Board of Health and said currently we do not have a farmer’s market or sampler’s license application or permit with the Health Department. He explained, we currently operate with a temporary food service permit, which technically only allows for 30 consecutive days, so that would mean, if an individual wanted to provide samples or provide foods that aren’t necessarily approved at a farmer’s market, they would have to reapply every 30 days, which is a $75 fee; whereas this permit will allow us to issue a once-a-year $100 permit and they would be able to come to the farmer’s market and promote their wares basically. He said farmer’s markets allow you to do cottage foods, whole produce, and nonhazardous foods.
