Public Hearing: Resolution No. 115-2025 — To amend the FY26 Capital Budget to authorize the County Executive to transfer $200,000 from the Hurdle Building Exterior Wall Project to fund the Nursing Home Generator Project. 

Mrs. Hurley explained, since the County no longer needs to spend $200,000 on repairs for the Hurdle building, the County Executive has proposed to move those funds to the Nursing Home Generator Project. She said this will cover the extra costs of upgrading the electrical system so the new generator can power the entire facility during outages. 

Mr. Cannon opened the Public Hearing. 

Mr. Joe White came to the podium and said I have nothing against doing the generator for the Nursing Home, I’m sure that’s a very necessary topic, but from a broader sense, back in April and May during the budget season, you had work sessions with the Executive’s Finance team about the Hurdle Building and the funds you were planning to spend there, most notably going towards the Verizon building. He said since then, publicly we have not heard anything about the proposed $2.75 million we were going to spend on the Verizon building. He said I know that was outside of what the Council in general was looking to spend, but if we’re going to start earmarking $200,000 here or there, the lease with Verizon runs out in October, so it would be nice publicly to know what the taxpayers are going to be expecting for improvements to the building.

There were no further public comments. Mr. Cannon closed the Public Hearing. 

Mr. Pate Matthews, General Services Supervisor, and Ms. Pam Oland, Director of Finance, came before Council. 

Mr. Cannon said in the last budget cycle, I know we had requested either with the CIP or with the annual budget that we have some type of addendum to that. He said I’m going to consider this to be a restricted fund once it’s been approved in the CIP and also for the first year of the budget, the capital expense. He then asked, is there an addendum that tracks that three-year period that shows us what those restricted funds are and the total amounts and the projects? 

Ms. Oland responded, when Council adopts the capital budget, they are adopting the ability to take the funds out of the general fund and move it into the capital project fund. She said you will see that this is going into fund 32, which is the capital project fund associated with the pay-go funds that the Council has approved. She said additionally, we identify what fiscal year these projects were originally started for the most part by the first two numbers of the project number, so the Hurdle Building money here was identified as being as what we thought we were going to do in 2024. She said ultimately, in 2024, we made the decision to buy the Verizon building, and thus, this money was set aside but not used for Hurdle because we made the decision to buy Verizon. 

Ms. Oland said we are going to be providing to Council during this upcoming CIP a holistic look at everything that is in our fund 32, what projects are completed, and where we stand on every project. She said it doesn’t make sense to add it to the CIP because that would make it an incredibly long document, but in this process, we are going to be identifying projects that may or may not need the funds that still sit there and asking Council during the overall capital aspects to be utilizing funds that have already been identified and have been set aside for other projects. She said this was kind of one that has come to us because right now the current generator we have does not operate the full facility. She said the new generator we are installing will operate the full facility, but the electric box that we have to connect through can’t handle that level of generator, and thus, we need to update the electrical system to be able to accept the generator so that the whole facility could be run on a generator if we would need to, which we hope we don’t need to. 

Mr. Cannon said the Nursing Home has always been that quasi fund, to which Ms. Oland responded, it’s along the lines of it can operate itself, it can afford to cover its operations, but significant capital projects are harder for them to cover, and this is one that had been put in the budget in 2022 to upgrade this system. She said like the Civic Center, it is a County-owned facility. She said the Civic Center can cover its operating costs, but its capital costs at times are beyond the ability of it to cover, and thus, we made the decision that this is one that, for the safety of the employees and the residents that we, the County, should be covering. 

Mr. Cannon clarified, the Nursing Home is really not an enterprise fund in itself, to which Ms. Oland responded, it is considered a component unit, if I’m remembering correctly, but it is a wholly-owned facility of the County. She clarified, we own the facility and it is recognized in our financial statements, so we have a financial obligation to ensure that this facility is operating at its best ability. 

Mr. Cannon said Dave always talks about sinking funds and things like that. He then asked, has the Nursing Home planned for a sinking fund for major capital expenditures in the next five to ten years, to which Ms. Oland responded, we have not looked at it in that regard. She said we could, but we have not at this moment.

Legislative Bill No. 2025-12 — An Act to amend Chapter 138 of the Wicomico County Code titled “Electrical Standards” to reflect the current edition of the National Electrical Code and to amend the process of promulgation of future editions. 

Mr. Andrew Illuminati, Esq., came before Council and said a few months ago we brought proposed Legislation forward to bring the International Residential Code (IRC), International Building Code (IBC) and a couple others up to date. He said the Electrical Standards are in a separate part of the code, not in the building portion of the code. He said the Electrical Board worked on this for the last couple of months and, when you look at A, B and C, they are all the same verbatim language that was done for the IRC, the IBC, and the IECC. He said this is just for the National Electric Code (NEC) to bring it up to the 2023 edition, and then go through the same process going forward of the 180 days and submit to Council with any modifications. He said the current Electrical Code actually didn’t have any modifications, so 138-2.1 modifications cleans up exception #2 and adds in exception #3 from the NEC, and that’s just sort of a best practice that they found going around. 

Mr. Illuminati thanked the Board for their hard work on this and said they had a couple of meetings and really worked on it and talked with the electrical inspectors about what should be some modifications to the outdoor outlets. He said otherwise, they found that the 2023 NEC is pretty up to date, and that’s why this is before you at this time — to get all of our codes up to date and to have a clean process going forward to amend when the 2026 version comes out in a couple of years. 

There being no further discussion, on motion by Mr. Hastings, seconded by Mr. Merritt, and by roll call vote, Mr. Hastings, aye; Mr. Winn, aye; Mr. Ennis, aye; Mr. Merritt, aye, and Mr. Cannon, aye, Legislative Bill No. 2025-12 was introduced. Joe Holloway was absent.
