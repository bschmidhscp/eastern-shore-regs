Presentation by Mr. Kevin Karpinski, Esq., and Mr. Paul Wilber, Esq. on Final Consent Judgement and Decree in Civil Action No. 1:23-CV-03325-MJM, Wicomico County Branch of N.A.A.C.P., et al, Plaintiff’s versus Wicomico County, Maryland, et al, Defendant, in the United States District Court for the District of Maryland: 

Mr. Paul Wilber, County Attorney, and Mr. Kevin Karpinski came before Council. 

Mr. Karpinski said I am serving as counsel for Wicomico County in connection with the lawsuit filed by the N.A.A.C.P. and other plaintiffs and I will start with a little background about the litigation and where we are in connection with it. He said this case was filed by a number of plaintiffs against Wicomico County, the Board of Education, the State Board of Elections, and the Wicomico County Local Board of Elections asserting a violation of Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act of 1965. He said the complaint, sort of stripped to its bare essentials, alleged that the current composition of the Council, namely five Council districts and two at-large seats, diluted the voting rights of African Americans within Wicomico County. He said the plaintiffs also alleged a claim under 42 USC Section 1983 that the current 5:2 composition also violated the Equal Protection Clause of the United States Constitution. 

Mr. Karpinski explained, the purpose of the Voting Rights Act is to ensure that the political process is equally open to all individuals including minority voters. He said, to that end, the Supreme Court in Thorn burg vs. Jingles set forth a number of factors that the court looks at in determining whether there is a viable potential Section 2 violation of the Voting Rights Act, and they are 1) that the minority group is able to demonstrate that it is sufficiently large and geographically compact to constitute a majority in a single-member district; 2) that the minority group is politically cohesive, and 3) that the majority<sup>votes</sup> sufficiently in a block to enable it in the absence of special circumstances to have a minority candidate elected. 

Mr. Karpinski said, in addition to those three factors, there is what we commonly call the nine Jingles factors, which get into some historical background, which I’m not going to really get into other than the point being that it’s a very factually intensive analysis of whether there could be a potential violation of Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act. He said in my view, the complaint that was filed alleged, and<sup>I</sup> emphasize alleged, sufficient facts that the court would not dismiss the case on a preliminary motion, and that would require that the court have an evidentiary hearing on a preliminary motion for a preliminary injunction and then a trial. He said I believe when I met with the Council many, many
