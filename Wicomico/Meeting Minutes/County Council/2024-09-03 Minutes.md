# County Council minutes 2024-09-03

The Wicomico County Council met in Legislative Session on Tuesday, September 3, 2024 at 6:00 p.m. in Council Chambers, Government Office Building, Salisbury, Maryland. 

In attendance: John T. Cannon, Council President; Jeff Merritt, Vice President; Joe Holloway, James Winn, Shane Baker, and Shanie Shields. Josh Hastings was absent. 

Present: Laura Hurley, Council Administrator; Lynn Ingold, Executive Office Associate, and Robert A. Benson, Council Attorney. 

On motion by Mr. Merritt and seconded by Mr. Baker, the Consent Agenda consisting of the following items was unanimously approved: 

- Legislative Minutes from August 20, 2024 

- Special Legislative Session Minutes from July 23, 2024 

- Open Work Session Minutes from July 16, 2024 — Regulations on Controlling Invasive Bamboo 

- Open Work Session Minutes from July 16, 2024 — Removal of Approximately 4.15 Acres of a Forest Conservation Easement — Eden Road 

- Open Work Session Minutes from July 16, 2024 — Design Funding for Public Library

# Laura Hurley, Council Administrator 

Mrs. Hurley announced, we have three Public Hearings on the agenda this evening, and each Public Hearing notice was published in the local newspaper and on the County’s website. 

Public Hearing: Resolution No. 105-2024 — To approve the relocation of a forest conservation easement containing 4.15 acres located on tax map 57, parcels 121 and 123 owned by Temo Power Incorporated to an offsite mitigation bank. 

Mrs. Hurley said Temo Power Incorporated has submitted a request to relocate the 4.15 acre forest conservation easement to an offsite mitigation bank. She said the relocation involves a 2:1 ratio meaning 7.30 acres of forest conservation will be conserved within a mitigation bank. She explained, the purpose of the relocation is for the construction of a utility scale solar project on parcels 121 and 123 located on the easterly side of Old Eden Road, and Council had a work session on this request on July 16. 

Mr. Cannon opened the Public Hearing. There were no public comments. Mr. Cannon closed the Public Hearing. 

There being no discussion, on motion by Mr. Baker and seconded by Ms. Shields, Resolution No. 1052024 was unanimously approved.

Mr. David Kenney came to the podium and said I am from Hebron and I am here to talk about Barren Creek Road. He said I understand that it was pulled from the priority list at the last meeting. He said I am actually representing two organizations that have an interest in repairing Barren Creek Road, first the Adkins Historical Museum Complex, which owns the property on the south side of Barren Creek Road from North Athol Road around the dam to the spillway on the north side of the pond. He said a little history about the road, dam, and pond, Howard and Louise Adkins researched Barren Creek Pond and wrote the long history. He said Mr. Howard Adkins grew up in the house at the southeast side of the dam. He said the pond and dam have been in place at least back to 1735 according to land records making it one of the oldest historical structures in the County. He said that makes it older than Maryland and also older than the United States. He said according to colonial law, a road was required across a dam, so Barren Creek Road started as a pathway for horse and wagon. He said later it was considered part of Route 213 until Route 50 was built and eventually became Barren Creek Road. He said also of note, in 1751 when the trans-peninsular line was being surveyed to find the midpoint between the Atlantic Ocean and the Chesapeake Bay, which is the bottom corner of Delaware by Mason Dixon, they recorded that the line came within one foot of the Miller’s house. He said there are markers on the Adkins property from that survey. He said the roadbed or the dam and the pond were private property and this always caused controversy as to who would take care of the road. He said, when Hurricane Sandy came through and dumped 10 inches of rain in the area, we almost lost the road. He said the property owner was absent and water was cresting the road. He said the County Executive had to get approval from the County Attorney declaring it a public emergency to pull the gates and the spillway. He said, once the first gate came out, the water started to drop and that incident spurred the County to acquire legal documents to gain control over the spillway and the operation of the spillway. He said that document was signed in September of 2013. He said coming forward, on July 13, 2016, a storm dropped several inches of rain in our area in a short period of time. He said on the morning of July 14, the water
