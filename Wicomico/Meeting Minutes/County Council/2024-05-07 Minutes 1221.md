Mr. Gene Malone came to the podium and said
I am the Chairman of the Wicomico County Board of
Education and the Board and our staff have been hard at work on the budget since last fall. He said this
year poses several challenges including the expiration of COVID-era federal grants, increasing
enrollment, and inventory of 24 school buildings with an average age of 33 years. He said despite these
challenges, Wicomico County citizens have a lot to be proud of in their public school system. He said our
students continue to succeed in the classroom, athletic fields, and the community. He said this success is
supported by the hard work of our over 3,000 employees. He said, as we near the end of fiscal year
2024,
I would be remiss if I didn’t recognize and show appreciation to our County Executive and County
Council’s leadership in providing above maintenance of effort (MOE) funding for the school system this
year. He said, as we look ahead to 2025, we are once again asking for above MOE funding and it is
important that we be transparent with the public as we make this request. He said, in FY2O1O, the
Wicomico County Government had revenue of nearly $125 million dollars. He said, of that, nearly 41
percent, a little over $50 million, was allocated to the Board of Education for operating expenses. He
said, in our current year, County revenue has grown to over $193 million, but of this only 26 percent,
less than $50 million, was allocated to public schools. He said in short, since 2010, the County has seen a
59.1 percent increase in revenue while the community’s public school system is seeing a net decrease of
2.2 percent in operating funds. He clarified, that’s right, the County’s appropriation to our public schools
was less in 2024 than 2010. He said, if funding for FY25 remains at MOE, the County allocation to the
public school system will once again be below what was received in 2010 for the 15th consecutive year.
He said, in our February 8th public budget hearing we shared that the cost of doing business for FY25
shows an increase of approximately $4.1 million. He said this does not include new programming,
instructional materials, or staff salaries. He said Superintendent Stauffer and staff shared an FY25
request of $4.1 million above maintenance of effort with County leadership. He said this request would
simply cover the increases in the cost of doing business. He said, as a Board, we appreciate the hard
work of our 3,000 employees and 15,000 students, and we look forward to continuing our collaboration
with County Government on the hard work required to make this request a reality.

sports, fine arts, and music. He said the local funds also help determine the environment that our
