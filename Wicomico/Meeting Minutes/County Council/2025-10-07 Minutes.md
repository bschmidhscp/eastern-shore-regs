Public Hearing: Resolution No. 153-2025 — To amend the FY26 Capital Budget to reallocate $184,750 from the Fuel Farm Project to fund the AP Tech Project at the Salisbury-OC-Wicomico Regional Airport. Mrs. Hurley explained, this is a request to amend the FY26 capital budget to transfer funds from the previously approved Fuel Farm Project in the amount of $184,750 to the AP Tech Park Project to cover increased construction costs. 

Mr. Cannon opened the Public Hearing.

Ms. Pam Oland, Director of Finance, and Mr. Tony Rudy, Director of Aviation, came before Council. Ms. Oland said, as discussed in September, the Fuel Farm Project is almost done and under budget, so there are remaining funds in that project. She said with it being under budget, it makes fiscal sense for this capital project to take funds from something that has already been appropriated and move it to a project that needs additional funds. She said the capital project fund is different than the general fund, as Council is aware; capital projects roll over year to year in the capital project fund, they don’t follow the fiscal year. She clarified, they follow the fiscal year associated with funding, but they don’t follow the fiscal year in result to timing and the project closing out. She said we come to Council when projects have a need, this project has a need, we have funds left over in a project that doesn’t have a need, so instead of taking money from fund balance, let’s take it from a project we already appropriated.

Ms. Oland said, as we discussed also in September, I mentioned that during this year’s CIP, our intention is to come to Council with projects that have been closed out and have additional funding remaining in them, so why appropriate more money — let’s take it from projects that have already been closed and not appropriate additional funds. She said the need for this project is that we need to get this done to be able to complete the project and we don’t have enough funds.

He said as we started the project, there were soils underneath the road and an unexpected amount were bad soils that couldn’t be backfilled, otherwise, the road wouldn’t be supported properly, so there was a need to find suitable fill for that process. Mr. Rudy said, with the work we did with the contractor and the cooperation we had with other County Departments, we were able to bring it down, and I fully agree that it is a large amount, but without that, we wouldn’t be able to finish the project and we would be pretty much at a standstill at this point.

Resolution No. 154-2025 — Bond Resolution 

Ms. Pam Oland, Director of Finance, came before Council and said this is the next step in the bond process as we have done over the last several years. She explained, Council has to pass legislation to give us the authorization to borrow funds, and that is what we did first back in August. She said then you have the 60-day referendum period that has now run out. She said this Resolution allows us to actually go out to borrowing. She said our intention, if this passes, is for us to send out the solicitations on Thursday. She said we did get back from S&P and Moody’s that a AA+ rating were affirmed, and as such, we are at the same level we were last year, and they had positive things to say in regards to our financial situation. She said with that, when we get our bids, which is the last step in this process, on Thursday we send out the solicitations and then at the next Council meeting on the<sup>21st</sup> we will receive our bids. She said I believe our bids close at 10:30 in the morning and we then have some backroom stuff that has to be done. She said we have to select the winner from the bidding and they then have to send us money to say they are committed to buying these bonds, and then we come back to Council typically at 2:00 p.m. to have Council accept the winning bid. She said we are right now looking at the maximum amount we would borrow to be $17,765,000. 

Mr. Cannon said, for the public, the projects include Emergency Services tower building rebuild at $1,050,000; the Public Library Flagship Library at Schumaker Pond at $866,000; Civic Center elevator replacement at $1,100,000; Rec & Parks Mason Dixon parking lot master plan at $1,500,000; Solid Waste landfill cell construction at $10,500,000, and landfill expansion at $2,500,000 with a contingency of $249,000 for a total of $17,765,000. 

Ms. Oland explained, the $249,000 is the biggest number that will fluctuate because we only use that contingency to pay for the cost of issuance, as we have to pay S&P, Moody’s, our bond counsel, and our investment advisors, and that comes out of this number. She said they give me all of those estimates, so at the closing table, we adjust that number, but we give ourselves a little cushion for something further to come in. She said we have to pay M&T Bank, as they are the entity that manages the payments of this stuff, so again, there is a list of people we have to pay, but that is what that contingency is for. She clarified, it’s not intended to be switched over to projects, it’s just to pay the cost of issuance. 

Mr. Holloway asked, how much does this increase our debt load, to which Ms. Oland responded, we are still well under - I think we’re at about 58 percent of our debt limit. Mr. Holloway asked, does this increase it greatly, to which Ms. Oland responded, no. She said based on how quickly we have been paying off our other debt, again, the rating agencies are very comfortable with the debt load we have and at the pace we pay it off. Mr. Holloway asked Ms. Oland to send him what the increase is when she has a chance, to which Ms. Oland responded, sure, I can have that for the next meeting when we talk
