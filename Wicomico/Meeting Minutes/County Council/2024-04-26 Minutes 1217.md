# County Council minutes 2024-04-26

<!-- page 1 -->
The Wicomico County Council met in a Special Legislative Session on Friday, April 26, 2024 at 9:00 a.m. in the DaNang Room at the Wicomico Youth & Civic Center, Salisbury, Maryland. 

In attendance: John T. Cannon, Council President; Jeff Merritt, Vice President; Joe Holloway, James Winn, Shanie Shields, and Josh Hastings. Shane Baker was absent. 

Present: Laura Hurley, Council Administrator; Lynn Ingold, Executive Office Associate, and Robert A. Benson, Council Attorney. 

Laura Hurley, Council Administrator 

Briefing with Brooke E. Lierman, Esquire, Comptroller of Maryland, regarding her role and partnership with local governments: 

Comptroller Lierman came before Council and introduced her staff, Michael Baird, Intergovernmental Affairs Director and Robin McCullough, Media Relations Director. 

Mr. Cannon said it’s always good to get a visit with the Comptroller and see how things are going, especially since we just finished with the budget. 

Ms. Lierman said I appreciate you being here and hosting me and my team. She said we are excited to spend a day on the Shore today and it looks like it’s going to be a beautiful day. She said the sun was rising as we went over the Bridge. She said I really appreciate the opportunity to spend some time with you and the people of Wicomico County. 

Ms. Lierman said I am the<sup>34th</sup> Comptroller of the State of Maryland and I’m excited to have the chance to speak with you this morning about the work we’re doing in the Office of the Comptroller. She said of course the Office of the Comptroller is one that has a wide range of responsibilities, so I wanted to talk through some of the work that we’re doing and what some of our responsibilities are, but really we are doing these visits to different County Councils to demonstrate sort of our dedication to working with all of you and local government. She said you are the tip of the spear, so to speak, working with and supporting our communities, our shared constituents, and Marylanders. She said we are here to support you and the work you are doing, and know that you support us as well. 

Ms. Lierman said the Office of the Comptroller is a unique State elected constitution in the country. She said there is no other statewide elected official that has the same job duties that I have. She said I’ve been looking to try to find a colleague, but it’s really three distinct roles. She said I serve as the elected CEO of the State in a sense. She said we see every dollar in and every dollar out of the State Government. She said I’m like the paymaster, we watch every dollar that goes out when we pay all of our employees and we of course do the revenue estimates for the State, so we know what we can spend in our State budget. She said we also balance all the books for the State and actually this week we released the State’s ACFR and we are continuing to work on next year, so on this current year. 

Ms. Lierman said second, I sit on several important State Boards where I serve as an independent voice really ensuring that we’re getting the best value for our tax dollars and focusing on open, accessible, and transparent processes for everyone. She said these include Boards like the Board of Public Works, which you know well, the State Retirement Pension Board, and the Board of Revenue Estimates, so important bodies where we are dedicated to serving you. 

Ms. Lierman said third, I serve as the State’s Revenue Administrator where I focus on tax collection, refunds, as well as how to oversee tax policy in ways that are efficient and equitable for Marylanders.

She clarified, I don’t write the tax policy, but we do work with the General Assembly to make sure that whatever they are thinking about we can fully implement in a fair way, and that is the role we play. 

Ms. Lierman said, after I was sworn in in January of 2023, about 15 months ago, we adopted a new vision for the agency to work in partnership, including with our local governments, working in partnership with you, to build a State that is more equitable, more resilient, and more prosperous so that every Marylander can reach their full potential. She said to do that we are guided by three core priorities, and much of the work we are doing within our agency, almost all of it, frankly, is within those three core priorities, and I know you have sheets where we list our vision, the core priorities, and then some examples of those. 

Ms. Lierman said our first priority is really about implementing critical agency-wide improvements to help State Government work better. She said, at the end of the day, we want to make sure that our agency is doing what it can to support businesses and communities and all of you. She said a cornerstone of the work we do is of course processing taxes, balancing books, getting out unclaimed property, and we need to modernize our systems. She said I inherited an agency with systems that were purchased, procured, and implemented by none other than Louie Goldstein. She said he used to brag that he took the Office of the Comptroller from pencil and paper to the mainframe; now it is time to move us from the mainframe to the cloud. She said this is both an economic necessity, but also one that will result, we believe, in better processes and more efficient processes for Marylanders as well as saving the State money over the years. She said we are moving our tax processing system from a COBOL based mainframe system to a new online modern system called Maryland Tax Connect, and this February we launched Maryland Tax Connect for all business taxes. She said now sales and use taxes, corporate income taxes, and withholding taxes can all be paid online through this new Maryland Tax Connect portal. She said then on the backend in our offices, rather than our agents having to stare at a green screen and hit the function key, they have a new system called Revenue Premier where they can process things and follow transactions more seamlessly. She said our goal is to roll out Revenue Premier and Maryland Tax Connect for all Marylanders — personal income taxes, passthrough entity taxes — by the end of 2025. She clarified, it is an ambitious goal, I’ll be honest, but we are excited. 

Ms. Lierman said this year, just to give you some context on taxes, we have processed more than 2.7 million personal income tax returns and issued more than $2.1 billion in refunds to 1.7 million Marylanders. She said, to put that in scope, we process about 3.2 million tax returns a year total and we’ve already done 2.7 million. She said tax season is really all year for us, but the bulk of the personal income taxes really come in through these first four months. 

Ms. Lierman said a second core priority is focused on making sure that the resources and the expertise in our agency reaches the people and the businesses of Maryland. She said this is so much about reaching out to communities, making sure we’re reaching out to all of you. She said in fact, we have a group of advisory councils now. She said one of them is a Local Government Advisory Council that assists us with aligning our priorities with the needs of constituents here in Wicomico County, so we have folks from all over the Shore and all over the State who are members of that, and I think Councilman Hastings is on that, so thank you. She said, if any of you are ever interested in joining in the future, every two years we are rotating, so just keep us in mind. 

Ms. Lierman said we also launched our first ever public engagement team, so we have individuals all over the State. She said we have five people who have divided up the State and do work on their own reaching out to communities. She said our Eastern Shore Public Engagement Officer is a woman named Mary Handley. She said, if your constituents have senior centers or schools or places where you think it would be great if somebody could bring resources on the earned income tax credit or the senior tax

Ms. Lierman said finally, I just want to talk about our State of the Economy report for a minute. She said this is the first ever report on the State of the Economy that we have done in our agency, but we felt it was very important because, when I first took Office we noticed that our revenues were not keeping pace with where we thought they would be going. She said in fact, our GDP we saw had been growing much more slowly than national GDP over the past seven or eight years, so we decided we needed to dig into this more. She said we held roundtable discussions across the State with business leaders and economic development leaders, and we had a great and very robust discussion here in Salisbury with a number of business leaders. She said then we crunched the numbers we have and we saw that, although our economy is strong, there are real challenges, and, in fact, a few key findings as they relate to the Shore and Wicomico are that roundtable participants really believe the pipeline for new workers and industries that require in-person work like healthcare, education, and public safety have slowed down precipitously. She said those are industries that are still experiencing elevated quit rates even after the pandemic, and local community colleges like Wor-Wic are reporting low enrollment in these critical workforce development programs. She said roundtable participants also cited the lack of housing as a top economic concern for retaining current residents and attracting new residents to the area, and I will tell you that housing was, in fact, the number one concern cited at every business roundtable that we had across the State. She said employers are increasingly concerned that their employees and the people they are trying to recruit do not have a place where they can live near their offices, so that has become a key concern. She said this really aligned with data analyzed by our Bureau of Revenue estimates and our policy teams, which showed housing affordability as a key concern across Maryland. 

Ms. Lierman said we are excited about the work we were able to do in that State of the Economy report, and a few key findings that we had as well really are around our workforce and our labor force participation. She said because we have lower labor force participation now and fewer people are in the workforce than before the pandemic, we are seeing constrained growth in private jobs and private industries, so we dug in a little on why this labor force participation is lower. She said we saw some correlations with the opioid epidemic and increased death rates. She said we also noted that women have left the workforce at higher rates in Maryland than nationally and, although women are not a
