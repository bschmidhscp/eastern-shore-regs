# Robert A. Benson, Esg., Council Attorney 

Public Hearing: Legislative Bill No. 2025-07 — An Act to amend Chapter 62 of the Wicomico County Code titled “Planning and Zoning Commission,” Section 62-1 titled “Repealer,” Section 62-2 titled “Creation,” Section 62-4 titled “Members Appointed; Chairman,” Section 62-6 titled “Legal Counsel,” and Section 62-7 titled “Establishment of Department of Planning, Zoning and Communication Development” to dissolve the Wicomico County Planning and Zoning Commission and create a Wicomico County Planning and Zoning Commission. 

Mr. Benson said, to remind Council and the public, traditionally, the Wicomico County Planning Commission and the City of Salisbury Planning Commission sat as a joint Commission. He said the decision has been made to separate the two and Wicomico County will have its own Planning Commission. He said as part of that process, this Bill has been introduced to basically recreate the Wicomico County Planning Commission, and that is the purpose of this Legislation. 

Mr. Cannon opened the Public Hearing. There were no public comments. Mr. Cannon closed the Public Hearing. 

Mr. Holloway said I would like to make an amendment to the Bill so we could set the time of the meeting through a Resolution. Mr. Winn seconded the motion. 

Mr. Hastings said, for clarity, that would mean, if they wanted to change the time, we would just pass a Resolution as opposed to having it set in law, to which Mr. Holloway responded, yes. 

Mr. Holloway explained, the reason for asking that question is that the Planning and Zoning Commission has always met at 1:00 p.m. and that kind of restricts some people from being able to serve on the Commission if they want to, and it also restricts people from coming to the meetings due to their work day. He said hopefully we can set it to be at 4:00 p.m. through a Resolution. 

Mrs. Hurley asked if they have the exact verbiage they want to put into the Bill, or do they want legal counsel to do that, to which Mr. Holloway responded, I would like to see legal counsel put it in there.

# Report on the Health of the County 

Mr. Matthew McConaughey, Health Officer, came before Council and introduced Ms. Christina Gray. He said she is my Director of Planning and Assessment and my epidemiologist as well, and will be covering the majority of the material. He said we will be discussing health statistics and our recently completed Community Health Needs Assessment. He said we will also cover our Community Health Improvement

<!-- page 9 -->
Plan, a respiratory update, measles update, summer fun safety tips, and talk about how federal funding has been affected at the local Health Department, and then we will cover environmental health. 

Ms. Gray said we are not very data heavy today, so more of a holistic general overview, but of course I welcome follow up questions for data points. She said the last time we were here in October, we mentioned we were getting ready to ramp up our next three-year Community Health Needs Assessment (CHNA) cycle, but as we sit here today in June, that process was just completed. She said CHNA is pretty much a data deep dive, and we do this every three years. She said we look at what our current health problems in the County are and we work with our partners — our hospital systems with TidalHealth, and our neighboring County health departments in Somerset and now Worcester as we onboard AGH into the TidalHealth system, and we figure out, of those problems, what do we need to prioritize for resources, and go where they are most needed in the community. She said our current partners are TidalHealth and Somerset County and we will have the latest report both on our Healthy Delmarva Partnership website as well as the County Health Department site hopefully in the next few weeks or so. She said in May, AGH did fold into the TidalHealth system, so as we work through some of these strategies, we do hope that Worcester County Health Department will be a big part of that as well. 

Ms. Gray said, as we collect and analyze primary data for this big process, we factor in how the social determinants of health are affecting our health outcomes in the County. She said as a reminder, these are our upstream factors that really affect our quality of life. She said these are the root of our economic stability, education quality, how we access healthcare, the quality of that healthcare, our neighborhoods, our built environment, and of course our social and community context. She said if our residents can’t meet their basic needs of food and housing, their health is going to be impacted, so they might make different choices based on those things, so we always want to make sure we’re looking at those SDOH factors as we prioritize the work we do. 

Ms. Gray said this process took months and months and we collected a lot of different data types, so in addition to the social determinates data, we look at several other pieces of information in the process. She said secondary data, or data points that are already out there, we can look at and they are usually provided by existing facilities and agencies, census data, how our population is growing, and how age groups and the composition of our population are changing over time. She said we look at risk factor surveys, and this is our gauge on self-reported risk factors among our residents, such as are they getting preventative screenings, are they using tobacco, are they getting physical activity, what is their weight status — all of that is in that source of data. She said vital statistics are pretty vital, which are birth and death certificate data, and that is published by the State on an annual basis. She said because this is very important, we want a lot of primary data to be collected as well, and we did that a few different ways. She said first we had focus groups, so we had small interviews with general residents, as well as some specific populations. She said we know that our baby boomer population is growing and we specifically focused one of these groups on older adult health as we move into this next cycle, so that was very important to us. She said we also collected primary data among our key leaders in the community, so not just our healthcare leaders, but local government, education, social services and how this all fits together to improve health in the County. She said because those are pretty time-intensive, we also had one-on-one interviews with key leaders. She said focus groups take time, so we had a separate set of surveys produced and distributed in the public, and this was just a way for us to expand on getting results and feedback from the community about their health perceptions and outcomes, and altogether we had a fantastic response, especially compared to previous cycles for this work among these four primary data avenues. She said this was over 1,200 community surveys, 28 leader surveys, 53 focus

Ms. Giordano said I want to thank the Department of Law for your detailed and well-reasoned review of Legislative Bill 2025-08. She said I want to express my full agreement with your conclusion that the bill is in direct conflict with the Wicomico County Charter, egregiously oversteps the Council’s authority, and, if enacted, would have deeply negative consequences for how we do business in this County.
