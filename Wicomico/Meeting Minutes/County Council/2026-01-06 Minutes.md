## Veto Message: 

Dear President Cannon and Council: 

Pursuant to my authority under the Wicomico County Charter, | hereby veto Legislative Bill 2025-14. 

This veto is based on substantive legal and governance deficiencies within the bill that undermine auditor independence, create inconsistent regulatory standards, and establish a precedent that is contrary<sup>tosound internalcontrol</sup> principles<sup>and</sup> public accountability. 

#### Lack of Structural Independence 

Legislative<sup>Bill 2025-14</sup> purports<sup>toestablishthe</sup> independence<sup>of the</sup> County<sup>Internal</sup> Auditor; however, the<sup>billfailstodosoin</sup> any meaningful<sup>orenforceablemanner.WhileSection</sup> 13-3(B) states that the Auditor “shall be considered independent,” other provisions of the bill directly conflict with that assertion. 

The bill permits the Auditor’s work to be assigned, directed, waived, or influenced by external governing bodies, including discretionary<sup>waivers</sup> by<sup>the</sup> County<sup>Councilfor audits</sup> involving<sup>the</sup> Council itself. Independence cannot be declared by statute while simultaneously allowing operational<sup>control and selective</sup> exemptions<sup>that</sup> compromise objectivity. Independence<sup>must be</sup> structural,<sup>not</sup> aspirational. 

As written, the bill creates the appearance of independence without providing the protections necessary<sup>toensure</sup> it, thereby exposing<sup>the</sup> County<sup>toboth</sup> reputational<sup>and</sup> legal<sup>risk.</sup> 

# Exemption<sup>of</sup> “Other Activities”<sup>FromProfessional Standards</sup> 

The most significant defect in Legislative Bill 2025-14 is its explicit exemption of “Other Activities” from the Global Internal Audit Standards. 

Section E(2) of the bill states that fiscal impact reviews, hotline administration and investigations, and assistance to individual County Council members “will not be subject to the Global Internal Audit Standards”. These activities are not ministerial or incidental in nature; they are substantive oversight<sup>and</sup> investigative<sup>functionsthat</sup> directly impact policy, personnel,<sup>and financial decision-</sup> making. 

By legislatively exempting<sup>theseactivitiesfrom</sup> recognized professional standards,<sup>thebillcreatesa</sup> dual audit framework in which some work is governed by global standards and other work is not.

> This inconsistency is indefensible from a governance, risk, or compliance standpoint and is fundamentally<sup>at oddswiththe</sup> purpose<sup>of</sup> adopting professional<sup>standardsinthefirst</sup> place. 

### Precedent and Governance Risk 

Codifying exemptions<sup>from</sup> professional<sup>audit standards setsa</sup> dangerous precedent.<sup>Once</sup> exceptions<sup>areembedded in</sup> County Code,<sup>future</sup> expansions<sup>of non-standardized audit</sup> activity become easier to justify, further eroding accountability and uniform oversight. 

This approach undermines internal controls, weakens public confidence, and increases the likelihood of disputes over audit scope, methodology, and conclusions. It also invites challenges regarding<sup>selective</sup> enforcement, political influence,<sup>andlack of due</sup> process—particularly<sup>where</sup> audit-adjacent<sup>activitiesare</sup> performed<sup>outside</sup> accepted<sup>standards.</sup> 

### Inconsistent and Selective Adoption of Standards 

While the bill repeatedly references the Global Internal Audit Standards, it adopts them selectively and inconsistently. Professional standards are not modular tools to be applied at convenience; they<sup>are</sup> comprehensive<sup>frameworks</sup> designed<sup>toensure</sup> independence, objectivity, consistency, and credibility. 

Partial adoption with express carve-outs does not strengthen the audit function—it weakens it.

For the reasons Stated above, Legislative Bill 2025-14 is legally and structurally flawed. Accordingly,<sup>|vetothisbill.</sup> | remain willing to work with the County Council on legislation that fully and consistently aligns the County’s internal audit function with recognized professional standards while preserving true independence and accountability.
