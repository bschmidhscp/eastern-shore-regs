Mr. Andrew Illuminati, Esq., and Ms. Tracey Taylor, Planning Director, came before Council.
Mr. Cannon said these are new legislative bills from the State of Maryland that are sort of imposing
a lot of additional regulations, and actually creating a lot of changes in our local zoning code that
are going to potentially impact neighborhoods, families, and property values to a certain degree.
He then asked for a quick overview for the public as to what the major changes exactly are.
Ms. Taylor explained, as part of the State’s effort to address the housing shortage in the State of
Maryland and also the housing affordability issue, one of the pieces of legislation that was passed
in the 2025 Legislative Session was Senate Bill 891/House Bill 1466, which basically preempts
local zoning authority and requires local governments to allow for accessory dwelling units in any
zoning district that allows for single family dwelling units by right. She said we were fortunate in the
sense that Wicomico County already had a section in the zoning code titled Accessory Apartments,
so we Sort of remanufactured that section of the code and changed the Accessory Apartment

<!-- page 2 -->
Section to reflect what is required in State law for accessory dwelling units. She said we retitled it,
we adopted the definition that is in State law with respect to accessory dwelling units, and also a
single family dwelling unit definition, which we thought met our needs better than ours did. She
said then we recrafted Section 225-60 that was formerly Accessory Apartments to Accessory
Dwelling Units, and quite frankly, for the most part, everything that you’re seeing that we’ve
recrafted in that section is consistent with what State law requires us to do.
Ms. Taylor added that it is also worth mentioning that accessory dwelling units do not count against
the density in a zoning district. She said that is specific in State law, so | think that is something that
needs to be mentioned as well.
Ms. Taylor said the State law had some parking requirements. She explained, if you did not have
parking requirements for accessory dwelling units, or if you were looking to increase the parking
requirements for accessory dwelling units, then you were required by law to do a parking study. She
said again, fortunately for us, we already had a parking standard for accessory apartments, so we
felt that exempted us from having to conduct a parking study.
Ms. Taylor added that another very important point is that State law says that the accessory
dwelling unit can be up to 75 percent of the square footage of the primary dwelling unit. She said
because it says “up to,” that gave us some flexibility and we elected to place the square footage no
more than 50 percent of the square footage of the primary single family unit on the property. She
said the reason we did that was because we felt that the accessory dwelling unit should still give
the appearance to be subordinate to the principle dwelling, and it should not look like there are two
primary dwellings on an unsubdivided parcel, and we felt, in looking at our numbers and crafting
this legislation, that 50 percent was adequately generous for an accessory dwelling unit.
Ms. Taylor said aside from that, we changed the table of permitted uses. She said anywhere that
accessory apartment previously was only allowed by special exception, you will see in that section
itis now a permitted use, and that is consistent with the zones where single family dwelling units
are allowed as permitted uses. She said we specifically did not allow, and the law doesn’t require
us to have, accessory dwelling units associated with multifamily in any way, so this is strictly for
single family—not duplex, not townhome, not multifamily—single family only.

Introduction: Legislative Bill No. 2026-11 -AN EMERGENCY ACT to add Chapter 49 of the
Wicomico County Code titled “Nursing Home” to establish regulations on the operation of the
Wicomico County Nursing Home, the appointment of a Director for the Nursing Home, and the
establishment of
a Nursing Home Commission.
Mr. Benson said Legislative Bill No. 2026-11 is being introduced as emergency action pursuant to
Charter section 311(g), which authorizes the County Councilto declare an action to be an
emergency measure affecting public health, safety, or welfare in
a matter which has suddenly and
unexpectedly arisen which requires immediate action in the public interest.
Mr. Benson said In the days priorto the Council meeting of August 4, 2026, Council begin to receive
calls, letters and emails regarding the operation of the Wicomico Nursing Home, including at least
one letter from a Delegate that represents the area where the nursing home is located. As a result
of the sudden public concern, Council held a closed work session to discuss the nursing home
before the August 4" meeting. During public comments at the same meeting, Council heard from
numerous individuals that raised serious allegations regarding the current operation of the nursing
home, including from recently-fired long-term employees of the nursing home that suggested that
conditions at the nursing home were endangering the current patients.
Mr. Benson said following the meeting, Mrs. Hurley and | began to research the status of the
nursing home. Mrs. Hurley found a 1972 Legislative Bill Chapter 5, Bill 5 that had been enacted by

<!-- page 5 -->
the then County Council. That bill authorized the County Council to acquire the nursing home and
it further authorized the County Councilto maintain and operate the nursing home. The bill
authorized the County Council to employ or to authorize the employment of personnel to operate
the home. Since the enactment of Legislative Bill Chapter 5, Bill 5, it does not appear that any
additional legislative action has been enacted.
Mr. Benson said we know that Wicomico Nursing Home is a wholly-owned entity of Wicomico
County with an administrative director, but there is no guidance or law that provides oversight for
the operation of the 102-bed nursing home operated by the County.
Mr. Benson said on August 5, 2026, there was a significant post on Facebook regarding the current
status of the nursing home, and in one of those threads, the County Executive stated that “we have
no say to operations of the nursing home” and added “I’m sorry, but the County can only intervene
where we are asked.”
Mr. Benson said at the agenda meeting for today’s meeting, the President and Vice President of the
Council determined that the situation at the nursing home and the confusion regarding oversight of
the nursing home had suddenly and unexpectedly arisen and needed to be addressed as soon as
possible since the operating conditions of the nursing home affected public health and safety, in
particular for the patients at the nursing home. The current bill was drafted to enact a new County
code section to recognize that the nursing home is an entity operated by the County.
Mr. Benson said the emergency legislation recognizes that, since 1972 there has been a Nursing
Home Administrator that has responsibility for the operation of the nursing home. The bill
authorizes the County Executive to appoint the administrator with the approval of the County
Council. The legislation sets forth responsibilities of the nursing home administrator, including that
the administrator shall meet with the County Council at least quarterly to report on the operation
and condition of the home.
Mr. Benson said the emergency legislation creates a nursing home commission to advise the
County Executive and the County Council on the operation of the nursing home. The commission
will be made up of various professionals and interested individuals and will meet at least monthly
at the nursing home. The nursing home administrator is mandated to cooperate and meet with the
commission. The commission is mandated to report to the County Executive and the County
Council at least once a year on the condition of the nursing home.
Mr. Benson said the bill also mandates that a personnel system for the nursing home shall be
recommended by the County Executive and established by the County Council.
Mr. Benson said to enact this bill on an emergency basis, the Council must determine that a
sudden and unexpected emergency has arisen that affects public safety and welfare, and must
enact the bill by an affirmative vote of five members.
