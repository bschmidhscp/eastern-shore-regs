He said despite the other disappointment the deputies felt over the Council vote, the only legal and logical course of action was to file for arbitration. He said this unforeseen burden by the Council’s vote on May 21 and the subsequent impasse process has caused the deputies to incur thousands of dollars in legal fees, uncertainty, and stress. He said this process has also cost the taxpayers, which includes your deputies, most of which live and pay taxes in this County, thousands of dollars in unnecessary legal fees paid to the County’s outside labor attorney, the County Council’s Attorney, the arbitrators, the arbitrator fees, and over $7,000 solely for an actuary study to supplement the existing pension study. 

Mr. Funk said the public should know that this contract as proposed was extremely reasonable. He said the deputies hesitantly accepted roughly a 1 percent raise in order to receive an increased retirement benefit. He said this benefit was also extremely reasonable as the benefit would still be far inferior to the benefits of the Statewide LEOP system, which is enjoyed by nearly every other surrounding agency. He said additionally, deputies agreed to almost wholly fund the retirement benefit through agreeing to increase their own pension contributions to a greater contribution rate than that of the State LEOP system. He said in short, deputies agreed to pay more for less and also agreed to a salary increase less than the 4 percent we now know every other County employee is receiving. He said despite the reasonable sacrifices the deputies made in this proposal, the Council still elected to reject the proposed contract. He said the deputies are now asking for this Council to take appropriate action by tabling this vote or rejecting the proposal and allowing this matter to proceed to a third-party arbitrator for a decision as outlined by the County Charter. He said due to the cost, uncertainties, and anxiety this process has caused, the CBU feels this is the only appropriate course of action. He said it is also the position of the CBU that this is the only legally sound decision the Council can make as it is the position of the CBU that the Council no longer has standing to vote on the matter as the CBU has already filed for impasse and both the County Executive’s office and the CBU have submitted final position statements to the arbitrator. 

Mr. Funk said, in closing, I will again respectfully request you honor the recommendation of the deputies and the legal process as outlined by the County Charter and allow this matter to be decided by the arbitrator, which will ensure the deputies in the County do not face the potential for an extended legal battle in court.

# Update on Collective Bargaining Agreement: 

Mr. Benson said I know the Council is well aware of all of this, but for the public’s benefit, the voters of Wicomico County authorized collective bargaining in Wicomico County, and as part of that authorization, the County Charter mandated that the County Council adopt a Code provision, which is Section 59 of the Wicomico County Code that details the requirements with regard to how collective bargaining is to be negotiated. He said, under that provision, the negotiations are to ensue to allow an 

8

<!-- page 9 -->
agreement to be reached on or before April 1. He said, if an agreement is not reached by April 1, the Council is supposed to be notified and the Council is required to approve or disapprove the CBA by May 1 of the year in which the CBA is negotiated. 

Mr. Benson said, in this year, the agreement was not reached. He said the Council was not provided with a copy by April 1 and the Council recognized that there was a timing problem. He said on April 26 the Council unilaterally acted to extend the time period in which it had to act, which under the Code they could extend until May 15. He said there is some language in that Code Section that provides that there can be a ten-day window after May 15 within which things can be done. 

Mr. Benson said on May 2, the Council was finally provided with a copy of the proposed CBA. He said the Council acted to hold a closed work session to review the CBA on May 7. He said, during that closed session, it requested that an actuarial report be done specifically with regard to the provision in the CBA providing a 2.5 percent cost of living increase to the Sheriff’s deputies. 

Mr. Benson said on May 21, the Council came back in a closed work session and was told that, while the actuarial report had been requested, it could not be provided by the June 21 date, but rather would be provided before the end of the month of June. He said on May 21, because they had to act no later than May 25, the Council voted to not approve the CBA. 

Mr. Benson said, as a result of that, under the Code provision, the Council was supposed to designate a representative to notify the parties, the County Executive and the FOP, of the reasons the agreement was not accepted. He said the County’s designated representative provided a written statement that the problem had to do with the 2.5 percent COLA and that Council felt that it could not approve it without having received an actuarial report. He said, under the Code provision, the FOP has the right but is not mandated to declare an impasse. He said the FOP elected to declare an impasse, which means that it moves through an arbitration procedure, and under those provisions, an arbitrator has been selected and there is arbitration scheduled for July 1 in a few days. 

Mr. Benson said where we are today is that you have received an actuarial report yesterday at about 3:30 p.m. He said Council has reviewed that in a closed work session and your options today, I thought before the Session that the options were two, and that was to either agree to the CBA or not agree to the CBA after you have had an opportunity to review the actuarial report. He said, during the prior open session, the FOP during public comments, and also through a written letter from the FOP’s attorney slightly earlier, requested that the Council table any decision with regard to this, so I guess there are three options presented to the Council at this point. He said they can vote to approve the CBA as presented, they can vote to not approve it, or they can vote to table it, and that is the situation where we find ourselves at this moment. 

# Laura Hurley, Council Administrator
