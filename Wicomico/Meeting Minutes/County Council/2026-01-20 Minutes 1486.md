Mr. Benson said just to be clear, the courts are not saying Council can’t begin its session with a prayer or
invocation; what the courts have said in this situation are that Council cannot begin the session with
Council-led prayer, and that Council-led prayer cannot be of one religion or one faith, so Council has
options and will discuss those during the work session as to whether they want to continue some form
of prayer or invocation. He clarified, the issue legally is not whether you want to do that, the issue is that
Council cannot have Council-led prayer that is of only one faith. He said the Supreme Court addressed
this in a case called Town of Greece. He said in that case from 2008, the Supreme Court held that an
invocation or prayer was legal, but in the Town of Greece case, the town process was that they had a
volunteer group of pastors who came in on a rotating basis and the pastor said the prayer. He clarified,
the pastor-led prayer was not one that the Council had any control over and the prayer was not limited
solely to the Christian faith; in fact, in the Greece case, it involved predominantly Christian faith, but it
could include the Jewish faith, Muslim faith, Bahai faith, and in at least one instance, an individual asked
to give the prayer and was allowed to give the prayer who was a Wiccan that the court said was valid.
Mr. Benson said Wicomico County is in Maryland and Maryland is part of the Fourth Judicial Circuit. He
explained, the United States is divided up | think in 13 Judicial Circuits or court of appeals, and we are in
the Fourth Judicial Circuit. He said the Fourth Judicial Circuit addressed this issue in 2014 in a case called
Lund, and in that case, the Councilmembers gave a prayer and rotated the prayer among themselves,
but it was Council-led prayer, and in that instance, all the prayer was Christian. He said in the Lund case,
the court established four principles that it would use in reviewing such cases: 1) Are the
Councilmembers the sole prayer givers; 2) Is the prayer of one faith; 3) Is there an invitation to
attendees to participate; 4) Is the setting a local intimate setting. He said the distinction in that meaning
of local intimate setting means is it a local governmental body as opposed to the United States Congress
or a state legislature. He explained, the courts have drawna distinction between the United States
Congress and state legislatures and local governmental bodies like the County Council, and in the Lund
case, the court said intimate settings of local government meetings present the heightened potential for
coercion as compared to loftier state legislatures or Congress in as much as citizens are both able to and
often must attend local meetings to protect their private rights, advocate for their preferred causes, and
maintain accountability of their elected officials. He clarified, what may be legal for the United States
Congress and what may be legal for the State of Maryland Legislature is not necessarily what is legal or
permitted by the courts for the Wicomico County Council.
