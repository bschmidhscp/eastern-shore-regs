The Wicomico County Council met in Legislative Session on Tuesday, January<sup>2,</sup> 2024 at 6:00<sup>p.m.in</sup> Council Chambers, Government Office Building, Salisbury, Maryland. 

In attendance: John T. Cannon, Council President; Jeff Merritt, Vice President; Joe Holloway, James Winn, Shane Baker, Shanie Shields, and Josh Hastings. 

Present: Laura Hurley, Council Administrator; Lynn lngold, Executive Office Associate, and Robert<sup>A.</sup> Benson, Council Attorney. 

On motion by Ms. Shields and seconded by Mr. Merritt, the Consent Agenda consisting of the following items was unanimously approved: 

- Legislative Minutes from December 19, 2023 

- Open Work Session Minutes from November 7, 2023 — Discussion on Proposed Fire Service Agreement

# Laura Hurley, Council Administrator 

Mrs. Hurley said at least once a year in the Council office we like to do some cross training, so with Mr. Cannon’s permission, I will have Mrs. Lynn Ingold read the agenda items this evening. 

Public Hearing on the purchase of real property known as 1401 Mt. Hermon Road, Salisbury, Maryland for the use of the Wicomico County Health Department in the purchase price of $3,650,000. 

Mrs. Ingold said a Public Hearing notice was posted on the County’s website and published in the local newspaper for each of the Public Hearings this evening. 

Mr. Matthew McConaughey, Health Officer; Mr. Bunky Luffman, Director of Administration; Ms. Julie Giordano, County Executive, and Mr. John McClellan with SVN Miller came before Council. Mr. Cannon suggested they give a brief overview for the public before the Public Hearing in case anything you might say would cause anyone to make a comment. 

Mr. McClellan said I wanted to give the public an opportunity to hear more about the proposed purchase of the Verizon building at 1401 Mt. Hermon Road and I have a few pictures. He said, for anyone who has been in Salisbury for any amount of time, driving by they have seen the property, although you may have noticed recently that the Verizon sign is gone and now the remnants of Bell Atlantic are underneath. He said the property is located with visibility on Route 50 East and West. He said there are 5.6 acres total and a 34,000 square foot building. Referring to his slide, he said you can see the outline of the proposed property that is actually under contract with the County at this point. He said the building and the parking lot surrounding it comprise about 250 parking spaces, a 34,000 square foot building, and there are three additional separately deeded parcels which can be used for expansion or ultimately could be sold off if that was ever the decision of the Council.

Mr. McClellan said, as mentioned, it is 34,000 square feet on two levels with elevator access, multiple stairs, and multiple entrances that could allow for separate functions. He said there is steel and concrete construction. He said the building has kind of a unique feature for this area with duplicate data fields, one from Mt. Hermon Road and one from Route 50, so should there ever be a failure of one feed, the building would not lose internet service. He said there is a new in 2020 HVAC system. He said the roof is approximately seven years old and we have had it inspected which I will touch on in a few minutes. He said there is an on-site generator sized to permit ongoing operations and the property is served by City water and sewer. 

Mr. McClellan said the contract price at $3,650,000 is $108 per square foot. He said our recent experience will tell you that new construction would be somewhere between $250 and $300 a square foot plus the cost of land as construction has really escalated, as you all know, over the last couple of years. He said this was built custom for Bell Atlantic in 1989, actually it was built for the Chesapeake and CMP Telephone Company. He said the building also has 260 parking spaces. He said Verizon has largely vacated the building and you may recall that, when we were there, the building was full of cubicles and other office equipment, and once the train started and they decided to move the cubicles, there was no stopping them, but their lease does run through October 31, 2025 and they are required to pay rent for that amount of time. He said the County can collect the rental income during the planning process working with the Health Department as they move forward with their transition. 

Mr. McClellan said also they are considering a donation. He explained,<sup>I</sup> was there this morning and I didn’t count them, but there are 150+ monitors, computers and keyboards, and they are considering donating those versus the labor and cost to take those somewhere. He said, frankly, if half of them work, maybe they can use them at the Health Department or someone somewhere else in the County could use monitors. He said they are nice, flat screen, fairly large monitors and could be very useful. 

Mr. McClellan said also we learned there is a backup power system tied into their entire building for all their computers. He said they looked at taking it and would like to use it, but it is too costly to move and disconnect, so it is going to stay and that is a very powerful asset. He clarified, I don’t have a price tag, but it’s definitely a bonus. 

Mr. McClellan said we have been through some due diligence, as you know, and the property went under contract on December 22 and we have until February 20 for 60 days due diligence and closing could occur any time before March 21. He said the inspections we have completed so far include a roof inspection, HVAC, elevator, structural, plumbing, electrical, generator, and an environmental phase 1. He said the environmental phase 1 and electrical and structural are not back, but I have been indicated by all of them verbally that there are no problems. He said Pate Matthews has reviewed all of the inspection reports to date and there is nothing that he considers a glaring problem. He said it is like a home inspection where you are going to find something, but nothing major. 

Mr. McClellan said we are requesting a tenant estoppel from Verizon, which is just a document that says a lease is in affect and they are obligated to pay rent, which is obviously an important factor.<sup>He</sup> said I have circulated a contract to Ms. Giordano and I will get one to Mr. Wilber regarding SVN Management managing the building so the County doesn’t really have any hassles. He explained, we will collect the rent, handle all the maintenance, handle the inspections, and respond to any problems for $1,000 a month which would be covered as part of the tenant’s operating expenses they pay into, so it would be no real cost to the County for doing that, but it would be one less thing you would have to worry about.

Mr. McClellan said one of the discussions of course is what we would do<sup>if</sup> they vacate the<sup>Hurdle</sup> building. He said we have inspected the property initially and are preparing a comprehensive analysis and marketing proposal for the Council to review at a future date. He said we<sup>will</sup> list the<sup>propertyina</sup> widespread marketing campaign to create competition and avoid any appearances of impropriety or dealing with one person as we believe in marketing properties on a widespread basis. 

Mr. Winn said, just for clarification for the public, the Hurdle building<sup>is</sup> where<sup>theHealthDepartmentis</sup> currently, to which Mr. McClellan responded, yes, at 108 E. Main Street, and that could be marketed. He said we are big believers in transparent marketing, so you are not going to deal with just one<sup>person,</sup> you are going to have competition, hopefully. He said we have identified several local<sup>andregional</sup> prospects, and, in fact, one heard about it today through this Public Hearing and wanted to know what was going on, and that was from Easton, so there is definitely some interest in the property.<sup>He</sup> said we have not concluded our evaluation and believe the Hurdle building coupled with the Verizon rental surplus will lead to a very reduced net acquisition cost for the Verizon building, especially compared to any new construction. 

Mr. Cannon said, just so the public realizes, the Councilmembers along with the Executive have toured the location as well, and we talked to Mr. McConaughey about it as far as how he thinks<sup>itwill</sup> fit within his program, so we are pretty positive about what we are looking at here. 

Mr. Hastings suggested doing an explanation for the public who may not fully understand<sup>whyweare</sup> going through this process. He said I know we talked about this in Closed Session, but maybe we need to talk about why we are looking at a different building. Mr. Cannon said they are looking to try to consolidate some with what they have at ES Adkins and give themselves a little more room than what there currently is in the Hurdle building. 

Mr. Hastings said we have had a lot of complaints over time with the buildings we currently utilize that either they are not the right capacity or about potential health concerns, so that is what I think helped bring all of this about to try to think through that. 

Mr. McClellan said the one big thing you are going to have is the efficiency of the layout. He said you have two, essentially, wide open 17,000 foot floors versus Hurdle that is 30,000 square feet and kind of on three floors of 10,000, which is just inherently less efficient from a layout standpoint. 

Mr. Holloway asked if they got appraisals, to which Mr. McClellan responded, I did, I just forgot to mention that as I just got it this afternoon. He said the property has appraised at $3,920,000. Mr. Cannon asked if he is talking about the Hurdle building, to which Mr. McClellan responded, no, the Verizon building. He said we do not have our valuations done on Hurdle. He said, if you have done any commercial appraisals, they get very conservative and they get very worrisome to value it at any higher than a purchase price, and that is kind of the history of banking. He said, if you remember 15 years ago, appraisals were kind of crazy and led to an economic collapse because appraisals were overstated. He said the one thing that is not factored into this appraisal is there should be some rental surplus. He said operating expenses are paid at a fixed rate and not reconciled, which more than cover the cost of the operating costs, and, as a result, the County should see a surplus, especially for a non-occupied building as you don’t have to clean it. He said, even if it snows, you aren’t going to plow all 260 spaces, you are going to plow firetruck access to the doors typically. 

Mr. Holloway asked if there is any estimate on the cost of retrofitting to make it ready for employees, to which Mr. McConaughey responded, I have not talked with an engineer or architect yet in regards to

that, but the way it is structured, there are concrete and steel structural columns throughout the building, so everything else can pretty much be retrofitted as long as you don’t mess with those<sup>integral</sup> structural pieces. He said it should be easily retrofitted with temporary walls, cubicle spaces, or with drywall fittings and studs. He said it should be easily completed once we sit down and review what space is needed and how it will be designed for efficient flow and activity. 

Mr. Holloway asked if Mr. McConaughey has any prices on desks and office equipment, to which Mr. McConaughey responded, mainly we probably would bring a lot of that equipment from our current facilities, so we already have that in place. He clarified, we wouldn’t try to get all new when we have what’s good for us now. 

Mr. Holloway clarified, we are going to get rent from Verizon, to which Mr. McClellan<sup>responded,</sup> yes. Mr. Holloway asked, when you take that off the purchase price, what is our price going to be, to which Mr. McClellan responded, the total revenue from Verizon starting January<sup>1is</sup> $1.7 million dollars.<sup>Mr.</sup> Holloway clarified, that is what they are going to pay the County, to which<sup>Mr.</sup> McClellan responded, yes. He said, out of that, I project about $300,000 of expenses. He said Verizon also continues to pay the real estate taxes on the building and the parking lots, and the County would, assuming you<sup>want totake</sup> them off the tax rolls, undertake the vacant lots. He said right now the landlord pays those taxes, but they are a minor part of that. He said, in round numbers, $1.4 million would be the net revenue to the County, so you are going to go in with about a $2.2 million dollar purchase price on a 34,000 square foot building with five acres, and that is a pretty good deal. 

Mr. Winn said I wanted to state the parking situation. He said we are going to have 260 parking spots and we need that many downtown right now, so we are going to save a lot of money in parking alone and alleviate some of the parking issues we have downtown right now. 

Ms. Giordano added, we will also save money because of the consolidation from ES Adkins. She explained, we pay rent to ES Adkins and we will be pulling that as well. 

Mr. McConaughey added, there are parking spaces that we currently pay for in the parking garage and that will no longer be an expenditure either. 

Mr. Winn clarified, I just wanted to state that so the public knows what is going on. 

Mr. Merritt said this is also going to increase access and ability to provide services to the public, to which Mr. McConaughey responded, yes. He said, with consolidating two facilities, we will be able to better provide a multitude of services instead of individual and needing multiple rides to different destinations, so we can provide more services at one location. He said we will also be on the Shore Transit route that runs down that road already, so there are transportation avenues. He said we have already talked internally about how we can structure a base to satellite transportation for any clients that may have transportation issues and how to get them from one facility to the other. 

Mr. Cannon opened the Public Hearing. There were no public comments. Mr. Cannon closed the Public Hearing. 

Mr. Cannon asked if this will come back on the next agenda, to which Mrs. Hurley responded, if all the inspections come back favorable to the County then we can certainly move forward with the Resolution. 

Mr. Cannon said we will wait to see how things move forward and then we will be glad to get this on the agenda as soon as possible.

Public Hearing: Resolution No. 01-2024 — Amending the FY24 Budget Position Control Document (Exhibit F) and the Pay and Classification Plan to create the position and title of a Court<sup>Technologistin</sup> the Circuit Court for Wicomico County, Maryland. 

Mr. Cannon opened the Public Hearing. There were no public comments.<sup>Mr.CannonclosedthePublic</sup> Hearing. 

Mrs. Melissa Lahey, Court Administrator, came before Council and said this actually<sup>iseliminatingthree</sup> current positions and creating a new position. She said we will have the retirement<sup>of ourlastfulltime</sup> real-time transcriptionist in the Circuit Court and that is sort of the way of the judiciary at this point. She said we have been working toward this point over the last few years to increase the technology<sup>to</sup> pick up the making of the record in the courthouse. She said this new position<sup>willtakeover thefunction</sup> both of the technology side making sure that the digital recording system<sup>is</sup> up and<sup>runningatalltimes</sup> that we have an accurate record and will also assign out the transcripts that have to be<sup>made.She</sup> explained, it is still the case in Maryland that an audio transcript is not an official record, so we have to send those out to transcriptionists. She said parties pay for those transcriptions as well. She said this individual will handle requests for transcripts, hire those out, make sure the system is working, and they will also assist with additional technologies like remote appearances, Zoom calls, and that<sup>kindof thing.</sup> 

Mr. Cannon clarified, so instead of having someone in the actual courtroom typing the<sup>dictation,they</sup> won’t exist, they will be more behind the scenes where it is recorded and they will just<sup>simplyhavea</sup> recording, to which Mrs. Lahey responded, sadly, yes. She said we are sad to see our court reporters go, but it is the way that the industry has gone. Mr. Cannon said hopefully they<sup>will</sup> accept<sup>anaudio</sup> recording and I don’t know why they don’t start accepting audio recordings, to which Mrs. Lahey responded, we are not there yet, but it is in the Maryland Rules that any official record for<sup>appealhas</sup> to be a written transcript. She said, again, that is the other function of this job, to make sure that<sup>the</sup> communication between our external transcriptionists and the court are handled. 

There being no further discussion, on motion by Mr. Merritt and seconded by Mr. Winn, Resolution No. 01-2024 was unanimously approved.
