ZONING RECOMMENDATION FOR ANNEXATION — N. SCHUMAKER DRIVE ANNEXATION — N. Schumaker Drive - Wicomico County R-20 Residential - M-0048, G-0006, P-0417 - (Z. White) 

Mr. Zack White and Ms. Betsy Jackson approached the table. Mr. White presented the Staff Report. 

The City Department of Infrastructure and Development has referred the North Schumaker Drive annexation to the Planning Commission for review and recommendation of an appropriate zoning designation. The property is located on North Schumaker Drive directly to the east of the development known as “Granby's Cove.” The total area of the lot is 12.870 acres, which includes the road and the parcel that stretches into Schumaker Pond. Approximately 0.28 acres of this total area will be allocated to the pond. 

The Planning Staff recommended that the parcel referred to as the “Lands of Nicholas Fenzel” be zoned R10-S upon annexation into the City limits. 

Mr. Chris Pfeifer, from George, Miles, & Buhr, LLC, and Mr. Brian Lloyd, the Developer, approached the table. Mr. Pfeifer said they are seeking an R8-A zoning so they can build townhomes on this site. The site is located in the low-density residential designation on the future land use map, which allows for four to five units per acre. He believes townhomes in this location would provide more affordable housing in the city. 

Mr. Lloyd mentioned he was born and raised in the area. He takes a lot of value and stock in what we do and what we're building. He is interested in this property because he saw the opportunity to build affordable housing. Our average household income in this community is still $62,000 per year, making it challenging to afford a $400,000 home. At $269,000 or $279,000 for townhomes, we believe this presents an opportunity for our teachers, law enforcement, firefighters, and hardworking families in our community to afford anew home. It will 100% be $100,000 more to buy a house in this neighborhood if we build single-family homes. There will be a dog park and a playground for children to play in. We are going to undertake extensive landscaping that will transform this into a neighborhood, not just a complex. 

Three individuals from the public requested to share their thoughts: 

- « Ms. Carolyn Wohigemuth: She lives at 1118 Granbys Run, which is in the community of Granby's Cove, and adjacent to this lot. Opposed. The City should not grant a zoning designation that would allow overdevelopment of this property at the expense of and detriment to the current adjacent city residents and the City's own valuable resource, Schumaker Pond. She proposed that the property be zoned R-10, which is compatible with existing low-density residential single-family homes along the north bank of Schumaker Pond. 

- e Mr. Ken Medearis: He lives at 821 Long Wharf Road backs up to the proposed property. Opposed. He has environmental concerns. He believes that higher-density development will bring about a significantly higher impervious surface and increased runoff to an already impaired Schumaker Pond. The pond is already in a fairly degraded state. Also, if sixty new townhomes are developed, there must be a way to ensure sufficient flow for all of the newly required in-home fire suppression systems. 

- ¢ Mr. Michael Langford: He lives at 1026 Arthur Court. Opposed. He believes the proposed townhomes cannot be considered affordable housing as defined by HUD. He said more thought should be given to this project before making a decision.

CITY OF SALISBURY ANNUAL REPORT for CY 2024 - Planning Commission Review for Submittal to the MD Department of Planning - (B. Jackson) 

It will not be official until it is scheduled before the City Council. Ms. Jackson presented the City’s Annual Report for 2024. 

The Maryland Department of Planning (“MDP”) requests that each local jurisdiction report significant growth changes and metrics as required by the General Assembly under the Land Use Article. The Planning Commission is tasked with reviewing the document and deciding whether to pass it along to the City Council and MDP with its approval. 

Section one of the report reflects the number of new residential building permits issued within the City of Salisbury municipal limits. In calendar year 2024, 171 new construction residential building permits were issued within the Priority Funding Area ("PFA"). This represents 32 more permits than last year and includes a mix of single-family, townhomes, and multi-family units.

Section two addresses amendments to municipal codes, boundaries, maps, and service areas. The calendar year 2024 included a small expansion of the corporate limits, as detailed in the two annexation resolutions. The only amendments to the Code were the issuance of ordinance permits and a zoning code amendment to regulate cannabis businesses. 

Section three concerns the Development Capacity Analysis ("DCA"). The DCA estimate is based on data from the 2010 comprehensive plan. 

Sections lV and VI do not apply to the City of Salisbury. 

Section five highlights the amount of residential growth inside and outside of the Priority Funding Area ("PFA"). Within CY2024, 2 major subdivisions with a total of 428 single-family lots were approved, all located within the PFA. This is 343 more lots than CY2023. This section also includes commercial growth metrics. In CY2024, a total of 10 commercial site plans were approved, covering 283,795 square feet of gross building area. This brings the total to 4 more commercial site plans approved, with a gross building area that is more than double that of CY2023. 

Chairman Dashiell requested a motion to approve the report as presented and then forward it to the City Council for their review and endorsement. Mr. Drew entered a motion to approve the 2024 Annual Report. The motion was seconded by Mr. Holloway and duly carried.
