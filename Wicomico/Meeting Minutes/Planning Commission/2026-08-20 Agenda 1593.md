**PUBLIC HEARING – ZONING TEXT AMENDMENT – An Act to amend Chapter 225 of the Wicomico County Code, titled “Zoning,” to establish and revise regulations pertaining to Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs) and related provisions as necessary to comply with the requirements of Acts of Maryland 2025, Chapter 196 (Maryland Senate Bill 891), amending the Land Use and Real Property Articles of the Annotated Code of Maryland to regulate Accessory Dwelling Units. (T. Taylor)** 

The Public Hearing began at 4:05 p.m. 

Ms. Gabby Carr read the Public Hearing notice. 

Ms. Tracey Taylor provided a brief overview of the Planning Director’s Report for the Zoning Text Amendment. Chapter 225 of the Zoning Code pertains to Accessory Dwelling Units (“ADUs”) and Maryland Senate Bill 891. This is State legislation and is directed at affordable housing options and providing opportunities for affordable housing. The County must comply with this legislation by October 1, 2026, by permitting ADUs by-right in our Zoning Ordinance for any zone where single-family dwellings are allowed by-right. Upon receiving the Planning Commission’s recommendation, this will be presented to the County Council. 

The Staff worked with Mr. Andrew Illuminati and Ms. Carr to draft the Text Amendment. Since a section already existed in the County’s Zoning Code that addressed Accessory Apartments, staff had the ability to use Section  225-60 and modify the Accessory Apartments section to become the Accessory Dwelling Unit section. The Senate Bill allows some flexibility in the percentage an ADU may occupy, based on the square footage of the primary dwelling on a site. The State allows up to 75% of the primary dwelling; Wicomico County has chosen to allow up to 50%. For comparison purposes, Worcester County kept its percentage at 35%,  and Somerset County put its percentage at 75%. 

The County staff felt the definition of a Dwelling Unit in the Senate Bill was better suited than the one previously noted in the Code. The proposed definition for a Dwelling Unit is “A single unit providing complete living facilities for at least one individual, at a minimum, provisions for sanitation, cooking, eating, and sleeping.” 

Staff recommended advancing a favorable recommendation to forward the proposed Zoning Text Amendments, as outlined in the Planning Director’s Report and detailed in Exhibit A, to the Wicomico County Council for their review and action based upon the following: 

1) The proposed legislation is required per State law (SB 891) passed in Maryland’s 2025 Legislative Session. 

2) The State law (SB 891) is preemptive legislation which becomes effective October 1, 2026; and 3) The proposed text amendments constitute revisions made to the existing “Accessory Apartment” provisions of the current County Zoning Code to address the new ADU requirements of SB 891. 

As there were no additional comments, Chairman Dashiell requested a motion to advance a favorable recommendation of the Proposed Zoning Text Amendment as outlined in the Planning Director’s Report and detailed in Exhibit A of the Staff Report to the Wicomico County Council for their review and action based on the three statements listed. Mr. Thomas moved to approve, which was seconded by Ms. Bynum-King, and the motion duly carried. 

Chairman Dashiell stated the motion was **APPROVED.** 

The Public Hearing ended at 4:52 p.m.

##### **<mark>WORK SESSION - WICOMICO COUNTY COMPREHENSIVE PLAN UPDATE – SWOT Analysis and Draft Survey (Wallace Montgomery Project Team)</mark>** 

##### **<mark>The Work Session began at 4:53 p.m.</mark>** 

Chairman Dashiell noted that the Comprehensive Plan Update discussion was a Work Session; therefore, public comment would not be accepted during this portion of the meeting. Public comment opportunities will be available through the County website at a future date. 

Ms. Nichole Wiley (Assistant Project Manager) with Wallace Montgomery approached the table. Ms. Wiley thanked the Commissioners for providing feedback on the survey and SWOT (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats) Analysis. The survey is ready to go live. Ms. Taylor explained that the County would like to do a Press Release once the website is ready. 

Ms. Wiley indicated a website bypass link would be provided to staff for review. The website will be a landing page for the Comp Plan. It will include frequently asked questions, the survey, and draft documents will be uploaded once approved by staff. Staff and the Commissioners agreed that the survey will be available for sixty (60) days to start. However, if the consensus is not yielding enough responses or a variety of respondents, it could be extended for a longer period. 

Ms. Wiley was asked how many survey respondents she anticipated. She thought 500 – 600 responses would be a great target. They will also map respondents so changes can be made if they are all coming from the same area. The changes could include updating the website or offering a QR Code at a sign-in table at events. Ms. Taylor also mentioned that the Planning and Zoning website will include links to the survey and encouraged everyone to post it on their social media pages. 

Ms. Taylor added that the SWOT Analysis and the Survey were emailed out to special interest groups, such as the Historical Commission, Natural Resources Conservation Advisory Committee, TriCounty Council, Chamber of Commerce, Greater Salisbury, Lower Shore Land Trust, Wicomico Environmental Trust, etc. These groups have strong membership and wish to be alerted when the survey goes out; they will spread the word by word of mouth, which will generate many responses. 

Ms. Wiley reviewed the SWOT Analysis with the Commissioners. She mentioned that Strengths can also appear as Threats and Weaknesses. The number one Strength was “Higher Education and Workforce Assets.” The number one Weakness was “Housing Challenges.” The number one Opportunity was “Location.” The number one Threat was “Environmental and Climate Risks.” She also mentioned how important the SWOT activity was and that they received a lot of usable data in return. This information will help to feed the Comp Plan chapters. 

The “Land Use Plan” was moved from September to November. Ms. Taylor explained that the reasoning behind the move was that Staff believed the most significant changes would occur to the land use map, as well as to the land use policies and recommendations. It is best to complete these changes after the election. 

Chairman Dashiell mentioned how helpful Ms. Wiley’s information had been. It helps identify what the overall project will look like. It starts to frame the overall project and how we think about our counties.

##### **I. PLANNING STAFF GENERAL COMMENTS:** 

<u>Summary of Proposal:</u> 

The applicant proposes the subdivision of 21 lots, averaging 27,238 square feet, from this 14.45-acre property near the intersection of Pemberton Drive and Crooked Oak Lane. All lots will front on newly constructed roads as depicted in the subdivision plat (Attachment A). The subject property is in a residential zoning district (R-20) to the rear of the Steeplechase Subdivision, of which this development will be Section Nine. The first draft of this subdivision plat received approval at Sketch Phase at the Planning Commission meeting on May 21, 2026.

<!-- page 20 -->
##### <u>Surrounding Area Development:</u> 

This property is across Pemberton Drive from Pemberton Historical Park, and is otherwise surrounded by single family dwellings (see Attachment B). It is served by the Salisbury Fire Department and is just under nine miles from the Salisbury Airport. Pemberton Elementary, Salisbury Middle, and James M. Bennett High serve this development. It is outside of the Paleochannel Overlay District, the Chesapeake Bay Critical Area, and the mapped floodplain. 

##### **II. INTERAGENCY REVIEW:** 

<u>Health Department:</u> 

The Health Department has reviewed this plat and provided comments to the surveyor. This site has been evaluated for on-site sewage disposal, and is suitable for a residential subdivision. Comments from the Director of Environmental Health are attached to this report. 

##### <u>Department of Public Works Comments:</u> 

Steeplechase Section Nine will be bound by the existing stormwater agreement in place for the Steeplechase Subdivision (Maryland Land Records Liber 2421 Folio 727), which was re-signed on October 3, 2025. A construction plan has been submitted to and approved by the Department of Public Works (DPW), and the pre-construction meeting is complete. Arabian Drive will be extended and improved into a cul-de-sac and Pinto Drive will continue as previously established on the Steeplechase Section Eight plat. Both roads shall be deeded to Wicomico County within 180 days of recordation of the Final plat. 

##### **III. PRELIMINARY/FINAL PLAT REVIEW:** 

Each lot in the proposed subdivision conforms to the Zoning Code minimum standards per the Schedule of District Regulations, §225 Attachment 3:1 and the minimum standards for lot design found in §200-13 “Lots” in the Subdivision Code. The subject property is designated Tier III on the Septic Tier Map, which permits minor and major subdivisions served by private, on-site septic. The 2010 Comprehensive Water and Sewerage Plan (WSP), designates this property as W-2, planned for water service, and to be served by the community water system installed in the Steeplechase subdivision. The WSP designates this property as S-6, not planned for community sewer, and each lot will have a private septic system. 

Steeplechase Section Nine has been reviewed and is in compliance with the Forest Conservation Plan in place for the Steeplechase Subdivision (FCP #2005-05). 

Prior to final recordation, all lots in the subdivision must be included in the Steeplechase Homeowners Association.

<!-- page 21 -->
##### **IV. STAFF RECOMMENDATIONS:** 

Planning Staff recommends that the Planning Commission grant Preliminary and Final approval to this Subdivision Plat, subject to the following conditions: 

1. The Final Plat shall comply with all requirements of the Wicomico County Subdivision Regulations. 

2. All lots will become members of the Steeplechase Homeowners Association and the Articles of Incorporation must be recorded prior to or simultaneously with the recordation of the Final Plat. In addition, a Covenants and Restrictions document shall be recorded in the Land Records of Wicomico County with the Final Plat. 

3. A deed conveying the newly-constructed roads within the subdivision must be provided to Planning and Zoning within 180 days of recordation of the Final Plat. 

4. This approval is subject to further review and approval and conditions imposed by the Planning and Zoning and Public Works Departments.

#### **I. PLANNING STAFF GENERAL COMMENTS:** 

#### <u>Summary of Proposal:</u> 

This plat proposes the creation of one new lot from a 39-acre parcel shown as Parcel 38 on Tax Map 42.  Parcel 38 is the remainder of three previous subdivisions, each of which created one new lot. The previous subdivisions met the criteria of §200-3 “Exclusions” in the Subdivision Section of Wicomico’s County Code and therefore were approved administratively. The subdivision proposed with this plat will create the fourth lot out of the parent parcel since the County’s regulatory date of April 1, 1968, and therefore requires Planning Commission approval.

<!-- page 29 -->
The proposed Lot One will be just under two and a half acres, and includes an existing dwelling and septic system. The lot area, configuration, setbacks, and road frontage meet the criteria of the Subdivision and Zoning chapters of the County Code. The driveway access to the lot is currently shared by the existing home and the agricultural structures to the rear of the remaining parcel (Lot Two). This plat will preserve the shared driveway access by creating an easement on land included in Lot Two for the continued use of Lot One. The shared driveway easement shall be shown on the final subdivision plat and recorded with the subdivision.
