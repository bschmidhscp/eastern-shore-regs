The County Charter requires that the Planning Commission review proposed capital projects in
order to determine “that said projects conform to the Master Plan of [the] County, as to both location
and use.” As defined in the Charter, capital projects include “the building or purchase of any physical
public betterment or improvement or any preliminary studies thereto; the acquisition of property of a
permanent nature; the purchase of equipment for any public betterment or improvement when first
erected or acquired.” The resurfacing of any road is specifically excluded.

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission approve the submitted capital projects as
to their location and use. Many of the projects involve rehabilitation or expansion of existing County
facilities and are consistent with the goals, objectives, and policies outlined in the Wicomico County
Comprehensive Plan, adopted in March 2017.

Ms. Justice added that the Commissioners’ recommendation will be presented to the County
Executive, who will submit the projects to the County Council on or before the third Tuesday in
December.

With no comments, Ms. Sara Bynum-King moved to approve the submitted Capital Projects for
location and use consistent with the Wicomico County Comprehensive Plan. Mr. Thomas seconded
the motion, which was duly carried.

Chairman Dashiell stated the motion was APPROVED.

CRITICAL AREA COMPREHENSIVE REVIEW DISCUSSION (C. Harrison and P. Johnston)
Mr. Colin Harrison and Mr. Peter Johnston, of Johnston & Associates, approached the table.

Mr. Harrison presented the Staff Report for the Chesapeake Bay Critical Area Ordinance
Update, Chapter 125, for Wicomico County. The State of Maryland adopted the Chesapeake Bay
Critical Area (CBCA) legislation in 1984 to improve water quality and protect the Chesapeake Bay and
its tributaries. According to Natural Resources Article 8-1809(g), each local jurisdiction shall
comprehensively review and update its local Critical Area Ordinance every 10 years.

This draft ordinance is a complete overhaul of our existing ordinance. State law has changed
substantially over the years, and our proposal aligns with the State’s model, with some additions to
address local concerns. The County has limited flexibility to modify our Critical Area Ordinance
because State Law prevails. The current draft ordinance and modifications have been reviewed by
Critical Area staff and other members of the Critical Area Commission.

TEXT AMENDMENT - HOUSE BILL 538 HOUSING EXPANSION AND AFFORDABILTY ACT OF 2025 (T. Taylor
and A. Illuminati)

Ms. Taylor thanked Mr. Thomas for bringing this topic to her attention.

The focus is on the impact on the County's Zoning Code regarding manufactured and modular
housing. The Bill states that in any zone where single-family dwellings are allowed by right, we must also
permit modular and manufactured housing, both single-wide and double-wide. This has not been the
normal practice.

Manufactured double-wide homes have been permitted only in the A-1 and V-C Zoning Districts
and TT by Special Exception. Manufactured single-wide homes were permitted only in the A-1 Zoning
District and V-C by Special Exception. Ms. Taylor does not know how this new law will affect
Homeowners Associations (“HOAs”). She has spoken with Mr. Illuminati about this, and the County may
enter a disclaimer on future building permits that says, “The issuance of this building permit does not
exempt you from any provisions of an existing Homeowners Association covenants and restrictions.”
The county does not monitor HOAs.

Mr. Andrew Illuminati indicated he is working on the draft of the Bill. There will be some Text
Amendments, but the main changes will occur in the Zoning Use Chart. State legislation preempts local
zoning authority, and Wicomico County has no authority to modify the language of this law. He added
that the biggest change was eliminating the distinction between single-wide and double-wide. The
State law relies on the federal definition. The State, federal, and County definitions all state that a
manufactured home is a structure transportable in one or more sections, which in the traveling mode
is eight (8) body feet or more in width or forty (40) body feet or more in length or when erected on site
is 320 or more in square feet.

Mr. Illuminati stated that there will be a Public Hearing next month and that a recommendation
will be forwarded to the County Council.

Ms. Tanesha Siggers inquired how this would affect the Historic District. Mr. Illuminati said there is
a qualification that requires a review of the Historic District. He also said the Historic District does have
some protection.
