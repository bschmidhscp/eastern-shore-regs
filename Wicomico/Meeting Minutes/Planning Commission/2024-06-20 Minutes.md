Charles “Chip” Dashiell, Chairman Jim Thomas Daniel Moreno-Holt Matt Drew Mandel Copeland D’Shawn Doughty

PUBLIC HEARING - TEXT AMENDMENT - An Act to amend Chapter 225 of the Wicomico County Code, titled “Zoning” Part 8, titled “Special Standards for Particular Uses,” Article XX, titled “Uses and Standards Enumerated,” Section §225-82.1 titled “Cannabis Businesses” to modify cannabis business zoning regulations in Wicomico County. (A. Illuminati) 

Mr. Andrew Illuminati read the advertisement for the Public Hearing. Ms. Laura Ryan administered the oath to Mr. Illuminati. Mr. Illuminati provided insight into the background concerning passing a Comprehensive Cannabis Bill to regulate adult use. The Bill went into effect on July 1, 2023. He has worked with the Planning Staff to provide the Wicomico County Council with a proposed Text Amendment, under State law. The County Council passed the Amendment and it was signed into legislation by the County Executive that went into effect during the 2024 General Assembly legislative session. 

The proposal that is now before the Planning Commission cleans up the Wicomico County Code regarding regulation. Further detail was discussed in the Planning Director’s Report. 

The Public Hearing concluded at 1:43 p.m. 

WICOMICO COUNTY TEXT AMENDMENT - Planning Director's Report - Cannabis and Cannabis Businesses use (L. Carter) 

Ms. Lori Carter brought to the Commission’s attention, three (3) items. 

1. Permitted Use: A licensed grower cultivating cannabis, exclusively outdoors and in an area zone only for agricultural use shall comport with the State law and the Separation requirements set forth in Wicomico County Zoning Code §225-82.1(A). 

2. Separation: A Cannabis Business may not locate within 500 feet of a pre-existing primary or secondary school in the State, or a pre-existing licensed child care center or registered family child care home; or a pre-existing playground, recreation center, library, place of assembly, or public park. A Cannabis business may not locate within 2,640 feet of another Cannabis Business. A Cannabis Business may not locate within 100 feet of a pre-existing residence. 

3. Board of Appeals: The Wicomico County Board of Appeals may modify the separation requirements in Wicomico County Zoning Code §225-82.1(A) by Special Exception. 

Ms. Carter added, that on April 18, 2024, the Commission completed their review of House Bill (“HB”) 805; then on May 16, 2024, the Commission held a work session during a regularly scheduled meeting; and then today, June 20, 2024, there was a Public Hearing scheduled. The Hearing was advertised and a 14-day review period was extended from June 6" through June 19, 2024. 

As there were no additional comments, Mr. Jim Thomas entered a motion to forward the recommendations contained in the Director’s Report Exhibit A to the County Council. The motion was seconded by Mr. Doughty and duly carried. 

Chairman Dashiell stated the motion was APPROVED.

# WICOMICO COUNTY ANNUAL REPORT for CY 2023 - Planning Commission Review for Submittal to the MD Department of Planning (G. Pusey) 

Mr. Gary Pusey presented the Wicomico County Annual Report for CY 2023. He explained in 2012 the State of Maryland adopted legislation that required every jurisdiction in the state that has Planning and Zoning authority to submit an annual report that summarizes the development that occurred in the previous calendar year. This report is for January 1, 2023, to December 31, 2023. The report comes to the Planning Commission for approval. When approved, the report is sent to the County Executive and County Council for knowledge purposes, and to the Maryland Department of Planning (“MDP”) to combine with all the annual reports submitted by alll jurisdictions. 

The short form was used to gather all the information because we only had 46 building permits issued for single-family homes. MDP needs to know if any of the single-family homes were within the Priority Funding Area (“PFA”). Of the 46, only 6 were within the PFA. 

The State wants to know how many agricultural land preservation acres are being preserved and paid for by the county. Since the county does not have a program we participate in the State’s program, Maryland Agricultural Land Preservation Foundation (“MALPF”). In the calendar year 2023 about 180 Acres were preserved through the MALPF program and another 86 Acres were preserved through the Rural Legacy Area Program. 

After a brief discussion concerning the County’s water and sewer plans, Mr. Thomas entered a motion to approve the 2023 Annual Report. The motion was seconded by Mr. Moreno-Holt and duly carried. 

Chairman Dashiell stated the motion was APPROVED.

ORDINANCE PERMIT- RETAIL CANNABIS DISPENSARY- Cathie Ralph, CEO, Elevated Mind & Wellness, LLC - 733 South Salisbury Boulevard- General Commercial Zoning District - M-0111, G-0023, P-0435 (A. Rodriquez) 

Ms. Rodriquez was joined at the table by Cathy Ralph (Owner and CEO of Elevated Mind & Wellness), Edward Scheirer (Attorney with McAllister, DeTar, Showalter & Walker), and Carl Wilson (with the Traffic Group). Ms. Rodriquez presented the Staff Report.

The applicant, Elevated Mind & Wellness LLC, applied for an Ordinance Permit for a cannabis dispensary to be located at 733 South Salisbury Boulevard (formerly Kitty’s Flowers). The 27,679-squarefoot property is zoned General Commercial and has a 4,753-sf building used as retail. 

Staff presented the General Commercial District Development Standards. These included the Minimum Lot Requirements, Building Setbacks/Spacing, Height, Parking/Loading, Access, Lighting, Sign Plan, Storage, Landscaping and Screening. 

Mr. Scheirer spoke about Cathy’s ability to be a professional businesswoman and her experience in compliance and risk management. 

Mr. Wilson spoke about the traffic study. Patrons would enter the facility from South Salisbury Boulevard and then circulate to Roland Avenue to exit. He believes there are no geometric improvements necessary to support the proposed use. 

Mr. Drew asked Mr. Wilson if they considered entering on Roland Avenue and exiting on South Salisbury Boulevard to alleviate the backup of cars attempting to enter the property. Mr. Doughty was also concerned with traffic in that area and wanted to prepare Mr. Wilson for questions that may arise at the City Council. 

Since the applicant made a considerable effort to address all concerns regarding use before the request, the Planning Staff recommended a favorable recommendation be made to forward this Ordinance Permit application to the City Council. Mr. Thomas entered a motion to forward a favorable recommendation to the City Council for this ordinance application. The motion was seconded by Mr. Drew and duly carried. 

Chairman Dashiell stated the motion was APPROVED.

Rodriquez) Ms. Rodriquez was joined at the table by Mr. Kevin Aydelotte (Parker & Associates), Mr. Andrew Roud (from St. John Properties), and Mr. Matthew Destino (St. John Properties). Included in this Staff Report were the items requested by the Planning Commission at the September 21, 2023 meeting. They included a Community Impact Statement, Final Comprehensive Development Plan, Sign Plan, Development Schedule, Traffic Impact Study, and the Statement of Intent to Proceed and Financial Capability. 

Parker and Associates, on behalf of the owner, has submitted a Final Comprehensive Development Plan (“FCDP”) for the development of four (4) flex/R&D buildings. 

Staff presented the Comprehensive Development Plan Review. These included the Site Plan, Building Elevations/Floor Plans, Sign Plan, Development Schedule, Community Impact Statement,

Statement of Intent to Proceed and Financial Capability, Fire Service, Stormwater Management, Forest Conservation Program, Transportation, Streets and Pedestrians. 

Staff recommended approval of the Final Comprehensive Development Plan for Westwood Commerce Park Block A subject to the ten (10) conditions below. 

1. The site shall be developed in accordance with a Final Comprehensive Development Plan Approval that meets all Code Requirements. Minor plan adjustments may be approved by the Salisbury DID. 

2. Complete a Text Amendment to amend PDD No 2, Westwood Commerce Park. 3. Complete a Corrected Plat to be recorded in Land Records showing the removal of the restrictions to access the State Right of Way. 

4. Create a shared Access and Parking agreement between the tenants for the lots to share the accesses and car parking on the site. 

5. Remove the City of Salisbury Utility Easements along the interior lot lines of Block A. 

6. Create a shared facilities agreement for stormwater and utility infrastructure servicing the site. 7. Include a shared pedestrian and cyclist path along the boundaries or provide a fee in lieu for future pedestrian connectivity. 

8. Provide dumpster locations with appropriate screening requirements as detailed in Ch. 17.220. 9. Provide a finalized sign plan with locations, size, colors, materials, and standards for Planning Commission approval prior to the issuance of sign permits. 

10. This approval is subject to further review and approval by the Salisbury DID, the Salisbury Fire Department, and other agencies as appropriate. 

Mr. Thomas entered a motion to approve the Final Comprehensive Development Plan for Westwood Commerce Park Block A subject to the ten (10) conditions listed in the revised Staff Report. The motion was seconded by Mr. Doughty and duly carried. 

Chairman Dashiell stated the motion was APPROVED.
