PUBLIC HEARING — REPEAL AND ENACT LEGISLATION —- Comprehensive Review: Wicomico County Code
Chapter 125 - Critical Area Resource Protection Ordinance. (C. Harrison)

The Public Hearing began at 4:06 p.m.
Ms. Gabrielle Carr read the Public Hearing notice.

Mr. Colin Harrison approached the table and then presented the Staff Report.

The State of Maryland adopted the Chesapeake Bay Critical Area legislation in 1984 to improve
water quality and protect the Chesapeake Bay and its tributaries. Wicomico County's Critical Area
Resource Protection Ordinance was adopted in 1989, with revisions in 2001 and subsequent text
amendments. This draft ordinance is a complete overhaul of our existing Critical Area Resource
Protection Ordinance and aligns with the State’s model, with minor additions to address local
concerns. The county has limited flexibility under our Critical Area Ordinance because State law
prevails.

Natural Resources article §8-1809(g) states that each local jurisdiction shall comprehensively
review and update its Critical Area ordinance every 10 years. Wicomico County Code Chapter 125,
§ 125-42, Section C procedures require a public hearing at which parties of interest and citizens shall
have an opportunity to be heard. A recommendation from the Planning Commission is required and
then forwarded to the Wicomico County Council.

The County Council will hold a public hearing before adopting the proposed ordinance.

Staff recommended that the Planning Commission forward a favorable recommendation to the
County Council for the repeal of Chapter 125, shown in Exhibit A, and the enactment of the Wicomico
County Critical Area Ordinance as proposed in Exhibit B. If adopted, the county will comply with the
most recently updated Critical Area regulations required by State law.

Mr. Thomas requested a brief summary of the changes for Wicomico County. Mr. Harrison stated
that there were new definitions, the addition of Resource Conservation Area (“RCA”) Buffers, and
updates to minor and major solar development.

Mr. Moreno-Holt questioned the change from a 100-foot Buffer to a 200-foot Buffer. Mr. Harrison
clarified that this change applies to new site plans within the Resource Conservation Area, the most
environmentally sensitive area. There are provisions for undeveloped lots that were created after the
adoption of our ordinance. Anything post-1989 must adhere to the 200-foot Buffer; anything prior is
grandfathered.

Mr. Harrison provided additional information regarding the Administrative Variance. In the past,
variances in Wicomico County for Critical Areas required Board of Appeals approval, including a
public hearing. The process can now be completed administratively.

With no additional comments, Ms. Sara Bynum-King moved to forward a_ favorable
recommendation to the County Council for the Repeal of Chapter 125, Exhibit A of the Staff Report,
and Enactment of the Wicomico County Critical Area Ordinance as proposed in Exhibit B of the Staff
Report. Mr. Thomas seconded the motion, which was duly carried.

Chairman Dashiell stated the motion was APPROVED.

PUBLIC HEARING — TEXT AMENDMENT - HOUSE BILL 538 HOUSING EXPANSION AND AFFORDABILTY ACT
OF 2025 (T. Taylor and A. Illuminati)

The Public Hearing began at 4:18 p.m.
Ms. Carr read the Public Hearing notice.

Ms. Tracey Taylor stated that Wicomico County is proposing the text amendment because we
are required by State law to do so to make our zoning compliant with the Housing Expansion and
Affordability Act (HB538), which was signed into law by Governor Moore on April 25, 2024. This law
made several changes to the Land Use Article, which governs our local zoning, and it requires local
governments to treat modular and manufactured housing under local zoning identically to single-
family dwellings and to allow different housing types and densities for certain qualified projects, as
defined by the Bill that went into effect in January 2025. The State says our zoning must comply with
this Bill.

Ms. Taylor summarized key provisions of the Bill.

e Any zone where single-family homes are a permitted use, we are now required to allow
manufactured and modular homes as a permitted use.

e Our definition of manufactured homes currently includes allowances for both single-wide and
double-wide manufactured or mobile homes.

e Because single-family dwellings are permitted in the LB2 zone, we will also have to allow mobile
homes in that zone.

e This law does not impact mobile home parks.
The qualified projects that are listed in the Bill by the State, for the most part, are probably not going
to impact us in the foreseeable future. Those projects are things around a transit station, lands
owned by non-profits, and federal and state-owned properties that could be used for some type
of adaptive reuse for residential purposes.

e = It still remains unclear how these provisions could be impacted by Homeowner Associations (HOA)
restrictions and covenants.

e We are making a change within our Department regarding all building permits that we issue. We
will include a disclaimer stating that the issuance of this building permit does not preclude any
restrictions or covenants that may apply if you are governed by a Homeowners Association.

Mr. Andrew Illuminati presented a PowerPoint on the criteria for a qualified project, the
standards for a manufactured home permitted on an individual lot, and the requirements that must
be met to place a manufactured home on an individual lot. He also discussed the hardship
requirements, which are stringent and not permanent. Hardship is granted only by a Special Exception
from the Board of Appeals and will be limited to one (1) year, which may be extended for an additional
period upon continuation of the medical hardship and subject to review by the Board of Appeals.
Hardship housing is permitted only when a medical hardship involving a member of the immediate
family exists; immediate family includes a child, grandchild, parent, grandparent, stepchild, or step-
parent. The dwelling must be either a single-wide or double-wide manufactured home.

Ms. Taylor added that mobile home parks are not covered under this Bill. The mobile home park
regulations are separate from the discussion today and will remain in effect as they have always been.
The Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD) proposed this Bill to address the
shortage of affordable housing.
