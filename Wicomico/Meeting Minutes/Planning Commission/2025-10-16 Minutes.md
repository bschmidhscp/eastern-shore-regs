PUBLIC HEARING - SUBDIVISION SKETCH PLAT —- RAEGAN'S RUN - Bierman Family LLC, rep. by Parker & Associates — Riverside Drive - TT Town Transitional - M-0047, G-0016, P-0065 - #WP-2301(B. Thayne) 

### The public hearing began at 4:13 p.m. 

Mr. Andrew Illuminati read the public notice. 

Ms. Becky Thayne and Mr. Brock Parker, of Parker and Associates, approached the table. 

Ms. Kaylee Justice took a few moments to describe the Septic Tier Law. The county adopted a tier map that guides where residential development can occur based on access to public sewer. Tiers

| and Il plans are to be served by sewer, while Tier Ill and IV are planned for on-site septic systems. Planning and Zoning staff plan to hold an educational session for the Commission, but we were unable to do so before hearing this case. The case before the Commission today concerns the submission of a sketch plat for a property located in Tier IIA on the Tier map. 

Ms. Thayne presented the Sketch Plat Report. Mr. Parker, on behalf of the applicant, proposed the subdivision of ten (10) lots averaging 1.445 acres each from the property on the southerly side of Riverside Drive. All lots will have frontage on a new interior cul-de-sac. The land area is in a Town Transition (“TT") Zoning District just northwest of the City of Fruitland. It is currently designated Tier IIA, which does not allow for major subdivisions with private septic systems. So, we are looking to have this area reclassified as Tier Ill, pending the approval issued today. 

Ms. Teri Denton mentioned a news headline she heard recently on WMDT. “The Maryland Water Infrastructure Financing Administration (“MWIFA") selected the Wicomico County Septic Elimination and Salisbury Sewer Extension Project for funding under the Federal Fiscal Year 2025 Water Quality State Revolving Fund (“WQSRF") Intended Use Plan.” The county's approved schedule requires construction to begin by December 2026. Ms. Denton wanted to know whether this information would affect the projected outcome for this case. Ms. Thayne indicated that, based on the timeline they are pursuing, she believes the project will be approved before December 2026 and will not be affected by this plan. 

Mr. Thomas inquired what the basic difference was between Tier IIA and Tier Ill. Ms. Thayne explained that Tier IIA is designated as areas planned to be served by public, community, or shared sewerage systems and located within a designated growth area. Tier Ill is designated as areas not planned to be served by a public community or shared sewerage system. An on-site individual sewerage system is allowed for major and minor subdivisions. 

### There were no public comments. 

Ms. Bynum-King was curious about Lot 9, where the Health Department had located the sewer in the middle of the lot. She said the alignment of houses in a subdivision is normally consistent, but in Lot 9, it will be set back further. Mr. Parker suggested setting back the house on Lot 8 between 7 and 9, and then pushing the house on Lot 10 backa little as well. 

Since the Commissioners had no additional questions, Chairman Dashiell stated he would entertain a motion to approve the Subdivision Sketch Plat - Raegan's Run for Bierman Family LLC. Mr. Moreno-Holt entered a motion to approve, seconded by Ms. Bynum-King, and the motion was duly carried. 

Chairman Dashiell stated the motion was APPROVED.

PALEOCHANNEL OVERLAY DISTRICT REVIEW - Salisbury Christian School, Inc., rep. by Parker & Associates - Outten Road/Beaglin Park Drive - Zoned R-15 & Paleochannel Overlay District - M-0029, G-0023, P- 0512, 536, 302 & 306 - #WP-2502 (C. Meadows) 

Mr. Clark Meadows and Mr. Keith Fisher, of Fisher Architecture, joined Mr. Brock Parker at the table. Ms. Tanesha Siggers recused herself from this project. 

Mr. Meadows presented the Staff Report, which, along with related exhibits, was entered into the record. Mr. Parker, on behalf of the applicant, is seeking the Planning Commission's review and

approval to develop the referenced property with the Salisbury Christian School athletic campus. The facility will include softoall and baseball fields, two (2) soccer/lacrosse fields, tennis courts, a combined concessions/restrooms/press box building, and parking. Since the project site lies within the boundaries of the Paleochannel Overlay District, §225-45 of the Wicomico County Zoning Code requires the Commission's approval prior to consideration of the use by the Wicomico County Board of Appeals for a Special Exception. 

The Staff recommended that the Commission decide that this use and development do not pose a risk of contamination to the Paleochannel and approve the application. 

Mr. Parker explained that Salisbury Christian School has identified the need for an expanded athletic campus. As private schools increase the number of competitive teams, demand for highquality athletic fields and programs is rising. This has becomea significant factor attracting charter and private schools. Their first priority is to develop a top-quality athletic campus to complement their school. This facility will be exclusively for Salisbury Christian School and will not be open to the public. 

Mr. Fisher mentioned that, with growth at Salisbury Christian, they are having trouble with traffic and are trying to improve school pick-up and drop-off procedures. Moving the athletic campus will create significant space to support the follow-up phases, making it easier to reduce traffic congestion, get cars off the road and onto campus, and eliminate much of the stacking problem. 

Mr. Thomas remarked that the parking was relatively modest for a facility of this size. Mr. Parker said this was the first phase of the long-range plans. The parking lot shows potential for expansion. The hatched areas on the right will be stabilized and compacted in the first phase. So, they will be drained and suitable to drive on. They will be sufficient for overflow parking. 

Mr. Meadows added that the use will require a Special Exception from the Board of Appeals, and traffic, parking, and site development specifically would fall under the Board of Appeals’ purview. So we are here today to consider the effects on the Paleochannel. 

Ms. Denton inquired about the mulched walking trail since the facility would not be open to the public. Mr. Fisher clarified that the area would not be monitored if the public wanted to use it. The main purpose was for parents who did not want to sit and watch the practice to use the walking trail. 

Since the Commissioners had no additional questions, Chairman Dashiell stated he would entertain a motion to approve the Paleochannel Overlay District Review — Salisbury Christian School, Inc. Ms. Bynum-King moved to approve, seconded by Mr. Thomas, and the motion was duly carried. 

Chairman Dashiell stated the motion was APPROVED.

The city has asked the county to hear its cases, as it has been dealing with a backlog since August. A memorandum has been drafted between the two parties. Ms. Bynum-King raised her concerns about hearing the city cases. She chose to be considered for the County Planning Commission to work on county matters, not the City of Salisbury. 

Ms. Denton asked why the Planning Commission split. Mr. Illuminati explained that when the city and county Planning and Zoning Departments were combined, the city used to compensate the county for county staff's time spent preparing documents, minutes, staff reports, advertising the public hearing, and producing all the briefing books, which were then disseminated. When the city created its own Planning Department, it stopped compensating the county but continued to rely on the county for services. The Memorandum of Understanding (“MOU") requires the city to compensate the county to the extent that county staff is utilized. Additionally, it gives the Planning Director discretion to determine whether a particular application will be heard by the Commission.
