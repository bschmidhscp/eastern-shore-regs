PRELIMINARY/FINAL DEVELOPMENT PLAN - ZAXBY'S — DELMARVA REAL ESTATE MANAGEMENT GROUP, LLC - Maryland Route 13 - C-2 General Commercial - M-0048, G-0013, P-0153 - (B. Thayne) 

Ms. Becky Thayne presented the Staff Report, which consisted of the master signage plan, the preliminary/final comprehensive development plan, and the proposal for the new drive-thru and fast food restaurant, Zaxby's. The individuals who spoke to the Commission members on behalf of the Zaxby's project were as follows: Mr. Brett Ewing, Senior Principal Planner at Lane Engineering; Mr. Curtis Snyder, General Counsel for Delmarva Real Estate Management Group, LLC. and co-owner of Zaxby's; and Mr. Alec Chosta, Civil Engineer at Lane Engineering. 

Ms. Thayne distributed physical full size copies of the comprehensive development plan and master signage plan per Ms. Taylor's request for all Commission members to view. Mr. Harrison presented the plans on the projector screen simultaneously. Ms. Thayne noted that the Community Impact Statement for the development plan for Zaxby's is similar to other Community Impact Statements previously approved by the Planning Commission. 

Additionally, Ms. Thayne provided staff recommendations and explained that final copies of the development plan will be on record in the Planning and Zoning Department. Ms. Thayne also stated that another request of the Planning and Zoning staff would be to include “do not enter” freestanding signs on the final master signage plan to improve traffic flow upon the visitors’ entrance and exit of Zaxby's. 

Mr. Ewing explained the general scope of the project, reiterating Ms. Thayne’s points. Furthermore, Mr. Ewing provided insights regarding the two requests for the Zaxby’s project; the approval of the preliminary and final development plan; and the approval of the master signage plan. Additionally, Mr. Ewing stated that the redevelopment of the site will comply with the county development standards. The site redevelopment will generate revenue for the county and create improvements to the Tony Tank area by allowing an 88-foot barrier from the flood zone. The State Highway Administration has approved ingress and egress for the proposed project; Mr. Ewing reassured that the concerns for queues along Route 13 have been addressed by providing a one-way in and out of the site for traffic flow and emergency services for safety concerns. More so, the purpose of the 2-lane drive-thru is intended to mitigate traffic during the grand opening and the overall guest experience during their visit. 

Mr. Snyder commented regarding the architecture of the building, which is intended to provide an overall upscale feeling upon entering the fast-food restaurant. Mr. Snyder provided an overview of the materials for construction of the new building and stated the signage plan is designed to guide the guest experience. A feature included in the signage plan would be similar to the Cambridge, Maryland location, in that, a mural of twelve different sauces would be included along the drive-thru before the pick-up window. 

After comments provided by the Zaxby's team, Chairman Dashiell welcomed comments and questions from the Commission members. Mr. Moreno-Holt asked if there was a reason for the impervious surface on the southern parking spaces compared to the other parking spaces. Mr. Ewing responded that the slopes were addressed and consulted with a Geotech engineer to include an infiltration practice near that slope to improve stability due to the soil conditions.

Another question addressed by Mr. Moreno-Holt was if the site plan would include an addition of a sidewalk along Route 13 as a pedestrian path. Mr. Ewing responded that this was a simple addition to the site plan as requested by Mr. Moreno-Holt.

## COMPREHENSIVE PLAN UPDATES - (Wallace Montgomery) 

Ms. Taylor provided updates on the comprehensive plan, which included the confirmation of a contract with Wallace Montgomery located in Hunt Valley. Ms. Taylor invited representatives from Wallace Montgomery to discuss the comprehensive plan; the two representatives in attendance were Mr. Ted Foglietta, Associate Vice President, and Ms. Nichole Wiley, Project Manager. 

Taking turns, both Mr. Foglietta and Ms. Wiley provided insights into the proposed comprehensive plan update process using a slide-show presentation prompted by Mr. Harrison. The comprehensive plan reflects materials based on input from Planning and Zoning staff. Furthermore, the plan includes information provided to Planning & Zoning for review and to offer any comments, questions and feedback. The comprehensive plan is updated every 10 years, but it typically reviewed every 5 years. Though, the goal is to have the Planning and Zoning Commission to review the plan annually. Ms. Wiley described that the comprehensive plan includes legislation provided to the county as the Planning & Zoning Department drafts the comprehensive plan; she encouraged the Commission members to utilize the State's resources in further developing the comprehensive plan. Additionally, there are now 8 planning principles, which were previously the 12 planning visions. 

Ms. Wiley then presented the work plan overview, which included Task 1_- Project Initiation & Data Gathering; Task 2 -- Development of a Community Engagement Plan; and Task 3 — Plan Drafting & Adopting. 

Ms. Wiley reiterated the importance of public involvement for community engagement. Previous examples provided by her included partaking in surveys, public events and workshops. Also, an accessible and interactive website will be created to further encourage public engagement. 

Ms. Wiley also provided an overview of the planning schedule for the following months of summer leading into the fall and winter of this year. The goal is to expand public outreach and to hold stakeholder interviews approved by staff for the stakeholder’s feedback in the planning process. Additionally, the team at Wallace Montgomery will start developing the chapters which will entail analyzing data to be distributed in the form of a report given to the Commission members and Planning and Zoning staff. 

By next spring, the goal is to have the chapters finalized and ready for adoption. The workflow process will enable the chapters to be reviewed in previous Commission meetings and approved for the current meeting. This method will allow a constant flow of review and approval for the chapters prior to adoption. Though, Ms. Wiley did note that any comments regarding older chapters can still be discussed at subsequent meetings. 

Mr. Foglietta then told the Commission members that the monthly goal of the chapters review is to have the chapters completed on a 12-month cycle. The continuous reviewing of the plan and chapters would allow the comprehensive plan to remain on schedule and avoid public fatigue. Ultimately, the goals of Wallace Montgomery are to encourage community outreach and to have a fully-staffed team for developing a current and future comprehensive plan. Mr. Foglietta then welcomed any questions or comments from the Commission members. 

Chairman Dashiell thanked Ms. Wiley and Mr. Foglietta for their presentation and Ms. Taylor for keeping the Commission members informed on the comprehensive plan updates. Chairman Dashiell then asked how the plan will be presented in coordination with staff and the Commission members. In response, Ms. Taylor stated that several Planning and Zoning staff members will be involved on this project; there will also be internal GIS staff who will review the project in coordination with staff at Wallace Montgomery. 

Ms. Taylor stated that the monthly schedule of work sessions between the staff at Wallace Montgomery and Planning and Zoning staff members will allow a designated time to openly discuss
