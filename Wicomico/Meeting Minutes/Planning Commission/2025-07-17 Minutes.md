FINAL COMPREHENSIVE DEVELOPMENT PLAN — PARKSIDE AT SCHUMAKER POND - Vantage Point Salisbury Partner 1, LLC, rep. by Parker and Associates - Snow Hill Road - GC General Commercial District - Parkside at Schumaker Pond - M-0048, G-0010, P-0282 - #24-013 (J. Jean) 

Mr. Brock Parker with Parker and Associates and Ms. Jackson joined Ms. Jean at the table. Ms. Jean presented the Staff Report. 

Parker and Associates, on behalf of the applicant, submitted a Final Comprehensive Development Plan for a residential subdivision consisting of 79 single-family lots and 135 townhouse lots. The applicant proposes to develop an existing 38.9-acre site into a 214-unit residential subdivision located on Snow Hill Road, directly across from Marley Manor. The site currently comprises three parcels, which are proposed to be consolidated into a single parcel for residential use. Staff presented the Comprehensive Development Plan Review, which included the Site Plan, Building Elevations/Floor Plans, Sign Plan, Landscaping Plan, Development Schedule, Community Impact Statement, Statement of Intent to Proceed and Financial Capability, Fire Service, Stormwater Management, Transportation, Streets, and Pedestrians. 

Staff recommended approval of the Final Comprehensive Development Plan for Parkside at Schumaker Pond, subject to the following five (5) conditions. 

1. The site shall be developed in accordance with the approved Certificate of Design and Site Plan, in compliance with all applicable code requirements. Minor adjustments may be approved by the Salisbury Department of Infrastructure and Development. 

2. The project is subject to further review and approval by: e The City Department of Infrastructure and Development e The City Fire Marshal e Other applicable agencies 

3. A comprehensive sign plan shall be submitted for Planning Commission approval prior to any sign installation. 

4. Lot 190 shall be removed to ensure compliance with the General Commercial District permitted uses. 

5. The three existing parcels shall be combined into one. 

6. Submit a revised Community Impact Study to include information on trash removal. 

Mr. Parker added that the project is essentially the same as the preliminary one; they have finetuned the construction drawings. Engineering involvement resulted in the relocation and/or adjustment of lines, which caused Lot 190 to extend into the General Commercial District. After the City Planners identified the error, Mr. Parker agreed to remove the single-family home on Lot 190. Mr. Parker said the townhouse to the right of Lot 190 will be converted from a four-unit to a seven-unit townhouse, occupying the land area. 

Mr. Parker also mentioned that this project was included in the traffic study for the traffic circle on Snow Hill Road from the beginning. During the preliminary findings, the Commissioners requested a vehicular connection through to Summersgate, and that has been included in these drawings.

Chairman Dashiell inquired if either Ms. Jackson or Ms. Jean would like to discuss the email that the Commissioners and City Staff received from Ms. Nancy Roisum. Ms. Jackson indicated she would address the concerns in the email. Ms. Jackson stated that the main concern raised was that the Community Impact Statement did not address landfill capacity. She believes the landfill capacity issue is much larger than this one project. 

A larger conversation ensued about the landfill capacity. According to Mr. Holloway, the construction of the new cell did not start when it was initially targeted to begin. The County is also addressing the issue of out-of-county trash being brought into the County, as our tipping fees are lower than those of adjacent counties. Mr. Parker offered to submit an amended Community Impact Statement next month, which would include the trash numbers. 

Ms. Tracey Taylor suggested having Technical Advisory Committee (“TAC”) meetings once the City and County Planning Commission is split. The meetings should include all relevant department heads and staff members who need to be involved in the crossover issues for county and municipal planning purposes. 

Mr. Thomas asked if Mr. Parker would explain how stormwater is calculated. Mr. Parker said a lot of the criteria are designed for a 2-year storm. When they refer to 2, 10, and 100-year storms, they are talking about storms that are statistically so strong that they occur only once every 2, 10, and 100 years, respectively. Gutters and surface drainage are designed to convey water safely during a 2-year storm. Pipes are sized to convey a 10-year storm. Stormwater management and ponds are designed to slow both 2-year and 10-year storms. 

Mr. Moreno-Holt inquired about the location of the mailboxes. After a discussion, Mr. Parker agreed to relocate the mailboxes next to the basketball courts for easier parking and mail retrieval. 

The Commissioners discussed adding a sixth condition concerning a revised Community Impact Statement to address trash removal. 

Mr. Thomas entered a motion to approve the Final Comprehensive Development Plan for Parkside at Schumaker Pond, subject to the five (5) conditions mentioned in the Staff Report and adding a sixth condition to Submit a revised Community Impact Study to include information on trash removal, seconded by Mr. Doughty, and duly carried. 

Chairman Dashiell stated the motion was APPROVED.
