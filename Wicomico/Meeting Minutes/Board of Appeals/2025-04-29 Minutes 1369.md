##### AGENDA #2 

### Findings of Fact: #WA-2507 - Kathuria Farms, LLC and International BioRefineries LLC 

Chairman Wright announced they would review the Resolution of the Appeal of a Decision by the Zoning Administrator for Kathuria Farms LLC and International BioRefineries, LLC, case #WA-2507. The purpose of this meeting is for the Board to review the Resolution of Decision and Findings of Fact and act upon them. 

We, the Board, have been provided with the Resolution of Decision and Findings of Fact as prepared by our legal counsel, Mr. Andrew Illuminati. 

On August 1, 2024, Mr. Rajneesh Kathuria applied for a Wicomico County building permit to construct a 45' x 165' agricultural storage building to replace an existing on-site storage building. The permit was for certain real property identified as Wicomico County Tax Map 0019, Parcel 74, ID # 11-013589, located at 8665 Hickory Mill Road, Salisbury, Wicomico County, Maryland (“Property”) in the A-1 (Agricultural-Rural) Zoning District. Wicomico County issued the permit based on the intended use listed by Mr. Kathuria on the application. 

The specific finding of the Board is that the decision of the Zoning Administrator is upheld. § 225-25(B) defines agricultural products as “cultivated or raised plant, animal, or marine life that has been harvested. This does not include processed products.” Specifically, the Board finds that the biorefinery process creates a processed product; therefore, the products are not agricultural products as defined by the Wicomico County Code. Additionally, the Board finds that Appellant’s proposed use of the Property is not an accessory use to an agricultural use. § 225-25(B) defines an accessory use as “a use which is customarily incidental and subordinate to the principal use of a lot or the main building thereon and located on the same lot as the principal use or building or an adjacent lot under the same ownership.” Further, § 225-25(B) defines agriculture or agricultural as “the raising of agricultural products including livestock, poultry, dairy products, farm crops, fruit, vegetables, timber, cultivated plants and nursery stock whether for gain or otherwise. Agriculture shall include the farming or culturing of aquatic plants or animals, or both, in natural or artificial water bodies or impoundments. Agriculture includes horticultural, chemical, or general farming, truck gardens, cultivation of field crops, and, in general, all uses commonly classified as agricultural, and the related buildings, structures, and appurtenances necessary to carry out the aforementioned activities.” The Board finds that the primary use of the Property is a poultry farm, which is agricultural; however, a biorefinery is not a customary use that is incidental to a poultry farm. No other poultry farm in Wicomico County utilizes a bio-refinery. On an appeal, for the Board to overturn a decision of the administrative agency or official, it is the appellant’s burden “to show that the action taken by the administrative agency or official was clearly erroneous, and/or arbitrary and capricious, and/or contrary to law.” § 7-5. The Board finds that, based on the evidence presented, the Appellant has not established that the Zoning Administrator's decision was clearly erroneous, /arbitrary and capricious, /or contrary to law.

Therefore, the Board found that the proposed use was incompatible with and would be detrimental to the use or development of adjacent properties or the general neighborhood. Further, the Board finds that the proposed use has not been designed to minimize possible adverse effects on adjacent properties or the immediately surrounding area. 

Chairman Wright asked the Board if there were any questions or discussions regarding the prepared Resolution of Decision and Findings of Fact. Mr. Meadows announced that Mr. Jim Morris had heard and participated in the original hearing but was absent this evening. Mr. Morris received a copy of the report and responded via email with his affirmation; a copy of his email has been saved for the record. 

The Wicomico County Board of Appeals considered the above-entitled matter at a special meeting on Tuesday, April 29, 2025. After presenting and adopting the Resolution of Decision and Findings of Fact, the Board affirmed its decision to uphold the decision of the Zoning Administrator that the processing of poultry litter generated on the property, through the operation of a biorefinery facility, is not an allowable accessory use in the A-1 Zoning District. Board Member William “Bill” Turner offered a MOTION to adopt the Resolution of Decision and Findings of Fact in case WA-2507. The motion was SECONDED by Michael Hoy. The motion was followed by David Ennis voting AYE, Chairman Wright voting AYE, and Jim Morris voting AYE, by proxy. 

Chairman Wright announced that the Appellant will have thirty (30) days from signing the Resolution of Decision to appeal this in Circuit Court. 

With no further business before the Board, the meeting adjourned at 5:38 P.M.
