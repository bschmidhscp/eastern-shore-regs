AGENDA #2

Findings of Fact: #WA-2504 - Salisbury Riverside West Solar, LLC, Special Exception - Minor
Solar

Mr. Andrew Illuminati called the first case.
Mr. Clark Meadows presented the Findings of Fact.

Salisbury Riverside West Solar, LLC, (Applicant) requested to construct a 2 MW solar
facility at certain real property identified as Wicomico County Tax Map 0047, Parcel 65, ID
#16-022527 (the “Property), located at 27881 Riverside Drive Extended, Salisbury, Wicomico
County, Maryland. The total lot area is approximately 52.94 acres.

The evidence received centered around the seven criteria for approval, set forth in
Wicomico County Code § 225-155, which must be satisfied for a special exception to be
granted. Those provisions are:

(1) The proposed use is consistent with the Comprehensive Plan or a specific area plan for
the physical development of the county or a portion thereof, as adopted by the County
Council.

(2) The proposed use will not adversely affect the health, safety, and general welfare of the
residents or workers in the area.

(3) The proposed use is compatible with and will not be detrimental to the use or development
of adjacent properties or the general neighborhood.

(4) The proposed use meets all of the standards set forth for each particular use for which a
special exception may be granted.

(5) The proposed use has been designed so as to minimize possible adverse effects on
adjacent properties or on the immediately surrounding area.

(6) The proposed use will not adversely affect the transportation network or unduly burden
water, sewer, school, park, stormwater management areas, including streams, or other
public facilities.

March 27, 2025 MINUTES Page | 2

<!-- page 4 -->
(7) The proposed use will not adversely affect the environmental, archeological or historical
assets of particular interest to the community as may be identified in any adopted plan of
the county.

The specific finding of the Board is that the Applicant’s special exception request does
not satisfy the provisions set forth in § 225-155. Specifically, the Board finds that the
proposed project is not compatible with the existing character of the surrounding area. § 225-
34(A) enumerates the purpose of the Town Transition District. Specifically, “this district
encourages development that is compatible with the existing character of the surrounding
area.” § 225-24(A)(2). The Board finds that the proposed site is located in a predominantly
residential area. The northern side of Riverside Drive Extended, directly across the street from
the proposed site plan, is entirely within the R-20 Zoning District. Additionally, multiple parcels,
improved with single-family homes, which abut the proposed site, located on the southerly
side of Riverside Drive Extended, are within the R-20 Zoning District.

Mr. Turner explained why he opposed the denial of the Special Exception.

Therefore, the Board found that the proposed use was incompatible with and would be
detrimental to the use or development of adjacent properties or the general neighborhood.
Further, the Board finds that the proposed use has not been designed to minimize possible
adverse effects on adjacent properties or on the immediately surrounding area.

The Wicomico County Board of Appeals considered the above-entitled matter at a
Public Hearing on Thursday, March 27, 2025. After presenting and adopting the Resolution
of Decision and Findings of Fact, the Board affirmed its decision to DENY the request for a
Special Exception - Minor Solar (Riverside West Solar, LLC). Board Member David Ennis
offered a MOTION to deny the Special Exception - Minor Solar. The motion was SECONDED
by Jim Morris. The motion was followed by Michael Hoy voting AYE and Chairman Wright voting
AYE to deny the Special Exception. William “Bill” Turner voted not to support the motion to
deny the request.

Chairman Wright reviewed the question before the Board and the process to ensure
the hearing was conducted properly. There is one issue on appeal before the Board. That issue
is whether the Zoning Administrator's decision from the Wicomico County Zoning Code
prohibits, as an accessory use in the A1 Zoning District, the processing of poultry litter
generated on the property through the operation of a biorefinery facility. The case will follow
the following format: the parties can make an opening statement if they wish. Following the
opening statements, the County will present its case. The County's initial burden is to present
sufficient evidence and argument to the Board to justify the order or appealed decision. Upon
the County meeting its initial burden, the burden of presenting evidence and argument to the
contrary then shifts to the appellant, who shall also have the burden of persuasion. After the
parties present their evidence, a period of public comment will occur. If you wish to speak, you
must provide your full legal name and residence address. Simply stating your County of
residence will not suffice. You will also need to state whether you have been sworn or affirmed.
If you have not been sworn or affirmed, the oath will not be administered before you speak. If
you refuse to take the oath, you will not be able to speak. This is a land use issue; personal
attacks will not be tolerated. Repetitive testimony will be cut short. If you provide a comment,
you are subject to cross-examination by the parties. After the close of public comment, the
parties will provide a closing statement, if they so choose. Neither the opening statement nor
the closing statement is evidence. The Board will then close the hearing and will deliberate.
The Board may make a final decision tonight or may reach a consensus but elect to have Staff
prepare a draft resolution of decision for consideration at the next meeting. Either way, the
30-day limitation within which a party has a right to appeal the decision of the Board shall run
from the date the Resolution of Decision is executed by the Chair.

#WA-2507 - Kathuria Farms, LLC and International BioRefineries LLC, in accordance with
Section 225-154 of the Wicomico County Zoning Code, appeals the Enforcing Officer’s (Zoning
Administrator) decision that the processing of poultry litter generated on the property through
the operation of a biorefinery facility is not ar allowable accessory use in the A-1 Zoning

IIluminati, LLP), and Mr. Clark Meadows approached the table.

Administrator based on his determination that a biorefinery facility is not an allowable
accessory use in the A-1 Agricultural Zoning District. To meet the initial burden, the County will

present the Staff Report through Mr. Meadows, which describes the basis for that decision,
including the application of the definitions within the zoning ordinance that are germane to
the operation of a biorefinery facility. Once the County meets the initial burden, it is then the
Appellant's burden to persuade the Board. Additionally, Mr. Kiya Safai, Wicomico County Chief
Building Inspector, will testify to his observations and present photographs of the site.

Ms. Diamond addressed the Board to bring forward interested parties in order to give
testimony, and then summarize other concerns for the record. Ms. Diamond requested that
the Board of Appeals affirm enforcement of the County's ordinance to protect the agricultural
area. The interested parties support the County's determination that the biorefinery is not an

Mr. Showalter addressed the Board and acknowledged there may be concerns about
industrial refining, but that is not what the appeal is about. The appeal is about agriculture,
and the limited nature of what is proposed on this property is entirely consistent with the
definitions and provisions of the County Code. The public reactions regard a technology, not a
use. The proposed use occurs on poultry and livestock farms throughout the county. The
Appellant’s argument is that the use is permitted by right or as an accessory use to agricultural
operations. Evidence presented by the Appellant will show that the County made an
interpretation that is not aligned with the Code.

Mr. Meadows presented the Staff Report, with related exhibits, into the record. In
summary, the Zoning Administrator determined the processing of poultry litter through the
operation of a biorefinery facility is not an allowable use in the A-1 zoning district. In
accordance with Section 225-154 of the Wicomico County Zoning Code, the Applicant
appealed the decision to the Wicomico County Board of Appeals. It is the position of Kathuria
Farms, LLC / International BioRefineries, LLC that the processing of poultry litter generated
on the farm through the operation of a biorefinery facility is allowed as an accessory use in
the A-1 zoning district.

Staff recommends the Board uphold the Zoning Administrator's decision that
processing of poultry litter generated on the property through the operation of a biorefinery
facility is not an allowable use in the A-1 zoning district.

Ms. Witz asked Mr. Meadows to define “agriculture,” “agricultural,” and “accessory
use.” According to Section 225-25 of the County Zoning Code, agriculture is raising
agricultural products, including livestock, poultry, dairy products, farm crops, fruit, vegetables,
timber, cultivated plants, and nursery stock, whether for gain or otherwise. Agricultural
products are defined as cultivated or raised plant, animal, or marine life that has been
harvested. This does not include processed products. Accessory use is customarily incidental
and subordinate to the principal use of a lot or the main building thereon and located on the
same lot as the principal use or building or an adjacent lot under the same ownership.
