AN ACT TO AMEND CHAPTER 99 OF THE WICOMICO COUNTY CODE, TITLED “VEHICLES, COUNTY,” TO REQUIRE DECALS TO BE PERMANENTLY AFFIXED TO THE EXTERIOR OF BOTH FRONT DOORS OF COUNTY VEHICLES IN A UNIFORM MANNER, AND TO EXEMPT THE CIRCUIT COURT FOR WICOMICO COUNTY FROM SECTION 99-3(E) THAT EMPLOYEES USE A COUNTY VEHICLE FOR OFFICIAL BUSINESS IF AVAILABLE OR NOT RECEIVE MILEAGE ALLOWANCE. 

WHERAS, the County adopted Chapter 99 and included a requirement that all county vehicles have a decal permanently affixed on each side and rear of the vehicle; and 

WHEREAS, the Council has found that the placement of decals on the sides of county vehicles has not been done in a uniform manner and has determined that an amendment to Chapter 99 to establish a uniform location for the decals on the sides of vehicles is advisable; and 

WHEREAS, the Circuit Court for Wicomico County has historically utilized personal vehicles for limited official travel by court employees; and 

WHEREAS, the Circuit Court for Wicomico County is a separate branch of government and does not utilize County-assigned vehicles; and 

WHEREAS, it is in the public interest to allow employees of the Circuit Court to receive mileage reimbursement when using personal vehicles for official business; and 

WHEREAS, Section 99-3(E) currently prohibits mileage reimbursement when County vehicles are available, which does not reflect the operational reality of the Circuit Court. 

SECTION 1: BE IT ENACTED AND ORDAINED BY THE COUNTY COUNCIL OF WICOMICO COUNTY, MARYLAND, TN LEGISLATIVE SESSION, that Chapter 99, titled “Vehicles, County,” be amended to add the following:

Dear President Cannon and Members of the Council: 

After careful consideration and review, I am formally vetoing Legislative Bifi 2025-09, which mandates that all County-owned vehicles display permanently affixed decals on the exterior sides of the vehicles. 

While I understand and appreciate the Councils intent to promote transparency and accountability through clear vehicle identification, this legislation presents significant concerns related to employee safety, operational discretion, and the fundamental balance of authority between the executive and legislative branches of county government. 

> 1. Safety Concerns for Certain Employees 

Several departments, particularly those involved in enforcement and regulatory compliance, rely on the ability to conduct work discreetly for the safety of their staff and the effectiveness of their duties. For example, Code Enforcement Officers regularly respond to complaints involving zoning violations, property maintenance issues, and unlicensed operations. In many cases, they are required to approach properties where emotions may run high and hostility toward government persormel may exist. 

Permanently marked vehicles unnecessarily identify these individuals in a way that could compromise their safety and impede their abifity to conduct site visits or investigations without confrontation. Providing these employees with unmarked vehicles—at the discretion of their department heads and the Executive—helps ensure they can carry out their responsibilities effectively and without fear of retaliation.

<!-- page 5 -->

## 2. Operational Decisions Belong with the Executive Branch 

The decision to mark or not mark specific county vehicles is inherently an operational and administrative matter, which falls within the authority of the Executive Branch. It is the responsibility of the County Executive to manage day-to-day operations, including policies governing the use of county vehicles. This bifi unnecessarily oversteps the legislative role and imposes blanket requirements that fail to account for the nuanced operational needs of each department. 

The County Executive, in consultation with department leadership, is best positioned to determine which vehicles should bear permanent identification and which should remain unmarked for legitimate and documented purposes. This discretion ensures both transparency and flexibility in vehicle use, while safeguarding personnel and respecting the unique functions of various county roles. 

3. One-Size-Fits-All Mandate is Impractical 

Legislative Bifi 2025-09 lacks the flexibility necessary for the wide array of functions performed by County departments. Not all vehicles are used in the same way, and a one-size-fits-all approach may hinder the effectiveness of certain operations. Some vehicles are used for sensitive investigations, joint agency operations, or intergovernmental collaborations where anonymity is required or requested. 

Mandating permanent decals on all vehicles creates logistical and budgetary burdens, especially if exemptions or changes are needed down the line. Such inflexibility does not serve the County’s best interest and may result in increased costs without corresponding benefit. 

For these reasons, I am returning Legislative Bifi 2025-09 with my veto. I respectfully urge the Council to reconsider the implications of this legislation and to allow the Executive Branch the discretion needed to manage county operations safely, effectively, and efficiently. 

Should the Council wish to revisit this topic in the future, I would be open to discussions around a policy framework that strikes the appropriate balance between transparency, operational functionality, and employee safety.
