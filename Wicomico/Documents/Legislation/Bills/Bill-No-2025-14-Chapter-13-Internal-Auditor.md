Re: Legislative Bill 2025-14 - County Internal Auditor 

Dear President Cannon and Council: 

Pursuant to my authority under the Wicomico County Charter, I hereby veto Legislative Bill 2025-14. 

This veto is based on substantive legal and governance deficiencies within the bill that undermine auditor independence,<sup>create inconsistent</sup> regulatory standards,<sup>and establisha</sup> precedent<sup>thatis</sup> contrary<sup>tosound</sup> internal control principles and public accountability. 

Lack of Structural Independence 

Legislative<sup>Bill2025-14</sup> purports<sup>toestablish the</sup> independence<sup>of the</sup> County<sup>Internal</sup> Auditor; however,<sup>the</sup> bill fails to do so in any meaningful or enforceable manner. While Section 13-3(B) states that the Auditor “shall be considered independent,” other provisions of the bill directly conflict with that assertion. 

The bill permits the Auditor's work to be assigned, directed, waived, or influenced by external governing bodies, including discretionary waivers by the County Council for audits involving the Council itself. Independence<sup>cannot be declared</sup> by<sup>statute while</sup> simultaneously allowing operational<sup>control and selective</sup> exemptions<sup>that</sup> compromise objectivity. Independence<sup>must be</sup> structural,<sup>not</sup> aspirational. 

As written, the bill creates the appearance of independence without providing the protections necessary to ensure it, thereby exposing the County to both reputational and legal risk. 

Exemption<sup>of “Other Activities”From ProfessionalStandards</sup> 

The most significant defect in Legislative Bill 2025-14 is its explicit exemption of “Other Activities” from the Global Internal Audit Standards.

Section E(2) of the bill states that fiscal impact reviews, hotline administration and investigations, and assistance to individual County Council members “will not be subject to the Global Internal Audit Standards”. These activities are not ministerial or incidental in nature; they are substantive oversight and investigative functions that directly impact policy, personnel, and financial decision-making. 

By legislatively exempting<sup>theseactivities from</sup> recognized professional standards,<sup>the billcreatesadual audit</sup> framework in which some work is governed by global standards and other work is not. This inconsistency is indefensible from a governance, risk, or compliance standpoint and is fundamentally at odds with the purpose of adopting professional standards in the first place. 

### Precedent and Governance Risk 

Codifying exemptions<sup>from</sup> professional<sup>audit standardssetsa</sup> dangerous precedent.<sup>Once</sup> exceptions<sup>are</sup> embedded in County Code, future expansions of non-standardized audit activity become easier to justify, further eroding accountability and uniform oversight. 

This approach undermines internal controls, weakens public confidence, and increases the likelihood of disputes<sup>over audit</sup> scope, methodology,<sup>and conclusions.Italso invites</sup> challenges regarding<sup>selective</sup> enforcement, political influence, and lack of due process—particularly where audit-adjacent activities are performed<sup>outside</sup> accepted<sup>standards.</sup> 

Inconsistent and Selective Adoption of Standards 

While the bill repeatedly references the Global Internal Audit Standards, it adopts them selectively and inconsistently.<sup>Professional standardsare not modulartoolsto be</sup> applied<sup>at</sup> convenience; they<sup>are</sup> comprehensive<sup>frameworks</sup> designed<sup>toensure</sup> independence, objectivity, consistency,<sup>and</sup> credibility. 

Partial adoption with express carve-outs does not strengthen the audit function—it weakens it. 

Conclusion 

For the reasons stated above, Legislative Bill 2025-14 is legally and structurally flawed. It does not establish a truly independent<sup>internal audit</sup> function,<sup>it</sup> exempts significant oversight<sup>activities from</sup> professional standards, and it sets a precedent that undermines sound public administration. 

Accordingly,<sup>Iveto this bill.</sup> | remain willing to work with the County Council on legislation that fully and consistently aligns<sup>the</sup> County’s<sup>internal audit function with</sup> recognized professional<sup>standards while</sup> preserving<sup>true</sup> independence<sup>and</sup> accountability.
