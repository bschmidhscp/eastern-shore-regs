DISCUSSION 
 
Worcester County recently completed a comprehensive review of both their Chesapeake Bay Critical 
Area Program and their Atlantic Coastal Bay Critical Area Program. Worcester County is the only 
jurisdiction to have two Critical Area Programs, as the Atlantic Coastal Bays were not added to the 
Critical Area law until 2002. As a part of this comprehensive review, the County combined and 
updated both Programs into one ordinance, while maintaining language that is specific to each 
program, as necessary. The County originally approved its Chesapeake Bay Critical Area Program on 
October 9, 1990 and last performed a comprehensive review of that Program in 2003. The County 
originally approved its Atlantic Coastal Bays Critical Area Program on November 13, 2002, and has 
not completed a comprehensive review since then.  
 
Natural Resources Article, §8-1809(g) requires each local jurisdiction to review its entire Critical Area 
program every ten years and ensure conformance with the requirements of the Critical Area law, 
criteria, and regulations. On July 2, 2024, the Worcester County Council voted to update and amend 
the County’s two Critical Area Program Ordinances into one Critical Area Program Ordinance through 
the adoption of Bill 24-05. On July 15, 2024, the County submitted the updated Ordinance to the 
Critical Area Commission for final review and approval.  
 
Summary of Ordinance Changes 
  
The majority of the updates to the County’s Critical Area Programs are related to combining the 
Chesapeake Bay Critical Area Program and Atlantic Coastal Bays Critical Area Program into one 
Critical Area ordinance, or they are related to changes made to the Critical Area law and regulations 
since 2002. These include provisions for reasonable accommodations related to disabilities, procedures

for lot consolidation and reconfiguration, updated Buffer provisions and updated water-dependent 
facility provisions.  
 
Additionally, the County is proposing to add language that would allow for special events in the 
Resource Conservation Area (RCA). The special events would be limited to sites that are 25 acres or 
greater and there are limitations on the number of events per year, as well as a requirement that all lot 
coverage related to the special events must be located outside of the Buffer. These provisions are 
meant to limit the impact to the RCA, consistent with the goals of the law. The Commission has 
previously approved similar uses and limits in other local Critical Area Programs.  
 
With this comprehensive review, Worcester County is opting in to the non-water dependent projects on 
piers language from the Critical Area law. The County is incorporating the language from the Critical 
Area law into their ordinance verbatim, including the language that allows for small scale renewable 
energy systems on piers. These uses will be allowed in both the Atlantic Coastal Bays Critical Area 
and the Chesapeake Bay Critical Area.  
  
Growth Allocation 
 
The Chesapeake Bay Critical Area is 9,829 acres, of which 9,530.3 acres are designated as Resource 
Conservation Area (RCA), and the remaining 298.7 acres are designated as Limited Development Area 
(LDA) and Intensely Developed Areas (IDA). The total growth allocation used by the County in the 
Chesapeake Bay Critical Area is 141.4 acres, and the total growth allocation remaining within the 
Chesapeake Bay Critical Area for the County is 335.1 acres. 
 
The Atlantic Coastal Bays Critical Area is 22,955 acres, of which 16,379 acres are designated as RCA, 
3,116 acres are designated as LDA, and 3,460 acres are designated as IDA. The total growth allocation 
used by the County in the Atlantic Coastal Bays Critical Area is 445.8 acres, and the total growth 
allocation remaining within the Atlantic Coastal Bays Critical Area for the County is 373.2 acres. 
There is a provision in the Critical Area law and the County’s updated Critical Area ordinance that 
allows for up to 150 acres of growth allocation to be transferred from the Atlantic Coastal Bays 
Critical Area to the Chesapeake Bay Critical Area, or vice-versa, as long as the remaining acreage in 
the designated pot is insufficient to allow approval of the growth allocation proposal for which the 
County is seeking approval. 
 
STAFF RECOMMENDATION 
 
Commission staff recommends that the Commission concur with the Chair’s determination that this 
comprehensive review can be processed as a refinement to the Worcester County’s Critical Area 
Program. Further, given the update is consistent with the purposes, policies, and goals of the Critical 
Area law, staff recommends that the Chair approve the comprehensive review as proposed.
