# Overview 

In January 1990, the Governor’s Task Force on Trees and Forests was created to assess the problems facing Maryland’s trees and forests, identify solutions to these problems and more importantly, promote good land stewardship and protection of Maryland’s trees and forests. The Task Force developed the _Report of The Governor’s Task Force on Trees and Forests in Maryland,_ July 12, 1990, which listed 21 task force recommendations. The seventh recommendation was to “Adopt a statewide tree and forest conservation, protection and reforestation law that provides for local implementation.” This recommendation led to the creation of the Forest Conservation Act. 

The Forest Conservation Act of 1991 (FCA) was enacted to protect the forests of Maryland by making the identification and protection of forests and other sensitive areas an integral part of the site planning process. It is administered by the Maryland Forest Service, but implemented by both state and local governments. The Maryland Forest Service is responsible for the effective implementation of the Forest Conservation Act through oversight of local forest conservation programs and direct review of state-funded and state agency projects. The law is designed to recognize that development will still occur, but that when it does it must take into account and retain the ecological significance of forest. The law is not designed as a “no net loss” policy; rather that goal is captured through Chapter 384 of 2013 - Natural Resources - Forest Preservation Act of 2013. The primary objective of the Forest Conservation Act is to minimize loss of forest land during development and ensure that priority areas for forest retention and planting are identified and protected during the development process. These priority areas include areas adjacent to streams and wetlands, on steep slopes and erodible soils, and in or adjacent to large contiguous forest blocks. 

The FCA established standards for local authorities to enforce during development. Identification and mapping of the priority areas occurs during the development review and approval of a Forest Stand Delineation. Protection of these areas occurs through the establishment of long-term protection agreements as part of the local approval of Forest Conservation Plans. In addition, sensitive areas located offsite provide opportunities for replanting, conservation and protection under a local comprehensive plan or when approved as a technique for afforestation or reforestation. 

Since July of 1993, the DNR FS has received annual reports from local governments that contain data on the implementation of their locally adopted forest conservation programs. The data submitted includes: number and location of projects; amount of acres cleared, conserved, and planted in connection with development projects; the amount of fee-in-lieu and penalties collected and expended; and the costs of implementing the local program. The DNR FS has compiled the information from the time period of January 1993 through June 2012 into a twenty year summary of forest conservation activities statewide. The data analyzed focuses on the amount of forest on the site prior to development and the amount of forest designated to remain or be planted on site after development.  The following is an analysis of the data.

## _Definitions_ 

- **Existing forest** – acres of forest under development review (forest conservation review) reported by the local jurisdictions and state programs. 

- **Retained forest** – acres of existing forest under an approved long-term protection agreement, commonly forest conservation easements, to meet required mitigation and cannot be cleared. 

- **Cleared forest** – acres of existing forest approved for clearing under development review (forest conservation review) reported by the local jurisdictions and state programs. 

- **Planted** – acres of trees planted to meet required mitigation and under an approved long-term protection agreement, commonly a forest conservation easement, as reported by the local jurisdictions and state programs. 

- **Banked** – acres of mitigation, planted and/or retained that meets the local jurisdiction’s requirement for forest mitigation banking.
