# County Commissioners minutes 2025-09-23

<!-- page 1 -->
**COUNTY COMMISSIONERS OF CAROLINE COUNTY** Caroline County Courthouse 109 Market Street, Room 110 Denton, MD 21629 

**Minutes September 23, 2025** 

**Special meeting with Maryland Association of Counties (MACo)** 

The Commissioners met with **Michael Sanderson, Executive Director of the Maryland Association of Counties (MACo),** and **Jack Wilson, MACo President and Queen Anne’s County Commissioner.** 

Mr. Wilson began by reflecting on MACo’s recent practice of conducting county visits following the close of the legislative session. Initially, these meetings were intended to provide local officials with a chance to look back on session outcomes, but he noted that the tone quickly became discouraging. Counties consistently expressed frustration over budget constraints and increasing mandates. To address this, MACo has sought to redirect the focus toward preparing for upcoming challenges, identifying opportunities, and equipping counties with a clearer picture of what to expect in Annapolis this winter. 

Mr. Sanderson expanded on this point by noting that state leaders have been referring to the most recent adjustments as “budget cuts,” but he clarified that in reality, no state programs have been outright eliminated. Instead, the State has shifted costs downward onto counties. He explained that while this tactic is politically expedient, it places counties in a precarious fiscal position and mirrors the broader federal trend of shifting financial burdens onto state governments. According to Mr. Sanderson, every county has a projection year—some sooner, some later—when its mandated contributions to the Blueprint for Maryland’s Future will surpass its entire available county revenue base. This looming reality, he emphasized, is one of the most pressing structural threats to county fiscal stability. 

Mr. Wilson provided a more specific example from Queen Anne’s County. Over the past three years, the Blueprint has cost them in excess of $30 million. While rising property tax revenues and the development of senior housing communities have temporarily provided new revenue streams, these cannot be relied upon indefinitely. He stressed that while Queen Anne’s has unique circumstances that help to soften the blow, many other counties do not have comparable resources or growth opportunities. Mr. Sanderson added that local school systems across Maryland are increasingly struggling to maintain both required programming and balanced budgets under the Blueprint mandates. He predicted that this tension will sharpen significantly and become one of the defining issues for the next political term in Annapolis. 

The discussion then shifted to housing policy. Mr. Sanderson explained that MACo expects the General Assembly to pass a significant housing bill during the upcoming session. However, he cautioned that Annapolis often takes a “one-size-fits-all” approach, applying policies equally to

large urban jurisdictions and small rural counties. This approach, he stressed, is problematic because the housing markets, infrastructure needs, and available land in places like Baltimore City or Montgomery County differ dramatically from those in Caroline or other rural counties. He encouraged the Commissioners to remain vigilant and provide testimony emphasizing the distinct challenges of rural jurisdictions. 

Energy policy was also highlighted as a critical issue. Mr. Sanderson described what he called a “triple threat” facing Maryland’s energy landscape: the aging of existing power generation infrastructure, surging demand for electricity, and a lack of investment in new power plants. He noted that the rapid growth of artificial intelligence applications has intensified the problem because data centers—driven by demand for AI services—consume enormous amounts of electricity. This rising demand is placing unprecedented pressure on the electrical grid and will likely drive up consumer prices. He stressed that while counties have limited direct control over energy markets, the policy choices made in Annapolis will have long-term effects on both affordability and local economic development. 

The Commissioners then raised the issue of Federalsburg and municipal charter stability. Commissioner Porter expressed concerns that Federalsburg could face bankruptcy and possibly forfeit its charter. Mr. Sanderson responded that although such scenarios are rare, they are not without precedent. He referenced the case of Sudlersville, where the county had to intervene, assume responsibility for water and sewer operations, and commission third-party audits to stabilize the situation. The Caroline County Commissioners pointed out that Federalsburg has not completed its FY2024 audit and is unlikely to complete its FY2025 audit on time. They also discussed Federalsburg’s exploration of disbanding its police force and contracting with the Caroline County Sheriff’s Office for law enforcement coverage. Mr. Wilson commented that Centreville had considered a similar consolidation in the past, but it ultimately did not go through. Commissioner Porter observed that the rising costs of law enforcement are making small-town police forces increasingly unsustainable. 

The Commissioners also raised questions about the State’s cannabis tax fund. Commissioner Breeding asked whether counties without officially designated “disproportionately impacted areas” could establish their own criteria and still qualify for funding. Mr. Sanderson responded that MACo has received similar inquiries and is committed to researching whether flexibility could be granted under the current statute or regulatory framework. 

The matter of legal advertising and publication of record was also discussed. The Commissioners noted that these requirements create ongoing costs for county governments. Mr. Sanderson explained that Dorchester County had previously pursued local legislation to reduce the burden, but the bill faced numerous caveats and strong resistance from press associations. He emphasized that the press often makes a compelling argument in favor of maintaining public advertising requirements, given the importance of transparency and the financial dependency of many local newspapers on this revenue stream. 

The discussion concluded with a brief update on solar policy. The Commissioners explained that Caroline County is in the process of reworking its solar ordinance. Commissioner Porter asked how much of the State’s budget is currently used to subsidize solar development. Mr. Sanderson

replied that the State has deliberately created an artificial market for solar providers by guaranteeing local demand, which is reinforced through various state subsidy structures. He noted that this market design continues to complicate local land use debates, especially in rural counties. The meeting concluded at 1:00 p.m., with both Mr. Wilson and Mr. Sanderson encouraging Caroline County to stay actively engaged in MACo’s advocacy efforts as these statewide issues evolve.
