# County Commissioners minutes 2025-02-11

# **County Meeting with Department of Housing and Community Development and Secretary** 

# **Day** 

February 11, 2025 Attendees: 

_Commissioner Porter, Breeding, and Bartz,  Daniel Fox Deputy Administrator, Stewart Barroll Attorney. County Administrator, Katheleen Freeman, PIO, Jennfier Reibly, Department of Housing Secretary Jake Day, DHCD: Joseph Pulver, Julia Glanz, Georgy Hare, Rosa Cruz, Robyne Chaconas_ 

# **Discussion of Dayspring Townhouses I & II:** 

# **The meeting adjourned at 3:30 PM** 

Deputy Administrator Fox opened the meeting by providing a brief background on the county’s involvement with Daysprings I and II. Daysprings I was built in 1994, and Daysprings II was built in 1996. Both projects were acquired and developed through a program with the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD). The county took out a loan for each, agreeing to continue ownership with restrictions identified in the agreement. The properties have been managed by Interfaith Housing, which was later transferred to East Coast Housing. The reasoning for this change was due to ongoing issues with the project. Both townhome complexes are outdated, and due to the revenue restrictions, the properties have been operating on a "dollar in, dollar out" basis. This has made it difficult to set aside funds for ongoing maintenance and repairs. 

# **Resident Concerns** : 

Residents reached out to the county after realizing that we are the title holders, complaining about the living conditions. The county staff then immediately worked to familiarize themselves with the available options. An overview of the situation was shared with DHCD, and the timeline was reviewed. 

# **County's Current Position** : 

Deputy Administrator Fox explained that the Attorney General's office confirmed that a transfer of ownership is possible, but the county must retain a share. However, Deputy Administrator Fox stated that the county’s goal is to exit the project completely and to do everything possible to facilitate the property remaining as low-income housing. 

# **Discussion of Options** : 

A call was set up to discuss the available options and to explore if DHCD or Secretary Day could assist. Commissioner Porter, who has a professional background in property management, stated that the units need significant renovations, with many still having their original structures. When these properties were built, Interfaith Housing was an active nonprofit organization, and the county needed to be the signatory. He stressed that 50% of the units are vacant due to nonpayment, and there is also a community activist group from Baltimore involved. Many cases are currently in court. He concluded that Caroline County does not have a housing authority and is not set up to own housing complexes. While the county hopes to keep the units affordable, the

<!-- page 2 -->
best solution may be to transfer ownership to a qualified nonprofit organization that is familiar with managing such properties. The county cannot continue managing them. 

# **Questions on Transfer and Ownership** : 

Commissioner Breeding also raised the question of what the county would owe upon transferring full ownership, and how this could be worked out. Secretary Day acknowledged the issue and noted that these types of situations arise from time to time. He stated that DHCD has examples of how similar cases have been managed. He mentioned Mitchell Landing and Riverside Homes, where two partners took ownership of the City of Salisbury’s properties. He emphasized that private partners have been part of successful solutions in the past and that DHCD could provide a list of successful entities. 

# **Mortgage Payoff** : 

Commissioner Porter inquired whether the mortgage would need to be paid off in full. Secretary Day confirmed that it would, but this could be done through the recapitalization of the property, as long as the property retains an aspect of affordability. 

# **Inspection and Regulatory Changes** : 

Commissioner Porter mentioned that a third-party inspection of the units was conducted, and while some regulations had changed, the biggest issue identified was with smoke detectors, which have since been corrected. Gregory Hare stated that the situation requires a holistic approach. He noted that simply transferring ownership will not solve the underlying issues. However, if a partner is found, DHCD could help with recapitalization, and additional options could be considered. The redevelopment process needs to be carefully managed. 

# **Unit Details** : 

Daysprings I has 26 units, and Daysprings II has 20 units, for a total of 46 units. Both projects were funded through program loans. Commissioner Breeding mentioned that the current payoff amount is $4,045,325. 

# **Exploring Ownership and Refinancing** : 

Mr. Hare acknowledged uncertainty about the income level of the properties but suggested that the county work with the Office of the Attorney General (OAG) to explore refinancing options. Joseph Pulver pointed out that the ownership issue is critical, as there must be some level of government ownership. He asked if there was a way to maintain local government involvement while proceeding with a transfer. Secretary Day asked if a limited ownership percentage could be arranged, where the county could be exempt from payments for the life of the loan. Mr. Pulvar explained that there is no recourse on these loans, meaning that if the county defaults, they would foreclose but could not be sued for the remaining balance. 

# **Future Considerations** : 

Commissioner Breeding stressed the importance of learning from past agreements to avoid future complications. He expressed that the county’s goal is still to get 100% out of ownership, and it seems the only viable option is to list the property for sale and pay off the loan. He asked if reserve funds accumulated over time could be used to pay down part of the loan. Mr. Pulvar agreed that there should be no issue with this.

# **Other Issues** : 

Commissioner Breeding also raised concerns about the location of the townhomes on an old wastewater treatment plant site. He pointed out that only minimal renovations had been made, such as roof repairs and some siding replacement. Even with the partnership, the county is under pressure to deliver high-quality housing but lacks the time and resources to do so effectively. 

# **Developer Interest** : 

Commissioner Porter mentioned that based on financial calculations, this could be an attractive opportunity for a developer. The property has been appraised, but they are awaiting final numbers. There may be a bidding process before any transfer occurs, and the mortgage will need to be paid off first. 

# **Questions on Project Lifespan** : 

Katheleen Freeman, Administrator  asked if there is a limit or intended lifespan for such projects. Secretary Day responded that these projects could last in perpetuity, often extending beyond the expected lifespan. 

# **Loan Repayment Issue** : 

Deputy Administrator Fox noted that the original documents from the 1990s indicated some disagreements regarding the repayment terms of the loan. He wanted to ensure that the county was on the same page regarding the full repayment of the interest at the time of the sale. Mr. Pulvar clarified that the loan document did not reference any special terms related to repayment. Stewart Barroll, County Attorney, added that the document was akin to a commitment letter, and there were no attorneys involved in drafting the paperwork. Mr. Pulvar offered to discuss this further with the Attorney General's office. 

# **Next Steps** : 

Deputy Administrator Fox asked if they could investigate the repayment terms. Mr. Pulvar inquired whether the primary options for the county involve selling the property. Ms. Freeman confirmed that the county’s preference is to fully transfer ownership to a qualified nonprofit and maintain the property as affordable housing. If that is not possible, the county would consider selling the property and paying off the mortgage. They would then come back to DHCD with the offers received. 

# **Bid Process and Follow-Up** : 

Commissioner Porter stated that the county will proceed with a bidding process and, once the appraisal comes back, will send it over to DHCD as soon as possible. It was confirmed that the reserve funds could be used to pay off part of the mortgage. 

There being no more discussion, the meeting ended at 4:17 p.m.
