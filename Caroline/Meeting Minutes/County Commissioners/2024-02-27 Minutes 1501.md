# **February 27, 2024** 

# **FY25 Budget Workshop: Budget Round Table Discussion** 

The Caroline County Commissioners hosted their annual roundtable budget discussion with external agencies receiving more than $25,000 in funding and internal County Department. President Breeding opened the meeting and thanked everyone for attending. He stated that they would go around the room and listen to each agency’s budget request, and asked folks to give a brief explanation of any request for increased funding. 

# **<u>INTERNAL AGENCIES</u>** 

**County Commissioners:** Katheleen Freeman, County Administrator, stated that the Commissioner’s Office is submitting a below flat budget, and she has been able to reallocate funds internally. 

**Office of Finance,** Daniel Fox, Deputy Director, stated that his team has submitted an above flat budget due to increases in contracted services. He stated that services such as Tyler Technologies, which is the financial and tax software they use to run the business, also comes with a host of yearly maintenance fees. 

**Office of Law** : Stewert Barroll, County Attorney, has requested some reallocations regarding conference expenses and continuing education line items to reflect actual charges. He explained that the legal service line item included in their budget is a catch-all from all the departments, but year to year it's subject to change, which is why we increased that line item. 

**Office of Human Resources:** Sherry Bratton, Director, stated that the Office of Human Resources is submitting an above flat budget due to actuarial services and recruitment, advertisement services. Ms. Bratton also stated that payroll services have been projected higher as well. Ms. Freeman stated that they are looking into Human Resources based software for the future as well that would enable tracking employees’ records. This would allow supervisor and employees full access to their files and workflows. 

**Planning and Codes:** Crystal Dadds, Director, stated that her department has submitted an above flat budget for several items. She stated that that the departments permitting software maintenance has a 4 ½% increase, which is fixed yearly. She also stated that she is asking for a $100,000 placeholder on the planning side of the department, which would allow contractual services for that year, if they don’t find a permit employee to fill a position. She also explained that costs have

increased for liquor licenses, due to an increase in printing and publications, as well as a line item for association dues. 

**Public Works** : Daniel Fox, Deputy Director, asked Bryan North, Road Superintendent and Kevin Brown, Facilities, to move forward. He stated that they had a sit-down with each internal department, in the division of Public Works.  He went on to explain that most of the departments have stayed flat or had a small increase due to contractual services with outside agencies, such as Maryland Environmental Services, who services the landfills. Mr. Fox explained that the county roads division went up around $16,000 due to the cost of roads maintenance. Mr. Fox explained that tar and chipping is out of the capital budget, and not reflected in operating. Commissioner Bartz asked to revisit the entire Public Works budget, to make sure they had reallocated enough funding to ensure projected projects could be finalized. Mr. Fox also reminded the public and Board that funding to surface dirt roads in the county, would be funded through a onetime ARPA (American Rescue Plan Act) payment. 

Mr. Fox then moved on to the budget for facilities, which had decreased $50,000. He stated that he is working with Kevin Brown regarding a realistic budget. Mr. Brown explained that decrease, which is due to several facilities projects around the county being finalized. Commissioner Porter expressed interest in the department allocating funding to update the clock tower on the Courthouse and fix the clock face. 

**Recreation and Parks:** Jamie Beechey, Director, stated that Recreation and Parks has submitted a below flat budget on the recreation side. Regarding parks, she explained that items have increased for insurance, repairs and maintenance which is the cost of doing business for the public. She also stated they are requesting to add an additional line item to add funding for recreational ball fields, which the department has lacked a line item for in the past budgets. Ms. Beechey explained her request for an additional park maintenance worker,  which is justified, to help finalize several of the capital projects her department has around the county. 

Commissioner Bartz stated told Ms. Beechey that he personally felt Recreation and Parks would benefit from adding more than one maintenance worker. Ms. Beechey stated that there may be room for a part-time position to fill in for seasonal gaps like grass cutting. 

Ms. Beechey wrapped up, by updating the board on Program Open Space funding. She explained that this is funding from that the state her department heavily relies on to accomplish capital projects. Although it has yet to be confirmed, funding is projected to decrease over 1/3 from previous years. 

**Department of Emergency Services** : Mark Sheridan, Director, stated that as usual his department has several increases due to the cost of doing business and updating equipment. For the administrative side of Emergency Services, he explained that there are no major increases. On the 9-1-1 Communications side, he emphasized the hard work the Office of Finance and his team put in to help find a solution regarding overtime. He stated that they were able to calculate leave hours to show the vaccines needed to be filled ahead of time, which should reflect a decrease in the budget regarding overtime. Mr. Sheridan stated that he would like to hire a part-time employee to help handle insurance duties.

Regarding Capital request for Emergency Services, Mr. Sheridan that they are looking to replace an ambulance and dispatch console in the 9-1-1 Center. He also stated that as the 9-1-1 Center grows they need to keep in mind the need for additional space. He ended by stressing the importance of updating all of the medical equipment used on calls, which is reflected in his request. 

**The Office of Technolog** **<mark>y</mark>** <mark>, Chris Rice, Director, s</mark> tated that IT has requested a budget increase of 5%. In previous years, Mr. Rice explained that he has submitted an 11% increase, so the department has caught up on some increases due to annual maintenance. Hardware line items have decreased since we have been able to update new hardware throughout departments. He stated that his overtime line item has also increased, from adding an hourly employee to his team. He stated that IT’s Capital requests total around $7,000 which captures updates regarding security features and replacing some software. 

**Economic Development** : Bob Zimberoff, Director, stated that this was his first time going through the county’s budget process, and he thanked Daniel Fox and Stacy Seward for their help. He went on to state that currently his budget is 10,000 below that flat and he stated that they shifted money around for additional marketing funds. He stated the intent is used for targeting ads on social media 

**Department of Corrections** : Charles Scott, Warden, stated that Corrections has several areas that they are adjusting, departmentally. He stated that bringing his staff up to competitive compensation is a main concern for him and is reflected in his request. He stated that his team has worked really hard in boosting morale and retention, however they need to remain competitive. Mr. Scott explained the need for a position to be funded for an officer to work on pretrial requirements and assessment. Currently the is one individual in that position who works Monday- Friday, and they need a position to cover weekends. The Warden stressed that in prior years the Department of Corrections made out well budgetary due to many items coming to them free due to the pandemic. However, he stressed that times have changed, and his budget reflects the shift in cost of maintaining the department. Mr. Scott stated that regarding Capital budget requests, he is looking for a new vehicle to transport inmates. He thanked the Caroline County Sheriff’s Office for lending old Tahoe’s and explorers; however, the Tahoe’s have been extremely unreliable. He stated that the explorers leave little to no room in transporting an inmate. 

The Commissioners thanked everyone for participating in the annual budget roundtable. President Breeding stated that balancing a budget is a difficult task, however having internal departments and outside agency’s working together makes the process easier and allows everyone to benefit. 

A 10-minute recess was taken. 

# <u>EXTERNAL AGENCIES</u> 

**Caroline County Circuit Court** : Heather Price, Judge, stated that the Circuit Court has requested a total $7,000 increase for employee compensation, due to employees taking on more responsibilities. Judge Price also stated that a request was being added to ensure funding is there to support their court recording system which is need of up updates. She stated that the current system is over 8 years old and cuts out during court hearings. Judge Price also stressed that she is

<!-- page 4 -->
the only Judge in the State of Maryland that does not have an employee and Judicial Assistant. She stated that funding for that position would be extremely beneficial to the court system. 

**State’s Attorney’s Office:** Sloane Franklin, State’s Attorney, stated that his office has requested a budget that reflects increase to allow for several employees to be financially compensated. Mr. Franklin stressed that when he was appointed as State’s Attorney his office was highly understaffed, and he would like to maintain and thank the employees that helped him rebuild the department. 

**Caroline County Sheriff’s Office:** Donald Baker, Sheriff, stated that the Sheriff’s Office has submitted a 5,000 increase regarding insurance increases. He stated that most of the Sheriff’s Office budget has been able to remain flat. His Capital request reflect an increase for Animal Control Officer to be compensated, as they are currently the lowest paid employees in the County. This request also captures their need for a new Van for Animal Control. 

**Board of Elections** : Alison Murphy, Director, Board of Elections, stated that she is presenting a budget with increases in funding due to it being a presidential election year. As always, Ms. Murphy thanked the board for understanding the state mandated increase her department must comply with. She stated that due to a change is legislation, Caroline County Board of Elections must now pay retuning elections judge’s an additional $100 over the original mandate salary. She explained that to alleviate the cost the State of Maryland is paying half of those costs. 

**Board of Education:** Dr. Derek Simmions, Superintendent, stated that the Board of Education had presented the Commissioners with a budget request which encompasses the mandated funding the County legally gives each year. Dr. Simmions stressed that it’s unfortunate they don’t have confirmed numbers from the State of Maryland regarding the Blueprint formula. The Commissioners stressed to the public that although the Board of Education makes up for the biggest budget expense for the County, currently as they are trying to calculate their budget, they still don’t know the total amount the State will mandate them to give. Dr. Simmons also went over the cut in the disparity funding, which set to be cut drastically for  Caroline County. 

**Chesapeake College** : Dr. Coppersmith President stated that Chesapeake College thanked the County for their continuing support of the college. He stated that enrollment is up and 40% of students are from Caroline County. He also stressed the increase in dual enrollment. Dr. Coppersmith stated that Chesapeake College is requesting a 4.5% increase in funding for FY2025. This is due to the community colleges losing over 10% in State funding due to the CADE formula. He stated that he and the Commissioners are both paying close attention to the loss of funding. Dr. Coppersmith stressed that they have been told some funds could be reinstated through the BRFA (Budget Reconciliation and Financing Act). 

Dr. Coppersmith stressed that the college has increased tuition by 2.5% to help offset the loss in funding. Commissioner Porter stated he and staff have been closing following the CADE funding cut. He stated that although it’s based off a percentage of what State colleges receive, only community colleges saw a drastic cut.

Dr. Coppersmith stated that the Cade Formula started in the 1990’s to improve equity at the community college level. He stated that Chesapeake College only became fully funded two years ago. Receiving a full 29%, only to have it cut two years later. 

**Caroline County Library** : Debby Bennet, Executive Director, thanked the board  for the continuing support of the local libraries. She emotionally expressed her gratitude for their leadership and partnership over her tenure. She stated that this would be her final budget workshop, as she is retiring in the fall. Most recently the central library branch located in Denton received major renovations. She stated that an official ribbon cutting ceremony was being scheduled to highlight the new amenities of the facility. Ms. Bennet stated that the library is requesting funding to give employee merit and cola increases. She stated that retaining staff has remained a top issue. 

**Upper Shore Aging,** Andy Hollis and Cory Peck, Vice president of Board, Mr. Hollis that the Commissioners for their continued support of Upper Shore Aging. Commissioner Bartz represents the Commissioner on the Upper Shore Aging Advisory Board. Mr. Hollis stated they are requesting funding in the amount of $40,000. He stated that those funds would pay for a part time employee to help with the Federalsburg Senior Center, as well as give some additional improvements to furniture and maintenance of centers. He stated that currently 18,900 Caroline County seniors receive services from their Meals on Wheels program. Mr. Hollis and Mr. Peck stressed the need to help spread the word on services they can provide to our seniors in the community. They stated they provide mental and physical education as walls as can provide medical supplies, wheelchairs, and even legal services. 

**Caroline County Volunteer Fire Companies** : Representatives from several local volunteer fire companies attend the meeting. Todd Berneski, President of Denton Volunteer Fire Company (DVFC) stated that the companies are requesting an 10% increase over last years funding due to inflations. He stressed the drastic change in the economy and the price it costs to provide life saving services. Mr. Berneski explained that a fire truck that cost $500,000 11 years ago, now cost $950,000.  Bryan Ebling, Ridgely Volunteer Fire Company representative, stressed the need for recruitment and retention of local volunteers. The Commissioners expressed the shared concern for retaining active members and stated that they are willing to work with the companies to find a solution. Commissioner Porter stated that Caroline County has contributed over $800,000 since 2019 to local fire companies. 

**Maryland Extension Services,** Paul Rickert, Area Extension Director, thanked the Board for their time and gave a summary of what the local Maryland Extension office provided to Caroline County. Mr. Rickert stated that his budget reflects an increase due to the cost of rent increase, as well as the annual merit increase for employees. 

**Caroline County Soil Conservation,** Trista North, Director of Erosion and Sediment Control, thanked the Board for their time. She stated that Soil Conservation is requesting $50,000 to allow for maintenance on the tax ditches located in the county.

**Caroline County Humane Society** : Charlotte Shearin, President and Cathie Moore, Tressure, stated that they have submitted a budget request in the amount of $60,000. Funding would also go towards medical expenses used for vets, and supplies and food for the animals in the shelter. They stated this would go towards the mandatory minimum wage increase for all full-time staff. Ms. Shearin stressed the positive outcome of the Trap and Return program which started in 2022. This program has allowed for over 1,500 cats become vaccinated and spayed/neutered. 

There being no further comments, the Commissioners adjourned their meeting at 12:40 p.m.
