# County Commissioners minutes 2025-04-17

<!-- page 1 -->
**Meeting Minutes Annual Budget Meeting: Chesapeake College and Five Counties Date:** April 17, 2025, **Time:** 6:20 PM – 7:20 PM **Location:** Chesapeake College 

**Welcome and Introductions** : The meeting officially commenced at 6:20 PM. Representatives from all five counties—Caroline, Dorchester, Kent, Queen Anne’s, and Talbot—as well as Chesapeake College staff, were in attendance. Each participant introduced themselves, 

**Chesapeake College Budget Discussion** : Dr. Cliff Coppersmith expressed his gratitude to all county representatives and college staff for their attendance and contributions in preparing for the meeting. He highlighted the college’s strong financial position, noting that enrollment numbers are at their highest level since 2017, a clear sign of recovery from the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic. Dr. Coppersmith also acknowledged the recent visit from the Governor, held in partnership with Queen Anne’s County, and attributed the college’s fiscal stability to the strength of the leadership team and the diligent efforts of Vice President Karen Smith. 

**Karen Smith, Vice President for Administrative Services, then presented the proposed budget for Fiscal Year 2025** . The total request includes a 5% increase in county contributions, amounting to $365,628, with each county’s portion varying based on its share of Full-Time Equivalent (FTE) students. The budget also includes funding for Phase 1 of the new Queen Anne’s Technical Building, which is a key component of the college’s future infrastructure development. 

**Dorchester County** : Commissioner George Pfeffer questioned the rationale behind the 5% increase in contributions. In response, Dr. Coppersmith explained that the increase allows the college to implement a 3% across-the-board salary adjustment for employees, which is essential to remain competitive and address historical undercompensating. He emphasized that inflation has impacted operational costs, and that salary competitiveness is crucial for staff retention and recruitment. 

Commissioner Mike Detmer elaborated on Dorchester County’s financial hardships, noting that the county is facing a $2 million shortfall due to Blueprint funding mandates. All departments have received budget cuts below previous year levels. He detailed additional financial pressures, including increased property taxes, the elimination of the tax differential for Hurlock and Cambridge, and a hike in the 911 fee to 4.25 cents per line. Detmer also pointed out that Dorchester is still paying $1.5 million annually through 2030 for its own technical center, and due to the geographic distance from Chesapeake College, its high school students are unable to utilize Chesapeake's technical offerings.

**Talbot County** : Representatives from Talbot County Chuck Callahan requested a breakdown of student enrollment. Dr. Coppersmith responded that approximately 37% of students are dual enrollment students, who are considered non-credit until after high school graduation. He added that the overall student body is evenly split, with half pursuing workforce development programs and the other half preparing to transfer to four-year institutions. Most dual enrollment students attend classes in their high schools, participate online, or travel to the Wye Mills or Cambridge campuses. These students typically receive financial support through Blueprint funding, and benefit from a 25% tuition and fee discount, which is reflected in the high school budgets. 

**Chesapeake College Capital Budget – Queen Anne’s Technical Building** : During discussion of the capital budget, Commissioner Larry Porter (Caroline County) inquired about the projected square footage and total cost of the proposed Queen Anne’s Technical Building. Karen Smith estimated that the facility will cost approximately $50 million, although the final amount remains subject to change. The current budget request pertains only to the design phase, which is part of a broader total request of $56 million across all phases. 

Commissioner Mike Detmer expressed Dorchester County’s concern about their role in funding the project, asking whether any commitment was required during the meeting. In response, Commissioner Jack N. Wilson, Jr. (Queen Anne’s County) offered a solution: Queen Anne’s County would cover Dorchester’s $180,000 share of the design phase in order to keep the project on schedule and prevent delays in the state’s funding process. 

Dorchester County representatives expressed their appreciation for Queen Anne’s support, and for the understanding shown by the other counties in allowing them to remain financially neutral during the design phase. Dr. Coppersmith and Commissioner Wilson reiterated that Dorchester’s contribution to the design phase could remain at zero and emphasized that this arrangement would not bind them to future commitments. 

Commissioner Wilson encouraged all counties to advocate for state bond funding and potential federal support for the technical building project.

County Open Discussion: Commissioner Larry Porter (Caroline County) raised concern about a major solar energy bill currently under consideration by the Governor. He cautioned that if the Public Service Commission is granted authority to approve 5% of Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs), it could lead to as much as 100,000 acres of prime farmland being diverted to solar development. Commissioner Porter urged his fellow county leaders to advocate for a gubernatorial veto or, at the very least, amendments to mitigate the bill’s impact on agricultural land. 

Commissioner Jack N. Wilson, Jr. (Queen Anne’s County) noted that a Trump-era executive order set to expire on April 20 could eliminate federal solar subsidies, shifting the financial burden onto the state. He proposed that Maryland establish a statewide solar trading system (TTR), which would allow counties with higher solar commitments to trade their obligations with those who prefer to limit solar development, fostering regional flexibility and cooperation.
