# County Commissioners minutes 2026-09-22

<!-- page 1 -->
**COUNTY COMMISSIONERS OF CAROLINE COUNTY** Caroline County Courthouse 

109 Market Street, Room 110 Denton, MD 21629 

**Minutes September 22, 2026** 

**Special meeting with Maryland Association of Counties (MACo)** 

Present: President J. Travis Breeding; Vice President Larry C. Porter; Commissioner N. Franklin Bartz; County Administrator Katheleen Freeman; Deputy Administrator Daniel Fox; Public Information Officer, Jennifer Reibly; and County Attorney, Jesse Hammock 

The Commissioners met with **Michael Sanderson, Executive Director of the Maryland Association of Counties (MACo),** and **M C Keegan-Ayer , MACo President and Frederik County Council Member.** 

Ms. Keegan-Ayer, President of the Maryland Association of Counties (MACo), welcomed everyone and explained that the meeting was part of MACo’s annual county tour. These visits provide an opportunity to meet individually with each county, discuss local needs and issues affecting staff and constituents, and receive updates on State matters from Mr. Sanderson. She described MACo as a nonprofit, nonpartisan organization focused on helping counties provide the services their communities need. 

Mr. Sanderson, Executive Director, thanked the County for hosting MACo again and provided a packet outlining the organization’s current work and legislative priorities. During a typical legislative session, MACo reviews between 200 and 300 bills and actively engages on approximately 50. He described its legislative portfolio as particularly broad and extensive because Maryland relies on county governments more heavily than many other states. 

Mr. Sanderson discussed several major issues raised during visits with other jurisdictions, beginning with affordability. Concerns initially centered on rising utility rates and electric bills but have expanded to housing availability and childcare costs. He estimated that Maryland is approximately 60,000 to 90,000 housing units short of where it should be, with development slow to catch up to demand. These combined expenses have contributed to growing concerns that Maryland is no longer affordable. 

Returning and newly elected senators, delegates, and county commissioners have heard these concerns repeatedly from voters. Mr. Sanderson observed that officials who hear affordability identified as the leading issue while campaigning may enter office only to discover how little direct control they have over costs driven by market conditions and supply and demand. 

Regarding housing, Mr. Sanderson anticipated that State lawmakers would propose changes to local approval processes and zoning requirements. In his view, those changes would not address the primary market pressures but could become another means of limiting local land use authority.

The conversation shifted to data centers and the strong opposition they have generated among voters. Participants discussed a Frederick County site with limited potential for other uses that nevertheless faced significant public resistance. The discussion also included a long-term Frederick County elected official whose loss of office was attributed to the data center issue. 

Potential revenue from the project included approximately $50 million in one-time recordation and property transfer taxes, along with possible tax revenue from equipment within the facilities. Mr. Sanderson referenced polling indicating that Americans would rather live near a coal power plant than a data center, illustrating the intensity of public opposition. Ms. Keegan-Ayer added that some residents characterize data center development as allowing Elon Musk to take over the area. 

Turning to the State budget, Mr. Sanderson described the General Fund outlook as the worst he has seen. The State is attempting to keep up with school construction and transportation commitments while largely operating in basic maintenance mode. Funding is focused on sustaining existing obligations, with little available for substantial new projects. Counties are also facing a decline in highway user funding. 

Mr. Sanderson reported that the Governor had asked agencies to identify reductions of 3% and 10% compared with the prior year, while placing limitations on which areas could be reduced. 

Commissioner Breeding discussed residents’ concerns about property taxes, noting that Caroline County is not among the highest-taxed jurisdictions. He compared Caroline’s rate of approximately 96 cents with Talbot County’s rate of approximately 70 cents. With lower-tax jurisdictions surrounding Caroline, including those in Delaware, residents frequently compare their tax bills with those of their neighbors. 

Commissioner Breeding emphasized the need for regulators to consider affordability and livability alongside environmental goals. He expressed concern that the pursuit of a cleaner environment is creating costs that residents cannot afford. 

Environmental health regulations and permitting were also discussed. Commissioner Breeding referenced regulations created by MDA and enforced by MDE, expressing frustration that the regulatory approach creates an atmosphere in which projects are difficult to complete. He described the County’s authority over these matters as limited beyond its role in hiring and removing the health officer. 

As an example, Commissioner Breeding recalled a meeting with the Environment Secretary during which County representatives presented the circumstances of a family that had another child and wanted to add a bedroom to its home. The family faced delays while waiting for a permit to make the addition. 

Commissioner Porter recalled testifying with former Commissioner Levengood about mandatory residential sprinkler requirements. He disputed the cost information presented by the Maryland State Firemen’s Association. Because Caroline County does not have public water systems in its unincorporated areas, they obtained actual estimates for installing sprinklers in homes served by

wells and presented those figures during their testimony. He recalled feeling that their testimony was unwelcome because it challenged the costs being presented. 

While acknowledging that sprinklers are needed in some settings, Commissioner Porter questioned requiring them in every home and expressed concern about adding unnecessary costs to housing. 

Commissioner Porter asked whether potential child abuse litigation costs were included in the State’s budget discussions. Mr. Sanderson explained that the issue was being considered separately while awaiting a fiscal forecast. Preliminary estimates were alarming, with potential costs of approximately $1 million per case and several thousand cases that could emerge following the removal of the statute of limitations for claims involving sexual abuse of minors. 

Mr. Sanderson also identified unresolved questions about what constitutes an occurrence when a person experienced multiple incidents. Whether liability is assessed by facility, perpetrator, or individual incident could significantly affect the total exposure, potentially resulting in billions of dollars in liability. 

Commissioner Breeding referenced the approximately $105,000 Caroline County receives annually in cannabis revenue. Reflecting on his three years in office, he expressed frustration with repeated cost shifts, State preemption, and the limited number of positive outcomes for counties. 

Commissioner Breeding described a lack of partnership and engagement with State leadership. The Governor had met with the Commissioners once, but there had otherwise been little interaction. He expressed concern about having so little direct engagement with the State while serving as a commissioner. 

Mr. Sanderson anticipated that State leaders would develop budget recommendations designed to secure sufficient votes for passage. These could include spending reductions, funding formula changes, and adjustments to school construction and other major programs. 

He acknowledged that State officials have heard counties’ concerns about the continued shifting of costs to local governments. Teacher pension costs, for example, increase pressure on property taxes and compound the affordability problem the State is attempting to address. 

Few opportunities remain for additional cost shifts involving the State Department of Assessments and Taxation (SDAT). Mr. Sanderson identified teacher pensions as one of the more readily available options, noting that the State has shifted half of those costs to counties and could seek to increase the county share to 100%. Wealthier jurisdictions generally pay higher teacher salaries, which would affect how those costs are distributed. 

The conversation returned to highway user funding, including a reduction from 28% to 20% and approximately $100 million in funding, with roughly $60 million associated with Baltimore City. Mr. Sanderson offered little optimism about the coming year.

The Commissioners expressed frustration that they do not feel like stakeholders in State decision-making. They described their role as responding to decisions made at the State level rather than having a meaningful opportunity to help shape them. 

During the discussion of redistricting, Mr. Sanderson cautioned against placing too much weight on early polling. Voters respond to the proposal presented to them, and several weeks of party messaging could influence the outcome. Framing the proposal as a response to President Trump and Texas could help secure support for its passage. 

Ms. Keegan-Ayer described the complexity of Frederick County’s legislative representation. Approximately 15 legislators are involved when overlapping districts are considered. Its core representation consists of two senators and six delegates, but district boundaries involving portions of Washington and Carroll counties create a hybrid arrangement. She also noted that eight delegates live in Frederick County. 

Participants contrasted this with Caroline County’s representation, including a delegate who represents only half of the County. Commissioner Porter noted that residents sometimes need a map to determine who their delegate is. Placing all of Caroline County in one district would require dividing Talbot County differently, an approach he indicated was not being pursued. 

Commissioner Breeding asked whether larger counties were pushing the State to address school construction funding. Caroline County is planning a middle school project and hopes to secure funding through the State process because the County cannot afford to fund the project upfront. 

Mr. Sanderson identified a committee to examine school construction funding as the most likely outcome. The discussion reflected concern that no clear or immediate solution to the broader funding problem was available. 

Ms. Keegan-Ayer explained that Frederick County had used approximately $50 million in recordation tax revenue from data centers to forward fund an elementary school because State funding was delayed. Frederick County has been advancing funds for school projects but now faces having to stop because the State may not be able to reimburse those costs. 

Commissioner Porter thanked Ms. Keegan-Ayer for the data center information she had previously shared. 

**The meeting concluded at 1:35 p.m.**
