1 \n Draft 2020 Comprehensive Plan R

The Town of Denton submitted its draft 2020 Comprehensive Plan to the Department of Planning
and Codes for review and comment. Ms. Grunden explained to the members that Maryland law
requires that local jurisdictions forward copies of the comprehensive plan (or amendments to the
plan) to relevant state agencies and adjoining local jurisdictions for their review at least 60 days
prior to the jurisdiction's Planning Commission’s public hearing for the document.

The 2020 Plan is an update of the 2010 Denton Comprehensive Plan and includes updated
population and demographic data for the last decade as well as updated data projections for the
planning period (data tables use 2018 American Community Survey (ACS) data due to unavailability
of detailed 2020 Census data at the time of the Plan update). The Plan generally updates the data
from the 2010 Comprehensive Plan with new data and addresses, to the extent possible, the new
State requirement to assess affordability and Fair Housing issues.

Mr. Peter Johnston, Planning Consultant for the Town of Denton, was present to give an
overview of the plan. Also present were Donna Todd, Director of Planning and Codes for
the Town of Denton, and Scott Getchell, Denton Town Administrator.

Mr. Johnston stated that they started the Comprehensive Plan Update process in 2019
‘and it was a lengthy process. Since the last update new minimum requirements have
been added such as addressing the workforce needs and low-income housing. Also new is
a housing element that must address affordable housing needs in the Town.

The Plan’s Municipal Growth Element demonstrates sufficient land capacity for planned
growth within the existing Town boundaries and growth areas. The Town proposes no
expansion of its existing growth area and focuses on accommodating development
through infill development and on vacant lands located within the Town.

Mr. Breeding asked if any rezoning is proposed within the Plan. Mr. Johnston stated that
the Town had about 25 properties that were zoned RP- Recreation and Parks. In his
opinion and the Town Planning Commission agreed that every property should have some
right to develop. They are proposing to eliminate the RP zone and will return to the
previous zoning. The Town posted a public notice inviting property owners who wished to
have their property zoning classifications changed to submit applications to the Planning
Commission for consideration. The Town had 14 applications for a zoning changes, in all
cases the Planning Commission felt approved the changes.

Mr. Barroll asked if residential parking was addressed with the four-plex housing units
proposed in the Housing Element. Mr. Johnston stated that the parking needs for the
proposed four-plexes were addressed .

‘The staff recommends the Planning Commission submit a favorable comment letter to the

Denton Planning Commission in support of the Plan’s adoption.

Bridgetown Mine:

Vilt- Final Site

n Review

Mr. Kaczynski reported that the applicant, Southstar Limited Partnership, has requested a
Major Site Plan Approval for a Mineral Extraction Facility located at 15890 Oakland Road
(Maryland Route 312) between Bridgetown and Baltimore Corner. The application involves
an expansion of an existing sand, gravel and mineral extraction facility known as
Bridgetown Mine. This application is for a proposed expansion of 26.75 acres, bringing the
operation to a total of 358.83 acres. The development of this facility will include an
additional wash plant and material stockpile areas north of the existing wash plant.

The current facility has been in operation since the Board of Zoning Appeals issued a
Special Use Exception (SUE) Approval in 1994. Since that time, the facility has had multiple
SUE Applications approved for expansion. The expansions were approved by the BZA in
1996, 1998, 1999, 2002, 2008 and 2014. The proposed wash plant shown on the site plan
was originally proposed as part of the Phase VI expansion, approved by the BZA in
December of 2008; however, this wash plant has not yet been established.

The Planning Commission reviewed the Preliminary Major Site Plan April 12, 2023 and
made a positive recommendation for the applicant to proceed to the Board of Zoning
Appeals. The BZA held a public hearing for a Special Use Exception Application in
conjunction with this project on June 20, 2023. The BZA approved the Special Use
Exception and the request for a reduction of the 200-foot setback requirement for mining
‘Area D. The applicant is now seeking a Final Site Plan Approval.

Mr. Kaczynski stated that Queen Anne’s County reached out to the Department for
comment because a proposed mine's truck route appears to enter Caroline County and
enter the Bridgetown mine. This truck route would increase the truck traffic to about 200
trucks a day. Mr. Sean Callahan with Lane Engineering, representing the owner, stated
that the route is accurate, but they will not be turning into the Bridgetown Mine. The
intent of the Palmatary mine in Queen Anne's County is to replace the Bridgetown mine
with the same number of trucks.

Mr. Callahan stated that this expansion will last for the next 4 to 5 years. Mr. Jackson
asked if mining operations and truck traffic at the mine will cease when this last 27 acres
has been mined. Mr. Murray confirmed that after this expansion, the mine will be shut
down and all operations will be moved to the Palmatary mine in Queen Annes County.

\Ggrow Twin Maples Farm Upd:

Clearinghouse Project Revie

The Conservation Innovation Fund is applying to Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE)
for funding from the Bay Restoration Fund (BRF) under the Maryland Clean Water Commerce Act for
environmental practices to be conducted on farms in Caroline and Dorchester counties.

At the August 9, 2023 Planning Commission Meeting, the members voted to submit a response to
the project that included an R2 ‘Contingent Upon Certain Items’ Code (generally consistent with
County plans, programs and objectives contingent upon certain actions being taken) and the
following comment:

Caroline County's water quality goals for wastewater treatment facilities and on-site
septic systems rely on Bay Restoration Fund funding for nitrogen removal upgrades.
‘The diversion of BRF funding to non-wastewater-related projects results in negative
impacts to the County's ability to achieve nutrient reductions from wastewater
sources, particularly in the septics sector, which relies solely on BRF funding as a
grant source for implementation projects. Therefore, while we acknowledge the
value of this project, the County urges MDE to fulfill the BRF funding commitment to
wastewater treatment facility and on-site septic system nutrient upgrades before
diverting critical BRF resources to different sectors.

Ms. Grunden reported that on September 22" staff met virtually with Walid Saffouri, Director of
MDE Engineering and Capital Projects Program and the Secretary's designee for the BRF Committee,
to discuss the County's response. Mr. Saffouri understood the Planning Commission's concerns and
supports the County's position that wastewater treatment plants and septics should be the top
priorities of the Bay Restoration Fund. He suggested that the County's request to prioritize funding
for septic connections to ENR plants could be achieved if the County pursues a legislative change to
the BRF law that would move the septic connections priority above the Clean Water Commerce Act
‘on the BRF priorities list.

Mr. Saffouri requested that the Planning Commission revise its original R2 code to an Ri response
code, which indicates that the project is generally consistent with County plans and objectives, but
the County qualifies its approval with a comment about the BRF priorities. He recommended we
include the comment submitted with our original response so that it remains a part of the record of
review.

Ms. Cawley stated that she spoke with the executive for the Maryland Grain Producers, and they
are in favor of the Clean Water Act but not necessarily the miscanthus since acres will be taken out
of grain production.

Annual Transferable Development Rights Program Review

The Planning Commission is required to review the County's Transferable Development
Rights (TDR) Program annually to consider boundary line adjustments to the TOR Receiving
Area. Any boundary line adjustments to the receiving area would need to be approved by
the County Commissioners.

In the past year, 37 building permits for new (not replacement) dwellings were issued by the
County. Based on these current conditions, 23.8% (up from 23.1% from 2022) of the TOR
Receiving Area is developed. in the past year, an additional 0.7 % of the TDR Receiving Area has
been developed. The current TOR Receiving Area is adequate to support additional
development through the TDR program; however, staff has provided additional information in
Support of investigating alternative areas and/or modifications to the existing boundary.

The Planning Commission has recently been reviewing the TOR program to see if it can be
a more productive program. Mr. Kaczynski reported that currently the average size for a
new lot for a new home is at least 1.5 acres with a minimum of half an acre reserved for
the well and septic. It may be more beneficial to expand the current Receiving Area due to
the soil quality and size of lots needed to accommodate wells and septic systems.

Mr. Breeding stated that the County's Rural Villages might be appropriate for
consideration as Receiving Areas due to the higher densities allowed in those zoning
districts, which might make wastewater facilities more feasible. The members discussed
the need to review the Rural Villages to determine which might be appropriate for
designation as Receiving Areas. Planning staff suggested that the review could be part of

Comprehensive Plan Update

The Board will review expanding the TDR Program Receiving Area boundaries over the
next year. The Maryland Department of Planning has added new requirements for
Comprehensive Plans, including a new Housing Element and planning for the effects of
climate change. The Housing Element will address affordable housing for low- and middle-
income homebuyers and fair housing practices. The County's Comprehensive Rezoning
will begin when the Comprehensive Plan update is completed.
