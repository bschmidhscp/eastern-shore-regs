# Caroline County: By-Right Playbook

Keys: [Fees] = `Documents/Fees/55_FY2027-Fee-Schedule` (FY2027); [App] = `Documents/Planning Applications and Forms/35_Application-for-Plats-and-Site-Plans_72024`; [SC] = `.../36_Surveyor-Checklist`. State rules (septic tiers, perc, forest, Critical Area, BAT) are in `01. Maryland/00. Summary.md`; not repeated here.

## 1. Bottom line

The path is the **Minor Subdivision**: up to **4 lots** total, approved by the **Zoning Administrator** with the Health Officer's signature, **no preliminary plat, no public hearing** [§ 162-13.A, C, D; § 175-38.A]. **No public notice of any kind** is required: the minor-subdivision section has no adjoining-owner letters, sign posting, publication or Planning Commission agenda; the PC only hears an appeal of a denial [§ 162-13.I]. In the R (Rural) district the 4 includes any residue under 20 acres and every lot cut from the original tract since 11-30-1972, so plan **3 new lots plus a small residue** [§ 175-38.A(2), C; § 162-5]. Timing is **not stated in code or minuted**; our estimate is about **6 months (range 4 to 9)** from first call to recorded plat, driven by the perc season. County fees are about **$1,500 to $1,900** plus a $5,000 per home school impact fee that is paid later by the builder at permit [Fees p.3; § 167-14.A].

## 2. Eligibility test

Parcel must pass all of these:

- [ ] **Zoning:** R Rural is the main target. Minor lots in R: 1 ac minimum (43,560 sf), 150 ft width, yards 40 front / 10 side / 10 rear [Summary Attach. 4, from CA1090-175d General Design Regulations, read directly]. Other districts (R-1, R-2, VN, VC) have smaller lots but then check the 4-lot minor definition and any town/Tier limits [Summary §2]. I-2 bars new residential [§ 175-12.E].
- [ ] **Lot count:** not more than 4 lots per "original lot, tract or parcel" [§ 175-38.A]. Count every lot, plat or division recorded after **11-30-1972** from the tract as it existed on that date, including earlier minor lots [§ 175-38.A(2); § 162-5.A]. Pull the deed chain and prior plats; the plat must carry a Lot History Statement [SC item 29].
- [ ] **Residue:** a remainder under 20 acres counts as a lot and must be treated as a new lot (field-surveyed, health approved) [§ 162-5.B; § 175-38.C; SC items 4, 28]. A residue of 20 acres or more is not counted but must keep at least one development right [§ 175-38.C].
- [ ] **Density:** overall gross density not more than 1 lot per acre [§ 175-38.A(1)]. Plat must assign remaining minor rights to a lot or the residue [§ 175-38.A(3)]. Lot-line additions cannot add rights [§ 175-38.A(4)].
- [ ] **Groundwater:** lot count and density can be cut by the Groundwater Protection Report in the CWSP [§ 175-38.D]. Ask staff for the density criteria for the area.
- [ ] **Septic tier:** Caroline kept its local 4-lot minor definition, no Tier IV exemption. Over 90% of the county is Tier IV, where only a minor subdivision on septic is allowed; a 5th lot is not available by right [MD Summary §2; Draft Plan pdf p.40, p.123]. Tier map is in the draft 2026 plan and may change.
- [ ] **Health approval:** every lot approved for individual (or community) water and sewer [§ 162-13.D; § 162-18.A]. Interim-system note on plat [§ 162-18.B].
- [ ] **Critical Area:** within 1,000 ft of tidal water the local program governs (**not found in corpus** [§ 175-15]); state RCA limit is 1 unit per 20 ac with no variance [MD Summary §1]. Avoid RCA; LDA/IDA need staff review. Plat fee is $450/lot [Fees p.3].
- [ ] **No new road:** every lot fronts an existing County or State road, or a private road with a recorded 30-ft right-of-way to a County/State road [§ 162-15.A]. New roads trigger sediment and stormwater plans [SC items 39-41] and are outside the minor path in practice.
- [ ] **Access:** flag/panhandle lots are **allowed in minors** (the ban in § 162-35.A applies to majors, rural majors and PDs) [§ 162-35.A; § 162-12 definition]. Lot depth-to-width 3:1 rule also does not apply to minors [§ 162-35.A].
- [ ] **Taxes:** all property taxes must be paid before subdividing [App p.1].
- [ ] **Wetlands, streams, ditches:** delineate; perennial streams 100 ft buffer, intermittent 25 ft; public ditches identified [SC items 30, 36, 37].
- [ ] **APFO:** roads, drainage, schools, fire, solid waste adequacy can support denial [§ 162-38 to 43]; confirm applicability to minors with staff.

## 3. Step-by-step path

1. **Pre-application call** (Planning and Codes, 410-479-8100). Who: planning staff. Confirm subdivision potential, lot history since 1972, zoning, Critical Area status, groundwater density. Fee: none. Duration: 1 to 2 weeks (estimate).
2. **Title and lot-history search** (our attorney). Establish the original tract as of 11-30-1972 and prior divisions [§ 175-38.A(2)]. Estimate: 1 to 3 weeks, can run alongside step 1.
3. **Boundary survey and concept layout** (surveyor/engineer). Locate lots on existing frontage, residue treated as a lot if under 20 ac, flag lots permitted. Field-locate all new lines; no deed points [SC item 28]. Estimate: 3 to 6 weeks.
4. **Perc and soils: file the Health Department "Plat Evaluation Application"** with Environmental Health (410-479-8000). Their receipt is required to file the plat [App item 12]. Wet-season testing window is roughly **Feb through Apr** (state rule; Health Dept says submit before **Dec 1** for the winter season; applications take up to 30 days) [MD Summary §1; Fee Findings]. Each lot needs 10,000 sf for an initial plus 2 replacement systems [COMAR 26.04.02.04F]. Fee: **not found**, ask Health Dept [§ 154-4]. **This step sets the calendar:** miss the window and wait a year.
5. **Forest conservation** (county Planning, forest conservation staff). Applies at 40,000 sf or more of land with a subdivision application [§ 109-3.A]. Submit a Declaration of Intent or Certificate of Exemption if one applies, or Forest Stand Delineation ($100 to $300) and Forest Conservation Plan ($300) [App p.2; Fees p.4]. Excluded: Critical Area land (handled by the CA program) [§ 109-3.B(2)]. Note single-lot and dwelling-for-owner exemptions are narrow and re-triggered on transfer [§ 109-3.B(8), (11)].
6. **Stormwater and E&S** (Planning and Codes with Soil Conservation District). Concept stormwater is submitted at initial submission of a minor [§ 158-5.A(1)]. Developments disturbing 5,000 sf or less are exempt [§ 158-3.B(3)]; a plat alone disturbs little. Fee $50 standard residential plan [Fees]. Full ESD and sediment plans normally come with the builder's building permit, not the plat (verify with staff, see §7).
7. **Submit the plat package** to the Zoning Administrator: signed application (owner signature), plat fee, 3 paper copies plus electronic (App) or 5 (SC) or 7 (§ 162-13.B); the three sources differ, ask which is current. Also: recent deeds, access/maintenance easement agreements, Critical Area form if applicable, surveyor checklist, Health receipt, forest conservation documents [App]. Plat size 18x24 (8.5x14 if one lot), scale 1"=100' or larger, Mylar for at least two prints [§ 162-14]. Required plat notes: Lot History, Agricultural Compatibility, Critical Area, Floodplain, dirt road, right-of-way agreement for county-road frontage [SC items 24, 29, 31-34].
8. **Staff review and revision.** Zoning Administrator may approve, condition or return for revision [§ 162-13.C]. Original plat fee covers the first submittal plus one resubmittal; a third submittal costs one third of the plat fee [Fees p.3]. Right-to-farm covenant recorded in R [§ 162-37.B]. Dedicate 25 ft from road centerline [§ 162-15.B]. Estimate: 3 to 8 weeks.
9. **Health Officer signs** the plat certifying all lots approved for water/sewer [§ 162-13.D]. The Zoning Administrator signs after that [§ 162-13.D]. Duration depends on perc results and Health review (estimate 2 to 6 weeks after tests).
10. **Owner's notarized certificate and dedication** signed [§ 162-14.G; SC item 14].
11. **Recording:** the Zoning Administrator records the plat with the Clerk of the Circuit Court; recording fee comes out of the plat filing fee [§ 162-13.E]. Other recordings (covenants, easements) are billed separately [App p.1]. One signed copy returned to us [§ 162-13.F]. Estimate: 1 to 3 weeks.
12. **Sell lots.** Impact fee ($5,000 per home) and building permits belong to the builder [§ 167-7.A]. We never pull permits.

**Timing estimate (not cited):** pre-app to recorded plat 4 to 9 months, typical 6, if tests are done in the Feb-Apr window. No minor-subdivision timing appears in the PC digest or minutes (administrative approvals are not minuted) [Summary §3].

## 4. Public notice

None found for the minor path. The minor-subdivision procedure (§ 162-13) has no notice, posting or hearing requirement, and the corpus search for notice, adjoining owners and posting returns notice rules only for other items (site plans for cottage industry, minerals, road abandonment) [§ 175-33.C; § 175-27.4; § 152-26]. The plat only lists adjoining owners as data [SC item 16]. The Planning Commission is involved only if the Zoning Administrator denies and we appeal [§ 162-13.I]. By contrast, 5+ lot majors go to the Planning Commission [§ 162-21.A; § 162-22]. Confirm by phone that no internal agency notice (e.g., Roads Board) is expected (see §7).

## 6. Off-ramps

| Trigger | Result | Design around it |
|---|---|---|
| 5th lot, or residue under 20 ac pushing count past 4 | Becomes rural major: Planning Commission, receiving area only, 1 per 15 ac or TDR [§ 175-38.B] | Keep to 3 new lots plus residue; or leave a 20+ ac residue |
| Prior divisions since 11-30-1972 use up rights | Fewer than 4 lots available [§ 175-38.A(2)] | Lot-history search first |
| Lot-line additions used to create rights | Disallowed [§ 175-38.A(4)] | Do not count on them |
| New road or no frontage | Outside § 162-15; likely major | Use existing frontage or a 30-ft recorded ROW |
| Perc failure or fewer systems than lots | Lot cannot be approved [§ 162-13.D] | Test early in window; size lots with 10,000 sf area plus reserve |
| Tier IV 5+ lots on septic | Barred [MD Summary §2] | Stay at 4 |
| Critical Area RCA | 1 per 20 ac, no variance [MD Summary §1] | Avoid RCA land |
| Variance (setbacks, width) | BZA hearing, notice, $300 [Fees] | Design to R district standards |
| Special exception or rezoning | Public hearing, $500 / $250 plus $25 per ac [Fees] | Stay in R, by right |
| W&S plan amendment for community system | $200, Commissioners action [Fees; Comm digest] | Individual wells and septic |
| Denial by Zoning Administrator | Appeal to PC (public) [§ 162-13.I] | Resolve with staff pre-submittal |
| Draft Comprehensive Plan and rezoning | May change density and tiers [Draft Plan pdf p.44, p.123] | Record promptly; watch PC agenda |
| Unpaid taxes, forest conservation or APFO issues | Application not accepted or denied [App; § 162-38 to 43] | Check before filing |

## 7. Open questions to ask staff

1. How many paper copies and what electronic format are needed now (7 in § 162-13.B, 5 in the surveyor checklist, 3 in the application)? Is there a published review schedule or a typical minor turnaround?
2. Does the Zoning Administrator count a residue of 20 acres or more as a lot? Does the "4 lots" limit mean 4 new lots plus a large residue, or 4 total including the original lot? Get a written example (the visual example on file under § 162-5).
3. Is any notice, posting, or agency circulation (Roads Board, County Engineer right-of-way agreement, SCD) triggered for minors in practice?
4. Is a stormwater plan or sediment control plan required for the plat itself, or only at building permit? What does the County Engineer require for the right-of-way deed and the road-frontage agreement?
5. What are the Health Department's plat evaluation fee, perc fee, exact wet-season testing window, and current backlog? Are lots over a given size required to have BAT outside the Critical Area?
6. What is the current indexed forest fee-in-lieu rate, has the county updated Ch. 109 to the state 1:1 rule, and is the 2026 draft plan or a rezoning going to change R density or tiers soon?
