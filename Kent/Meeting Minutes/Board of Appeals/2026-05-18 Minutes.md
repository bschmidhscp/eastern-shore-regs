# **APPLICATIONS FOR REVIEW** 

_26-09: Kevin and Jennie Leghart – Variance – Front Yard Setback 21251 Kansas Avenue, Chestertown – Sixth Election District – Zoned CR_ 

**Public Notice Information** : Mr. Johnson read into the record the public notice that was published in the Kent County News on May 7, 2026. Mr. Johnson also noted letters were sent to adjacent property owners on April 17, 2026, and the property was posted on April 21, 2026. No correspondence was received. 

**Applicant Testimony:** Ms. Leghart explained that they had purchased the adjacent lot at 21251 Kansas Avenue, which contained a dilapidated, overgrown structure that had to be demolished. After clearing and demolishing the structure, they decided to build a new single-family home on the lot, which they intend to use as their primary residence. 

Ms. Leghart stated that they selected a single-level cottage-style home to match the character of the neighborhood, which was historically developed as a summer resort area known as Tolchester Estates, featuring many small cottage-style homes. The neighborhood includes a mix of developed and undeveloped lots, with wooded land across the street and no nearby neighbors who would be adversely affected. Ms. Leghart indicated she had spoken with both adjoining property owners, who expressed no objection.

The need for a front yard setback variance arose from the placement of a new well. The original well on the lot had to be capped as part of the demolition permit and was deemed non-viable by the Kent County Health Department due to insufficient records. A new well must be drilled and must be located at least 30 feet from the proposed house and from property lines. After consulting a professional well-driller and the Health Department, it was determined that the required well placement forced the house toward the front property line. The well location has already been approved by the County. Ms. Leghart noted that the old house had a setback of approximately 9 feet from the street and that his existing home next door had approximately a 10-foot setback, making the proposed placement consistent with both the prior structure and the neighboring home. 

In response to a question from Board members, Ms. Leghart confirmed that the proposed front porch is an open porch and that construction has not yet begun. She also confirmed that county sewer is available and the connection infrastructure remains in place from the prior dwelling; a new well is required, as there is no county water service. 

**Staff Report** : Holly Baldwin, Associate Planner for Kent County, testified regarding the Legharts 24-foot variance to the front yard setback for the construction of a single-family home at 12151 Kansas Ave. The standard front yard setback in the CR district is 35 feet; the applicants are requesting an 11-foot front yard setback. Ms. Baldwin confirmed that the practical difficulty stems from the required placement of a new well in the rear yard, which must maintain a 30-foot setback from the house and property lines. She noted that the proposed setback would be consistent with the Legharts existing home on the adjacent lot, providing congruence along Kansas Avenue. The proposed home is similar in style and scale to the applicant’s home next door and to other cottage-style homes in Tolchester Estates. The Comprehensive Plan encourages residential development within the Community Residential district and supports neighborhood stylistic and bulk consistency. The Planning Commission forwarded a favorable recommendation to the Board of Appeals, and Staff also recommended approval. In response to a question from the Board, Ms. Baldwin confirmed that the setback is measured from the front porch and that this is consistent with the Planning Department’s requirements. 

**Board Discussion** : Chair Massey reviewed the required findings of fact for the variance, noting the following conclusions based on the applicant’s testimony: 

1. The variance will not cause a substantial detriment to adjacent or neighboring properties, as the applicants propose to build a house similar in style to others in the neighborhood. 

2. The variance will not change the character of the neighborhood or district, as the proposed home is a residential structure in a residential neighborhood. 

3. The variance is consistent with the Comprehensive Plan and the general intent of the ordinance, as it supports residential development on a residential parcel. 

4. The practical difficulty is caused by the shape and size of the property and the required placement of the new well; the applicants did not create this difficulty. 

5. The proposal represents a reasonable use of the parcel for a residential purpose. 

**Motion** : Mr. Hill moved to approve the variance to allow an 11-foot setback based on the findings of the fact presented to the Board of Appeals by Kevin and Jennie Leghart, the Planning Commission, and the summary of staff that was presented to the Chair. Mr. Legow seconded, and the motion passed unanimously.
