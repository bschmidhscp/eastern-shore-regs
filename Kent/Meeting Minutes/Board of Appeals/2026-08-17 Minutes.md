_26-04: Towerco/Stephen and Mary Etta Stoltzfus — Special Exception — Personal Wireless Facility Tower Morgnec Road, Kennedyville (Map 30, Parcel 5)_ 

**Background:** The Chair noted that this hearing was a continuation of the July 20, 2026, hearing, at which a member moved to table Application 26-04 until the Board's August 17, 2026, meeting for the purpose of allowing the applicant to provide additional information regarding (a) alternative sites considered and exhausted on the subject property, and (b) further detail supporting the demonstrated public need for the facility; the motion was seconded and passed. 

Adopted on September 21, 2026

**Applicant Testimony:** Sean Hughes, Esq., counsel for Towerco, stated that the applicant had two witnesses to address the Board's two directives and had submitted supplemental materials, including a report dated August 14, 2026. Board members noted that some materials had been received only hours before the hearing; Mr. Hughes acknowledged the timing but stated the applicant's witnesses would explain the materials and answer questions. He reviewed the public-need and alternative-site-exhaustion standards under Article 3, Section 2.118 of the county code, stated that the applicant's burden of proof for a special exception is by a preponderance of the evidence, and cited _Schultz v. Pritz_ as governing case law on special exceptions, under which a special exception may be denied only where the facts show an adverse effect on adjoining properties different in kind from the effects inherent in the use generally. He reviewed setback distances previously placed in the record, including approximately 3,000 feet to Morgnec Road and to River Road, and approximately 600 feet to the nearest off-site property line not owned by the Stoltzfus family. 

Paul Dugan, radio frequency engineer with Millennium Engineering, testified that since the July 20, 2026, hearing he and a colleague had returned to the area on July 24, 2026, and collected Verizon and T-Mobile signal-strength, throughput, and latency measurements at 20 locations, documented in a letter dated August 14, 2026. He testified that a baseline measurement taken near an existing tower site in the Town of Galena showed strong, reliable service (download speeds of approximately 499 megabits per second), while measurements taken at and around the proposed site and along River Road showed weak or nonexistent signal and data connectivity. He testified that shifting the site north would compromise the specific coverage objectives for River Road (Route 291) and the surrounding area, and that other underserved rural areas to the north remain outside the scope of this particular application but may be addressed by future Verizon facilities. Board members and members of the public questioned Mr. Dugan extensively, including on: why the proposed facility appears to duplicate existing coverage south of the site rather than extend coverage to underserved areas farther north; how many people would be served within the coverage area; the reliability of variable field-test results; and a tower located at 10416 Shadding Reach Road, Millington, raised by a member of the public as a possible closer alternative. Mr. Dugan testified he had not previously evaluated that tower but believed, based on its location along an already well-covered corridor, that it would not meet the coverage objective for this application. He also testified generally regarding emergency-911 service, satellite-connectivity features on newer phones, and the standard five-mile minimum-separation guidance between towers, describing that distance as insufficient for reliable coverage between sites. 

Carrie Fazzolari, representing Towerco, testified regarding the company's site-selection methodology, which considers zone ability (code compliance including setbacks, height, and visual mitigation), constructability (access, land disturbance, wetlands, and stormwater impacts), and landlord willingness. She testified that Towerco evaluated four quadrants of the Stoltzfus property and four quadrants of the adjoining Delmarva Feed property (eight locations in total) and rejected each of the alternatives for reasons including: lack of tree coverage and resulting visibility; disturbance to active farmland; lower ground elevation requiring a taller tower (over 200 feet) and FAA lighting; proximity to wetlands; need for new or extended access roads causing greater land disturbance; and, for the Delmarva Feed sites, interference with the landowner's active feed business and lack of landlord interest. She testified that the proposed site (Quadrant 3 of the Stoltzfus property) best satisfies the applicable criteria, including tree coverage on three sides, an existing access road requiring no new land disturbance, adequate elevation to avoid a height variance or FAA lighting, and full compliance with required setbacks. In response to questions, she testified that the proposed compound is approximately 50 feet by 50 feet (or possibly 75 feet by 75 feet; she was not certain which), surrounded by a chain-link fence, and confirmed that Towerco discussed relocating the site farther north and west along the access road with the landowner but

Larry Stoltzfus, property owner, testified in support of the application, stating that Towerco had been professional to work with, that reliable wireless service is increasingly necessary for farming and daily life, and that he believed the proposed location was less visible and less disruptive than any other location on the property. **Public Testimony:** Several members of the public testified in opposition to the application. Michelle Bonetta, 29134 River Road, Millington, whose property adjoins the Stoltzfus farm, testified comparing radio-frequency test results at the location nearest her property to those at the proposed tower site, questioning how signal levels could vary so significantly over a short distance. She testified that the Planning Commission had recommended, and the Board had twice requested, that Towerco identify specific alternative sites on the roughly 220-acre property, and that in her review of the supplemental submission Towerco had provided only general rationale for approximately 55-acre quadrants rather than identifying a specific alternative location. She raised five points for the Board's consideration: (1) whether alternative sites were adequately considered and exhausted; (2) whether public need under Article 3, Section 2.118 was demonstrated; (3) whether the five-mile minimumseparation standard and its public-need exception were satisfied, referencing the Shadding Reach Road tower as an omitted nearby facility; (4) the county's stated goal of minimizing the number of towers; and (5) visual impact and nuisance to neighboring properties, noting that her family has farmed the adjoining property for 51 years. In response to a Board member's question about whether she would support the tower's placement in one of the alternative quadrants Towerco evaluated, she stated her primary personal interest would be not having the tower visible from her property. 

Janet Christensen Lewis raised a procedural question about the scope of testimony permitted on a continued hearing, which the Chair addressed by confirming that testimony was limited to the two topics identified in the July 20, 2026, tabling motion. Extended legal argument followed addressing: Section 332(c)(7)(A) of the federal Telecommunications Act, which preserves local zoning authority over wireless facility siting; Maryland case law requiring a case-by-case evaluation of the probable benefits versus probable detriments of a special exception at a specific location, rather than treating impacts as fungible across the zoning district; Article 2 of the county's land use ordinance, which implements the Comprehensive Plan's goals of protecting the county's agricultural, historic, scenic, and rural character; and a further review of the supplemental site-alternatives and public-need materials submitted by Towerco on August 14, 2026, arguing that open questions remained regarding the number and location of people to be served and whether alternative locations on the property had been adequately evaluated under the applicable siting criteria. Ms. Lewis posed several questions for the Board's consideration regarding whether the applicant had met its burden on alternative sites, public need, the five-mile separation standard, minimization of the number of towers countywide, and visual impact, and asked that the Board not approve the application unless it could conclude the record supported the required findings. 

**Staff Report:** Carla Gerber, Planning Director, testified that staff was not aware of any other pending or proposed wireless communication facility applications in the county at this time. She testified that she was not in a position to offer a technical opinion on the relative merits of the proposed site versus

alternative locations but confirmed that the application's siting within existing tree cover is consistent with the county's general preference under the zoning code, and that alternative locations without an existing access road would likely require additional land disturbance and infrastructure cost. 

**Closing Arguments:** Counsel for the applicant asked to recall Mr. Dugan briefly on rebuttal to address the Shadding Reach Road tower; Mr. Dugan testified that, based on its location along an already-covered corridor to the south/east, that tower would not meet the coverage objective of this application. Counsel for the applicant then gave a closing argument reiterating that public need had been established through the county Office of Emergency Services letter, radio-frequency testing, and field testing; that the applicant had provided extensive additional information on alternative sites in response to the Board's directive, even though he asserted alternative-site review is not an explicit code requirement; and that the favorable staff report and unanimous Planning Commission recommendation supported approval. He cited case law for the proposition that speculative harm, without probative evidence, is not a sufficient basis to deny a special exception. 

**Board Discussion:** Board members discussed the application at length. Members generally agreed that the application meets most of the applicable zoning criteria and is supported by the staff report and Planning Commission recommendation and noted that the proposed site's setbacks and tree screening are better than typical for such applications. Several members nonetheless expressed continuing concern that the facility appears to extend or duplicate coverage into an area already served to the south rather than addressing more significantly underserved areas farther north in the county, such as along Route 213. Members also discussed the absence of any countywide master plan identifying anticipated future tower locations and observed that Towerco does not currently have other proposed sites in the county; the Board's attorney advised that the existence or absence of such a master plan is not part of the record before the Board on this application. Members concluded that Towerco had provided additional information directed by the Board's July 20, 2026, motion regarding alternative sites and public need sufficient to allow a decision on the application. 

**Motion:** Mr. Hill moved to approve the special exception for the personal wireless facility tower to be located on the Stoltzfus farm property, based on the finding that the testimony given was true and accurate. Mr. Davis seconded the motion, and the motion passed unanimously. 

Following the vote, Chair Massey encouraged the applicant to consider future facilities addressing coverage needs in the northern part of the county, particularly along Route 213. 

_26-32: Carl and Mary Schumaker — Lot Coverage Variance_ 

_5886 Whale Point Lane, Rock Hall_ 

**Applicant Testimony:** Carl Schumaker testified that he was requesting a lot coverage variance in order to install a four-foot-wide concrete apron around his in-ground pool. He had built the pool last year without the apron due to the lot coverage limit. He had explored other options such as a deck, which turned out to be impracticable. He testified that they had already removed a coal bin, a wishing well, and a portion of a gravel parking area (replacing it with grass) in an effort to reduce impervious lot coverage and offered to remove additional gravel/stone surface behind a barn currently used to park a boat. 

Mary Schumaker testified regarding the history of the application, explaining that the property is smaller than neighboring parcels and that the applicants had attempted to purchase additional land from adjoining neighbors to accommodate the apron, but were unable to do so because of Health Department

**Board Discussion:** Board members discussed the amount of lot coverage the applicants would need to remove to eliminate or reduce the need for a variance and discussed the Critical Area Commission's recommendation that any variance granted be the minimum necessary. The matter was continued to the Board's September meeting. 

# **GENERAL DISCUSSION** 

Staff reported that no countywide master plan exists identifying planned or potential wireless communication tower locations. Staff also reported that the Board's next meeting is expected to include a buffer variance application for a replacement dwelling and a special exception application for a cottage industry, the latter having been referred to the Board rather than handled administratively. Staff further reported a change in planning department personnel, noting that a new planner, Paul Keen, started August 3, 2026.
