The Planning Commission met in regular session on Thursday, September 3, 2026, in the County Commissioners' Hearing Room at 400 High Street, Chestertown, Maryland. Members of the public were invited to attend in person or via conference call. 

The following members were in attendance: Vice Chair Paul Ruge, Sean Jones, Ray Strong, Jonathan Quinn, and Bill Crowding.

# **APPLICATIONS FOR REVIEW** 

**24-60: Wharf at Handy's Point — Preliminary and Final Site Plan — Boat Storage Area 23153 Green Point Rd, Worton — Third Election District — Zoned M — PC Decision** 

Ms. Gerber presented the staff report for a 2.8-acre out of water boat storage area on a 39-acre site. The plan includes estimated space for 124 boats with a 7-foot chain link security fence with barbed wire above up to 8 feet. Existing vegetation was deemed sufficient for screening by staff as part of the TAC review. Staff recommended preliminary and final approval, conditioned on MDE permit approval and submission of required sureties for sediment control and stormwater management. No correspondence had been received. 

Buck Nickerson and Chris Meyer, representing the applicant, testified that the plan was substantially unchanged since concept review a year earlier apart from the addition of a centrally located dumpster, and that no lighting was proposed because the site is fenced and secured. They stated the principal delay had been completing a HydroCAD stormwater analysis to confirm that an existing on-site pond could adequately store runoff from the proposed grading. In response to member questions, the applicant confirmed the site has an on-site manager who lives on the property and that security cameras are in use. The applicant confirmed that the MDE stormwater discharge permit application remains under review. No members of the public offered comment. 

Mr. Crowding made the following motion: _"I move that we grant major site plan, preliminary and final, approval for the Wharf at Handy's Point, Incorporated. We find they have met all pertinent data related to Article 4, Section 3, of the Kent County Unified Development Ordinance, subject to approval from the Maryland Department of the_

# **25-47: Kent County Government — Preliminary and Final Site Plan — New Millington WWTP Map 31, Parcel 6, Lot 1 — First Election District — Zoned MXDCA — PC Decision** 

County Attorney Mowell recused himself from this application. 

Ms. Gerber presented the staff report for the proposed regional wastewater treatment plant on a newly created 6.6-acre lot to serve Millington and the surrounding growth area. She noted the parcel lies entirely within the Mixed-Use Development critical area district, is consistent with the comprehensive plan, and that the plant, as a county-owned and operated public utility, is a permitted use by right. She reviewed applicable standards under Article 4, Section 3.6, and noted that the project engineers were aware of landscaping and certified engineer's report standards but had not yet submitted those materials. Staff recommended preliminary approval, noting the Commission could at its discretion grant final approval as well, contingent on the outstanding items. No correspondence had been received. 

Jim Lober of KCI Technologies, representing the applicant, stated the design team was aware of staff's comments and explained that the landscaping plan had intentionally not yet been submitted pending resolution of grading changes related to stormwater comments. 

Members discussed site access, including the temporary single point of access via a private road, with a permanent public road to be constructed as the surrounding property is developed; the applicant explained that access agreements are incorporated into the sale of the 6.6-acre parcel. No members of the public offered comment. 

Mr. Crowding made the following motion: _"I move that we grant preliminary site plan approval to Kent County Government for their Millington wastewater treatment plant on Map 31, Parcel 6, Lot 1. We find that they have substantially answered the questions in Article 4, Section 3.6, of the Kent County Unified Development Ordinance."_ 

Mr. Quinn seconded the motion. The motion passed unanimously.

Mr. Roland explained that the preference for an above-ground crossing at Worton Road stemmed from concerns about existing utilities (sewer and fiber lines) and possible PFAS contamination in the soil near the proposed boring underground location. # **26-43: Joseph and Giulia McPherson — Buffer Variance, 5017 Hillmont Dr, Rock Hall — Fifth Election District Zoned CAR — PC Recommendation to BOA** 

Ms. Gerber presented the staff report on a request for a 24-foot buffer variance to construct a replacement dwelling 26 feet from the water on a just-over-half-acre property, where a 50-foot buffer is required under Article 6, Section 2.8. She described the property's unique wedge shape and noted the proposed dwelling could meet the required 50-foot buffer and 30-foot rear yard setback, but the applicant sought to consolidate the existing structures into a single new dwelling on the current, non-conforming footprint. Reviewing the variance criteria under Article 4, Section 5.6, staff concluded that strict application of the ordinance would not create an unwarranted hardship, since the applicant could build the proposed dwelling without a variance; that granting the variance would confer a special privilege; and that it would be inconsistent with the critical area program. Staff reported that the Critical Area Commission had reviewed the application and did not recommend approval, for the same reasons. No other correspondence had been received. 

Joseph McPherson, the applicant; Marsha Usilton of Lane Engineering; and George Korolakis of CCH Builders testified in support. Mr. McPherson stated the property has water on three sides, including a tidal inlet not addressed in the Critical Area Commission's letter, and that the 50-foot buffer requirement was updated after he began the planning process for the proposed dwelling. He stated the proposed dwelling would consolidate the existing dilapidated house and several accessory structures into a single elevated structure on the existing, already-disturbed footprint, avoiding disturbance of the undeveloped rear portion of the lot, and that the new structure would have a smaller combined footprint than the existing structures. He stated the hardship was primarily the cost of redesign necessitated by the buffer requirement change since purchase, along with a preference to avoid disturbing previously undisturbed land, and acknowledged the property was purchased in part for its water view. 

Members questioned whether the increased cost of an alternative design constitutes an unwarranted hardship, noted that a substantial majority of neighboring houses are set closer to the water than the proposed dwelling, and discussed whether the applicant could have built under prior setback standards. Staff clarified the legal standard for unwarranted hardship — that without the variance, the applicant would be denied reasonable and significant use of the parcel — and noted this is a finding for the Board of Appeals to make, subject to potential appeal by the Critical Area Commission. A neighboring property owner testified regarding an existing drainage swale shared between properties and asked whether relocating the structure would affect it; the applicant confirmed a complete stormwater management plan had been prepared for the proposed location but not for an alternative, setback-compliant location. 

Mr. Jones made the following motion: _"I move that we send an unfavorable recommendation to the Board of Appeals for the variance, based on the fact that it has not been proven to constitute an undue or unreasonable hardship, and that the dwelling could be required to be constructed within the 50-foot buffer standard."_ 

Mr. Crowding seconded the motion. On a roll call vote, the motion passed unanimously.

# **26-44: Thomas Parks — Special Exception — Cottage Industry — Automobile and Marine Repair** 

# **12901 Irelands Corner Rd, Galena — First Election District — Zoned CR — PC Recommendation to BOA** 

Mr. Keen presented the staff report on a request for a special exception to operate an automobile and marine repair business as a cottage industry within an existing approximately 2,300-square-foot accessory pole building on the applicant's 3.5-acre residential property. He noted all business activity would occur entirely within the existing structure, no new structures or landscape removal are proposed, the applicant would operate only during standard daytime hours, and no more than two additional staff would be hired. He reviewed the applicable cottage industry provisions under Article 3, Section 258, and noted staff's view that the proposal aligns with the comprehensive plan's goal of supporting cottage industries and imposes no significant new infrastructure demand. Staff recommended a favorable recommendation to the Board of Appeals. 

Thomas Parks, the applicant, testified that he intends to perform classic car restoration and body work — not mechanical or marine work, despite the marine repair reference in the application. He is a certified mechanic and welder with 31 years' experience at a Chevrolet dealership. He confirmed the work would occur entirely inside an insulated, air-conditioned structure with a vehicle lift, that vehicles would not be stored outside overnight, and that he did not anticipate significant vehicle traffic given the slow, hobbyist nature of the work. Members noted the applicant had previously restored vehicles at the property without complaint from neighbors. No members of the public offered comment. 

Mr. Jones made the following motion: _"I move to send a favorable recommendation to the Board of Appeals for Thomas Parks' request for a special exception to operate an automobile and marine repair business as a cottage industry at 12901 Irelands Corner Road, Galena. The applicant meets the requirements of Article 4, Section 3, of the Unified Development Ordinance, and therefore I recommend a favorable recommendation."_ 

Mr. Crowding seconded the motion. The motion passed.
