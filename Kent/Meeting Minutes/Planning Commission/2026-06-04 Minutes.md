The Planning Commission met in regular session on Thursday, June 4, 2026, in the County Commissioners' Hearing Room at 400 High Street, Chestertown, Maryland. Members of the public were invited to attend in person or via conference call. 

The following members were in attendance: Chair Joseph Hickman, Vice Chair Paul Ruge, James Saunders, Sean Jones, Ray Strong, Jonathan Quinn, and Bill Crowding.

# **APPLICATIONS FOR REVIEW** 

# **26-13: Mixed-Use Development District Master Plan for Millington Growth Area Lands in Vicinity of US Route 301 and MD Route 291** 

Mr. Hickman noted for the record an appearance of conflict with respect to a letter received from the Eastern Shore Land Conservancy stating that he is a board member of that organization. However, he had no part in its communications regarding this matter and believed he could act fairly and honestly. 

Ms. Gerber explained that the property owners within the Mixed-Use Development District have submitted an application for review of their District Master Plan. The submitted plan contains the three elements required by the Unified Development Ordinance: a land use plan, a connectivity plan, and an open space plan. The plan is a conceptual illustration of the entire district and is not a review of specific bulk, subdivision, design, or environmental standards. Ms. Gerber noted that the District Master Plan complies with the requirements of the ordinance, and that any development of any individual phase would need to comply with the District Master Plan as amended. Each phase will be required to follow the applicable subdivision or site plan review process and comply with all applicable zoning, design, and environmental standards. 

Ms. Gerber noted that this is a new process, as the land use ordinance requires the Planning Commission to approve the District Master Plan, which differs from the Commission’s usual role with concept plans. She clarified for Commission members that the three elements to evaluate are: (1) the land use plan, showing generalized land

use designations; (2) the connectivity plan, showing the road and pedestrian/bicycle network; and (3) the open space plan, showing generalized locations of parks, open spaces, and natural resource areas. She emphasized that approval of the District Master Plan would not lock down exact lines, and that any subsequent changes would require the plan to be amended and returned to the Commission. 

Russ Richardson, principal, and Jim Constantine, planner with LRK, were sworn in and presented the District Master Plan. Mr. Richardson provided introductory remarks noting the applicants’ background in New Urbanism and mixed-use planning. He stated the applicants are not unwilling to donate land for an elementary school and suggested a possible land swap with the adjacent Good Hope Family Farm to provide a centrally located, walkable school site. He also addressed potential impact fees, noting that the project’s industrial and commercial uses— including two 257,000-square-foot logistics buildings and significant highway commercial potential along Howard Johnson Road—are expected to generate substantial tax revenue that would help offset the costs associated with residential growth. He indicated a willingness to follow up with the school system regarding enrollment projections and land needs. 

Mr. Constantine presented the District Master Plan using a slide deck. Key elements of the presentation included: The District Master Plan includes properties both within the Mixed-Use Development District (MXD) and the Mixed-Use Development Critical Area District (MXDCA), which carries a higher open space requirement of 40%. The plan is expected to exceed minimum open space requirements of 15% in the MXD and 40% in the MXDCA. 

The central organizing feature is the proposed Millington Crossing Parkway and Linear Park system—an East–West green spine connecting open spaces, parks, and trail systems throughout the district, extending to the town of Millington via stubs off School Street. Land uses include multifamily residential near the existing Food Lion and planned retail hub; townhomes and single-family neighborhoods to the north; highway commercial, hotel, and business park uses along Howard Johnson Road; a 60-acre woodland and park reserve along Mills Branch; and two previously approved logistics buildings on the west side of US 301. The Good Hope Family Farm (142 acres, under contract with LaCrosse Homes) is shown as an extension of the plan, with residential uses and a Parkway connection into the town of Millington. 

The connectivity plan shows major roadways including the proposed Richardson Drive, extensions of existing stub roads, and trail connections. Local street networks and block structure will be determined at the site plan stage. The phasing plan identifies Phase 1 as the logistics buildings on the west side of US 301 (already approved) and the Kent Mills property on Howard Johnson Road; Phase 2 as residential development on the east side of US 301 (not the Good Hope Farm); and Phase 3 as the retail area between the wastewater treatment plant and the existing Food Lion. Mr. Richardson clarified that actual phasing will ultimately be driven by market demand, and that any changes to phasing or the plan itself require return to the Commission. 

Buildout was estimated at approximately 900 to 1,200 housing units on the Richardson properties, plus 400 to 700 units on the Good Hope Farm, subject to wastewater treatment plant capacity. Phase 1 of the treatment plant permits approximately 970 EDUs; Phase 2 would add capacity for approximately 1,400 units. Full buildout was estimated at approximately 20 years. 

# **Planning Commission Discussion** 

Schools and emergency services: Members expressed concern about the need for an elementary school and adequate fire and emergency services for the anticipated population. The applicants acknowledged this concern and committed to meeting with the new School Superintendent to discuss enrollment projections and potential land dedication.

Infrastructure costs: Members asked who would bear the cost of roads, sewer, and water infrastructure. Ms. Gerber and Mr. Richardson noted that the Public Works Department’s current position is that all infrastructure from the connection point to the treatment plant, as well as internal site infrastructure, is the developer’s expense. Some details remain under negotiation. 

Scope of approval: Members asked what specifically the Commission would be approving and what recourse would exist afterward. Ms. Gerber confirmed that approval locks in the generalized framework—land use, connectivity, and open space—and that every subsequent phase and any changes to the plan must come back before the Commission for approval. 

Unincorporated parcel: Members asked about the single parcel in the center of the plan area not included in the application. Ms. Gerber explained that the owner chose not to be rezoned and is therefore not part of the District Master Plan, though the plan allows for future amendment if circumstances change. 

Traffic: Members raised concerns about vehicle traffic and road capacity, given that most residents may commute outside the district. 

Business park and data center uses: Members asked about the nature of proposed business park uses. Mr. Richardson indicated these could include light manufacturing, computer centers, or data centers, and noted he has had preliminary conversations with data center operators. 

Succession: Members inquired about plans for the project’s continuity. Mr. Richardson noted that his daughter, who holds degrees in architecture and construction management, will carry on his interests. 

# **Public Comment** 

Owen Bailey, Eastern Shore Land Conservancy acknowledged that the growth area designation is appropriate to direct growth away from rural lands but urged the Commission to require strong feedback loops as the project develops incrementally over time. He raised questions about the plan’s connectivity to the existing town of Millington, noting no representatives from the town were present. He expressed concern that the layout—with commercial uses separated from residential areas—could undermine walkability and create automobiledependent patterns. He also raised questions about long-term infrastructure maintenance costs and fiscal sustainability and asked whether the development would generate sufficient property tax value to maintain the infrastructure it creates. 

Janet Lewis stated her concern that the plan starts on the highway and leaves the existing downtown of Millington out of the equation, moving commercial and retail development several miles from Main Street. She argued that development should start at Millington and expand outward. She also questioned whether the infrastructure financing structure would ultimately burden ratepayers and taxpayers rather than the developer and noted the risk of a single-owner plan lacking community-wide input. 

Judy Gifford expressed concern about the number of unknowns in the plan, including the viability of proposed industrial and data center uses given current market conditions. She questioned whether approving a plan with so many moving parts was premature and urged caution before placing any stamp of approval on the project. 

Ms. Gerber confirmed that the Commission had received written letters from Shore Rivers and the Eastern Shore Land Conservancy, both of which were included in the packet. Both organizations acknowledged that directing

# **GENERAL DISCUSSION** 

2026 Transportation Priority Letter: Ms. Gerber presented the draft 2026 Transportation Priority Letter for the Commission’s review and feedback before submission to the County Commissioners. She reported that responses had been received from Galena and Chestertown, but that Betterton, Millington, and Rock Hall had not yet responded, and staff would follow up with those municipalities before finalizing the letter. In response to a question about whether any items from the prior year’s letter had been addressed, Ms. Gerber indicated nothing had been completed. She noted Chestertown’s submission had changed somewhat from the prior year. 

Bill Crowding made a motion to forward the letter to the County Commissioners for the Kent County 2026 Transportation Priority Letter. Jonathan Quinn seconded. The motion passed unanimously. 

Utility-Scale Solar Review Process: The Commission discussed the process for reviewing utility-scale solar applications, specifically whether the Planning Commission should conduct formal site plan review before or after an applicant has obtained a Certificate of Public Convenience and Necessity (CPCN) from the Maryland Public Service Commission (PSC). This item was placed on the agenda at the Commission’s request. 

Ms. Gerber provided background on House Bill 1036 (now Chapter 624 of the Annotated Code), which took effect in July 2025. She explained that under this law, if a solar application for a generating station greater than 1 MW meets the state’s mandatory sitting and design requirements, the county must approve it and cannot impose requirements beyond those regulations. She noted that a closed session has been scheduled for June 9 for the County Commissioners to receive legal advice on the interpretation of the obligation to “expedite” the site plan approval process. 

Janet Lewis addressed the Commission and distributed written materials. She urged the Commission and county leadership to defer formal Planning Commission review—including site plan review and public hearings—until after the PSC has issued a CPCN. She argued that planning staff should remain available for advisory consultation during the CPCN process, and cited the example of Queen Anne’s County, where the planning director communicated the county’s concerns directly to the PSC without requiring formal Planning Commission proceedings. She provided documentation of several PSC cases in which solar applications were withdrawn or materially changed after filing to illustrate that solar applications are frequently not viable or are substantially redesigned before construction. She also cited a utility law judge’s finding in the Bloomfield Solar case (Case No. 9799) regarding inadequate public information at PSC hearings. Ms. Lewis recommended that the county adopt a framework deferring Planning Commission review until after PSC approval, thereby conserving public and staff resources for projects that are viable and clearly defined. 

Following further discussion, members indicated a preference for deferring Planning Commission review until after the CPCN is issued but recognized that the legal interpretation of the “expedite” requirement is unsettled and that the Commissioners’ closed session on June 9 would be relevant. No formal motion was made. The Commission agreed to monitor the outcome of the Commissioners’ closed session and await further guidance.
