The Planning Commission met in regular session on Thursday, August 6, 2026, in the County Commissioners' Hearing Room at 400 High Street, Chestertown, Maryland. Members of the public were invited to attend in person or via conference call. 

The following members were in attendance: Chair Joseph Hickman, Vice Chair Paul Ruge, James Saunders, Ray Strong, and Jonathan Quinn.

# **APPLICATIONS FOR REVIEW** 

**25-11: Jones Farm Station/Lester C. Jones & Sons, Inc. — Major Site Plan Review (Preliminary and Final) — Structure for the Processing and Sale of Animal Products, 32545 Galena Sassafras Road, Galena — First Election District — Zoned AZD — PC Decision** 

Ms. Gerber presented the staff report for preliminary and final site plan review of a structure for the processing and sale of animal products, agritourism, and agricultural education at an existing facility on a 134-acre farm zoned AZD at 32545 Galena Sassafras Road. She noted the application had previously received a special exception and variance for the proposed use and setbacks. She noted one letter had been received from a neighboring landowner, Mary Tazewell of the Hurtt Farm, expressing no objection. 

Matthew Jones, representative, testified that he had nothing to add to staff's presentation and thanked the Commission and staff for their review. 

In response to member questions, Mr. Jones explained that wastewater from the milk processing operation would be collected on-site, hauled to their dairy farm, and applied to cropland in accordance with a nutrient management plan, at a rate of less than 5,000 gallons per week at full production (approximately one truckload per week), with hauling scheduled during off-hours to avoid conflicts with customer traffic. Members discussed traffic flow and pedestrian circulation on the site given the location of parking behind the buildings and the mix of delivery vehicles and visitor traffic and discussed the project's agricultural-education component. Members expressed general support for the application as a positive addition to the county's agricultural sector.

# **26-32: Carl and Mary Schumaker — Lot Coverage Variance, 5886 Whale Point Lane, Rock Hall — Fifth Election District — Zoned RCD — PC Recommendation to Board of Appeals** 

Ms. Gerber presented the staff report on a request for a 523-square-foot lot coverage variance to construct a four-foot-wide concrete apron around an in-ground pool constructed the previous year. She explained that, under the grandfathering provisions of Article 6, Section 2.41(C) of the Unified Development Ordinance, the applicants are permitted 8,687 square feet of lot coverage. To stay within that limit after building the pool, the applicants removed some stone parking area and demolished two small sheds. The proposed pool apron would bring total lot coverage to 9,210 square feet or 17.56% of the lot. The applicable variance standards are found in Article 4, Section 5.6 of the Unified Development Ordinance (critical area variance criteria). Ms. Gerber noted the application had been sent to the Critical Area Commission for review; formal comments had not yet been received, though she had been advised informally that the Commission might ask whether additional lot coverage elsewhere on the property could be reduced. Staff recommended a favorable recommendation to the Board of Appeals, conditioned on approval of a buffer enhancement plan, with the variance to lapse after one year absent substantial construction. 

Carl Schumaker, applicant, testified in support, explaining that a concrete apron was needed to eliminate the use of iron stakes driven into mud to secure a winter pool cover, and to allow safe walking access around the pool rather than across mud or existing decking. He described a temporary fence currently in place, to be replaced by a permanent fence attached to the outside of the concrete apron. He discussed prior efforts to remain within the lot coverage limit, including relocating the driveway, demolishing two outbuildings (including an unused coal bin), and exploring a lot line adjustment/property purchase from a neighbor — an option that could not proceed because it would have reduced the neighboring parcel below the acreage needed to maintain an approved or potential septic reserve area and agricultural exemption. He outlined a preliminary concept for the required buffer enhancement plan, including planting near the relocated driveway and along garden areas near an existing boat ramp, with final planting locations to be determined. 

In discussion, members asked about the variance criterion addressing whether the need for the variance resulted from the applicant's own actions. Ms. Gerber explained that the original building permit for the pool was issued based on an under-estimated lot coverage figure; when a subsequent survey showed greater existing lot coverage than originally calculated, the applicant's pool contractor removed the apron from the original permit in an effort to comply, and the applicant later pursued a lot line adjustment (denied for reasons related to Maryland Department of the Environment septic requirements, not zoning) before turning to the variance

# **GENERAL DISCUSSION** 

# **2025 Annual Report Form Letter to MDP** 

Ms. Gerber presented the county's draft 2025 annual report to the Maryland Department of Planning, required under the Land Use Article for the state's collection of annual development data. She reported that 28 permits were issued for new dwellings in 2025, of which 15 were within a Priority Funding Area and 13 were outside; of those 13, only 4 were within a Priority Preservation Area, with the remaining 9 in designated neighborhood development areas — a distribution Ms. Gerber characterized as a positive statistic for the county. She noted the county's Unified Development Ordinance became effective in 2025 following Critical Area Commission approval. Members requested that the report reflect the county's ongoing interest in bicycle and pedestrian planning near a county school. Members and staff also discussed several other broad topics addressed in the report, including sustainable growth and workforce housing; Ms. Gerber noted the Commission works on many of these areas on an ongoing basis even where not separately highlighted this year, and that they would receive more focused attention as part of the county's comprehensive plan update, expected to begin in summer 2027 ahead of its 2028 statutory deadline. 

Mr. Strong moved to accept the 2025 annual report. Mr. Saunders second. There was no discussion, the motion passed unanimously.

Ms. Gerber provided an update on a previously approved solar facility with outstanding landscaping and berm compliance issues: a surveyor was engaged to verify berm heights at the county's request, with a final report pending; planting of remaining landscaping has been suspended until the fall planting season due to summer heat, though dead plantings are being identified and will be replaced; and the facility's irrigation system remains under installation after the first well was found to have insufficient pressure, requiring a second well. 

Members and staff discussed ongoing county economic development efforts, including concerns raised about the county's limited retail and commercial base relative to neighboring jurisdictions. Ms. Gerber reported that the county has received grant funding from the Maryland Department of Housing and Community Development and the Upper Shore Regional Council to conduct a countywide housing study, for which a consultant has been engaged; Commission members will be involved in the process, which is intended to identify housing conditions, gaps, and market needs to help guide future development. She also noted that the Kent County Economic and Tourism Development Board has issued a request for proposals for an economic development strategy, intended in part to support a designation that could open access to additional federal funding. Members briefly discussed the status of a previously approved warehouse development project in Millington, including staff's understanding that the original applicant may no longer be proceeding, though the associated site plan application has not been formally withdrawn; it was noted that confirmation of the application's status could be relevant to a related pending appeal. 

# **ADJOURN** 

Mr. Saunders moved to adjourn. The motion passed unanimously, and the meeting adjourned at approximately 2:30 p.m.
