# **<u>CODE HOME RULE BILL NO. 9-2025 CANNABIS PROCESSOR IN THE COMMERCIAL DISTRICT (C)</u>** 

On a motion by Commissioner Nickerson, seconded by Commissioner Price, the Commissioners unanimously agreed to open the public hearing to discuss Code Home Rule Bill 9- 2025. The public hearing commenced at 10:06 a.m. to discuss Code Home Rule Bill No. 9-2025, Cannabis Processor in the Commercial District (C), AN ACT to amend Chapter 222, Zoning, of the Code of Public Local Laws of Kent County, Maryland, also known as the Kent County Unified Development Ordinance, in order to permit a land use in the C District by amending Article III, Uses, Section 2, Land Uses and Accessory Uses and Structures, #95 by adding “with the exception of cannabis processor as defined by the State of Maryland located on properties served by public water and sewer.”

# **<u>BONNIE PEARSALL, INSPECTOR, KENT ALCOHOL AND TOBACCO ENFORCEMENT (KATE)</u>** 

Inspector Pearsall conducted inspections for the FY2026 Second Quarter. Her report is as follows: 

<u>Alcohol Establishments: Inspector Pearsall conducted routine inspections of sixty-seven (67)</u> out of sixty-eight (68) alcohol establishments. Inspector Pearsall was unable to inspect one establishment because it was operating on seasonal hours (it was closed). Waterman’s Crab House was issued a verbal warning for an underage person serving alcohol behind the bar. Inspector Pearsall received information regarding an allegation that Betty’s Bar was overserving patrons. Inspector Pearsall issued a thirty-day notice to Betty’s Bar for overserving patrons. Regarding LTO, also referred to as The Retriever, there were also allegations received concerning serving alcohol to underage persons (college students) with fake identification. Inspector Pearsall issued a notice for serving underage persons. 

<u>Tobacco Establishments: Compliance checks were conducted at all thirty tobacco</u> establishments in November and December 2025. On November 9, 2025, one establishment, 7- Eleven, sold a tobacco product to an Authorized Underage Person (AUP). As a result, the clerk at this establishment received a fine of $300.00. 

On December 11, 2025, a follow-up check was performed at the 7-Eleven using another AUP, and once again, the establishment sold a tobacco product. The clerk received an additional fine of $300.00.  Inspector Pearsall stated the establishment will be referred to the Alcohol, Tobacco, and Cannabis Commission (ATCC) for further review. 

The KATE team continues to seek responsible 18- to 20½-year-olds for compliance checks with tobacco retailers, as well as responsible 21- to 27-year-olds to serve as “Mystery Shoppers” who will use caution or thank-you cards to check alcohol establishments for compliance, ensuring they ask for proper identification from their patrons. 

At 11:43 a.m., a motion was made by Commissioner Nickerson, seconded by Commissioner Price; the Commissioners unanimously agreed to adjourn the meeting. The Board is scheduled to meet again on Tuesday, January 27, 2026, at 10:00 a.m.
