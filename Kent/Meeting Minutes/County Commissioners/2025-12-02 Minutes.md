# **<u>APPOINTMENT</u>** 

Dr. Dwayne Joseph, University of Maryland Extension Educator, provided an update on the Agriculture and Food Systems Program. The program serves Kent County through partnerships with Washington College, the Kent County Farm Bureau, the Kent County Economic Development Corporation, the Kent Soil and Water Conservation District, and through Community and Stakeholder Engagement with Corteva – Chesapeake Farms, National Corn Growers Association, Chestertown Christian Academy, Kent County High School, and Kent County Noxious Weed Control Advisory Board. The program had a total of 2,688 direct contacts statewide, comprising 38 individual consultations. There were three on-farm research projects in Kent County, covering a total of 8,122 acres, focused on nutrient management. The economic and environmental impact of nutrient management plans in Kent County was $146,196.00, and reduced runoff tonnage. Programs and teaching initiatives included the Kent County Agronomy Breakfast, Transition to Organic Production Seminar, Private Pesticide Applicator Training, and Kent County Public Works Pesticide Training. The Agriculture and Food Systems future programs include strengthening weed management education, expanding on-farm research, improving outreach and farmer engagement, enhancing nutrient management support, and supporting County agencies and partners.

# **<u>PARKS AND RECREATION</u>**

The Community Center has secured grant funds for Turner’s Creek Solar well and a new Mobi-Mat for Betterton Beach. <u>Funding Announcement: Ms. McNamara shared that Kent County was awarded</u> Congressionally Directed Spending (Federal Earmark Appropriations) in the amount of $6,200,000 to support the construction of a new, relocated Wastewater Treatment Plant serving the town of Millington. # **<u>OFFICE OF FINANCE</u>** 

Pat Merritt, Chief Finance Officer of the Office of Finance, reviewed the proposed FY2025 Amended Budget. <mark>Budget adjustments are made throughout the year. Additionally, approvals are obtained throughout the fiscal year for adjustments to the use of fund balance and contingency. Adjustments moving funds within departmental budgets are approved by the department heads. The proposed FY2025 Amended Budget incorporates all changes in the final amended budget document, which was made available for review during the meeting. The FY2025 Adopted Budget was $67,582,418.00, and the proposed FY2025 Amended Budget was $66,055,687.00. Discussion took place. On a motion made by Commissioner Price and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved the FY2025 Amended Budget by Resolution 2025-20.</mark> 

<mark>Ms. Merritt provided information regarding the FY2025 Financial Statements that included General Fund Statement of Revenues, Expenditures, Explanation of Major Variances, Financial Ratios, Water and Wastewater Services, and Capital Projects Fund. Favorable variances occurred in revenues and expenditures. The FY2025 Financial Statements were submitted for review.</mark> 

<mark>Ms. Merritt provided an update on the estimated potential cost savings for the Kent County Detention Center (KCDC) facility under scenarios in which the County contracts with another detention center facility to house all of Kent County’s incarcerated individuals. There are still some significant unknowns, such as the daily per diem rate that Kent County will pay to the facility housing the County’s inmates. Once further information becomes available, cost estimates can be updated accordingly. Commissioner Price asked Dennis Hickman, Sheriff, and Roland Sheppard, Director of Operations, KCDC, to join the discussion. A lengthy discussion ensued. Each Commissioner raised questions, stated their viewpoint, and position on the matter. On a motion made by Commissioner Price and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved putting the Detention Center’s operations back in service and bringing all piloted incarcerated individuals back to Kent County. Staff will schedule a closed session meeting to discuss personnel and revoke the resolution that assigned Dennis Hickman, Sheriff, to oversee the Detention Center.</mark>

<mark>potential maximum amount of funding for each County, with Kent County eligible to receive up to $1,425,289.00 with a 10% match ($142,528.00) for a total of $1,583,654.00 in total project costs that can be supported. # **<u>COUNTY ATTORNEY</u>** 

<mark>The Wicomico Hunt Club Fox Hunting Kennel offered to surrender its license, effective March 31, 2026, voluntarily. The hounds have been relocated to their winter enclosure, a secure interior building with four walls, which should alleviate the neighbors' concerns until they are removed from the property. Genevieve H.R. Macfarlane, Esquire, Law Offices of Stevens Palmer, LLC, Attorney Representative of WHC, and Ed Fry, Master of Foxhounds, were present to answer any questions. Discussion took place. Mr. Mowell explained that if the Board agrees with the request from WHC. The Board can forgo its right to hold a revocation hearing within the next three months and accept the terms of the surrender. Each Commissioner offered comments concerning the licensing process and wished WHC well in its future endeavors. On a motion made by Commissioner Price and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously accepted the voluntary surrender of the license on or before March 31, 2026.</mark>
