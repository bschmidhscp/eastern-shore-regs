# **<u><mark>ROBERT G. MOWELL, COUNTY ATTORNEY</mark></u>** 

<mark>Mr. Mowell presented a request for an easement agreement for a small cemetery on an 8.11acre residential property surrounded by a larger 220-acre farm owned by Henry Dierker. Linda Dierker passed away on October 20, 2025, and her dying wish was to be buried in her rosary garden behind the house. The rosary garden is completely screened from Maryland Line Road. Mr. Dierker would like to create two 3.5’ x 10’ burial plots within the rosary garden for himself and his wife, Linda. The burial plots will be 819’ from the road and 62’ from the nearest property line. As a result of the special exception, the applicant has prepared a deed containing an easement for access and creating a restrictive covenant for perpetual maintenance. The proposed deed includes the County as a party, granting it the right to enter to ensure compliance with maintenance, and, among other things, authorizing the County to enforce the covenant and recover attorney's fees. Without an agreement, the County could repair or maintain the burial site only with the owner's permission (Md. Code Ann., Real Prop. § 14-122(b)). However, the enforcement of a perpetual maintenance agreement is typically handled by a cemetery preservation board, which could create additional obligations for the County that it has not traditionally undertaken. The question before the Commissioners was whether the County should be a party to the deed/agreement. Mr. Mowell</mark>

# **<u>DAN MATTSON, DIRECTOR OF PUBLIC WORKS</u>** 

<mark>Mr. Mattson provided an update on the weather event that just occurred, and the measures taken to work through it.</mark> 

<u><mark>Environmental Operations Division - Mr. Mattson requested funding for a facility project.</mark></u> <mark>The groundwater treatment facility at the Nicholson Landfill utilizes two granular carbon filters to remove volatile organic chemicals from migrating offsite. The filters operate in an alternating series. Staff obtained a proposal from Evoqua Water Technologies for $16,085.27 to replace the filter vessel and carbon, and to dispose of the used carbon, including that from the filter removed from last year. Evoqua Water Technologies is recommended to perform the work, as they have previously completed carbon and vessel replacements and have demonstrated in the past to be the lowest-cost, closest, and most responsive vendor for this work. The two vessels will be hauled by staff to a recycler for salvage funds. The department recommends approval of the proposal so the replacement work can be done in a timely manner. On a motion made by Commissioner Price and seconded by Commissioner Fithian, the Commissioners, by a majority vote, approved allocating $10,000.00 in contingency funds for the project and approving the reallocation of departmental funds as presented.</mark> 

<mark>Mr. Mattson requested approval to apply for grant funding from the Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE). The MDE is soliciting applications for capital funding for various types of financial assistance for water quality infrastructure projects, including wastewater. The department seeks to submit two applications: one for funding to support the construction of the new Millington Wastewater Treatment Plant, and a second application for funding to support demolition of the existing Wastewater Treatment Plant and conveyance costs to transport wastewater to the new plant. For each of these funding applications, they are submitted to be considered for the best possible MDE funding package. While funding applications for federal appropriations and the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD) grants have been successful, additional funding is still needed to support the total costs of the Millington Wastewater Treatment Plant. Department of Natural Resources (DNR) funding is expected to support costs related to improved Enhanced Nutrient Removal (ENR) standards, allowing the new site to improve the surrounding environment. On a motion made by Commissioner Price and seconded by Commissioner Fithian, the Commissioners, by a majority vote, approved submitting grant applications to MDE for the construction of the new Millington Wastewater Treatment Plant and the demolition of the existing plant.</mark>

<mark>Mr. Mattson gave a general status update on the Rock Hall Dredging project. This project is a joint effort between Kent County and the Army Corps of Engineers (ACoE).
