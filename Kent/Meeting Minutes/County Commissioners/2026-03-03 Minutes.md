# **<u>DAN MATTSON, DIRECTOR, PUBLIC WORKS</u>** 

<u><mark>Water and Wastewater Division: Ms. McNamara joined Mr. Mattson to present the FY2027</mark></u> <mark>Federal Appropriations Request for the Millington Wastewater Treatment Plant. Ms. McNamara reported that in FY2026, Kent County requested $12.8 million in federal appropriations to support the construction and relocation of the facility and successfully secured $6.2 million through the Agriculture Appropriations Bill, sponsored by Congressman Harris and Senators Chris Van Hollen and Angela Alsobrooks. Congressman Harris’s office advised that the County may pursue an additional $6.2 million in FY2027. With the federal request period now open, staff recommends submitting a second request for this amount. Ms. McNamara noted that the project has an estimated total cost of $17.1 million, and the Department of Public Works is actively assembling a funding package that currently includes $800,0000.00 from the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD), a pending Maryland Department of the Environment (MDE) application, and the $6.2 million federal award received to date, representing 40 percent of total project costs. If the County receives the second federal earmark, secured funds would cover approximately 77 percent of the project. Any remaining costs not met through grants will require County appropriations or loan financing, and staff will continue seeking grant opportunities to minimize local funding obligations. On motion by Commissioner Nickerson, seconded by Commissioner Price, the Commissioners unanimously approved the submittal of the application for a Federal Appropriations request for FY2027 in the amount of $6.2 million to support costs related to the construction and relocation of the Millington Wastewater Treatment Plant.</mark>

# **<u>SHELLEY L. HELLER, COUNTY ADMINISTRATOR</u>** 

<u>Proclamation, Endometriosis Awareness Month, March 2026: Ms.  Heller, on behalf of</u> Shannon Hannawald, Endo Warriors of the Eastern Shore, requested a proclamation for Endometriosis Awareness Month, traditionally recognized in March. Ms. Heller read the proclamation into the record and encouraged citizens to wear yellow to raise awareness. 

Ms. Heller, on behalf of Craig Kuhn, Program Manager at the Maryland Department of Agriculture, discussed a funding request. The Maryland Department of Agriculture (MDA) has identified two areas in Kent County totaling 302 acres where spongy moth populations could lead to defoliation (a map was provided for reference). Mr. Kuhn is seeking confirmation on whether the County would be interested in participating in a spongy moth aerial suppression project scheduled for the spring of 2026. It is important to note that the County's participation will be limited to costsharing for non-state-owned lands, specifically the identified 302 acres. Under this cooperative suppression program, funding has traditionally been shared among the US Forest Service (50%), the State of Maryland (25%), and the County (25%). Based on previous estimates, MDA anticipates the County's contribution to be approximately $15.00 per acre, totaling $4,530.00. Discussion took place. <mark>On motion by Commissioner Price, seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously ap</mark> proved the allocation of $4,530.00 in contingency funds to the spraying of the gypsy moth.
