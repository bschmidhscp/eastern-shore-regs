<u><mark>Dr. Chad Rooney, Town Manager, Town of Chestertown-Rail Trail Mowing Request:</mark></u> <mark>Dr. Chad Rooney, Town Manager, Town of Chestertown, requested assistance from the County (Public Works) with mowing of the Chestertown Rail Trail, between Mill Street and Morgnec Road. Maintaining this section of the trail is important, but the Town lacks the appropriate equipment to service the area. The Rail Trail is a valued community asset, and County assistance would help ensure it remains safe, accessible, and enjoyable for residents and visitors alike. If County roads could include this segment in the current seasonal mowing schedule, the Town would be very grateful. Carrie Klein, Deputy Director, Public Works, joined Dr. Rooney in the discussion to provide further information. Ms. Klein stated that the County Roads staff has reviewed the scope of work and estimated that this task will require one operator in a Tractor with an A-Boom mower. The Town requested the County donate services to maintain the trail. Ms. Klein stated that the County roads can accommodate the request within their current workload. Ms. Klein provided the Commissioners with the following options to consider: providing maintenance of the Rail Trail as a donated service to the Town, providing maintenance at a cost, or not maintaining the Rail Trail at all. Discussion took place. On motion by Commissioner Price, and seconded by Commissioner Fithian, the Commissioners unanimously approved County Roads' maintenance to assist the Town of Chestertown Rail Trail with the boom mower to trim the trail debris back off the trail, and it will be a donation from the County to the Town.</mark>

<u>Law Enforcement Officers Pension System Report for Emergency Medical Services:</u> Pete Landon, Director, Office of Emergency Services, joined Mr. Sutton to provide an overview of the

Law Enforcement Officers Pension System (LEOPS) Report for Emergency Medical Services. In fiscal year 2026, the Commissioners approved an actuarial study for LEOPS to consider having the County's Emergency Medical Services (EMS) employees, including paramedics and Emergency Medical Technician (EMT) B’s, participate in the LEOPS pension plan. The plan is a richer plan with a reduced number of years to reach full retirement. This means LEOPS costs more than the plan for which most of our employees are eligible. However, with some of our neighboring jurisdictions' EMS participating in LEOPS, the goal is to remain competitive and to allow the recruitment and retention of the most qualified medical professionals possible. The actuarial study was conducted by Bolton. A summary of their report was provided for review. Discussion took place. On a motion by Commissioner Price, and seconded by Commissioner Fithian, the Commissioners unanimously approved to continue the official application process with the state to ascertain if we qualify to be entered into the state LEOPS retirement program for 2028 for the Paramedics and EMT-Bs.
