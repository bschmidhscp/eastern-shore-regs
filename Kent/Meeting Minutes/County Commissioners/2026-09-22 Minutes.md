# **<u>PETE LANDON, DIRECTOR, OFFICE OF EMERGENCY SERVICE, DAN MATTSON, DIRECTOR, PUBLIC WORKS ENVIRONMENTAL OPS DIVISION</u>** 

<u>U.S. Geological Survey, Chester River Flood Gaging Station - Quaker Neck Landing:</u> The U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) Groundwater and Streamflow Information Program supports communities nationwide by providing reliable, real-time water data essential for flood forecasting, emergency response, and long-term resource planning. As part of this mission, the USGS has proposed the installation of a new streamflow and flood-monitoring gauge on the Chester River, located on the pier at the Quaker Neck Landing site. This gauge will enhance Kent County’s ability to monitor river conditions, anticipate flood risks, and strengthen situational awareness during severe weather events. Importantly, the installation, operation, and maintenance of this gauge will be completed by USGS at no cost to Kent County. All equipment, labor, and ongoing support will be fully funded through the USGS Groundwater and Streamflow Information Program. Once operational, the Chester River gauge will provide continuous, publicly accessible data through the USGS National Water Information System (NWIS). This information will benefit emergency services, local planners, environmental managers, and the community by improving flood preparedness, supporting river-related decision-making, emergency preparedness, and contributing to broader Chesapeake Bay watershed monitoring efforts. Discussion took place. On motion by Commissioner Price, and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved the US Department of the Interior, US Geological Survey, to install a

# **<u>JAMIE WILLIAMS, DIRECTOR, ECONOMIC AND TOURISM DEVELOPMENT</u>** 

<u>FY2027 Destination Marketing Organization Grant Agreement: Each year, a grant</u> agreement is executed between the Maryland Department of Commerce and the Maryland Tourism Development Board to distribute funding through the Maryland Office of Tourism Development (OTD) to each of the state’s Destination Marketing Organizations (DMOs). Funding allocations are determined using a formula that considers increases in local lodging tax revenue, the level of county investment in tourism marketing, and year-over-year increases in qualifying tourism marketing expenditures. For FY2027, Kent County received an increase of $33.00, bringing the total grant award to $37,279.00. The increase in lodging taxes collected is based on a 3-year average (FY2023-2025), and we should begin to see an increase in the FY2028 grant award when the increase in FY2025 & FY2026 lodging taxes is considered. On motion by Commissioner Price, and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved and signed the grant agreement. 

Ms. Williams provided an update on the Comprehensive Economic Development Strategy (CEDS) plan.

# **<u>PUBLIC HEARING</u>** 

County Commissioners Ronald H. Fithian, Albert Nickerson, and John F. Price were in attendance. Shelley L. Heller, County Administrator; G. Robert Mowell, County Attorney; Lacey M. Townsend, County Clerk; and Roni L. Thomas, Deputy Clerk II were also present. 

On a motion by Commissioner Price, and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously agreed to open the public hearing. 

G. Robert Mowell, County Attorney, read the protocols and the public hearing notice into the record. The public hearing was duly advertised in the Kent County News on September 17, 2026. The rules for a public hearing were in effect. 

# **<u>CODE HOME RULE BILL NO. 4-2026 PURCHASING</u>** 

The public hearing commenced at 10:14 a.m. in the County Commissioners’ Hearing Room, R. Clayton Mitchell, Jr., Kent County Government Center, 400 High Street, Chestertown, MD. A public hearing to discuss Code Home Rule Bill No. 4-2026, Purchasing, AN ACT to rescind and replace in its entirety Chapter 49, “Purchasing,” of the Public Local Laws of Kent County.  For the purpose of modernizing the purchasing laws of Kent County by increasing delegated purchasing thresholds and references to current purchasing systems, codifying certain protections and prohibitions in county contracts, and clarifying procedures to resolve disputes. 

Mr. Mowell stated that during the introduction, the Commissioners had asked for a comparison between the current legislation and the newly proposed legislation. Joanne McNamara, Director, Grants and Contracts, gave a presentation that supplied this information and offered additional background information on the proposed legislation. Discussion took place. 

# <u>PUBLIC COMMENTS / WRITTEN COMMENTS</u> 

Citizens were invited to sign the testimony log and comment during the public hearing. No comments were made, either spoken or written, before or during the hearing from the Citizens. Jamie Williams, Director, Economic, Tourism, and Development, provided testimony in support of the proposed legislation. Ms. Williams read the Kent County Mission Statement and Kent County Vision into the record to establish that the proposed legislation correlates with them. Dan Mattson, Director, Public Works, provided testimony in support of the proposed legislation. 

On a motion by Commissioner Price and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously agreed to close the public hearing at 10:32 a.m. and return to regular session. The legislative bill is scheduled to be considered for a vote on Tuesday, October 6, 2026.
