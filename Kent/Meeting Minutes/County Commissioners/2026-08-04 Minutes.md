# **<u><mark>JOANNE MCNAMARA, DIRECTOR, GRANTS AND CONTRACTS, AND CARRIE KLEIN, DEPUTY DIRECTOR, PUBLIC WORKS</mark></u>** 

<u><mark>FY2027 Program Open Space Allocation:</mark></u> <mark>Maryland Department of Natural Resources (DNR) Program Open Space (POS) provides annual grant allocations to each of Maryland's counties and Baltimore City for park acquisition and development projects. POS funding may be used to cover 90% of the costs of the proposed projects, with a 10% match required by the local government. In FY2027, Kent County received a POS allocation of $171,621.00 in new funds, which will be available to the County after July 1, 2026. Due to the passage of HB717, Kent County can now use an unencumbered balance of $203,857.00 from its prior-year POS allotments reserved for new parkland acquisitions toward park development projects. These two factors combined now present a total of $375,478.00 in currently available POS funding that can be used for the FY2027 year and beyond ($171,621.00 in new FY2027 funds, combined with the $203,857.00 in prior acquisition allocations that can now be used for parks development). Parks & Recreation seeks to use these funds to complete a project identified as a need by the Parks & Recreation Advisory Board (PRAB) and in the Kent County Land Preservation, Parks and Recreation Plans (LPPRP), adding lighting to the softball field at Worton Park. The project may also include completion of an additional dugout cover, as funds allow, after the softball lighting project is completed. The estimated cost of softball field lighting ranges from $350,000.00 to $400,000.00. As such, Parks & Recreation proposes a total POS project budget of $410,000.00 to cover the completion of the softball field lighting and account for potential increases in material costs between now and the FY2027 spring softball season. To contribute toward project costs and reduce cost impact to the County, Kent County Parks and Recreation (KCPR) also proposes to request that DNR revert a current open POS project to design and build a restroom at Toal Park (Galena) – a project with a $105,000.00 budget ($94,500.00 POS grant funds and $10,500.00 County match) – and instead put these funds toward the Worton softball field lighting project. The request was presented to the Commissioners at this time as a contingency request, due to the timing of the Toal Park reversion and the availability of POS acquisition funds that are now available to be used. It is noted that although the County must make a commitment of a 10% match to move the project ahead for DNR approval, the project does not have to be completed in FY2027, allowing for County match funds to be appropriated either in FY2027 or an upcoming fiscal year, so long as the commitment to appropriation is made for purposes of submitting the requests to DNR. Ms. MacNamara and Ms. Klein provided further information regarding the POS projects and the deductive reasoning for handling them in this manner. A discussion took place. No decision was made on these projects because the Commissioners requested additional information to make a more informed decision, as this wasn’t a time-sensitive decision. Ms. MacNamara and Ms. Klein are to gather further information and return to discuss the details with the Commissioners at an upcoming meeting.</mark>

<u>Residential Solid Waste Disposal Permits - Six Month Extension:</u> The current Solid Waste Permits ('stickers') are set to expire on December 31, 2026. Late in the FY2027 budget process, Public Works became aware that the Midshore Regional Recycling Program (MRRP) would be revising the fee structure, including an increase in regular tipping fees and the imposition of a $30.00 surcharge per mattress received. Data from previous years and recent months, prior to the new MRRP fee structure, indicate that the solid waste program spends significantly more than it brings in from the current coupon program. While action is needed regarding permits, it would be most appropriate for that to occur after a thorough cost evaluation is completed. Any changes or updates to the permit program should occur at the same time as any potential changes to fees/coupons, etc., associated with the program, so that residents and attendants can adapt to all changes at once and PW staff can accurately track their impact. Appropriate Public Notice must be given for any changes to permits and/or fees, so that residents and attendants have advance notice and an 'adjustment period' to minimize disruption to the Solid Waste Operation. Public Works proposes extending the validity of current permits by six months, making them valid through June 30, 2027. This will allow several months for the collection and analysis of relevant data, and the development of potential recommendations for revisions to current policies/fee structures/etc. Public Works would then present various options/alternatives early in Calendar Year 2027, along with backup data justification, for the

# **<u>SHELLEY L. HELLER, COUNTY ADMINISTRATOR</u>** 

<u><mark>Indivior Direct Settlement, Opioids Implementation:</mark></u> On July 1, 2026, a letter from the State of Maryland Office of the Attorney General (OAG) was received inviting Kent County to join the Indivior Settlement. Kent County is estimated to receive $1,672.86 (25%) in direct funds and $3,011.16 (45%) in abatement funds for an estimated total of $4,684.02. The State recommends using the same percentages as previously agreed upon, and the local jurisdictions are agreeable. Maryland’s estimated potential total share, to be divided among the State and its subdivisions, is approximately $1,550,375.19 (about 2.11 percent of the national abatement amount). The settlement also provides injunctive relief that will help protect Marylanders from future harm. The County Administrator has previously signed settlement documents electronically and is requesting approval to allow signing authority for this settlement as well. The Participation Package, the letter from the State of Maryland Office of the Attorney General (including Exhibit A), and the Subdivision Participation and Release Form (Exhibit G) were provided for review. On motion by Commissioner Price, and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved participating in the New National Opioids Settlement in association with Indivior Settlement and to delegate signature authority to the County Administrator, allowing the signing of the Subdivision Participation and Release Form.
