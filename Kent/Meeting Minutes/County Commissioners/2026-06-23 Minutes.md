# **<u>HILARY SIAKOR-SIRLEAF, WARDEN, KENT COUNTY DETENTION CENTER AND DAN MATTSON, DIRECTOR, PUBLIC WORKS</u>** 

On May 13th, Public Works and Detention Center Staff conducted a detailed walkthrough of the facilities to review existing conditions and discuss potential short, medium, and long-term improvements to address the HVAC system issue. The intent of this walkthrough was to identify any immediate needs and to begin developing a long-term Capital Improvement Plan for the building.  During this walkthrough, several issues related to the building's HVAC system were identified, with varying stages of need. 

Public Works coordinated a subsequent inspection with representatives of Johnson Controls to evaluate the building's mechanical equipment and control systems, and to identify upgrades that would yield the greatest efficiency. This inspection was performed on June 1st. During both walkthroughs, temperature and humidity were identified as immediate concerns in the Detention Center's administrative areas (Warden's Office and main office). Temperatures in these areas are currently severely affected by external weather conditions, and existing HVAC systems are unable to stabilize climate control. Further, humidity and condensation issues will, at times, create wet floors that present slip hazards to staff. After reviewing options with Johnson Controls, it was determined that the most efficient way to correct these issues would be to install two 'mini-split' units to provide more effective climate control in these areas. Public Works coordinated proposals for these installations, in the amounts of $21,228.75 & $14,272.19, for a total project cost of $35,500.94, split between two proposals. Detention Center Staff reviewed available funding sources with the Finance Office and confirmed that the necessary funding is currently available in the Salaries & Benefits budget, which is projected to go unspent in FY2026. Public Works has confirmed that all work can be completed by Johnson Controls before the end of FY2026. Warden Siakor-Sirleaf and Mr. Mattson requested that the County Commissioners approve the reallocation of $35,500.94 from the Detention Center Salaries and Benefits budget to the Detention Center Capital Budget to fund the two installations. On a motion by Commissioner Price and seconded by

# **<u>SCOTT BOONE, DIRECTOR, INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY</u>** 

Kent County is developing an Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy in response to a growing number of departments requesting to use AI tools in their daily operations. While these technologies offer opportunities to improve efficiency and service delivery, they also introduce new risks related to data security, privacy, and inconsistent use across departments. To protect county information, ensure responsible adoption, and provide a unified, secure platform for all staff, Kent County is establishing a countywide AI policy. This policy will guide the selection, access, and use of AI, ensuring that all departments benefit from the technology while maintaining compliance, security, and public trust through our Microsoft-managed environment. Human Resources reviewed the policy and suggested changes, which were implemented. On a motion by Commissioner Price and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved the Kent County Government Responsible AI Policy Version 1.0 and adopted Resolution No. 2026-14 as presented. 

# **<u>ROBERT GAST, CHIEF FINANCE OFFICER, AND ASHLEY ALBIA, TREASURY ACCOUNTANT, OFFICE OF FINANCE</u>** 

The current $10 million CD with Pinnacle Bank matured at the beginning of June. The Office of Finance issued a request for proposals for six-month investment options for these funds. The objective is to maximize the return on public funds while maintaining appropriate liquidity. The Finance Office reached out to seven banks to ask for the options they were willing to offer. Out of the seven banks, three did not submit an offer for consideration. An Investment Bid Summary was provided for review. Details in the Chesapeake Bank and Trust proposal include a component on community investment and economic development in Kent County, which was recommended that the board consider before approving the staff recommendation. It is anticipated that the Federal Reserve will maintain current interest rates through the end of 2026. On a motion by Commissioner Price and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved the investment of $10.5 million for a six-month period at a fixed interest rate of 4.03% in Pinnacle Financial Partners Certificate of Deposit Account Registry Services (CDARS).
