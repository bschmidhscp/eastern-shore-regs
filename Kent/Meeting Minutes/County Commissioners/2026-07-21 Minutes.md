# **<u><mark>CARLA GERBER, DIRECTOR, PLANNING, HOUSING, AND ZONING</mark></u>** 

<u><mark>Kent County Housing Study – Recommendation for Selection of Consultant:</mark></u> <mark>In FY2026, Kent County Department of Planning, Housing, and Zoning received grant funding in the amount of $30,000.00 from the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD) and $13,000.00 from the Upper Shore Regional Council, for a total project budget of $43,000.00, to conduct a comprehensive countywide housing study to determine current and projected housing needs in Kent County with detailed analysis by income demographics and other population-based characteristics. After grant funding was secured, a team comprised of the Directors of Planning, Housing, and Zoning, Economic & Tourism Development, Local Management Board, and Grants and Contracts, developed a Request for Proposals to identify a qualified consultant vendor to undertake this study. A significant response was received with seventeen proposals submitted. The review team selected the top three highest-scoring proposals for interviews and submitted a recommendation for a consultant to the Commissioners for consideration. The recommended vendor, Urban Partners, is a Philadelphia-based consulting practice with 46 years of experience providing planning, housing, and economic development consulting to communities across the nation. While Kent County received a number of highly qualified proposals from experienced vendors, the above vendor scored highest amongst the committee for the mix of desired skills, experience, and methodology and approach responsive to Kent County’s needs and our rural character. Discussion took place. On motion by Commissioner Price, and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved the recommended consultant, Urban Partners, to conduct a comprehensive countywide housing study and the delegation of signature authority for the contract to the County Administrator as requested.</mark>

# **<u>DAN MATTSON, DIRECTOR, PUBLIC WORKS</u>** 

<u>Rock Hall Dredging - Tipping Fees Contract Award:</u> The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (USACE) is currently planning the Rock Hall Harbor and Channel Maintenance Dredging Project for Fall 2027. However, USACE has recently expressed renewed interest in advancing the project to late 2026, provided the dredged material placement site can be prepared in time and other internal factors align with an accelerated schedule. In order for either schedule to remain viable, a permitted placement site must be available prior to the commencement of dredging. Orem Pit, owned by David A. Bramble, Inc., is the only viable local placement site with the necessary property rights, mining permit, and ability to construct the additional containment capacity required for the project. The site currently has 18,800 Cubic Yards (CY) of capacity, while the target dredge volume is 92,000 CY. The Army Corps recommends 104,200 CY to ensure the project can be fully completed. The proposed contract will ensure adequate capacity is available at the time of dredging.  It also establishes a unit price per load of hauled dredge material. A "guaranteed minimum" is established, as Bramble is undertaking significant efforts to prepare the site and requires both reassurance and cash flow to fund those activities should dredging not occur until Fall 2027. It has been structured to maximize the likelihood of achieving the maximum scope eligible for Grant Funding. Mr. Mattson provided several options for the Commissioners' consideration. Discussion took place. On motion by Commissioner Price, and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved the Agreement with David A. Bramble, Inc. for dredged material placement services associated with the Rock Hall Harbor and Channel Maintenance Dredging Project and execution of the Agreement. On motion by Commissioner Price, and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved that if we are unsuccessful in obtaining grant funding, we will go to our fund balance to pay the required amount of $250,000.00.

<u>Bayside Public Landing, Rock Hall: Commissioner Fithian spoke about a local business that</u> recently purchased a pontoon boat as a water taxi for the harbor in Rock Hall. They are inquiring about tying up and parking the boat at the public landing to load and unload passengers during the water taxi’s operating hours, which are roughly four hours a day. The owner has been taking riders to various businesses in Rock Hall Harbor, from marinas to restaurants. He has a lot of local support from businesses in the area, and they have placed advertisements on his boat. Both Commissioner Fithian and Commissioner Price have been to the public landing to check into where the Water Taxi is requesting to dock. Commissioner Fithian suggested putting up a reserved sign for the water taxi. Commissioner Price spoke about installing railings on each side of the dock for safety and installing two cleats for tying up the water taxi. Commissioner Price provided pictures for Mr. Mattson to review, helping him understand the suggested safety measures to implement at the dock for this request. Mr. Mattson spoke about forms the county requires, such as the use facilities form, as well as the need to submit insurance paperwork to the county for liability purposes. Mr. Mattson suggested drafting an agreement to present to the business owner for consideration. On motion by Commissioner Price, and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson the Commissioners unanimously approved the public works director and county attorney to work together and take a look at providing a part time spot for the water taxi to tie up and unload passengers at the County ramp, and also take into consideration the railing, cleats and signs, that need to be installed, contingent upon that there is insurance being carried by the water taxi owner to cover the County if there is any accident or injuries.

# **<u>CODE HOME RULE BILL NO. 3-2026 COMMUNITY REINVESTMENT AND REPAIR COMMISSION</u>** 

The public hearing commenced at 6:06 p.m. in the County Commissioners’ Hearing Room, R. Clayton Mitchell, Jr., Kent County Government Center, 400 High Street, Chestertown, MD. A public hearing to discuss Code Home Rule Bill No. 3-2026, Community Reinvestment and Repair Commission, AN ACT amending Chapter 15 Community Reinvestment and Repair Commission, Section 15-7 Duties; grant process; allocation process; use of funds and Section 15-8 Reporting. For the purpose of permitting the Community Reinvestment and Repair Commission to administer the grant distribution process, authorizing more than one distribution per calendar year, and making the reporting requirements consistent with state law. By repealing Chapter 15 Community Reinvestment and Repair Commission, Section 15-7 Duties; grant process; allocation process; use of funds, and Section 15-8 Reporting, and reenacting Chapter 15 Community Reinvestment and Repair Commission, Section 15-7 Duties; grant process; allocation process; use of funds, and Section 15-8 Reporting with amendments. 

Mr. Mowell provided a brief overview of the bill. Rosemary Ramsey Granillo, Director, Kent County Local Management Board, offered support and provided additional background information on the proposed legislation. Mr. Mowell stated that the public comment period would remain open until the close of business on Friday, July 24, 2026. 

# <u>PUBLIC COMMENTS / WRITTEN COMMENTS</u> 

Citizens were invited to sign the testimony log and comment during the public hearing. No comments were made, either spoken or written, before or during the hearing. 

On a motion by Commissioner Price and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously agreed to close the public hearing at 6:10 p.m. and return to regular session. The legislative bill is scheduled to be considered for a vote on Tuesday, August 4, 2026.
