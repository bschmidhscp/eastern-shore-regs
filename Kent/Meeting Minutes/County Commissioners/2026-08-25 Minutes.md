<u>Residential Stormwater Review Process Improvements: Kent County currently uses a</u> "Standard Stormwater Management Plan" for qualifying single-family residential projects, while all other projects require an Engineered Stormwater Management Plan. County Staff recently completed a review of the residential stormwater procedures utilized by neighboring counties and MDE guidance to identify opportunities to streamline the process, reduce costs, and plan preparation/review times for both applicants and County reviewers.  Proposed revisions establish two streamlined pathways for qualifying residential projects, proposed to replace the current Standard Plan: (1) a simplified fan wherein projects meeting applicable thresholds where stormwater requirements can be satisfied entirely through rooftop and non-rooftop disconnections; or (2) a standard plan wherein projects meeting applicable thresholds that require County-approved standard stormwater practices beyond disconnections. 

The proposed disturbance threshold for the simplified and standard pathways is proposed to be increased from 20,000 square feet to 30,000 square feet, consistent with the MDE model Standard Stormwater Management Plan framework and neighboring Queen Anne’s County.  The hard-limit of fifteen percent impervious coverage is proposed to be eliminated and replaced with a reliance on the Engineering Judgement of the reviewer.  This aligns with the approach Queen Anne’s County has taken for over a decade, which allows flexibility and consideration of specific field conditions for a given project. Modifications to the disturbance square footage and impervious percentage thresholds will substantially increase the number of projects that qualify for the Simplified and Standard Plan review pathways.  The newly developed Simplified and Standard plan applications are more user-friendly and decrease resident / builder reliance on design professionals. This will also simplify County reviews and inspections. Ultimately, the net result is reduced project costs associated with stormwater design, permitting, and construction on qualifying single-family residential home projects.  Average Stormwater Management review times are also anticipated to decrease because of the new forms. County Standard Stormwater Management Details, designed and implemented in late 2023, will remain in effect and serve as the basis for the simplified and standard plans. A Guidance Document has also been prepared to detail the specifics of each review pathway (simplified, standard, and engineered) as well as the responsibilities of both applicants and reviewers under each pathway. # **<u>BRIAN PEARSALL, EMERGENCY PLANNER, OFFICE OF EMERGENCY SERVICES</u>** 

<u>Resolution 2026-17, A Resolution to Adopt the 2026 Kent County Hazard Mitigation Plan:</u> The Kent County 2026 Hazard Mitigation Plan (HMP) is our community guide for evaluating risks, identifying local capabilities, and setting strategies to reduce or eliminate long-term damage from natural disasters or human-caused disasters. The mandatory creation of HMP revisions is required by regulation at 44 CFR § 201.6 and provides states and FEMA Mitigation Planners an opportunity to provide assistance and feedback to local governments, including special districts. All local, state, and tribal governments must create and update these plans every five years to remain eligible for non-emergency disaster funding from agencies such as FEMA. In Kent County, the County and incorporated municipalities, including Betterton, Chestertown, Galena, Millington, and Rock Hall, participated as stakeholders in the 2026 Kent County HMP creation. 

Key Components of the 2026 Kent County HMP include: 

-Risk Assessment: Identifies past hazard events, profiles local threats (such as floods or severe storms), and maps vulnerable areas or populations. 

-Capability Assessment: Reviews existing local laws, policies, staff resources, and funding tools currently available to handle disasters. 

-Mitigation Strategy: Sets clear goals and lists specific actions—such as elevating buildings, updating zoning laws, or public education—to protect property and lives. 

-Plan Maintenance: Outlines how the community will track progress, evaluate success, and formally renew the document every five years. 

Core Benefit of the 2026 Kent County HMP: 

-Grant Eligibility: HMP adoption qualifies Kent County, Betterton, Chestertown, Galena, Millington, and Rock Hall for pre- and post-disaster hazard mitigation project grants. Once the Kent County 2026 Hazard Mitigation Plan is successfully adopted by the County Commissioners of Kent County via Resolution 2026-17, OES will facilitate the towns of Betterton, Chestertown, Millington, and Rock Hall to do the same, ensuring Kent County's full compliance with 44 CFR § 201.6. On motion by Commissioner Price, and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved to adopt Resolution 2026-17, A Resolution to adopt the 2026 Kent County Hazard Mitigation Plan.
