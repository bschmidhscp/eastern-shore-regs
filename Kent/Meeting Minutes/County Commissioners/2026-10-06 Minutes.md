<u>Melba Taylor, National Federation of the Blind of Maryland - White Cane Awareness Day, October 15, 2026, Proclamation: On October 15, White Cane Awareness Day is</u> celebrated. While it was not uncommon throughout history for blind people to use a stick or cane to navigate, society largely didn't accept that blind people could travel by themselves until recently. In the 1960s, the National Federation of the Blind became a leader in fighting for the rights of the blind and in pioneering innovative training programs using the white cane. The United States Congress adopted a joint resolution in 1964 designating October 15 of each year as White Cane Safety Day and recognizing that white canes enable blind people to travel safely and independently. While the white cane does keep blind people safe (because drivers and other pedestrians can easily see it), it is also a tool they use to explore and navigate our environment. For this reason, the emphasis of White Cane Safety Day has shifted over time from safety to independence and equality, which is why it is important to recognize this day for its role in raising awareness. The proclamation will be placed in the mail to Ms. Taylor.

# **<u>CARLA GERBER, DIRECTOR, PLANNING, HOUSING, AND ZONING</u>**

LaBella Associates, DPC, on behalf of Chestertown Road Solar 1, LLC and New Leaf Energy Inc., in accordance with COMAR 20.79 pre-application consultation requirement, submitted to the Department of Planning, Housing, and Zoning, materials for a proposed 5 MW utility-scale solar energy system that will occupy – including panels, fencing, and landscaping – 39.6 acres. The submitted materials include a concept site plan depicting the proposed solar array, perimeter fencing, and outlines of the landscape buffer; and a draft Environmental Review Document that summarizes consistencies with the Kent County Comprehensive Plan and the county-wide standards for utility-scale solar energy systems, as well as information on the cultural and natural features of the site and surrounding area. On September 1, 2026, the Commissioners met in closed session to discuss the senior housing project at the former elementary school site in Millington. The deed and real property transfer agreement from the County to the Town contains a reversion clause that could be exercised by the County within three years if certain conditions were not met, and after five years if the housing project was not completed. The Commissioners agreed to extend the time by five years. An agreement was prepared, but Millington has indicated that the investors are concerned about the reversion if conditions are not met and are requesting that the reverter be removed entirely or extended to a flat ten-year deadline to complete the project.
