# **<u>OCTOBER 28, 2025, SPECIAL WORK SESSION HELD</u>** <u>:</u> 

A special work session was held on October 28, 2025, at 11:00 a.m., at the Comptroller's Office in Annapolis, Maryland, with staff members Ben Seigel, Dani Depietro, Stephen Harrington, and Michael Bayrd.  Commissioner Ronald H. Fithian; Commissioner John F. Price; Shelley L. Heller, County Administrator; Pat Merritt, Chief Officer of Finance; Dr. Mary McComas, Superintendent, and Kreigh Kirby, Interim Director of Finance, Kent County Public Schools; and Kent County’s lobbyist, Jonathan Carpenter, with Greenwill Consulting Group, LLC., were also in attendance. The public was offered a phone number allowing them to listen to the meeting. 

<mark>This meeting was scheduled in response to a letter of invitation sent to Brooke Leirman, Esq., Comptroller of Maryland, regarding the State Spending Series: School Construction Report Dated September 2025.</mark> 

<mark>Topics that were discussed include, but are not limited to:</mark> 

<mark>1. Share local data and perspectives about Kent County’s facilities and fiscal constraints;</mark> 

<mark>2. Explore possible adjustments or refinements to the cost share formula;</mark> 

<mark>3. Identify possible models or best practices from other jurisdictions that could help Kent County with more equitable access to state investment; and</mark> 

<mark>4. Discuss how the Comptroller’s office might assist in elevating awareness and legislative interest in addressing these funding inequities.</mark> 

The special work session concluded at 12:30 p.m.

<u>Proclamation - Operation Green Light: Rose Osborn, Administrative Specialist, of</u> Economic and Tourism Development, gave an overview of the Operation Green Light project. Staff hosted two meetings to discuss future events in Kent County. <mark>America’s counties have a long and proud history of serving veterans. This legacy continues to this day, as work with federal, state, and local partners ensures that former service members have access to the resources they need to thrive. This Veteran’s Day will celebrate the fifth anniversary of Operation Green Light. The National Association of Counties (NACo) and the National Association of County Veterans Service Officers (NACVSO) have invited the nation’s 3,069 counties, parishes, and boroughs to join in showing support for veterans by lighting up buildings in green from November 4-11, 2025. # **<u><mark>PUBLIC WORKS</mark></u>** 

<mark>Dan Mattson, Director, Public Works, and Sam Leaverton, Conservation Planner at Kent Soil and Water Conservation District (KSWCD), presented a request. The request is that Kent County make a used 3/4-ton pickup truck from its fleet, which is scheduled for replacement, available to KSWCD.  KSWCD proposes that the terms of this arrangement be mutually agreeable. KSWCD is willing to assume all responsibility for the vehicle, including costs for title transfer, inspection, insurance, maintenance, and fueling. There are currently two (2) 2016 Ford F250s available for resale, through enterprise, donation, or sale to KSWCD. Both are from the Roads division and have a combined mileage of slightly over 105,000 miles. Enterprise has estimated the value of each vehicle to be approximately $12,500.00. KSWCD has inspected the vehicles and confirmed that they meet the request. The replacement vehicles 'freeing up' the two subject trucks were received in early October, so immediate sale/donation to KSWCD is available. On a motion made by Commissioner Price and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved the donation of a 2016 Ford F250 from County Roads to Kent Soil and Water Conservation District.</mark>

# **<u>PLANNING, HOUSING, AND ZONING</u>** 

<mark>Carla Gerber, Acting Director of Planning, Housing, and Zoning, presented the first of two letters and preliminary reports for the Certificate of Public Convenience and Necessity (CPCN) for solar projects. Application of American Kestrel Solar Project for the installation of a 5 MW utilityscale solar energy system on Chestertown Road. On August 8, 2025, ARM Group, LLC, on behalf of Clean Capital Holdings, LLC, in accordance with COMAR 20.79 pre-application consultation requirement, submitted to the Department of Planning, Housing, and Zoning, materials for a proposed 5 MW utility-scale solar energy system that will occupy – including panels, fencing, and landscaping – 39.43 acres. The submitted materials include a concept site plan depicting the proposed solar array, perimeter fencing, and outlines of the landscape buffer; and a draft Environmental Review Document that summarizes consistencies with the Kent County Comprehensive Plan and the county-wide standards for utility-scale solar energy systems, and information on the cultural and natural features of the site and the surrounding area. The site is on Chestertown Road near Fairlee. The 104.94-acre property is zoned Agricultural Zoning District (AZD). In accordance with COMAR 20.79.01.05, the applicant has requested a preliminary report from Kent County containing (a) a statement or finding whether the proposed project is consistent with the current comprehensive plan of the county or municipal corporation; (b) a statement or finding whether the proposed project is consistent with the current zoning ordinance of the county or municipal corporation; and (C) suggestions for improving or modifying the application before submission of the application with the Public Service Commission. Discussion took place.  On a motion made by Commissioner Price and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved the letter as presented.</mark>

# **<u>HUMAN RESOURCES</u>** 

<mark>Michael Sutton, Director of Human Resources, discussed an amendment to update the Kent County Employee Handbook. The previous Anti-Harassment policy has not been revised since being published in 2017. The Human Resources (HR) department is hosting the Local Government Insurance Trust (LGIT) to provide all County employees with “refresher" training on harassment. As a means of providing training, LGIT reviews the current County Anti-Harassment policy to recommend any necessary changes, such as regulatory or compliance updates. HR, in conjunction with Matt Peter, LGIT Executive Director and Attorney, has amended this policy to include revised protected classes and language changes. On a motion made by Commissioner Price and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners approved the amended Anti-Harassment policy and adopted Resolution 2025-19.</mark>
