# County Commissioners minutes 2025-10-07

October 7, 2025 

# **<u>SPECIAL WORK SESSION HELD ON OCTOBER 7, 2025</u>** 

A budget work session was held on October 7, at 4:00 p.m., with the following present: Commissioner Ronald H. Fithian, Commissioner John F. Price, and Commissioner Albert H. Nickerson. Shelley L. Heller, County Administrator, G. Robert Mowell, County Attorney, and Pat Merritt, Chief Finance Officer, Office of Finance, were also in attendance. 

Pat Merritt, Chief Finance Officer, Office of Finance, shared financial information. Then, the challenges of funding the proposed new Kent County Middle School project—the project's cost, debt service, Blueprint funding, the general fund budget FY2020 vs. FY2026, and funding opportunities—were discussed. Kent County Public Schools and its Board of Education were represented by Dr. Mary McComas, Superintendent; Kreigh Kirby, Interim Director of Finance; Dan Divilio, Public Relations and Communications Specialist; Trish McGee, President; Frank Rhodes, Member; Francois Sullivan; and Dr. David Lever, Consultant. The work session ended at 5:39 p.m. 

# **<u>REGULAR SESSION</u>** 

A meeting of the County Commissioners of Kent County was called to order at 6:00 p.m., with Ronald H. Fithian presiding, Albert H. Nickerson, and John F. Price. The meeting was convened with the Pledge of Allegiance. Commissioner Fithian asked everyone to observe a moment of silence to remember Blanche Scott. Ms. Scott was a postmaster in Worton for 40 years. 

Shelley L. Heller, County Administrator, and G. Robert Mowell, County Attorney, were also present. 

# **<u>APPOINTMENTS</u>** 

<u>Maryland Department of Transportation, Consolidated Transportation Program (CTP) Tour:</u> Commissioner Fithian welcomed Samantha Biddle, Acting Secretary, Will Pines, Administrator of Field Operations State Highway Administration (SHA); Melissa Williams, Director of Planning, and Program Development Maryland Department of Transportation Authority (MDTA); <mark>Lynn Winchell-Mendy, Deputy Director of the Office of Local Transit Support M</mark> aryland Transit Authority <mark>(MTA); Rhashad Johnson, Director of Field Operations M</mark> aryland Vehicle Administration <mark>(MVA); Sean N. Hammer, Deputy Director, Office of Regional Aviation Assistance M</mark> aryland Aviation Administration <mark>(MAA); f</mark> or the annual CTP tour. 

Ms. Biddle briefly stated the Federal Government Shutdown and its effect on Maryland families. Governor Wes Moore is taking action to support and protect all Marylanders affected by the lapse in federal funding by ensuring protection for as long as possible for federal workers and vital federal programs. Resources for Unemployment Insurance, Housing, Utilities, and loan programs can be found on the Governor’s website. 

Ms. Biddle gave a brief overview of the Draft FY2026-FY2031 Maryland CTP: 2026 State Report of Transportation, which details MDOT’s six-year capital budget. The Secretary’s Office (TSO); the MAA; the Maryland Port Administration (MPA); the Motor Vehicle Administration (MVA); the SHA; the MTA; and authorities that are part of the MDOT, including the MDTA and the Washington Metropolitan Area Transit Authority (WMATA); offered division updates. September 15, 2025, on behalf of Ken Fender District 2 Engineer, Darlene Koch submitted documentation highlighting SHA’s FY2025 system preservation accomplishments and FY2026 priorities. The Commissioners thanked MDOT and SHA for their efforts with former and current projects throughout the County. Senator Steven Hershey, Jr., and District 36 Delegates, Jay A. Jacobs, and Steven Arentz, discussed the need for enhanced maintenance for Chester River Bridge, MD 290 north of Galena, and safety concerns with U. S. Route 301 toll diversion at MD 313. 

The audience offered no comments. Discussion took place. The Commissioners also invited comments from the Mayors and Councils of Kent County’s Municipalities. Positive comments were offered, especially to Mr. Fender and the SHA team. Mayor of Chestertown, David Foster, offered gratitude and acknowledged SHA’s work. Tyler Carpenter, Mayor of Galena, requested a meeting with SHA to discuss pedestrian safety and additional traffic lights. Bob Resele, Town Manager for Rock Hall, expressed concerns about the lack of crosswalks in the town. Mr. Fender provided an update on the MD 301 and Route 405 construction projects aimed at reducing accidents in the area. 

<u>District 36 Delegation: Senator Hershey and Delegates Jacobs and Arentz spoke regarding</u> the upcoming 2026 Maryland General Assembly legislative session and listened to the Commissioners' concerns and requests. The delegation also offered insight for each respective committee in which they are seated, including energy, permits, housing, solar, and areas of accountability, and a summary of last year's efforts to close the State’s budget deficit and the effects on taxpayers. 

Commissioners Fithian and Price stressed the effect of the wealth formula's unfavorable impact on the burden to Kent County’s taxpayers and the financial constraints of funding the Blueprint. Last year’s legislative bill tried to close the funding gap. The bill ultimately failed; however, some steps were taken to receive preauthorization in the state’s capital budget. The Board requested support to continue legislative efforts to increase the State’s cost share for constructing a new middle school. 

Commissioners’ Requests: Commissioner Price made a motion to amend the agenda to permit a motion to build a new school. Commissioner Nickerson seconded the motion, and the vote was unanimously approved. Commissioner Price motioned to sign a letter of intent to build a new Kent County Middle School on the current site in Chestertown, enabling the Kent County Board of Education to continue progress with preconstruction bids without delay. The letter of intent will have a contingency clause requiring final approval from the Commissioners before any site work. This final approval will be contingent, pending fair and equitable State funding cost share that the Commissioners deem appropriate and consistent with other counties with similar total wealth. Commissioner Nickerson seconded the motion, and the vote was unanimously approved.

# **<u><mark>PUBLIC WORKS</mark></u>** 

<mark>Carrie Klein, Deputy Director of Public Works, requested contingency funds for $16,800.00 to remove property debris at 8669 Georgetown Road. The property is a public health hazard due to trash and debris. The Department of Health raised the issue after resident complaints, which led to a Court Order on October 4, 2024, giving the property owner 30 days to clean it up. The site remains in poor condition. Despite outreach from County staff, the owner has not cleaned the property. In August 2025, quotes for the cleanup came in, with the lowest from Danny McHenry Backhoe & Paving Inc. at $16,800.00. This includes five 30-cubic-yard dumpsters at $2,000.00 each, plus costs for removing four boats and four RVs, and $1,200.00 for site restoration.</mark>
