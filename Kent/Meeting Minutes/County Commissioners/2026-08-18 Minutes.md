# **<u>HILARY SIAKOR-SIRLEAF, WARDEN AND ROLAND SHEPPARD, DIRECTOR OF OPERATIONS, KENT COUNTY DETENTION CENTER</u>** 

<u>Kent County Detention Center - Prime Care Contract:</u> Kent County is responsible for providing comprehensive medical and behavioral health services to all individuals housed at the Kent County Detention Center. Prime Care Medical, Inc. has served as the County’s contracted healthcare provider since July 17, 2023, under the existing Comprehensive Health Services Agreement. Prime Care was recently awarded Contract #RFP-26-006 through a competitive procurement conducted by Cecil County, Maryland. Kent County now seeks to “piggyback” on that competitively bid contract, replacing the current agreement with a new Comprehensive Health Services Agreement effective July 1, 2026. Under the proposed agreement, Prime Care will continue providing medical care and mental health services, adding Medication-Assisted

The new agreement establishes a four-year term ending June 30, 2030, with three optional one-year renewals subject to mutual written agreement. # **<u><mark>JOANNE MCNAMARA, DIRECTOR, GRANTS AND CONTRACTS</mark></u>** 

<u><mark>Approval for use of Opioid Restitution Funds:</mark></u> <mark>Kent County receives Opioid Restitution Funds through the national opioid settlement, which are held separate and apart from County funds in a Special Revenue Fund. Use of these funds are governed by the terms of the opioid settlement agreements, which specify that funds may only be used for authorized purposes related to the treatment, prevention, or mitigation of harms from the opioid use epidemic. Kent County receives grant funding from the Maryland Office of Overdose Response (MOOR) to support costs related to the provision of Medication Assisted Treatment (MAT) medical services in the Kent County Detention Center, a program mandated and required by law, and which provides treatment for opioid use disorder amongst our incarcerated population. As the County has closed out its grant period that was aligned with the FY2026 fiscal year period, the MOOR office determined that a portion of our contractual costs with the University of Maryland Medical Center to provide MAT telemedicine and telecounseling services were not reimbursable with their grant funds. This has resulted in a shortfall of $5,268.19 between the expenses incurred to provide MAT services to incarcerated individuals at the Kent County Detention Center and the grant funds available for reimbursement of these expenses. Ms. McNamara requested use of the County's Opioid Restitution Fund to cover the balance of those costs. This is an allowable use of funds under the terms of the opioid settlement agreement, and the ORF Advisory Committee has unanimously recommended use of ORF dollars for this purpose. On motion by Commissioner Price, and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved the use of County Opioid Restitution Funds to supplement the balance due of $5,268.19 for the Medication-Assisted Treatment (MAT) services in the Kent County Detention Center.</mark>
