# **<u><mark>SPECIAL RECOGNITION</mark></u>** 

<u><mark>Heat Safety Week, May 18 – 22, 2026: Pete Landon, Director, Office of Emergency</mark></u> <mark>Services (OES), provided background information regarding Heat Safety Week. The National Integrated Heat Health Information System (NIHHIS) has designated May 18-22, 2026, as National Heat Safety Week, a week-long campaign to raise awareness of the dangers of extreme heat and promote heat safety actions nationwide. Individuals across Kent County are encouraged to take action to protect their neighbors, colleagues, and loved ones, including pets, from the dangers of extreme heat. Extreme heat now kills more people in the United States than any other weather-related hazard, and as climate change accelerates, heatwaves are becoming more frequent, longer lasting, and more severe. Heat-related illnesses are preventable, and public awareness is among the most powerful tools we have to protect our community members, especially outdoor workers, the elderly, children, and those without access to a cooling environment. Commissioner Fithian read the proclamation into the record and presented it to Mr. Landon.</mark> 

<u><mark>Emergency Medical Services (EMS) Week – Improving Outcomes Together, May 17 – 23, 2026: Pete Landon, Director, Office of Emergency Services (OES), provided background</mark></u> <mark>information regarding EMS Week. In 1974, President Gerald Ford authorized EMS Week to celebrate EMS Practitioners and the important work they do in our nation’s communities. Kent County and the surrounding communities are partnering to celebrate EMS Week, May 17-23, 2026, to recognize the important contributions of EMS practitioners in safeguarding the health, safety, and well-being of our communities. EMS Week is an annual observance in the United States that celebrates the work of emergency medical services professionals, including paramedics, EMTs, and other prehospital care providers. Commissioner Nickerson read the proclamation into the record and presented it to Mr. Landon.</mark>

# **<u><mark>SHELLEY L. HELLER, COUNTY ADMINISTRATOR</mark></u>** 

<mark>Ms. Heller presented a request for a letter of support for the Kent County Public Library Chestertown Branch roof replacement. The library began pursuing funding for the roof replacement in 2024 and secured support from elected officials and partner organizations. An initial application was approved by the state and has been generously carried forward by the Maryland State Library Agency (MSLA) to the next fiscal year. Since the initial application, other quotes have been received that are significantly below the original estimate. The MSLA has recommended that the requested amount and support remain the same as the original request to account for potential overages, and that a simplified process be used rather than revising the request to the lower amount. The final step is the letter of certification for matching funds. The MSLA has informed the library that it may release any remaining unused funds and is required only to match the state funds expended; accordingly, the letter states the maximum amount budgeted rather than a minimum amount. Natalie Hagan, Executive Director, and Chris Walmsley, Chief Operations Officer, Kent County Public Library, were in attendance to provide additional information. On motion by Commissioner Price, seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved to sign the letter of support for the Kent County Public Library, Chestertown Location, Roof Replacement as submitted.</mark>

# **AGRICULTURAL LAND PRESERVATION DISTRICTS** 

County Commissioners Ronald H. Fithian, Albert Nickerson, and John F. Price were in attendance, as were Shelley L. Heller, County Administrator, G. Robert Mowell, County Attorney, and Holly Baldwin, Associate Planner of Planning, Housing, and Zoning. 

On a motion by Commissioner Price, seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved going into a public hearing. 

G. Robert Mowell, County Attorney, stated that the public notice was duly advertised and he read it into the record. The rules for a public hearing were in effect. 

The public hearing commenced at 6:33 p.m. to discuss the voluntary creation of three Agricultural Land Preservation Districts. Holly Baldwin, Associate Planner of Planning, Housing, and Zoning, provided a summary of each proposed district and stated that the Kent County Planning Commission and the Agricultural Preservation Advisory Board recommended approval of the applications as follows: 

1. #25-01 – Duck Puddle Farming Enterprises, LLC, wish to create an Agricultural Preservation District on their 88.098-acre farm located on Carroll Clark Road, Massey, in the First Election District. The farm consists of 87 acres of crop land. All the soils are considered Class I or II. There is no dwelling on the property. The farm is zoned Agricultural Zoning District (AZD) and is adjacent to the Priority Preservation Area. The farm is adjacent to over 2,200 acres of agricultural preservation districts and easements. It is outside the 10-year water and sewer plan service areas. 

2. #25-02 – Jennifer Wright and Charles Kealey wish to create an Agricultural Preservation District on their 199.72-acre farm located at 10412 Augustine Herman Highway, Chestertown, in the Third Election District. The farm consists of 147.1 acres of crop land. Approximately 66% of the soils are considered Class I, II, or III. There is one dwelling on the property. The farm is zoned Agricultural Zoning District (AZD) and is within the Priority Preservation Area. The farm is adjacent to over 4,900 acres of agricultural preservation districts and easements. It is outside the 10year water and sewer plan service areas. 

3. #25-03 – Robert and Amy Crow wish to create an Agricultural Preservation District on their 83.502-acre farm located at 30189 Duck Puddle Road, Kennedyville, in the Second Election District. The farm consists of 66 acres of crop land, and approximately 92% of the soils are considered Class II & III. There is one dwelling on the property. The farm is zoned Agricultural Zoning District (AZD) and is within the Priority Preservation Area. The farm is adjacent to over 9,000 acres under easement. It is outside the 10-year water and sewer plan service areas.
