# **<u>OFFICE OF EMERGENCY SERVICES</u>** 

<mark>Pete Landon, Director of Office of Emergency Services (OES), Brad Russum, Chief of Communications and Kent County Chief Association Liaison, and John “Otis” Darling, representing the Kent County Chief Association, requested funding for the Volunteer Fire Companies’ Length of Service Award Program (LOSAP). Mr. Landon also noted that Danny Quinn and Steve Bisbee, LOSAP coordinators for the Kent County Fire Chiefs, were in the audience in case a question arose that they could assist with. In 2005, the County adopted the LOSAP on behalf of the Fire Chiefs Association and all Volunteer Fire and EMS companies in Kent County. Over the years, the program's cost has increased significantly, nearly doubling, due to both growth in the number of eligible volunteers and the effects of inflation. Despite these rising costs, LOSAP remains a vital tool for recruitment and retention. In FY2025, the LOSAP program cost $164,986.00, and in FY2026, the price increased to $172,931.00. Historically, the program’s cost has been as low as $82,000.00 per year; however, it has steadily increased over time. To support the accuracy and integrity of the program, the Kent County Fire Chiefs have designated a group of volunteers to maintain accurate rosters throughout the County. Discussion took place. On a motion made by Commissioner Price and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved $50,469.00 out of the FY2025 fund balance to pay for the Volunteer Fire Company’s LOSAP plan for FY2025. On a motion made by Commissioner Price and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved $2,873.00 out of the FY2026 fund balance to pay for the Volunteer Fire Company’s LOSAP plan for FY2026.</mark> 

# **<u><mark>OFFICE OF FINANCE</mark></u>** 

<mark>Pat Merritt, Chief Finance Officer of the Finance Department, discussed investing the County’s surplus funds. Periodically, the County solicits bids to invest surplus County funds. In October, the department issued a request for proposals for investment instruments to manage the surplus funds. Three responses were received, and the investment opportunities included three options: 1) Variable interest rate instrument tied to the federal funds rate, where the funds are immediately available, 2) Fixed interest rate for a term of six months, and 3) Fixed interest rate for a term of twelve months. As always, the goal is to maximize the County’s return while balancing the need for liquidity. While variable interest rates tied to the federal funds rate are currently higher than fixed rates, it is anticipated that the Federal Reserve will lower interest rates by at least 0.25% at its December meeting and that additional rate cuts are likely to occur during 2026. Discussion took place. The department recommended investing $10,000,000.00 for a period of six months at a fixed interest rate of 3.79% in the Pinnacle Banks Certificate of Deposit Account Registry Service</mark>

# **<u>COUNTY ADMINISTRATOR</u>** 

Shelley Heller, County Administrator, presented the 2026 Maryland Association of Counties (MACo) Legislative Committee Nominations – Member and Alternate for discussion. <mark>The 2026 General Assembly Session is just around the corner, and it is again time to nominate county elected officials to serve on MACo’s Legislative Committee. Each jurisdiction has a member and an alternate who serves on the committee. Counties with a county executive and council may have a member and an alternate for each branch (those members will split their county’s vote, with a halfvote each, so that MACo maintains the “one county, one vote” rule). There is no requirement to change the member and alternate each year. In 2025, Commissioner Nickerson served as a member, and Commissioner Price served as an alternate. The Board can choose to keep the same individuals as representatives or make a change. On a motion made by Commissioner Nickerson and seconded by Commissioner Price, the Commissioners unanimously approved to keep Commissioner Nickerson as a member and Commissioner Price as an alternate.</mark>

# **<u>MATTER NO. 2: PERSONNEL</u>** 

Topic to be discussed: Qualifications and job performance of candidates for the Compensation Review Commission. 

Reason for closing: The qualifications of certain individuals to fill personnel vacancies are confidential and should be protected. 

Code Home Rule 8-2025, relating to the Administration of Government, will take effect on November 21, 2025. This legislative bill includes a summary discussing the establishment of the Compensation Review Commission (CRC). The CRC will consist of five (5) citizens appointed by the County Commissioners, including one Chair. All members of the Commission must have been residents of Kent County for at least three (3) years prior to their appointment. They should possess an outstanding reputation for civic pride, integrity, responsibility, and professional or business ability. Additionally, no member should have any direct or indirect financial interest in the compensation and allowances of a County Commissioner. 

A discussion occurred regarding each Commissioner's prospective candidates for the Kent County CRC. Ms. Heller and G. Robert Mowell, County Attorney, discussed any potential conflicts of interest or any that would disqualify a candidate for the Board’s consideration.

<u>FY2026 First Quarter Report: Inspector Pearsall conducted routine inspections of sixty-five</u> (65) out of sixty-six (66) alcohol establishments. In addition to the inspections, Main Wine and Rock Hall Brewing Company were revisited to deliver informational/educational materials, which included the required parking lot signage prohibiting consumption in that area. Great Oak Landing was found to be in violation for failing to verify photo IDs. A verbal warning was issued by Inspector Pearsall, placing the establishment on notice of the violation and the ramifications if it were to happen again. The KATE team continues to seek responsible 18 to 20½ year-olds for compliance checks for tobacco retailers, as well as responsible 21 to 27 year-olds to serve as “Mystery Shoppers” who will use caution or thank-you cards to check alcohol establishments for compliance, ensuring they ask for proper identification from their patrons.
