<u>Kim Silpath, President, Nathan's Story, Childhood Cancer Organization</u> 

-Proclamation, Childhood Cancer Awareness Month, September 2026: Kim Silpath, President of Nathan's Story, a Childhood Cancer Organization, requested a Childhood Cancer Awareness Proclamation for September 2026. Ms. Silpath was accompanied by Amy Dolgos and Patty Williams, family members of children who have been diagnosed with cancer. Nathan's Story has been a non-profit in Kent County for fourteen years, founded by a local child, (Ms. Silpath’s son) Nathan Silpath, when he was informed in 2012 that he had relapsed with osteosarcoma (bone cancer) at the age of eleven. Despite multiple cancer diagnoses, he attended Kent County schools, was an above-average student, received his varsity letter and pin in golf as a freshman, and received the Sportsman Award that year at Kent County High School. Through the whole process, all Nathan wanted to do was go to school, make a difference, and raise awareness for other children while enduring all this himself. Nathan wanted to start a nonprofit to make a difference for other children diagnosed with cancer. With this proclamation, Ms. Silpath hopes to bring much-needed awareness to Kent County that kids get cancer too, and that everyone needs to help make a difference and create community support for children and their families in these most difficult times. Nathan passed away at sixteen on January 9, 2018, not only fighting for himself but also for all the other children diagnosed with cancer. Nathan's Story continues to keep his legacy and dreams alive through his incredible work. Commissioner Fithian read the proclamation into the record, and the Commissioners presented the proclamation to Ms. Silpath, Ms. Dolgos, and Ms. Williams.

<u>Dan Mattson, Director, Public Works, Roads Division - Recognition of Life-Saving Efforts by County Staff: On September 10, 2026, a County Roads Division employee, Billy Skinner,</u> experienced a severe medical emergency while working with a Roads crew performing asphalt patching along Irelands Corner Road in Galena. Members of the crew immediately recognized that Mr. Skinner was experiencing an unusual and potentially serious medical event. They quickly called 911, provided first aid, remained with and assisted the employee until Kent County Emergency Medical Services personnel arrived and assumed his care. The Roads employees responding on scene included Shawn Rideout, Curtis Merritt, Mitchell Gunther, Bryan Jackson, Steve Yeager, Joseph Johnson, Josh Goldstein, and Ty Bottomley. Several members of the crew brought experience from their service as volunteer firefighters, which contributed to the crew's ability to quickly recognize the seriousness of the situation and respond appropriately. Kent County Emergency Medical Technician Amanda Cox and Paramedic Sarah Torrey subsequently provided emergency medical care and transported the employee to Christiana Hospital in Delaware, where he received immediate cardiac treatment. Notably, Mr. Skinner had originally been scheduled to perform roadside mowing that day - a task that is performed alone and often in isolated areas of the County. A mechanical issue with his assigned equipment led him to join the asphalt crew instead. That unforeseen change placed him alongside coworkers who recognized his condition and immediately requested and provided assistance when the emergency occurred. The quick recognition and actions of the Roads crew, followed by the response and medical care provided by Kent County EMS personnel, were critical to the employee receiving the emergency treatment he needed. Their actions demonstrate the importance of preparedness, teamwork, and employees looking out for one another in the workplace. Mr. Mattson felt it was imperative to recognize the efforts of the county staff and the emergency medical crew who ensured Mr. Skinner's safety and survival. 

Commissioner Fithian asked for an update regarding the Millington Wastewater Treatment Plant. Mr. Mattson provided an update accordingly. Discussion took place. 

# **<u>JOANNE MCNAMARA, DIRECTOR, GRANTS & CONTRACTS</u>** 

<u>Contract with EnergyWise, LLC for Kent County Residential Energy Equity Program and Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) with Rebuilding Together for Kent County Residential Energy Equity Program: In April 2026, Kent County received a grant award under the Maryland</u> Energy Administration Residential Energy Equity program to provide energy efficiency and electrification measures to eligible low- to moderate-income residents in Kent County free of charge. Funding can be used to hire Building Performance Institute (BPI) certified energy audit professionals to perform energy audits on the residences of County-approved income-eligible residents. After the audit, grant funds will be used to support implementation of the recommended energy efficiency measures, which may include air sealing and insulation upgrades, HVAC replacement and repairs, duct sealing and insulation, water heater replacement and improvements, appliance replacements, lighting, window repairs and replacements, and other enabling repairs that may be needed to enable other energy efficiency measures to be installed. Following notice of award, Kent County published a Request for Proposals (RFP) to solicit proposals from qualified BPI-certified energy auditors. Two proposals were received in response to the RFP: one from a local business with an office in Kent County and a second from a business with an office in Towson, MD. Ms. McNamara recommended approval of a contract with the local Kent County provider, EnergyWise LLC. This entity is not only local and will be more able to quickly schedule

<u>Approval of FY27 Grant Application to Continue Residential Energy Equity Program:</u> Maryland Energy Administration (MEA) has opened the application period for continued funding of its Residential Energy Equity Program through the FY2027 Funding Opportunity Announcement. Kent County Local Management Board (KCLMB), in partnership with Rebuilding Together Eastern Shore, has just launched its local program using FY2026-awarded funds and is seeking to apply for the subsequent round of funding to continue this program in future years. The FY2027 funding opportunity provides for similar grant program parameters and the ability to provide energy efficiency and electrification measures to eligible low to moderate-income residents in Kent County free of charge. This may include air sealing and insulation upgrades; HVAC replacement and repairs; duct sealing and insulation; water heater replacement and improvements; appliance replacements; lighting; window repairs and replacements; and other enabling repairs needed to install other energy-efficiency measures. Award recipients will be able to use funds over a 36-month (3-year) period, anticipated to run from mid-2027 through mid-2030. Due to the extended time period and ability to use funds over a 3-year period, the KCLMB sought authority to apply for a total of $488,750.00 in funds. This represents a significant increase over the current award ($151,800.00) and will allow the County to provide home energy-efficiency repairs and improvements for an estimated additional 20-40 homes at no cost to residents. On motion by Commissioner Price, and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved the FY2027 Residential Energy Equity grant application as presented.
