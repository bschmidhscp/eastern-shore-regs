# **<u><mark>G. ROBERT MOWELL, COUNTY ATTORNEY, LEGISLATIVE SESSION</mark></u>** 

<u><mark>Code Home Rule 3-2026 Community Reinvestment and Repair Commission - Introduction, First Reading: Chapter 15 of the Public Local Laws of Kent County governs the</mark></u> <mark>Community Reinvestment and Repair Commission and the use of special funds that may only be used for, among other things, community-based initiatives intended to benefit low-income communities. As our laws are written, grant applications are due on May 1, and awards may only be made once each calendar year. The amended legislation seeks to remove this barrier, authorize the Commission to regulate the distribution of grants, and bring them before the County Commissioners for approval on a rolling basis. Mr. Mowell provided an overview of the legislative bill. On motion by Commissioner Price, and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved to introduce Code Home Rule Bill 3-2026 and for the Bill to be scheduled for Public Hearing.</mark>

<u><mark>Resolution 2026-15 For the Purchase of Property for the Millington Wastewater Treatment Plant: The current design of the conveyance pipe that will run from the existing</mark></u> <mark>Millington WWTP site to the proposed WWTP site requires the establishment of an easement across a private property within the Town of Millington known as 373 Cypress Street (the "Property"). Attempts to negotiate an easement on the property failed. On October 28, 2025, the County Commissioners of Kent County authorized the Director of Public Works to negotiate a sale of the Property to Kent County. On May 29, 2026, Mike and Jeneen McClain, the owners of the Property, agreed to sell it to Kent County for $550,000.00, with a targeted closing date of July 21st. This would expedite the easement process and mitigate design and permitting delays. Resolution 2026-15 authorizes the sale of the Property. On motion by Commissioner Price, and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved to adopt Resolution 2026-15 as presented.</mark>

# **<u><mark>BRAD RUSSUM, CHIEF OF COMMUNICATIONS AND PETE LANDON, DIRECTOR, OFFICE OF EMERGENCY SERVICES</mark></u>** 

<u><mark>Portable Radio Replacement Agreement: The majority of Kent County's portable radio</mark></u> <mark>fleet was purchased in 2011 and has reached the end of its expected service life. These radios are now approximately 15 years old, and Motorola Solutions has announced end-of-support for the APX6000 platform. As a result, repair options, replacement parts, and manufacturer support will become increasingly limited, creating operational and reliability concerns for public safety personnel. This request provides funding to replace fifty law enforcement portable radios with the Motorola N70 platform. The project includes fifty portable radios, fifty spare batteries, fifty desktop chargers, and fifty remote speaker microphones. The new radios include SmartConnect and Critical Connect capabilities, allowing users to maintain communications over LTE and WiFi networks when operating outside normal radio coverage areas. Discussion took place. On a motion by Commissioner Price, and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners</mark>

<u>Pay Tel Incarcerated Individual Telephone / Video / Tablet Services: Kent County</u> Detention Center and Pay Tel Communications, Inc. previously entered into a Master Services Agreement (MSA) on April 23, 2019, which had been amended over time and was set to continue through June 18, 2030. Both parties now seek to replace the prior agreement in its entirety with an updated MSA that reflects current regulations, operational needs, and technological updates to inmate communication services. Under the new MSA, Pay Tel Communications replaces the 2019 contract and continues Pay Tel’s exclusive provision of inmate communication services at the Kent County Detention Center through June 18, 2030, with automatic renewal provisions. # **<u>CARLA GERBER, DIRECTOR, PLANNING, HOUSING, AND ZONING</u>** 

<u>Maryland Agricultural Land Preservation Foundation FY2027/2028 Easement Application Rankings:</u> The Maryland Agricultural Land Preservation Foundation (MALPF) is accepting applications for the FY2027/2028 easement acquisition cycle. Ms. Gerber provided an overview of the process and the necessary steps to apply for a MALPF easement. The applications were evaluated using the County's approved Easement Prioritization Formula, and the rankings were reviewed by the Agricultural Preservation Advisory Board. State regulations require that the County Commissioners review and approve the ranked applications submitted to MALPF for appraisal and consideration for easement acquisition. State regulations also require that the ranked list remain confidential until the cycle is declared closed. On motion by Commissioner Price, and seconded by Commissioner Nickerson, the Commissioners unanimously approved the Maryland Agricultural Land Preservation Foundation, also known as MALPF, easement applications as ranked. 

<u>2026 Maryland Department of Transportation Annual Transportation Priority Letter:</u> Kent County sends a letter to the Secretary of the Maryland Department of Transportation (MDOT) each year outlining the County’s transportation priorities. This year is no different. The Department of Planning, Housing, and Zoning has reached out to all Towns, and changes requested by responding Towns are included in the draft letter. The Planning Commission reviewed the draft at its June meeting and recommended approval. Discussion took place.
